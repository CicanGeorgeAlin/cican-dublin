CICAN is an independent, evidence-led visual archive documenting historic places and the overlooked details of Dublin’s built environment. It is created by CICAN GEORGE ALIN.

Why the archive exists

Dublin’s heritage is not limited to famous landmarks. Historic streets, building details, monuments, railings, post boxes, inscriptions, industrial traces and small pieces of street fabric can all help tell the story of a place. CICAN DUBLIN aims to document these discoveries, connect them to reliable historical evidence and make their locations easier to explore.

The archive is a growing research and fieldwork project. It does not claim that every heritage feature in Dublin has already been found, photographed or verified.

How the research works

Each physical discovery is given a unique archive record. Historical statements are checked against traceable references, and the archive keeps the evidence status visible rather than presenting every lead as established fact.

  • Verified: the record meets the project's verification requirements for the claims being made.
  • Partially verified: some details are supported, while other points still need checking.
  • Research lead: a possible discovery or archival clue that has not yet met the standard for a verified catalogue record.

Source-derived map coordinates are distinguished from founder-recorded GPS observations. A coordinate may identify a wider building or site rather than the exact position of a small feature; field confirmation remains outstanding unless the record explicitly says otherwise.

Photography and visual preservation

Online reference photographs, contextual images and original founder photographs are treated as different kinds of evidence. Image attribution and licensing details are retained where available. A location is not marked as founder-photographed simply because a third-party image exists.

Original field photographs may later be preserved alongside carefully prepared line-art interpretations. These visual records support future archive publications and themed heritage colouring books. An illustration is a derivative artwork, not a substitute for the original photograph or historical evidence.

Founder

CICAN GEORGE ALIN is the founder and creator of CICAN DUBLIN. The project is developed as an independent digital archive, with research, mapped discoveries and fieldwork notes published through the CICAN DUBLIN website.

Open-data attribution and image rights

The NIAH Building Survey record data used in this archive is sourced from the National Inventory of Architectural Heritage, Department of Housing, Local Government and Heritage. It is licensed for reuse under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) licence. Attribution: “Contains Irish Government Data licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) licence.”

This attribution applies to NIAH dataset record data. It does not automatically cover photographs linked from NIAH records, third-party mapping, or other third-party material. Such images are not reused unless their separate rights and licence have been checked and recorded.

Evidence before claims

The guiding principle is straightforward: do not invent a historical detail, location, photograph, source or verification result. Where evidence is incomplete, the uncertainty should remain visible. Corrections and better references should improve the archive over time.
