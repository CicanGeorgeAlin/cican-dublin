# ALWAYS IN THE LIGHT — CICAN DUBLIN Discovery Expansion Strategy

**Project:** CICAN DUBLIN  
**Founder:** CICAN GEORGE ALIN  
**Adopted:** 29 October 2026  
**Status:** Research and catalogue operating strategy  
**Principle:** Hidden in plain sight. Evidence first. Every discovery brought into the light.

## 1. Mission

CICAN DUBLIN is not merely a photographic guide to famous landmarks. It is an evidence-first discovery archive of the historic environment at every scale: buildings, monuments, small street objects, architectural details, industrial remnants, archaeological traces, historic surfaces, inscriptions, public art and overlooked places.

The goal is to help residents and visitors recognise the history around them: an object they may have passed for years, without knowing its name, date, function, maker, story or significance.

The ambition is to build an exceptionally comprehensive archive of Dublin's documented and field-discoverable heritage. Do not claim that it is already the largest or first of its kind; demonstrate quality and coverage through transparent records, evidence, provenance and measurable progress.

## 2. Geographic expansion

The current 195-record catalogue is a starting collection concentrated in Dublin 1, Dublin 2 and Dublin 3. It is not a complete survey and must not be treated as the boundary of the project.

Expand systematically across:
- Dublin City, district by district and neighbourhood by neighbourhood.
- County Dublin beyond the current central-city footprint.
- Each relevant local-authority area, with the authority and administrative geography recorded accurately.
- Historic villages, town centres, coastlines, river corridors, canals, rail corridors, parks, estates, industrial areas, suburbs and rural townlands.

Maintain explicit geographic coverage tracking. A district is not complete merely because a batch has been researched. Distinguish areas searched, areas partially surveyed, areas with records, areas awaiting fieldwork and areas not yet researched.

## 3. Discovery categories — minimum research checklist

Search for individual objects as well as whole buildings and sites. Categories are prompts for investigation, not claims that an object exists at a particular location.

### A. Historic public realm and street fabric
- Granite kerbs, setts, paving flags, historic steps and thresholds.
- Jostle stones, bollards, ward stones, milestones and boundary markers.
- Coal-hole covers, cellar lights, pavement prisms, gratings, drains and manhole covers where historical significance can be established.
- Boot scrapers, water troughs, drinking fountains, horse-related fittings and hitching posts.
- Historic post boxes, lamp standards, railings, street signs, street name plates and cast-iron furniture.
- Tram tracks, track plates, cable infrastructure and surviving transport fittings.
- Historic shopfronts, painted advertisements, ghost signs and trader names.

### B. Architectural micro-details
- Date stones, foundation stones, plaques, inscriptions and carved names.
- Door surrounds, fanlights, knockers, hinges, locks, steps and threshold details.
- Carved stone, capitals, corbels, gargoyles, rainwater heads, downpipes and roof finials.
- Boundary walls, gate piers, gates, railings, entrances and former estate features.
- Historic windows, industrial glazing, vents, air bricks and unusual construction details.
- Individual details on protected and unprotected buildings, with parent-child links where appropriate.

### C. Industrial, engineering and utilities heritage
- Mills, factories, warehouses, chimneys, engine houses and workshops.
- Gas, electricity, water and sewerage infrastructure.
- Substations, valve chambers, pumping stations, reservoirs, waterworks and historic utility covers.
- Cranes, dock equipment, quay fittings, mooring rings, bollards and port infrastructure.
- Bridges, culverts, locks, weirs, aqueducts, canal fittings and river engineering.
- Railway stations, signal boxes, mileposts, bridges, tunnels, goods yards and remnants.
- Tram depots, former tram routes and surviving physical evidence.

### D. Archaeology and older landscapes
- Recorded monuments, medieval fabric, churchyards, grave markers and fragments.
- Historic wells, holy wells, boundary banks, earthworks and field systems.
- Former gates, walls, town defences, lanes and settlement traces.
- Archaeological discoveries and excavated features, clearly distinguishing buried/removed remains from visible features.
- Historic landscape features and place-name evidence.
- Former structures known from excavation or maps but not presently visible; label as non-visible or uncertain, never imply that visitors can see them.

### E. Civic, social, cultural and memorial heritage
- Memorials, statues, fountains, plaques, public art and commemorative objects.
- Historic schools, hospitals, fire stations, police stations, markets, baths and public facilities.
- Sports grounds, handball alleys, club buildings, bandstands, shelters and recreation structures.
- Religious buildings, denominational and community landmarks, graveyards and processional routes.
- Sites connected to labour, migration, trade, education, public health and everyday life.

### F. Coastal, maritime and rural heritage
- Lighthouses, beacons, sea walls, piers, slipways, landing places and coastguard remains.
- Harbours, fishery structures, bathing places, lifeboat heritage and maritime equipment.
- Historic farm buildings, cottages, bridges, estate walls, gate lodges and landscape structures.
- Quarries, kilns, lime works, water mills and rural industrial remains.

## 4. Research-source programme

Use multiple independent source families. A catalogue entry or search snippet is a lead, not proof that the physical feature survives or is correctly located.

### National and statutory sources
- National Inventory of Architectural Heritage (NIAH) and the Historic Environment Viewer.
- National Monuments Service Sites and Monuments Record, Historic Environment Viewer, files and published guidance.
- Record of Protected Structures and Architectural Conservation Area documentation.
- Dublin City Development Plan and public-realm / historic-ground-surface schedules.
- County and local-authority development plans, heritage plans, conservation plans and archaeological strategies.
- Industrial heritage surveys and relevant spatial datasets.

### Local authority and archaeological archives
- Dublin City Archaeological Archive and excavation reports.
- Dublin City and County Archaeology GIS Project and its mapped licensed-activity reports.
- Local-authority archaeology, conservation and heritage offices.
- Planning applications, conservation reports, environmental impact assessments and archaeological monitoring reports.
- Public-realm improvement records and historic street-furniture schedules.

### Libraries, archives and universities
- Dublin City Archives and Dublin City Library & Archive.
- Digital Repository of Ireland.
- National Archives of Ireland.
- National Library of Ireland catalogues, maps, photographs and manuscripts.
- UCD Digital Library and other university repositories.
- Dublin Port Archive and relevant transport, utility, institutional and company archives.
- Historic newspapers, directories, maps, valuation records, engineering drawings and photographs.

### Specialist and community sources
- Heritage organisations, local history societies, conservation groups and museum collections.
- Published books, peer-reviewed articles, theses and specialist architectural / archaeological surveys.
- Local-history websites and discussion forums as leads only; verify claims independently.
- Community submissions and resident knowledge, recorded with attribution and checked against evidence.

## 5. The no-photo-is-no-problem rule

A missing online photograph is not a reason to reject a discovery.

Every evidence-supported discovery should proceed through the research and location workflow even if:
- No image can be found.
- The only available image is low quality.
- The available image is contextual rather than exact-object.
- The available image is copyrighted or has unclear reuse rights.
- The feature is too small or obscure to have been photographed before.

Where no suitable reference image is available:
1. Keep the discovery in the catalogue if evidence and identity requirements are met.
2. Record its photograph status as awaiting founder photography.
3. Create a precise photography brief describing the angles and details needed.
4. Record the best defensible location and coordinate provenance.
5. Add access, safety, privacy and permission notes.
6. Put it into a practical fieldwork route when appropriate.

Do not invent, scrape or reuse images without permission. A rights-restricted image may be described as a research lead, but it is not an archive asset.

## 6. Evidence and identity rules

- Give each distinct physical object or feature a unique archive ID.
- Use parent-child relationships for details belonging to a larger structure, without collapsing individually significant objects into a generic building record.
- Search for existing IDs, aliases and duplicates before creating a record.
- Separate a surviving physical object from a former building, an archaeological find, a historic location and a conjectural site.
- Record what is known, what is inferred, what is disputed and what remains unknown.
- Use primary and official sources where possible; seek independent corroboration for important claims.
- Preserve source URLs, titles, authors, dates, page references, access dates and relevant excerpts.
- Do not infer age, maker, function or historical significance from appearance alone.
- A mapped point is not a field GPS fix. Preserve original grid references and coordinate transformations.
- Record whether a site is public, private, restricted, inaccessible or uncertain.

## 7. Image and photography protocol

Maintain distinct states:
- **Founder original:** captured by CICAN GEORGE ALIN; requires confirmed capture and provenance.
- **Licensed exact-object reference:** shows the precise object and has verified reuse permission and complete attribution.
- **Context reference:** helps identify the location but does not show the target object clearly enough to count as its photograph.
- **Rights-pending lead:** visually useful for research, but not cleared for publication or import.
- **No reference found:** no suitable photograph located after documented search; remains a fieldwork target.

Never count an online reference image as founder photography. Never count a context image as an exact-object image. Never treat a search result thumbnail as a verified photograph. Preserve original founder files separately from A4 line-art derivatives. Create line art only from the corresponding founder-captured original unless the project explicitly establishes another rights-cleared workflow.

## 8. Fieldwork preparation

Each field target should include:
- Archive ID, object name and exact address/location description.
- Source-derived coordinates and coordinate provenance, or an explicit reason why no safe point can be set.
- What to photograph: overall view, close-ups, inscriptions, makers' marks, construction details and relationship to surroundings.
- Public viewpoint or access instructions.
- Safety, privacy, security and photography restrictions.
- Historical questions to resolve on site.
- Space for founder observations, actual capture date, GPS fix and original file reference.

Never instruct the founder to trespass, enter restricted infrastructure, cross barriers or photograph sensitive security areas. If a feature is on private property, plan only lawful public viewpoints or seek permission.

## 9. Research batch workflow

Run two parallel workstreams:

**Discovery expansion**
1. Select a geographic zone, source collection or heritage theme.
2. Extract candidate places, objects and details from primary records, maps, reports and archival catalogues.
3. Resolve identity, exact location, survival status and historical claims.
4. Check for duplicates and parent-child relationships.
5. Add approved records or preserve unresolved candidates as research leads.
6. Update geographic and category coverage.

**Visual discovery**
1. Search for exact-object images.
2. Inspect the original source page and the image itself.
3. Verify the object and location.
4. Establish photographer, date, licence, permission and attribution.
5. Register only when the project's reuse requirements are satisfied.
6. Otherwise retain the image as a rights-pending lead or record that none was found.

At the end of each batch, validate JSON/schema, unique IDs, source references, coordinates, image counts and the difference between founder originals and third-party references. Do not change the locked map baseline or redesign the website as part of research-only batches.

## 10. Measures of progress

Do not measure success only by photographs found. Track:
- Total physical-object records.
- New discoveries added per batch.
- Research leads awaiting verification.
- Records by local authority, district, neighbourhood and category.
- Records with primary-source support and independent corroboration.
- Records with defensible coordinates.
- Records awaiting founder photography.
- Founder-original photographs captured and field-verified.
- Licensed exact-object reference photographs.
- Context-only and rights-pending image leads.
- A4 line-art derivatives completed from founder originals.
- Duplicates, corrections and unresolved questions.

Never describe a zone or the whole archive as complete unless the survey scope and evidence support that statement.

## 11. Immediate operating change

Starting with the next research batch:
- Do not spend disproportionate time trying to find a photograph for an obscure object.
- Record the strongest exact-object image if available, with rights status.
- If none is found, complete the discovery record and fieldwork brief anyway.
- Allocate a substantial share of each batch to finding additional objects, including small and overlooked details.
- Search beyond the current 195 records and beyond Dublin 1–3.
- Use official schedules, archaeological reports, industrial surveys, historic maps and archival collections to uncover objects not yet represented in the catalogue.
- Keep a clear audit trail so the founder can physically visit, photograph and add each discovery.

**ALWAYS IN THE LIGHT.** The archive's purpose is to make Dublin's overlooked history visible, with evidence and respect for the real places and people connected to it.
