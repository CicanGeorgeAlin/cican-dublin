# CICAN Dublin Archive — Barnewall–Sharl Tomb, Lusk Research Batch 184

**Date:** 17 November 2026  
**Scope:** Parent-linked micro-detail research for the Barnewall–Sharl funerary monument within the St MacCullin's Church / Lusk Heritage Centre complex, Church Road, Lusk, County Dublin.  
**Status:** Research note only. Existing parent record DUB-BLD-0177 remains the building-level record. No new permanent object ID, source-registry entry, visual record or production map pin created because the complete canonical catalogue and component relationships cannot be validated in this pass.

## 1. Object identity and parent relationship

- **Object name:** Barnewall–Sharl Tomb; also described in historic catalogues as the tomb/monument of Sir Christopher Barnewall and Marion Sharl.
- **Parent:** DUB-BLD-0177 — Saint MacCullin's Church, Lusk (existing CICAN parent record).
- **Setting:** Within the medieval tower / belfry of the historic church complex at Lusk.
- **Monument type:** Renaissance table tomb with paired recumbent effigies and sculpted heraldic/inscription panels.
- **Primary research reference:** John Bradley and Heather A. King, *County Dublin Urban Archaeology Survey*, Part VIII(iv), 1988, Lusk section, pp. 265–266, Fig. 48.
- **Fingal guide entry:** Barnewall Tomb, in the medieval tower of Lusk Church.
- **Access:** Fingal County Council's 2012 archaeological heritage guide records OPW ownership and limited access via keyholder. Do not imply that the interior is freely open or that the tomb can be photographed on an ordinary walk-in visit.

## 2. Date reconciliation — death and monument are different events

The evidence supports a clear distinction:

1. Sir Christopher Barnewall died in 1575. The Dictionary of Irish Biography records his death in July 1575.
2. The monument inscription, as transcribed in the 1988 OPW-commissioned survey, includes the dates 1589 and 1575, with wording identifying 1575 as the year of death.
3. Fingal County Council's heritage guide states that Marion Sharl married Sir Lucas Dillon after Barnewall's death and that Dillon erected the monument; the guide quotes the commemorative inscription ending with “Wish well to Dillon, 1589”.
4. The Royal Society of Antiquaries of Ireland preserves George Victor Du Noyer's March 1849 drawing title, which explicitly notes the two dates, 1575 and 1589.

**Recommended archive date:** “Late 16th century; commemorates Sir Christopher Barnewall (d. 1575); inscription records 1589, associated with the monument's erection.” Avoid labelling the tomb simply “1575” as its construction date.

## 3. Physical description from the 1988 survey

The 1988 survey describes a Renaissance table tomb with high-relief effigies of a knight and a lady:
- The knight wears highly ornamented armour, described by the survey as Milanese or German in style.
- The lady wears a full gown with a broad embroidered collar and winged sleeves.
- Their heads rest on a tasseled and embroidered cushion.
- The cushion bears a Latin inscription: “SOLI LAUDES DEO / SI DEUS NOBISCUM QUIS CONTRA NOS”.
- The western end panel displays the arms of Barnewall and Sharl.
- The eastern end panel carries a strapwork-decorated plaque with the main commemorative inscription.
- The north panel carries Dillon arms and the motto “WISH WELL TO DILLONE”, together with the names of the couple's children.
- Another panel includes initials P.B. and A.L. and Barnewall/Luttrell heraldry.
- The south panel displays Barnewall and Sharl arms.

The 1988 survey records approximate measurements:
- Table top: 202 × 120 × 26 cm.
- Chest: 195 × 113 × 76 cm.
- Base: 213 × 131 × 11 cm.

These are published survey measurements, not new measurements taken by CICAN. The survey's material terminology and later descriptions are not perfectly consistent; do not state a definitive stone type without direct material analysis or a specialist conservation record.

## 4. Inscription and family-history controls

The principal inscription identifies Sir Christopher Barnewall of Turvey and states that Sir Lucas Dillon and Marion Sharl were responsible for the monument. It records that Marion married Dillon three years after Barnewall's death and commemorates their children.

The north panel preserves a Dillon motto and a list of children's names. The 1988 survey transcription contains uncertain spellings and line breaks; do not silently modernise or “correct” the inscription. If a transcription is later published, preserve the source's original reading and mark uncertain characters.

The inscription and arms provide evidence for:
- Barnewall and Sharl family identity.
- The later marriage of Marion Sharl to Sir Lucas Dillon.
- The commemorative role of Dillon.
- The monument's late-sixteenth-century date context.

They do not independently establish every detail of the family's genealogy or the exact number of surviving children. The guide and survey give a total of five sons and fifteen daughters; preserve this as what the inscription is reported to state, rather than as an independently verified family register.

## 5. Historical visual documentation

A strong historical visual chain exists:
- **1849:** George Victor Du Noyer drew the tomb and recorded its two dates. The RSAI/Discovery Programme record describes the medium as pencil on paper, with an object size of 23.7 × 30.7 cm. The catalogue also gives alternative titles referring to Sir Lucas Dillon's tomb and the tomb of Sir James Bermingham.
- **1988:** Bradley and King included a photograph as Fig. 48, captioned “Monument to Christopher Barnewall and Marion Sharl 1589”.
- **1996:** Nigel King's NCAD thesis, *The Ecclesiastical Buildings at Lusk*, is cited by the later Love Lusk heritage page as containing archival photographs of carved side panels and heraldic detail (Plate 7).
- **2007:** Edwin Rae's Trinity College Dublin collection has a digital image record described as showing the James Bermingham effigy and the tomb of Sir Christopher Barnewall and his wife.

The 1849 drawing is an archival record, not a photograph of current condition. The 1988 image and 1996/2007 image records are historical documentation. Their presence does not grant CICAN permission to reproduce the image files. Obtain and record explicit reuse terms before copying any image into the production visual archive.

## 6. Location, access and conservation cautions

The monument is located inside the historic church/belfry complex, not an outdoor grave marker to be pinned independently from the church without exact geometry.

Fingal County Council's 2012 guide states:
- Setting: within the medieval tower of Lusk Church.
- Ownership: OPW.
- Access: limited, via keyholder.

The site is also used as Lusk Heritage Centre. Fingal's visitor information describes the church complex and its exhibition, but this does not override the more specific limited-access note for the tomb interior. Confirm opening arrangements and permission with the site operator before a founder visit.

No current condition assessment has been found in this batch. Do not describe the effigies, inscriptions, heraldry or masonry as intact, damaged, restored or accessible today without a current conservation report or field inspection. Do not touch, clean, chalk, wet or rub the carved surfaces.

## 7. Founder photography brief — only with permission

If access to the tomb interior is granted, photograph:
1. Complete monument in its architectural setting, with orientation recorded.
2. Both recumbent effigies, full-length and from a safe level viewpoint.
3. Cushion inscription and carved textile/armour details.
4. West and south end panels with Barnewall/Sharl heraldry.
5. East end commemorative inscription, square-on and with raking light where permitted.
6. North panel: Dillon arms, “Wish well to Dillone” motto and family-name panels.
7. P.B. / A.L. initials and Barnewall/Luttrell arms.
8. Base, chest and table-top profiles, joints and visible repair interfaces.
9. Wider relationship to the belfry interior, without entering restricted areas or using flash if prohibited.

Capture full-resolution originals before any crops or line-art derivatives. Include a scale only if permitted and without placing it on the monument. No rubbing, tracing, contact measurement or lighting equipment should touch the historic fabric.

## 8. Relationship to other Lusk monuments — keep records separate

The same church complex contains other distinct funerary monuments described in the 1988 survey:
- Walter Dermot tomb, dated 1535x8 in the survey, with a Holy Trinity relief and marginal inscription.
- James Bermingham monument, dated 1637, with an armoured knight effigy and heraldry.
- Seventeenth-century chimney-breast plaque with heraldry and Annunciation.
- Octagonal limestone font, tentatively dated to the seventeenth century.
- Pink granite bullaun stone beside the south entrance.
- Carved stone head high on the southeast corner of the tower.

These are separate physical features and must not be merged into the Barnewall–Sharl tomb record. They may be parent-linked child records under DUB-BLD-0177 only after the full catalogue and component relationships are validated.

## 11. Conclusion and next steps

The Barnewall–Sharl tomb is a high-value, individually identifiable funerary monument within the existing Lusk church parent record. The most important clarification is chronological: 1575 is Barnewall's death year; 1589 is the date recorded on the monument and associated with its erection. The monument's paired effigies, heraldic panels, Latin motto and family inscription are documented in detail by the 1988 survey and an 1849 archival drawing.
