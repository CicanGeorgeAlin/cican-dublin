# CICAN DUBLIN — Buzzardstown / Mulhuddart Detail Research Batch 152

**Date:** 17 November 2026  
**Site:** St Mary's Church and graveyard, Buzzardstown, Mulhuddart, Dublin 15  
**Status:** Research-only. No catalogue objects, permanent CICAN IDs, source-registry entries, visual records or map pins created.

## 1. Why this site matters

The 2008 Fingal Historic Graveyards Project identifies Buzzardstown as FHG 8. The 2024 Fingal-commissioned Conservation Management Plan (CMP) and a 2021 archaeological, architectural and cultural heritage impact assessment provide later evidence to check what survives and to distinguish nearby holy-well records.

The site comprises a raised historic graveyard and church ruin. The 2024 CMP records the church as DU013-010001 and graveyard DU013-010003; the church is RPS 670 and the graveyard is NIAH 11346003. The 2008 survey's site-information box instead prints RMP DU013-01002 for the graveyard, apparently a different/shortened or erroneous reference. Use the later CMP's explicit church/graveyard identifiers as the working reference, and flag the discrepancy rather than silently normalising it.

## 2. Individual features worth recording

### A. Comins / Warren mural tablet — high-priority visual detail

The large eighteenth-century mural tablet is set into the exterior west wall of the residential tower. The 2008 survey transcribed a long inscription commemorating Denis Comins, Mary Warren, their daughter Margaret, and later generations of the Comins family. It records dates including 1675, 1709 and 1748, but the inscription is damaged and portions are illegible.

The 2024 CMP confirms the tablet survives, but says modern interventions have raised it above its historic position and it is now illegible from ground level. It is stable, but exposure to the elements is causing deterioration; removal for repair could risk further damage. The CMP recommends a suitable preservative treatment and 3D laser scan.

**Archive treatment:** candidate micro-detail attached to parent church/tower, not a freestanding monument. Record both the 2008 transcription and the 2024 condition assessment with clear dates and source attribution. Do not silently reconstruct missing words.

### B. Smaller tablet above the Comins tablet

The 2024 CMP identifies a smaller companion panel above the main tablet. Its relationship to the rolled moulding is not the same as the main tablet, and the report says it may not have been originally part of that arrangement. The CMP does not establish its inscription in the cited passage.

**Archive treatment:** photograph and describe separately only if visual review confirms it is a distinct physical panel. Preserve uncertainty about original placement.

### C. Mary Mears T-shaped cross, 1764

The 2008 survey and 2024 CMP both identify an unusual T-shaped cross dedicated to Mary Mears, dated 1764, east of the church.

**Archive treatment:** strong candidate for a separate grave-marker object, subject to complete catalogue duplicate check and a current photograph confirming survival and exact position. Do not assume its current condition from the 2008 record alone.

### D. Tower vault and wickerwork centering

The 2008 survey describes the ground floor of the west residential tower as vaulted and retaining traces of original wickerwork centering. A 2021 heritage assessment repeats the feature and gives approximate vault dimensions (external dimensions stated as 1.745m by 4.9m). The 2024 CMP confirms the residential tower survives to first-floor level and documents its substantial modern repair and earlier fire/graffiti damage.

**Archive treatment:** architectural construction detail belonging to the tower, not a separate map point. Photograph only from safe, permitted access; do not enter unstable or restricted fabric.

### E. Church remains and possible earlier phase

The 2024 CMP describes the present church as an undivided rectangular nave and chancel with a later residential tower at the west end. The eastern gable is now barely visible above ground and possible subsurface walling extends east of it. The CMP discusses a possible earlier church phase and possible early medieval origins, but these remain hypotheses rather than proven surviving features.

The CMP also notes that no grave markers appeared to be present within the tower at the time of its site visit, while burials and collapsed material remain within the church interior. The oldest graveyard section has the greatest surviving concentration of memorials around the church, especially southwest and east.

**Archive treatment:** record the surviving fabric and clearly distinguish observed remains from interpretive phasing hypotheses.

### F. Graveyard markers and conservation state

The 2024 CMP notes:
- the oldest graveyard section contains memorials on all sides of the church;
- many monuments in the southwest and southeast are obscured by ivy and vegetation;
- two railed enclosures south of the church are engulfed by ivy;
- collapsed masonry and gravestones create uneven ground and visitor hazards;
- abrasive cleaning on some headstones has damaged stone surfaces.

The 2008 survey also noted reused architectural fragments, cast-iron crosses, table tombs, broken slabs and damaged stones, with a recommendation to identify fragments and accurately record their positions before any movement.

**Archive treatment:** do not identify unidentified rubble fragments as distinct objects until their form, material and location are documented. No clearing, cleaning, moving or resetting of stones.

## 3. Our Lady's Well — do not conflate separate records

The 2008 FHG 8 entry says Our Lady's Well is on the right-hand side of the road some distance from the graveyard, and records local pattern-day traditions on 25 March, 15 August and 8 December. It provides no exact coordinate or separate monument number for this well in the FHG 8 entry.

A separate 2021 Fingal-commissioned assessment identifies **CH003, Lady's Well, Tyrrelstown**, with RMP DU013-009----, RPS 671 and NIAH 11353010. It describes a roadside vaulted well-house approached by stone steps, with two roof finials, one carved with a cross and the other an inscribed niche. It was still venerated according to that report. The assessment places it about 400m from the proposed cemetery-development area, with ITM_N 741013 and ITM_E 706926.

The FHG 8 Our Lady's Well and CH003 Lady's Well may be related in the broader Mulhuddart landscape, but the sources do not conclusively establish they are the same physical well. Do not merge them or place a pin until their identities are reconciled against the NMS record and field evidence.
