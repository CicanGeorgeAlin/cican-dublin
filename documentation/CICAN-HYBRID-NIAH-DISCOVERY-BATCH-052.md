## DUB-BLD-0286 — Henrietta Place — c.1810 warehouse

- **Official individual NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50011213/henrietta-place-dublin-dublin-city
- **Registration:** 50011213
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Technical
- **Original use:** Store/warehouse
- **Recorded current use:** Office
- **Date range:** 1800–1820 (built c.1810)
- **Date recorded:** 08/01/2014
- **Description:** Detached nine-bay three-storey warehouse, built c.1810, located on a lane off Henrietta Place. It has a hipped slate roof, stepped red-brick eaves course and cast-iron rainwater goods. The walls are random coursed rubble calp limestone with red-brick quoins. Square-headed gauged-brick window openings have granite sills and replacement six-over-six-pane timber sliding sash windows. The easternmost bay openings are wider former pitching doors with steel windows; there is a double-leaf steel gate to the laneway between this building and a second warehouse to the south.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal identifies this as a large-scale warehouse retaining its original form and fabric, with utilitarian nineteenth-century materials. Its limestone walls and red-brick dressings contribute to the industrial heritage of the area, alongside nearby linen and yarn halls and a paper-making site.

## DUB-BLD-0287 — Chapel Lane — c.1860 warehouse

- **Official individual NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50060505/chapel-lane-dublin-1-co-dublin
- **Registration:** 50060505
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural
- **Original use:** Store/warehouse
- **Recorded current use:** Store/warehouse
- **Date range:** 1850–1870 (built c.1860)
- **Date recorded:** 02/09/2014
- **Description:** Attached five-bay five-storey warehouse, built c.1860, opening onto the narrow site on Chapel Lane. The roof is pitched slate with masonry verges and rendered eaves. The front north elevation and exposed gable are faced in coursed rock-faced limestone over a cement-rendered ground floor, with limestone quoins. Upper-floor segmental-arch window openings have brick jambs and voussoirs with projecting masonry sills. Central former loading-door openings are now fitted with replacement uPVC windows. Ground-floor openings include steel grilles, two double-height segmental-headed openings with steel fire doors and roller shutters, and two additional square-headed fire doors.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal describes a Victorian warehouse associated with nineteenth-century industry. Despite incremental changes associated with continued industrial use, it retains its original form and regular openings arranged around a central vertical row of loading doors. The appraisal identifies it as a relatively rare example of industrial architecture in this part of the city.

## DUB-BLD-0288 — 21 Sycamore Street — former furniture workshop

- **Official individual NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50020082/21-sycamore-street-dublin-2-co-dublin
- **Registration:** 50020082
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Social
- **Original use:** Workshop
- **Recorded current use:** Disused (as recorded by NIAH; current status unverified)
- **Date range:** 1840–1860 (built c.1850)
- **Date recorded:** 28/02/2015
- **Description:** Attached two-bay two-storey former workshop, built c.1850 and recorded by NIAH as disused. The hipped roof is set perpendicular to the street and hidden behind a lined-and-ruled rendered parapet with cut-granite coping to the west elevation. The rendered walls have quoins and a string course above a smooth rendered plinth, with uncoursed rubble limestone visible at the plinth and brown brick visible to the north elevation. A round-headed first-floor window has a cut-granite sill and multiple-pane timber-framed window; the ground-floor window and door openings have later metal grilles and a metal door.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal notes that Thom’s Directory of 1862 describes this building, together with three adjoining buildings to the south on Dame Street, as being used by a furniture manufacturer. The repeated opening sizes, continuous plinth and lined-and-ruled elevation support its identification as a good example of nineteenth-century industrial architecture in an urban setting.

## DUB-BLD-0289 — 28 Brookfield Road — vernacular house

- **Official individual NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50080168/brookfield-road-kilmainham-dublin-8-dublin-city
- **Registration:** 50080168
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Social
- **Original use:** House
- **Recorded current use:** House
- **Date range:** 1760–1800 (built c.1780)
- **Date recorded:** 29/04/2013
- **Description:** Attached three-bay single-storey house with attic accommodation, built c.1780. A recent glazed sun-room is attached to the north-east rear elevation. The house has a pitched slate roof with rendered chimney stack, coping and cast-iron rainwater goods, clay ridge tiles and painted rubble-stone walls. Square-headed window openings have masonry lintels and granite sills with timber-framed windows. The front door is timber-battened and opens onto a granite step.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal identifies the house as an increasingly rare example of a traditional vernacular architectural style in an urban context. It retains much of its original form and fabric; its small windows and high roof pitch are characteristic of rural counterparts. Its painted rubble-stone walls contrast with the predominantly red-brick street frontage.

## DUB-BLD-0290 — National College of Art and Design — former Thomas Street Fire Station

- **Official individual NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50080582/national-college-of-art-and-design-104-108-thomas-street-dublin-8-co-dublin
- **Registration:** 50080582
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Artistic, Historical, Social
- **Original use:** Fire station
- **Recorded current use:** University
- **Date range:** 1910–1915 (built 1911)
- **Date recorded:** 04/11/2013
- **Description:** Attached five-bay three-storey former fire station, built 1911, now in use as an art college. A glazed extension was added to the rear north elevation c.2009. The building has red-brick chimney stacks behind red-brick parapets with carved sandstone capping and a central panelled sandstone block flanked by decorative scrolls. Red brick and sandstone pilasters support a carved sandstone entablature; carved sandstone platband and cornice frame the first and ground floors. The façade includes timber sash windows, an arcaded ground floor with elliptical-headed arches, carved architrave surrounds, scrolled keystones, half-glazed timber panelled doors and spoked fanlights.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal identifies this as one of four early twentieth-century city fire stations designed by city architect Charles J. McCarthy and as Dublin’s first motorised fire station. The station was planned from 1898 by Chief Fire Officer Captain Purcell. The NIAH account records responses by its crew to fires during the 1913 Lock Out, at Magazine Fort during the 1916 Rising, at the Custom House in 1921 and at the Four Courts during the Civil War. The building now forms part of the National College of Art and Design campus and has been renamed after illustrator and stained-glass artist Harry Clarke.

- **Field status:** Current condition, exact feature position, access and founder photography remain unverified.
