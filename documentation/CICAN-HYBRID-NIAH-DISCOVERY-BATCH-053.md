## DUB-BLD-0291 — Morningstar Hostel — former North Union Workhouse

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50070342/morningstar-hostel-morningstar-avenue-dublin-7-dublin
- **Registration:** 50070342
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Historical, Social
- **Previous name:** North Union Workhouse
- **Original use:** Workhouse
- **Recorded current use:** Hostel (charitable)
- **Date range:** 1875–1880 (built 1879)
- **Date recorded:** 29/10/2012
- **Description:** Detached irregular-plan multiple-bay three-storey former workhouse, built 1879, with full-height single-bay projections, a three-bay three-storey extension and single-storey block to the front (south), and later extensions to the front and rear. Now in use as a hostel. Hipped slate roof, red-brick chimneystacks, some cast-iron rainwater goods, dressed calp-limestone walls, block-and-start quoins and stepped buttresses. Square-headed windows have red-brick voussoirs and surrounds, concrete sills and replacement uPVC/aluminium windows. A stained-glass window is recorded at ground floor to the front; a chapel is recorded internally.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal records that the House of Industry stood here from 1773 and was converted into a workhouse in 1841, with extensions by George Wilkinson in subsequent decades. It describes the building as a physical reminder of the workhouse system, the Great Famine and the destitution of the surrounding decades. Its calp-limestone construction and regular fenestration contribute to the institutional streetscape.
- **Access / photography:** The NIAH records a charitable hostel. It is not an unrestricted public attraction. Do not enter or photograph residents; seek permission for non-public access and use only a lawful public viewpoint.

- **Field status:** Current condition, exact on-site position, access and founder photography are not field-verified.

## DUB-BLD-0292 — Carmichael House — pair of gate lodges

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50070345/carmichael-house-brunswick-street-north-dublin-7-dublin
- **Registration:** 50070345
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Social
- **Previous name:** Saint John's Convent
- **Original use:** Gate lodge
- **Recorded current use:** Apartment/flat (converted)
- **Date range:** 1855–1865 (built c.1860)
- **Date recorded:** 29/10/2012
- **Description:** Pair of two-storey gate lodges flanking gates and railings to Carmichael House, built c.1860 and incorporating earlier fabric. One lodge is two-bay and the other single-bay, each with an angled inside corner bay. Hipped slate roofs, red-brick chimneystacks with granite banding and clay pots, red-brick eaves courses, cast-iron rainwater goods and rubble calp-limestone walls. Timber sash windows include four-over-four, six-over-six and two-over-two pane patterns. The gateway has calp-limestone piers with pointed capping and red-brick bands, double-leaf wrought-iron gates and a pedestrian gate, with associated granite walls and railings.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal identifies the modest pair as contextual elements associated with the former Carmichael School of Medicine. It suggests they may relate to an entrance to buildings marked as a former asylum on the first-edition Ordnance Survey map. Their practical gatekeeping function and expression of security and status contribute to the streetscape; the pair retain a recognisable relationship despite alterations.
- **Access / photography:** The lodges are recorded as converted apartments and are not open for entry. Photograph only from a lawful public viewpoint; do not enter private grounds or obstruct residents.

## DUB-BLD-0293 — Saint James's Hospital — former workhouse linen factory

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50080177/saint-jamess-hospital-jamess-street-dublin-8-dublin
- **Registration:** 50080177
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Social
- **Previous name:** Foundling Hospital and Workhouse of the City of Dublin
- **Original use:** Workhouse
- **Recorded current use:** Faculty building
- **Date range:** 1710–1750 (built c.1730)
- **Date recorded:** 10/05/2013
- **Description:** Attached sixteen-bay two-storey former workhouse linen factory, built c.1730, with a recent full-width single-storey extension to the rear (west). Attached at the north end to the former workhouse master's house. Now in use as a third-level education department. Pitched slate roof, brown-brick chimneystacks and dressed calp-limestone walls. Square-headed windows have brown-brick surrounds and granite sills; small-pane timber casement and pivoting windows survive, with some eight-over-eight timber sash windows to ground floor. The main entrance has a recent glass door, steps and ramp; other openings include timber battened doors and a round-headed opening with fanlight.
- **Appraisal / significance:** Construction on the Saint James's site began in 1703 as the city workhouse. By 1730 it was used primarily as a foundling hospital and home for abandoned children. The linen factory was probably used to manufacture clothes and bedding for the workhouse. Along with the adjoining former master's house, it is one of two surviving eighteenth-century buildings identified on the campus by NIAH. Early fabric remains in the stonemasonry and timber windows. Extended and remodelled in 1999 by Moloney O'Beirne and Partners.

## DUB-BLD-0294 — Saint James's Hospital — former workhouse master's house

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50080178/saint-jamess-hospital-jamess-street-south-circular-road-dublin-8-dublin
- **Registration:** 50080178
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Social
- **Previous name:** Foundling Hospital and Workhouse of the City of Dublin
- **Original use:** Workhouse
- **Recorded current use:** University
- **Date range:** 1730–1750 (built c.1740)
- **Date recorded:** 10/05/2013
- **Description:** Attached five-bay three-storey former workhouse master's house, built c.1740, with a later three-storey flat-roofed rear return, attached at the south to the former linen factory. Now in third-level educational use. Hipped slate roof with gablets to north and south, lower hipped rear roofs and granite eaves course. Rubble calp-limestone walls, brown-brick chimneybreasts, rear arcade, square-headed windows with granite sills and surviving two-over-two timber sash windows. The north elevation has a round-arched second-floor window. The round-arched entrance has a carved granite Gibbsian surround and lintel, segmental-headed sidelights, timber-panelled door and plain fanlight.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal records that the site began as the city workhouse in 1703 and was used primarily as a foundling hospital by 1730. This former master's house and the adjoining linen factory are identified as the only surviving eighteenth-century buildings on the campus. Notable early features include its roof structure and carved granite door surround. Its survival is notable because most Irish workhouses post-date the Poor Law Unions established in 1838.

## DUB-BLD-0295 — The Ireland Institute — former Pearse family house and sculptor's premises

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50020326/the-ireland-institute-27-pearse-street-dublin-2-dublin
- **Registration:** 50020326
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Artistic, Historical, Social
- **Previous name:** Pearse & Sons Ecclesiastical and Architectural Sculptors
- **Original use:** House
- **Recorded current use:** Building misc (cultural centre)
- **Date range:** 1815–1825 (built c.1820)
- **Date recorded:** 12/03/2015
- **Description:** Terraced two-bay three-storey over-basement former house, built c.1820, now used as a centre for theatrical performances, rehearsals, exhibitions, conferences and meetings. M-profile pitched slate roof, red-brick parapet with granite coping, rendered chimneystack and clay pots. Red brick in Flemish bond to upper front elevation, moulded masonry fascia, rendered ground and basement levels and carved granite plinth. A carved limestone plaque depicts William and Patrick Pearse in relief. Upper windows include six-over-six timber sliding sashes, granite sills and first-floor cast-iron balconettes. Ground floor has segmental-headed windows with moulded surrounds, polished-granite colonnettes and marble foliated capitals. The round-headed entrance has a moulded surround, panelled entablature, cornice, fanlight, panelled timber door, granite steps and wrought-iron railings.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal states that the terrace on the north side of former Great Brunswick Street was built by 1823. The building was the birthplace of Patrick Pearse in 1879. His father James operated a monumental and architectural sculptor business at this address from 1875 until his death in 1900; his son William continued the business. The appraisal records the family's association with the 1916 Rising and notes the commemorative plaque. The building is significant for its architectural fabric and Pearse-family historical associations.
- **Access / photography:** The building is recorded as a centre for cultural events and meetings, not as an unrestricted public interior. Check current programme and access arrangements before visiting. Photograph the exterior from the public pavement; obtain permission for interior photography.
