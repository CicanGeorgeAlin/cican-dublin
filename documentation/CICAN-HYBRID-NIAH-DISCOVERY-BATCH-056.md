### DUB-BLD-0306 — Guineys, 93 Talbot Street, Dublin 1, DUBLIN

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010217/guineys-93-talbot-street-dublin-1-dublin
- **Registration:** 50010217
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural
- **Original use:** House
- **Recorded current use:** Shop/retail outlet
- **Date:** 1830–1850 (built c.1840)
- **Date recorded:** 09/11/2011
- **Description:** Terraced two-bay four-storey house, built c.1840, now in commercial use. The building has an M-profile pitched slate roof, red-brick chimneystacks and a raised parapet. Its front elevation is painted red brick laid in Flemish bond, with a rendered eaves course, channelled quoins and a masonry string course above the shopfront. Upper floors retain timber sliding sash windows with gauged-brick openings and masonry sills. The ground floor has a later panelled timber shopfront with glazed openings and steel rolling shutters.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal identifies this as a simple Georgian house adapted for commercial use in 1913 and one of the few Georgian buildings remaining on the south side of Talbot Street. It retains much of its original form and fabric, including timber sash windows and render details.
- **Access / photography:** Commercial premises. Record the exterior only from a lawful public pavement; interior access and photography require the occupier’s permission.
- **Coordinates:** Not imported. The individual page's displayed coordinate pair is not used because its CRS is not explicitly labelled.
- **Field status:** Current condition, exact feature position, access and founder photography remain unverified.
- **Photography priorities:** Full Talbot Street frontage and shopfront; Upper-floor brickwork, Flemish bond and rendered details; Timber sash windows and gauged-brick openings; Roofline, parapet and chimneystacks from a safe public viewpoint.

### DUB-BLD-0307 — Law Centre, 45 Gardiner Street Lower, Dublin 1, DUBLIN

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010165/law-centre-45-gardiner-street-lower-dublin-1-dublin
- **Registration:** 50010165
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Artistic
- **Original use:** House
- **Recorded current use:** Office
- **Date:** 1820–1830 (built c.1825)
- **Date recorded:** 04/10/2011
- **Description:** Terraced two-bay four-storey house over raised basement, built c.1825, now in office use. It has an M-profile slate roof hidden behind a parapet with granite coping, brown brick walls in Flemish bond over a chamfered granite plinth, and gauged-brick window openings with granite sills. Cast-iron balconettes survive at first-floor level. The round-headed entrance has a stucco surround and painted masonry Ionic doorcase with engaged columns, a panelled lintel cornice and glazed fanlight. Granite steps and a raised platform bridge the basement area, with railings and a granite plinth wall.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal states that the house formed part of a terrace of eight houses laid out in 1790 by Luke Gardiner and developed in the 1820s. The terrace linked the Custom House with Mountjoy Square and was initially occupied by members of the legal profession. The surviving doorcase, ironwork and overall composition contribute to the coherent Georgian streetscape.
- **Access / photography:** Office premises; no public interior access is implied. Photograph the exterior from the public pavement and seek permission for any interior photography.
- **Coordinates:** Not imported. The individual page's displayed coordinate pair is not used because its CRS is not explicitly labelled.
- **Field status:** Current condition, exact feature position, access and founder photography remain unverified.
- **Photography priorities:** Full façade and relationship to the Gardiner Street terrace; Ionic doorcase, fanlight and panelled entrance; Granite steps, raised platform and basement railings; Cast-iron balconettes, brickwork and granite plinth.

### DUB-BLD-0308 — The Granary, 20 Temple Lane South, Cecilia Street, Dublin 2, DUBLIN

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50020132/the-granary-dublin-city
- **Registration:** 50020132
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Social
- **Original use:** Store/warehouse
- **Recorded current use:** Shop/retail outlet
- **Date:** 1830–1850 (built c.1840)
- **Date recorded:** 14/04/2015
- **Description:** Corner-sited three-bay four-storey former warehouse, built c.1840, now used as a shop with apartments to the upper floors. The front elevation has a rendered parapet and rendered wall over a masonry plinth; the east elevation has a yellow-brick gabled parapet. The west elevation is in yellow brick laid in Flemish bond and includes a dual arcade at ground floor. Upper openings retain timber sliding sash windows, while the front includes a round-headed entrance and later steel doors. Granite paving and kerbstones are recorded to the front and west.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal identifies the gable-fronted elevation and arcaded ground floor as reminders of the building’s former warehouse use. Temple Lane is described as a medieval lane, formerly known as Hogg’s or Hogges Lane and later Dirty Lane. By the nineteenth century it had become more commercial, with warehouses including this building; by the 1890s it formed part of a complex used by Wood, Webb & Co. Wholesale Grocers.
- **Access / photography:** Mixed commercial/residential building. Photograph exterior details from public streets; do not enter residential or commercial areas without permission.
- **Coordinates:** Not imported. The individual page's displayed coordinate pair is not used because its CRS is not explicitly labelled.
- **Field status:** Current condition, exact feature position, access and founder photography remain unverified.
- **Photography priorities:** Corner composition showing the gabled east elevation; Arcaded ground floor and former warehouse openings; Yellow-brick Flemish-bond masonry and parapet; Timber sash windows, granite paving and kerbstones.

### DUB-BLD-0309 — Dublin City Council, Wood Quay, Fishamble Street, Dublin 8, DUBLIN

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50080528/dublin-city-council-wood-quay-fishamble-street-dublin-8-co-dublin
- **Registration:** 50080528
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Archaeological, Architectural, Historical, Social
- **Original use:** Office
- **Recorded current use:** Office
- **Date:** 1980–1985 (built 1983)
- **Date recorded:** 28/10/2013
- **Description:** Attached pair of square-plan multi-storey office towers over basements with chamfered corners, built in 1983 as the first phase of Dublin Corporation’s Civic Offices. The towers have cut-granite cladding, a granite-tiled skirt roof and recessed horizontal strip windows. Recessed ground-floor door openings have glazed doors, and a later glazed atrium connects the towers.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal records that the Wood Quay site was allocated for Dublin Corporation’s headquarters in the 1940s, but construction was delayed by the discovery of remains of the Viking city. The towers were designed by Sam Stephenson; the original proposal included two further towers to the north-west, while a later phase was designed by Scott Tallon Walker in the late 1990s. The building remains a prominent and controversial landmark in the city.
- **Access / photography:** Civic offices and associated grounds. Do not assume interior access; observe current public-building security and photography rules. Photograph from lawful public space.
- **Coordinates:** Not imported. The individual page's displayed coordinate pair is not used because its CRS is not explicitly labelled.
- **Field status:** Current condition, exact feature position, access and founder photography remain unverified.
- **Photography priorities:** Overall pair of granite-clad towers from public space; Chamfered corners and horizontal window bands; Granite cladding, skirt roof and ground-floor entrance details; The later glazed atrium linking the towers.

### DUB-BLD-0310 — 5 Parnell Square East, Dublin 1, DUBLIN

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50011017/5-parnell-square-east-dublin-1-co-dublin
- **Registration:** 50011017
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Artistic, Historical
- **Original use:** House
- **Recorded current use:** Guest house/B&B
- **Date:** 1750–1760 (built c.1755)
- **Date recorded:** 26/09/2011
- **Description:** Terraced two-bay four-storey house over exposed basement, built c.1755 as a pair with No. 6, with a three-storey rear addition. The roof is concealed behind a rebuilt red-brick parapet with granite coping. The front combines Flemish-bond red brick, a granite plinth and rendered basement walls. A plaque commemorates Senator Oliver St. John Gogarty. The entrance has a painted stone doorcase with rusticated pilasters and an open-bed pediment; cast-iron lamp posts and bootscrapers flank the approach, which is enclosed by iron railings. The NIAH record also describes surviving decorative interior plasterwork and historic room arrangements.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal describes the house as part of the historic Parnell Square Georgian streetscape. The site was leased in 1754 by Henry Darley, master mason to Dr Bartholomew Mosse, and the house was the birthplace and home of Senator Oliver St. John Gogarty. It retains significant Georgian fabric, including its doorcase, brickwork and historically sympathetic sash windows, as well as decorative interior details.
- **Access / photography:** Recorded as a guest house/B&B. Do not assume interior access or permission to photograph guests or private areas; photograph the exterior from public space and seek permission for interior views.
- **Coordinates:** Not imported. The individual page's displayed coordinate pair is not used because its CRS is not explicitly labelled.
- **Field status:** Current condition, exact feature position, access and founder photography remain unverified.
- **Photography priorities:** Full frontage and relationship to No. 6; Painted stone doorcase, pilasters and open-bed pediment; Cast-iron lamp posts, bootscrapers, steps and railings; Commemorative plaque and brickwork details from public space.
