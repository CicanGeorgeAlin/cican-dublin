### DUB-BLD-0311 — 77 Parnell Street, Dublin 1, DUBLIN

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010616/nearys-77-parnell-street-dublin-dublin
- **Registration:** 50010616
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural
- **Original use:** House
- **Recorded use:** Shop/retail outlet
- **Date:** 1840–1860 (built c.1850)
- **Date recorded:** 04/11/2011
- **Description:** Terraced two-bay four-storey former house, built c.1850, formerly used as a hotel and then as a retail outlet. The NIAH survey described it as vacant at the time of recording. It has a hipped M-profile slate roof with clay ridge tiles and two rendered chimneystacks behind a rendered parapet with squared granite coping. The south-facing front is rendered, with diminishing square-headed window openings, rendered reveals and granite sills; the openings were recorded as boarded with timber. A rendered and timber shopfront was inserted around 1960.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal describes the building as a well-proportioned part of its streetscape, slightly taller than its neighbours. It notes that subtle differences in building width and treatment add variety, and that the building appears on Brookings' 1728 map, suggesting it may incorporate earlier fabric.

### DUB-BLD-0312 — National Library of Ireland, Kildare Street, Dublin 2, DUBLIN

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50100219/national-library-of-ireland-kildare-street-dublin-2-dublin
- **Registration:** 50100219
- **Rating:** National
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Artistic, Cultural, Social, Technical
- **Original use:** Library/archive
- **Recorded use:** Library/archive
- **Date:** 1880–1895 (built 1885–1890)
- **Date recorded:** 08/08/2016
- **Description:** Attached symmetrical E-plan library over a raised basement, built 1885–1890, facing south. The composition centres on a three-stage rotunda with a top storey set back, linked to two-storey blocks and flanking pavilions, with a semicircular Doric colonnade to the front. The exterior combines ashlar granite, channel-rusticated lower pavilion walls and yellow sandstone dressings. The rotunda and pavilions have elaborate classical detailing, including Corinthian columns, pediments, carved stonework, Venetian windows and balustrades. The NIAH record also documents richly decorated interiors, including stained glass, mosaic flooring, a bifurcated stone staircase and the domed main reading room.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal places the library within the major state and cultural complex on Kildare Street, opposite the National Museum and beside Leinster House. It links the building to the Dublin Science and Art Museum Act of 1877 and the nineteenth-century movement toward public benefit. Designed by Thomas Newenham Deane and his son Thomas Manly Deane after two competitions, it is assessed as an impressive composition whose granite, yellow sandstone, marble and composite-stone finishes enrich the classical ensemble. Its interior is noted for exceptionally well-preserved late-Victorian craftsmanship and its association with the 'Scylla and Charybdis' episode of Ulysses.
- **Access / photography:** Public library. Exterior photography from public space is appropriate subject to current site rules. Interior photography, access to controlled areas and any tripod or commercial activity require the Library's permission; do not photograph readers or staff without consent.
- **Coordinates:** Not imported. The page-displayed coordinate pair is held out of the map layer pending exact national CSV row matching, explicit ITM field confirmation and accuracy assessment.
- **Field status:** Current condition, exact on-site position, access and founder photography remain unverified.
- **Photography priorities:** South-facing composition showing rotunda, Doric colonnade and flanking pavilions; West elevation on Kildare Street and granite/sandstone material contrasts; Carved classical details, columns, pediments and balustrades; Entrance lettering, gates and forecourt relationship, subject to site rules.

### DUB-BLD-0313 — 2 Lennox Place, Dublin 8, DUBLIN

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50110133/2-lennox-place-dublin-8-dublin
- **Registration:** 50110133
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Artistic
- **Original use:** House
- **Recorded use:** House
- **Date:** 1790–1810 (built c.1800)
- **Date recorded:** 22/03/2018
- **Description:** Attached three-bay two-storey house over a raised basement, built around 1800 as a pair with the house to the north, with a return to the rear east elevation. It has a pitched slate roof concealed behind a brick parapet with cut-granite coping, brick chimneystacks with clay pots and cast-iron rainwater goods. Red brick laid in Flemish bond rises above a masonry plinth, with rendered basement walls. Square-headed windows have rendered reveals, masonry sills and two-over-two pane timber sliding sash windows. The round-headed entrance has a timber doorcase with panelled pilasters and cornice, a plain fanlight and glazed panelled door; granite steps, a surviving bootscrape and cast-iron railings complete the approach.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal identifies the double-fronted composition, centrally placed doorcase and regular fenestration as characteristic of late Georgian architecture. The simple cast-iron railings add decorative interest. It is one of a small group of buildings from Portobello's earliest development phase, associated with construction of the Grand Canal harbour in 1801.
- **Access / photography:** Private residential property. Photograph only from lawful public space, without entering the curtilage or obstructing residents. No interior access or permission is implied.

### DUB-BLD-0314 — 1 Harrington Street, Dublin 8, DUBLIN

- **Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50110205/1-harrington-street-dublin-8-co-dublin
- **Registration:** 50110205
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural, Artistic
- **Original use:** House
- **Recorded use:** Apartment/flat (converted)
- **Date:** 1820–1840 (built c.1830)
- **Date recorded:** 21/05/2017
- **Description:** Attached four-bay three-storey former house over a basement, built around 1830 as part of a terrace of four and now recorded as apartments. The front roof has a parapet with cut-granite coping. The building has a rendered chimneystack to the east gable and shared brown-brick chimneystacks; the front wall is brown brick laid in Flemish bond, while the east elevation is in English garden wall bond. A cut-granite plinth sits above the rendered basement. Square-headed windows have granite sills and rendered reveals. The round-headed entrance retains an Ionic doorcase with entablature, spoked fanlight and timber panelled door, approached over a granite platform and steps.
- **Appraisal / significance:** The NIAH appraisal notes that the house retains much of its early form. Its well-proportioned openings and cut-granite details articulate the façade, while the Ionic doorcase and fanlight demonstrate skilled craftsmanship. It shares characteristics with the adjoining houses to form a coherent streetscape and belongs to one of the earliest terraces on Harrington Street, where residential development gathered pace in the mid-nineteenth century.
- **Access / photography:** Recorded as an apartment building. Photograph the exterior from lawful public space only; do not enter the residential curtilage or photograph residents. No interior access is implied.

### DUB-BLD-0315 — The Goodwill Thrift Shop, 142 Capel Street, Dublin 1, DUBLIN

- **Registration:** 50010446
- **Rating:** Regional
- **Special interest:** Architectural
- **Original use:** House
- **Recorded use:** Shop/retail outlet
- **Date:** 1810–1830 (built c.1820)
- **Date recorded:** 24/11/2011
- **Description:** Terraced two-bay three-storey house, built around 1820 as one of a pair, with a replacement shopfront at ground floor. It has a triple-span slate roof, stepped brick chimneystack to the north party wall and a further stack to the rear, with clay pots. The roof is concealed behind a rendered parapet with a deep moulded cornice. Brown brick walls are laid in Flemish bond with some lime pointing retained. Gauged-brick flat-arch window openings have rendered reveals, granite sills and replacement six-over-six pane timber sliding sash windows.

By 1800 it had become one of the city's principal commercial thoroughfares. The house was built as one of a pair and, despite replacement of much of its original exterior material, contributes to the variation and character of this early streetscape.
