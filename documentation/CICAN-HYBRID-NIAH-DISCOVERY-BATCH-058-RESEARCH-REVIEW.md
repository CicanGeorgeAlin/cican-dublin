## 1. NIAH 50010559 — The Parnell Mooney, 72–74 Parnell Street

**Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010559/the-parnell-mooney-72-74-parnell-street-dublin-dublin

The NIAH describes a terraced six-bay, three-storey red-brick and stone building dating to 1879, designed with a mirrored elevation and an elaborate stone public-house frontage. The upper floors appear to have been rebuilt in the twentieth century. The ground-floor composition retains limestone ashlar, polished pink-granite pilasters, Corinthian capitals, dentilled cornices and pedimented openings. The appraisal attributes the design to Frederick Morley and identifies the retained pub front as an unusually decorative survivor on a heavily altered street.

**Fieldwork brief:** Photograph the complete mirrored frontage; limestone and granite shopfront; capitals, consoles, pediments and carved details; upper-storey brickwork and window arrangement. Public pavement only; interior access requires permission.

## 2. NIAH 50010561 — Patrick Conway, 70 Parnell Street

**Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010561/patrick-conway-70-parnell-street-dublin-dublin

The NIAH describes a corner-sited four-storey commercial building dating to around 1880, with a two-storey return and a continuous pub frontage wrapping the corner. Notable fabric includes red-brick Flemish-bond walls, decorative brick cornices, diminishing segmental-headed windows, timber sliding sashes and a detailed timber shopfront with fluted pilasters, glazed entablatures and cast-iron cresting. The appraisal notes the site's historical association with the surrender of Pádraig Pearse on behalf of the Provisional Government after the 1916 Rising.

**Fieldwork brief:** Record both street elevations and the angled corner entrance; shopfront pilasters and cresting; brick cornices and sash windows; document the relationship to the Rotunda Hospital streetscape. Public pavement only.

## 3. NIAH 50010562 — 71 Parnell Street

**Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010562/71-parnell-street-dublin-dublin

The NIAH describes a terraced two-bay, four-storey brick building dating to around 1830, with a staggered elevation and a traditional-style timber shopfront. It has yellow brick in English garden-wall bond, gauged-brick flat arches, granite sills and original timber sliding sash windows. The appraisal identifies it as a rare nineteenth-century survivor in a street substantially altered by road-widening schemes and later commercial redevelopment.

**Fieldwork brief:** Capture the staggered façade; yellow-brick bond and gauged arches; surviving sash windows and granite sills; shopfront cornice and street relationship. Public pavement only.

## 4. NIAH 50010615 — 76 Parnell Street

**Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010615/76-parnell-street-dublin-1-dublin

The NIAH describes a terraced two-bay, four-storey house dating to around 1850, formerly used as a factory, hotel and retail outlet. The survey record described it as vacant at that time; this is not evidence of present occupancy. The building has a hipped roof running perpendicular to the street, a rendered parapet with squared granite coping, rendered walls and quoin band, square-headed windows with granite sills, and a replacement timber shopfront. The appraisal notes that the site appears on Brookings' 1728 map and that Shaw's 1850 directory associated it with Naughton and Co., bread and biscuit manufacturers.

**Fieldwork brief:** Check present condition and occupancy before approaching; photograph frontage, quoin band, parapet and roofline, window openings and surviving shopfront fabric from lawful public space.

## 5. NIAH 50010617 — 78 Parnell Street

**Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010617/78-parnell-street-dublin-dublin

The NIAH describes a narrow, single-bay, four-storey house over basement dating to around 1850, formerly used as a hotel and then a shop. The survey record described it as vacant at the time of recording; present occupancy must be checked. The façade includes a hipped slate roof, rendered parapet with granite coping, channelled render quoin band, square-headed windows with granite sills and the remains of a timber shopfront. The appraisal highlights the unusually narrow frontage and a combined shopfront door-and-window opening as features of streetscape interest.

**Fieldwork brief:** Photograph the full narrow frontage without perspective distortion; quoin band, parapet and roofline; surviving shopfront cornice and combined opening; relationship to adjoining 76 and 77 Parnell Street. Public pavement only.
