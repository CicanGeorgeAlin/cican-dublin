# CICAN DUBLIN — Cloghran Historic Graveyard Detail Research Batch 154

**Date:** 17 November 2026  
**Scope:** FHG 11, Cloghran on Corduff Road, Blanchardstown; FHG 12, Cloghran on Stockhole Lane, Swords  
**Status:** Research-only. No permanent catalogue objects, IDs, source-registry entries, visual records or map pins created.

## Important identity distinction

The Fingal Historic Graveyards Project Volume 2 contains two different sites both named Cloghran:

- **FHG 11:** Corduff Road, Blanchardstown, Dublin 15; survey site coordinate 309185, 241707; RMP DU013-00802 and DU014-046; RPS 674; no NIAH number listed.
- **FHG 12:** Stockhole Lane, Swords; survey site coordinate 317820, 243994; RMP DU014-00902; RPS 609; no NIAH number listed.

They are distinct sites, with different historic church remains and different monument inventories. Do not merge them under one archive record or confuse their inscriptions.

The coordinates are the 2008 survey's site-level grid references, not founder GPS and not coordinates for individual memorials.

## 1. FHG 11 — Cloghran, Corduff Road, Blanchardstown

**Survey date:** 15 October 2008  
**RMP:** DU013-00802, DU014-046; church remains DU013-00801  
**RPS:** 674  
**Location:** Corduff Road, Blanchardstown, Dublin 15, County Dublin, Ireland  
**Survey grid reference:** 309185, 241707

### Archaeological and historic context

The graveyard occupies a significant rock outcrop, quarried away to its edge by surrounding development. The surviving remains consist of grass-covered rubble-limestone wall footings of a small church, up to approximately 0.5m high, with openings in the north and south walls. The east end is covered by a large tree. A grassed-over wall extends from the southeast corner. The church was associated with Finglas parish before the early fourteenth century, later becoming a chapel on lands of All Hallows; it was granted to Dublin Corporation at the Dissolution in 1538.

The 2008 survey records the graveyard as overgrown with grass and brambles, poorly maintained and no longer in use. This is a dated condition report, not confirmation of present conditions.

### Principal object candidates

1. **Henry Roe family headstone — Margaret Roe alias Veldon, died 5 July 1737.** The significant stone is at the interior of the east wall of the church. It is broken; the section bearing the family crest is embedded upside down in the ground. The survey transcription reads: “IHS This stone was placed here by Henry Roe of the Grange for him and his family. Here let the body of Margarett Roe alias Veldon late wife of Henry Roe who departed this life July 5 1737 and left issue two sons and two daughters”. The stone and crest-bearing fragment are parts of one broken memorial, not two separate memorials unless later evidence establishes otherwise.
2. **Thomas Mahon headstone, 1900.** A large early twentieth-century slab with a pointed top. The survey identifies it by name and date; inscription details beyond this were not transcribed in the site description.
3. **Trades and occupations recorded on grave markers.** The survey notes tanners, bakers and a coach maker represented among the memorials. These are potential individual biographies/inscriptions to extract from the survey plates and field photography, not yet individually identified objects.
4. **Freestanding crosses and headstones.** The graveyard contains eighteenth-, nineteenth- and early twentieth-century markers, mostly south of the church, with some inside the church. Many inscriptions are worn or illegible.
5. **Church wall-footing openings and southeast wall continuation.** These are architectural/archaeological features belonging to the church remains, not separate free-standing objects.

### Conservation concerns

The survey records leaning and broken headstones, badly worn and laminated stone surfaces, tree-root damage at the east end, and poor fencing. It recommends specialist advice and careful hand-only clearance; this is a conservation recommendation from 2008, not permission for volunteer clearance. CICAN fieldwork must remain non-invasive.

## 2. FHG 12 — Cloghran, Stockhole Lane, Swords

**Survey date:** 4 November 2008  
**RMP:** DU014-00902 (graveyard); medieval church DU014-00901  
**RPS:** 609  
**Location:** Stockhole Lane, Swords, County Dublin, Ireland  
**Survey grid reference:** 317820, 243994

### Archaeological and historic context

The 2008 survey identifies foundations of a medieval church in the northeast of the graveyard. The eighteenth-century parish church, re-erected in 1712 on the site of an earlier church, survives only as a low grass-covered platform near the centre. A square enclosure of squared rubble limestone with stone coping in the northeast contains a concrete cross and base.

The survey describes the graveyard as well maintained in 2008 and no longer in use. The site is on a quarried rock outcrop with a steep precipice to the north and east; field photography must respect safe access and boundaries.

### Principal object candidates

1. **John Murphy and Margred alias Youstas pillar stone.** A tall pillar stone in the south of the graveyard, leaning at the time of survey. The transcription reads: “This ston(e) belongeth to John Murphy and his wife Margred alias Youstas. Also the body of Richard Murphy who departed this life ye 20th of February 1742 in the 29 years of age Rest in Pe.....”. The final phrase is incomplete in the source. The survey warns that the stone is leaning and should be checked professionally for stability.
2. **1732 earliest inscribed marker.** The inscription had laminated and become illegible; the survey says the headstone had been replaced by a modern marble marker. Do not present the original 1732 stone as currently surviving unless field evidence confirms it. Treat as a historic memorial record / lost-or-replaced marker pending verification.
3. **Harriet Agnes Rose small slab.** A small slab with a simple etched inscription, located among small undecorated stone markers in the northeast. Candidate for a distinct micro-detail, subject to current survival and duplicate check.
4. **Two mural tablets on the east wall.** One eighteenth-century and one nineteenth-century. They are separate wall-mounted memorials, but inscriptions are not transcribed in the survey passage; inspect plates and field evidence before separate registration.
5. **Two family vaults in the northeast corner.** The survey identifies vaults dedicated to the Woods and Tymons and Fitzgerald families, covered by an earthen bank. The text does not unambiguously establish whether there are two vault structures or a shared/compound arrangement; verify from survey plates and site plan before counting.
6. **Mitchell family railed plot.** Large plot in the northwest, extended south, with seven marble headstones dedicated to members of the Mitchell family, enclosed by cast-iron railings on a low granite plinth. Treat the plot as a parent feature and the seven markers as children only if each can be individually identified and positioned.
7. **James Carroll memorial, died 24 February 1926, aged 10.** The latest memorial identified in the 2008 survey; inscription: “In loving memory of our darling son James Carroll, died 24th February 1926 aged 10 years RIP”.
8. **Large limestone pillar in southeast corner.** Distinctive architectural/monument feature noted by the survey, but no inscription or function provided. Keep as an unidentified candidate until further evidence.
9. **Concrete cross and base within square enclosure.** Separate from the older memorials; its relationship to the medieval church remains requires careful description and dating, which the survey does not supply.

### Conservation concerns

The survey records heavily leaning and fallen headstones, a weathered and illegible early marker, and particular instability of the Murphy/Youstas pillar. No attempt should be made to straighten or clean it. The report recommends professional stability assessment and careful clearance of growth where affecting memorials.
