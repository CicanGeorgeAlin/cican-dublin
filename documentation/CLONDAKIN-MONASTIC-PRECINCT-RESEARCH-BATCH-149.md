# CICAN DUBLIN — Clondalkin Monastic Precinct Research Batch 149

**Date:** 17 November 2026  
**Scope:** Tower Road and historic ecclesiastical core, Clondalkin, County Dublin  
**Status:** Research-only checkpoint. No catalogue objects, source-registry entries, visual records or map pins created.

## Research focus

Batch 149 follows the Clondalkin lead identified after Batch 148. The aim is to separate the round tower, former church site, graveyard, crosses, font, ecclesiastical enclosure and other recorded monuments rather than treating the whole precinct as one undifferentiated attraction.

## 1. Round tower — existing national monument

- **Record:** DU017-041006 (RMP); National Monument in State Care, No. 32.
- **Location:** Tower Road, Clondalkin, Dublin 22.
- **Source locator:** ITM 706910, 731398; Excavations.ie gives WGS84 53.322035, -6.395278. These are published site locators, not founder GPS.
- **Documented fabric:** Rough local calp-limestone masonry, projecting/buttressed base, raised entrance, four upper windows aligned to compass points, small mid-shaft south-facing window and conical stone cap.
- **Chronology:** The monastery is associated with St Mochua / Crónán Mo-Chua and an early medieval foundation. South Dublin County Council describes the tower as probably around 790 AD; Heritage Ireland describes the foundation in the seventh century. Avoid treating the tower's exact construction year as settled.
- **Access:** Heritage Ireland explicitly states external viewing only and that internal access is not permitted. Photograph only from lawful public/visitor viewpoints; no climbing or entry.

## 2. Two recorded crosses — separate monument candidates

South Dublin County Council's adopted County Development Plan 2022–2028, Appendix 3B, lists two separate Clondalkin cross records:
- DU017-041003 — Cross, Clondalkin.
- DU017-041004 — Cross, Clondalkin.

The same appendix separately lists the font, church, ecclesiastical enclosure, graveyard, round tower, castle records and water mill. The two cross references must not be collapsed into one object or assigned new CICAN IDs until the Historic Environment Viewer / underlying SMR records establish their individual descriptions and present locations.

## 3. Medieval font — distinct from the church building

- **RMP:** DU017-041007 — Font, Clondalkin.
- The Record of Monuments and Places identifies this as a distinct monument record, separate from the church and graveyard.
- A secondary graveyard directory mentions an old medieval baptismal font among remains associated with the former church, but this is not sufficient to identify its precise location or present survival. Treat that statement as a lead only.
- Do not create a separate catalogue object or map pin until the official SMR description and current location are established.

## 4. Former church and graveyard

- **RMP:** DU017-041002 — Church, Clondalkin; DU017-041012 — Graveyard, Clondalkin; DU017-041001 — Ecclesiastical enclosure.
- Heritage Ireland records that the old church was damaged in the 1787 gunpowder-mill explosion and that its remains were subsequently demolished. The current St John's Church of Ireland stands on the site of the earlier church.
- NIAH 11209026 describes St John's Church, built 1789, with a surrounding graveyard containing gravestones from medieval times to the present, mature yew trees, a brick barrel-vaulted tomb west of the church and stone rubble boundary walls.
- Do not infer that every medieval fragment mentioned in secondary sources survives in situ. The historic church, current church and graveyard are related but distinct catalogue entities and should be reconciled with the existing master catalogue before any change.

## 5. Archaeological evidence around the tower

Excavations.ie report 2016:647 (licence E004609) documents archaeological excavation between 9 March and 11 April 2016 in the vicinity of the round tower. Medieval features included two curvilinear gullies or slot trenches interpreted as relating to structures, a linear gully, six large pits with animal bones, a smaller pit and a possible post-hole. Medieval pottery and a polished bone pin were recovered. The report states that the impacted features were fully excavated to natural subsoil.

These excavated features and finds are important evidence for the medieval settlement, but they are not extant visitor-visible objects and must not be represented as surviving surface features or assigned current field-photo targets.

## Evidence boundaries

- RMP entries are official monument identifiers; they do not automatically prove current survival or public access.
- NIAH 11209026 is a post-1700 architectural record for St John's Church and does not replace the RMP records for the medieval precinct.
- The Historic Environment Viewer is the appropriate next source for individual SMR record descriptions; the National Monuments Service explains that SMR data is available online and as open data.
- No exact micro-feature coordinates have been inferred from a site-level point.
- No photographs have been fabricated or imported.
- No field visit, present-day observation or founder verification is claimed.
