# CICAN Dublin Archive — Clonmethan & Ballymadun Detail Research (Batch 159)

**Status:** Desk research only — not approved for catalogue publication, permanent object IDs, or map-pin creation.  
**Research scope:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project (FHG) 20 and 21, with cross-checks against later Fingal County Council and archaeological records.  
**Archive rule:** Keep site-level records separate from individual memorials, architectural fragments, nearby commemorative features, and unverified leads.

## 1. Clonmethan — FHG 20

### Site identity and location
- Survey reference: FHG 20; survey date: 24 June 2008.
- Townland: Glebe (ED Clonmethan); parish: Clonmethan; address in survey: Clonmethan, Oldtown, Swords, County Dublin, Ireland.
- Survey grid reference: ITM 311193, 254558.
- RMP: DU007-00602 (graveyard); associated church site DU007-00601.
- RPS: 146; NIAH: 11320004 (the FHG survey labels this as the graveyard, while the later archaeological report identifies NIAH 11320004 with St Mary's Church).
- Ownership recorded by FHG: Representative Church Body.
- Independent 2023 archaeological report gives ITM 711120, 754580 and WGS84 53.529412, -6.323942 for the St Mary's Church/graveyard site. The 2008 FHG grid reference and 2023 report grid reference are different coordinate systems/records and should not be conflated without GIS conversion and boundary checking.

### Distinctive memorial and smaller details
1. **Early-19th-century rounded headstone with circular emblem** — explicitly illustrated as Plate 20.2 in the FHG survey. The photograph shows a rounded-top stone with a carved circular emblem near the top and a faint inscription. The survey does not transcribe the inscription or identify the person commemorated. Record as an unidentified memorial candidate only after field/photo confirmation.
2. **Bryan Kearnan / Bartholomew Kearnan memorial** — the survey describes this as one of the earliest inscribed markers and transcribes: “IHS This stone and burying place belongeth unto Bryan Kearnan and his posterity. Here lies ye body of his father Bartholomew Kearnan who departed this life April ye 3r 1754 aged 67 years”. The original stone's exact position, dimensions, condition and whether all lettering remains legible require field verification.
3. **Lamb of God and IHS symbols** — the survey notes these motifs on some headstones. Each distinct stone should be individually photographed and catalogued only after it can be distinguished from other markers.
4. **Celtic Revival crosses and small undecorated markers** — noted as types present, but the report does not enumerate them. These are discovery targets, not yet separate object records.
5. **Boundary bank and ditch** — a bank and ditch along the north side may represent an earlier enclosure. This is an archaeological landscape feature, not a grave marker; keep it as a separate potential site component if later verified.

### Church and graveyard fabric
- The current St Mary's Church of Ireland was built c.1818 by the Board of First Fruits, on the site of a medieval parish church dedicated to St Mary. The earlier church is not visibly traceable according to the FHG survey.
- The church has a two-stage crenellated west tower, snecked limestone construction, a three-bay nave and blocked pointed-arched openings on the south elevation. The building was unroofed at the time of the FHG survey.
- The graveyard is sub-rectangular, with a substantial rubble limestone wall to the north and lower stone walls on other sides; square entrance pillars, cast-iron gates and a stone stile are recorded.
- The FHG survey describes the graveyard as no longer in use and maintained in 2008. It reports flaking inscriptions and leaning stones, and deterioration of the boarded-up, unroofed church.

### Later conservation evidence
- A 2023 archaeological monitoring report confirms the medieval parish church site (RMP DU007-006001-) and graveyard (RMP DU007-006002-). It states that the current St Mary's Church was erected c.1818 and remained in use until deconsecration in the mid-20th century; the graveyard remains in use.
- The 2023 works focused on the bell tower: vegetation removal, repointing with compatible mortar, resetting loose masonry and stone cappings, and fitting gates. No groundworks were undertaken and no archaeological features, deposits or objects were observed during monitoring.
- The report warns that sections of the graveyard enclosure wall, especially north-east, east and south-east, were bulging outwards and at risk of collapse. It proposed a condition survey of historic graves. Do not assume the 2008 condition description remains current.

### Clonmethan fieldwork targets
- Photograph the Plate 20.2 circular-emblem headstone from front, reverse, profile and base; capture a scale and raking-light inscription photographs.
- Locate and transcribe the Kearnan memorial; record all names, dates, symbols and orientation.
- Photograph every distinct Lamb of God/IHS carving and each Celtic Revival cross without assuming motifs make stones identical.
- Record the church tower, blocked openings, entrance pillars, gate, stile, wall construction and northern bank/ditch as separate architectural/landscape subjects.
- Check current condition and access with the site custodians; do not touch, clean, straighten or clear vegetation from historic fabric.

## 2. Ballymadun — FHG 21

### Site identity and location
- Survey reference: FHG 21; survey date: 4 November 2008.
- Townland: Glebe (ED Garristown); parish: Ballymadun; County Dublin, Ireland.
- Survey grid reference: ITM 306285, 254823.
- RMP: DU006-00202 (graveyard); associated medieval church DU006-00201.
- RPS: 129; NIAH: 11319004 (graveyard).
- Ownership recorded by FHG: Fingal County Council.
- Fingal's Buried in Fingal page gives cemetery coordinates 53.53278707, -6.39795726 and describes the site as Ballymadun Cemetery, Ashbourne, Co. Meath. The administrative and historic site context is Fingal/County Dublin; retain the official coordinate as a locator pending GPS field confirmation and boundary comparison.

### Church remains and architectural evidence
- The medieval parish church was dedicated to All Saints and was probably erected before 1275. It was ruinous by the mid-17th century; the Civil Survey described “walles of ye parish Church”.
- The FHG 2008 survey describes the standing remains conservatively: a south wall about 0.5m high, an entrance at the west end with rebates on either side, an exterior buttress, and a low platform to the north corresponding to the original church width. Part of the wall appears rebuilt and loose masonry was visible inside.
- The site is within a walled, raised graveyard. Flat-faced stones in the boundary wall may be reused grave markers. The survey also notes possible reused architectural fragments.
- **Unverified architectural lead:** a more specific claim about a c.1300 east window with a carved female-head label stop was not corroborated in the FHG 2008 survey or the Fingal County Council archaeological-resource summary consulted for this batch. It must not be entered as a confirmed Ballymadun feature until a primary architectural survey, RMP file, measured drawing or clear site photograph establishes its provenance. It may refer to another church or to a displaced fragment.

### Memorials documented in the FHG survey
1. **Thomas Callan slab, 1729** — the survey identifies this as the oldest slab and transcribes: “Here lyeth the body of Thomas Callan who dyed Apl ye 6th 1729 aged 80”. Its exact position and present legibility need confirmation.
2. **Russell family headstone/plot** — a limestone headstone with bell-shaped top and IHS in a sunburst. The survey transcribes: “Erected by Pat Russell of Ballymadun in memory of his wife Mary Russell”. The survey's historical-context section separately refers to a Russell tombstone dated 1816 with Crucifixion and angel symbols. Do not assume these descriptions refer to the same object until compared against the survey photographs and the 1992 memorial transcription.
3. **Boundary-wall stones** — flat-faced stones embedded in the boundary wall may be reused grave markers. Record each visible candidate by wall segment and orientation before considering separate object entries.
4. **Undecorated markers and possible architectural fragments** — the survey notes these but does not enumerate or identify them. Treat as a field-discovery list, not confirmed object records.
5. **Celtic Revival cross in pieces** — the survey reports one cross in several pieces and recommends recording fragment positions and relocating only if appropriate under conservation guidance. This is a high-priority photographic target; do not move fragments.

### Claims requiring reconciliation before publication
- **“Macabe” memorial dated 1720:** not identified in the 2008 FHG survey text examined. Search the full graveyard transcription, survey plates and on-site inscriptions before accepting the name or date.
- **1798 Rebellion plaque:** an independent 2005 cemetery transcription mentions a memorial to the 1798 Rebellion near the N2 approach to Ballymadun. This wording describes a nearby roadside memorial/landmark, not necessarily a plaque inside the graveyard. Keep it as a separate nearby candidate until its exact position and text are verified.
- **Large Byrne granite tomb with four memorial inscriptions:** not identified in the FHG survey text examined. It may be a later/overlooked marker or a misattributed memorial. Verify material, family name, all four inscriptions, location and date from a current photograph and an independent transcription before adding it.
- **Arched tomb recesses / female-head label stop:** not documented in the FHG survey's description of the surviving church fabric. Treat as unverified until a reliable source ties the feature to this church.

### Ballymadun fieldwork targets
- Photograph the surviving south wall, west entrance rebates, buttress, northern platform, loose masonry and all visible architectural fragments with scale and context.
- Photograph the Thomas Callan 1729 slab and transcribe the full face.
- Locate the Russell memorial(s) and compare the 1816 Crucifixion/angel description with the bell-topped IHS stone.
- Record each boundary-wall stone that may be reused, noting exact wall segment and whether lettering or tooling is visible.
- Photograph and map the broken Celtic Revival cross fragments in situ.
- Investigate the Macabe, Byrne and 1798 claims as separate leads; do not merge the roadside memorial with the cemetery.
- Confirm current access and permission before fieldwork.

## 3. Archive integrity decisions

- No permanent archive IDs assigned.
- No new catalogue records or map pins created.
- No object has been declared field-verified.
- FHG survey descriptions and plate captions are source-derived; current condition and exact object positions remain unverified.
- Do not use a single parent site record as a substitute for distinct child objects. The church, graveyard, boundary wall, each memorial, reused stone, architectural fragment and nearby roadside memorial require separate identities if and when confirmed.
- The 2008 FHG survey and later sources disagree or differ in level of detail in places. Preserve each source's wording and date; do not silently harmonise.
- Full master catalogue access and duplicate checks remain a prerequisite for permanent IDs and publication.
