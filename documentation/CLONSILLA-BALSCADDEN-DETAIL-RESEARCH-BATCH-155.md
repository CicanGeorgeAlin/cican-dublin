## 1. FHG 13 — St Mary's, Clonsilla

**Address:** Clonsilla, Dublin 15, County Dublin, Ireland  
**Survey grid reference:** 304981, 238219  
**RMP:** DU013-01702 (graveyard); DU013-01701 (site of medieval church)  
**RPS:** 705  
**NIAH:** 11353005 (church)  
**Survey date:** 17 August 2008

The current church was built in 1846 on the site of a medieval parish church, of which no visible trace was recorded in 2008. The graveyard remains in use. Excavations in 2004 recorded skeletal remains from the late nineteenth century and remains of 32 individuals dating from the early nineteenth century onwards.

### A. Three significant flat memorial stones at the south wall

The survey identifies three large flat stones on the path at the base of the church's south wall:

1. **Frances Lady Newcomen memorial, 1687.** The historical section says a tombstone commemorating Frances Lady Newcomen, who died in 1687, stands in the graveyard. She was the wife of Sir Thomas Newcomen of Sutton and daughter of Sir William Talbot of Cortown, Baronet.
2. **Fitzsimmons and Moore memorial, 1736.** The second flat stone is dated 1736 and associated with the Fitzsimmons and Moore names. The survey does not provide a full transcription.
3. **Unidentified / illegible memorial.** The third stone's inscription was illegible in 2008.

The 2008 survey says all three show cracking, lamination and wear. It recommends protective barriers to prevent further abrasion. The Lady Newcomen tombstone mentioned in the historical narrative may be the 1687 flat stone at the south wall, but the survey does not explicitly connect the two descriptions. Treat them as a single candidate pending comparison of the plan, plates and memorial transcription; do not create two objects from the same possible stone.

### B. Luke White Esq. family vault, 1824

A significant, well-carved vault for Luke White Esq. (1824) and his wife is located northeast of the church, behind the parish centre. It is a discrete structure and a strong object-level candidate, subject to current visual verification and catalogue duplicate check.

### C. Second World War memorial to Major James David Barry

The graveyard contains a memorial dedicated to Major James David Barry, who died in 1941. The survey passage does not give a full inscription or exact position. Photograph and transcribe before assigning an individual object record.

### D. Other architectural and graveyard details

- The walled area north of the church contains architectural fragments and one table tomb.
- Small unmarked stones lie south of the church.
- The graveyard has two levels, with the church on higher ground.
- Cast-iron entrance gates, ashlar limestone gate pillars and a simple stone stile are recorded.
- A segmental-headed opening with a timber door survives in the boundary wall northwest of the church.

Do not assign separate records to unidentified fragments or unmarked stones until they can be identified and located. The church boundary and openings should be recorded as architectural components where the schema supports parent-child records.

## 2. FHG 14 — St Mary's, Balscadden

**Address:** The Square, Balscadden, County Dublin, Ireland  
**Survey grid reference:** 317099, 264607  
**RPS:** 6  
**NIAH:** 11304008 (church)  
**RMP:** not listed in the 2008 survey  
**Survey date:** 18 August 2008

The present Roman Catholic church was built by subscription in 1819. The graveyard remains in use. The western boundary curves and may preserve part of an earlier enclosure, but the survey does not establish this conclusively.

### A. 1920 high cross memorial to Seamus Lawless and Sean Gibbons

A significant high cross dated 1920 commemorates Seamus Lawless and Sean Gibbons, described in the inscription as IRA members killed while in British custody during the sack of Balbriggan on 20 September 1920. The survey transcribes the memorial's political and commemorative wording, ending with the Irish-language phrase “Ar deis dé go raibh a namh” (retain the source transcription exactly in any future record until checked against the stone; do not silently correct spelling or grammar).

This is a distinct, important memorial object. The event description and inscription should be recorded as what the memorial states, with historical claims separately checked against independent records before presenting them as established fact.

### B. Tall pedestal monument with sculpted figure — identity unresolved

Plate 14.2 depicts a tall stone pedestal with a sculpted standing figure on top and an inscription panel. The 2008 survey's text does not identify the figure, dedicatee, sculptor, date or inscription. Do not infer it is a saint, angel, or named person from the photograph alone. This is a high-priority photo/transcription candidate.

### C. Nicholas Tolan family headstone — earliest marker identified in survey

The survey identifies the oldest marker in the graveyard as a memorial erected by Nicholas Tolan of Doolagh in memory of his mother Mary Tolan, who died 16 October 1841, aged 64. The inscription also commemorates John Tolan (d. 12 September 1816, aged 34), Nicholas Tolan (d. 7 November 1858, aged 57), his brother John (d. September 1890, aged 81), nephew John Tolan (d. 30 August 1898, aged 57), and daughter Emily F. Halpin (d. 10 April 1943). The survey says the marker appears to pre-date completion of the 1819 church; the recorded dates and this interpretation should be preserved as the survey's account.

### D. Other graveyard fabric

- Celtic Revival crosses and later marble memorials.
- Cast-iron markers and plots enclosed by cast-iron railings on low granite plinths.
- Stone entrance piers with ball finials and cast-iron gates.
- Terraced ground sloping down westward, with yew and beech trees.
- Curving western boundary, a possible earlier enclosure trace.

The survey reports some leaning stones and heavy overgrowth at the west end in 2008. This does not establish current condition.

## 3. Conservation and fieldwork safeguards

Fingal's Volume 1 conservation guidance identifies the three Clonsilla stones at the south wall as significant worn memorials and recommends protective barriers. The wider Fingal guidance warns that almost all historic graveyards are protected archaeological monuments and that even removing ivy can damage historic fabric. No cleaning, highlighting, moving, straightening, vegetation removal or disturbance of grave markers is part of CICAN fieldwork. Photography must be non-invasive and respect current access.
