## New catalogue discovery

### DUB-INF-0003 — Wood Quay surviving Hiberno-Norse city wall section

**Location:** Wood Quay Venue, Dublin City Council Civic Offices, Wood Quay, Dublin 8  
**Period:** c.1100  
**Category:** INFRASTRUCTURE  
**Verification:** VERIFIED from official Dublin City Council sources  
**Photography:** AWAITING FOUNDER PHOTOGRAPH  
**Coordinates:** 53.344229874547, -6.2708973884583  
**Coordinate status:** SOURCE_DERIVED — venue coordinates, not a field GPS fix for the precise wall alignment

Dublin City Council describes the Wood Quay Venue as featuring a stretch of the original Hiberno-Norse city wall dating from approximately 1100 AD. The Council's archaeological projects review describes a section within the Civic Offices basement approximately 18 metres long and 3.5 metres high, plus a further section approximately six metres long extending outside to the west. The full below-ground extent is not known.

The catalogue record distinguishes the surviving visible wall from other parts of the medieval circuit known only through excavation or below-ground archaeology. It does not imply that every part of the historic wall is visible today.

**Access caution:** The internal wall is within a managed Civic Offices venue. Public access and photography permission must be confirmed with Dublin City Council before visiting. Do not enter staff-only or restricted areas. Any exterior photography must be from a lawful public viewpoint.

**Founder photography brief**
1. Confirm access with the venue before planning an interior visit.
2. Photograph the full accessible wall section and its relationship to the modern venue.
3. Capture stone construction, joints, repairs and transitions.
4. If visible from public space, photograph the external western continuation without entering restricted areas.
5. Record interpretation panels and current condition.
6. Record actual field GPS and capture date separately from the source-derived venue coordinate.

No third-party photograph was imported. The new visual record is explicitly awaiting founder photography.

## Official discovery-source programme identified

Dublin City Council's Heritage Plan page states that the Dublin City Industrial Heritage Record (DCIHR) mapped **1,219 sites** across Dublin City and produced written records and an extensive photographic record of **3,866 photographs**. The Council says the survey was later converted to GIS and published on HeritageMaps.ie.

A separate Dublin City Council highlights document reports **1,218 sites surveyed**, of which **514** had some degree of surviving remains (42%). These two official publications give slightly different survey totals (1,219 versus 1,218). This discrepancy is recorded rather than silently reconciled. The DCIHR is a major next-stage discovery source, but its site records still require identity, location, survival and duplicate checks before any catalogue additions.

The GIS layer's published fields include Name, Purpose, Description, Appraisal, Map Reference and PhototID. This makes it a promising structured lead source. No bulk GIS records were imported in this batch because the complete feature payload and individual record-level evidence were not validated here.
