## Research outcome

This batch investigated the four current Dublin City Council protected-structure entries RPS 7378–7381 along Ringsend Road. It identified one additional extant building with corroborated current use and historical identity: the **Dublin United Tramway Company Powerhouse**, now Windmill Lane Recording Studios, at 20 Ringsend Road.

### Identity and use reconciliation

Dublin City Council's current RPS Volume 4 lists RPS 7381 as “Ringsend Shelbourne Pierrot Club (former Bovril building): commercial warehouse/sound studio”.

The current occupier, Windmill Lane Recording Studios, states that its building at No. 20 Ringsend Road was once the electrical power station for the tramline depot and that the studios have occupied the protected Art Deco building since 1990. Its 2020 press release independently describes the building as the former electrical power station for the tramline depot.

Archiseek's specialist architectural-history entry identifies it as the 1932 DUTC Powerhouse, designed by Vincent Kelly. The same source notes that the Art Deco piers and sun-ray gates are missing. This is important: do not confuse missing decorative gates at the powerhouse with the separate four granite gate piers listed at RPS 7380.

The evidence supports the building as one physical structure with successive uses. The exact chronology and details of the intermediate Bovril phase remain an archival research question; the RPS wording and occupier's account are preserved rather than silently harmonised.

## RPS leads reviewed but not added as map pins

### RPS 7378 — Former Reg Armstrong Works entrance

The current RPS describes a classical cut-stone carriage entrance with Ionic columns on Ringsend Road. A recent planning-register proximity result places the protected feature close to the Grand Canal Dock development, but proximity is not proof of survival or exact location. No catalogue record was created. Further inspection of the DCC Docklands Architectural Survey, planning drawings and current imagery is required.

### RPS 7380 — CIE bus depot gate piers

The current RPS describes four rusticated granite gate piers. A secondary summary of the 1996 Dublin Docklands Architectural Survey reports four granite gates at the CIÉ depot, but a current authoritative visual confirmation of the precise four piers was not established in this batch. No catalogue record was created. The piers should be checked from public viewpoints and against historic survey photographs before mapping.

### RPS 7379 — Former Irish Glass Bottle Company office

This is distinct from the demolished sheet-glass factory at Charlotte Quay investigated in Batch 101. The current RPS describes a three-storey brick office at the corner of South Docks Road with a glass-block entrance, feature window and clock. Its physical survival and current fabric still need separate verification. Do not conflate the office with the factory.
