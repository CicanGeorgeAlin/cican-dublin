## Research scope

Continue south-east from Ringsend and Irishtown into the Poolbeg Peninsula, Pigeon House Harbour and the Great South Wall. The aim was to identify individually meaningful, photographable maritime and industrial heritage while separating current physical objects from protected-structure listings and historical leads.

- **Location:** Eastern end of the Great South Wall (South Bull Wall), Dublin Bay, Dublin 4
- **Historical period:** 1766–1767; first lit 29 September 1767
- **Category:** ARCHITECTURE
- **Status:** VERIFIED; source-derived coordinates; founder photography pending

### Evidence and chronology

Dublin Port's official port history states that the South Bull Wall replaced an earlier bank in 1753 and that Poolbeg Lighthouse at its end was first lit on 29 September 1767, replacing a floating light. The Dictionary of Irish Architects records John Smyth's responsibility for the lighthouse's construction in 1766–1767 and the eastern abutment of the South Wall in or after 1771. The source notes conflicting earlier claims for the start of construction; the archive therefore uses the better-supported 1766–1767 construction period and the documented first-light date rather than repeating the earlier 1762 claim.

## Wider Pigeon House / Great South Wall findings

The current Dublin City Development Plan 2022–2028, Volume 4, lists the following protected structures:

| RPS | Register description | Batch decision |
|---|---|---|
| 6793 | Former St Catherine's Hospital: surviving parts, including northern and western site boundary walls | Research lead; current fabric and exact extent require inspection |
| 6794 | Remnants of Pigeon House Fort | Research lead; 2009 archaeological survey documents remains, but 2026 survival/access not verified |
| 6795 | Former Pigeon House Hotel | Research lead; exact current condition and access not established |
| 6796 | Former red-brick Pigeon House power station | Research lead; do not confuse with the modern Poolbeg Generating Station |
| 6797 | Former sea wall and sea wall at various locations along Pigeon House Road | Research lead; multiple segments require separate location checks |
| 6798 | Great South Wall (to lighthouse) | Linear heritage asset; full alignment and micro-features require separate mapping |

Dublin City Film Office describes the former Pigeon House power station as a vast industrial ruin built in three main phases: 1902–03, 1911–13 and 1933–40. It records decommissioning in the 1970s and states that one of the original twelve chimneys remains. Its gallery images were not imported because image-specific reuse rights were not established.

Excavations.ie report 2009:357 records an archaeological and architectural survey of Pigeon House Fort (SMR DU019–027; licence 09E0259), including a survey of accessible standing remains and potential subsurface remains. This establishes documented remains in 2009 only, not current condition or public access.

The 2022:294 excavation report concerns trial-pit monitoring at the modern Poolbeg Generating Station in 2021 and reports no archaeological features or finds of significance in those test pits. It is a separate site context and is not evidence about the former Pigeon House Power Station.

## Access and safety

Dublin Port's Heritage Conservation Strategy identifies port security and safety constraints and describes restored pedestrian access from Ringsend to Pigeon House Harbour via the Great South Wall as a **long-term aspiration**. The archive does not assume that the Pigeon House precinct is currently open to public access. Do not enter operational port land, restricted compounds or derelict structures. Check current access notices and weather before any visit to the exposed Great South Wall.
