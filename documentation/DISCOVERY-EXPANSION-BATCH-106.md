## Research basis

This batch uses Dublin Port Company's 3FM Environmental Impact Assessment, Appendix 16-1, *Catalogue of Cultural Heritage Assets*. The catalogue states that its entries draw on the National Monuments Service RMP, NIAH, Dublin City Council RPS, Dublin City Industrial Heritage Record, National Civil Engineering Database and Dublin Docklands Industrial Archaeology Survey, supplemented by Dublin Port Archives and project field walkover inspection.

The report's ITM coordinates were converted from EPSG:2157 to WGS84. These are source-derived catalogue points, not founder GPS fixes. Accuracy is unspecified.

## Three new catalogue records

### DUB-MON-0008 — Half Moon Battery (RMP DU019-028)
- Built by the Board of Ordnance in 1793 on the Great South Wall; an addition is recorded in 1795.
- The 3FM EIAR Appendix 16-1 describes five 24-pounder guns controlling the river channel and waters around Poolbeg Lighthouse.
- The battery was later dismantled. The report describes the south-facing granite perimeter wall as its most intact upstanding element, a possible gun-swivel curvature in the Great South Wall surface, and a stone slipway extending west from the battery.
- ITM 722275, 733893 → WGS84 **53.3411221304087, -6.163808146643519**.
- **Important source discrepancy:** Dublin Port's Heritage Conservation Strategy describes the battery as a three-gun platform, while Appendix 16-1 says five 24-pounder guns. The catalogue preserves this disagreement rather than choosing one account without further archival evidence.
- The site is associated with a swimming club. Respect club operations and photograph only from lawful public access.

### DUB-INF-0009 — Former Pigeon House Lifeboat House site (DCIHR 19-09-008)
- Lifeboat presence at Pigeon House is recorded as early as 1815; the catalogue identifies the c.1850 lifeboat-house site.
- This is a **site with remains, not a complete standing building**.
- Appendix 16-1 describes lengths of ruined wall stubs in red brick and granite, a breached old concrete enclosure wall and a ruinous perimeter path affected by coastal processes.
- A 1939 Dublin Port Archives photograph records the former station when more of its remains were standing.
- ITM 721370, 733814 → WGS84 **53.34062080720545, -6.177421145216574**.
- Do not climb on or cross unstable remains; confirm safe lawful access.

### DUB-BLD-0098 — Sluice House, South Wall (DCIHR 19-09-009-01)
- Built c.1881 in association with Londonbridge Road Pumping Station and the Rathmines and Pembroke Main Drainage Scheme.
- Detached three-bay, single-storey masonry building.
- The EIAR describes squared-random rubble stone walls, concrete coping, rusticated quoins, blocked segmental-headed windows with block-and-start surrounds, and a square-headed doorway with rusticated granite surround and a later steel door.
- It housed machinery operating two penstock valves to manage sewage flow and seawater pressure at the sewer outlet.
- ITM 721427, 733833 → WGS84 **53.34077839985886, -6.176558384835216**.
- Described as abandoned at the time of survey; current access and condition require verification. Do not enter.
