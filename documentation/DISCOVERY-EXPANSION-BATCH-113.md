## Key findings

- Grand Canal Docks opened in 1796 to designs by William Jessop. The NIAH describes a 25-acre basin designed for 150 ocean-going vessels, with limestone quay walls, granite coping, bollards and mooring posts.
- The sea-lock group comprises Westmoreland, Buckingham and Camden Locks, built to connect the canal basin with the tidal Liffey. Waterways Ireland reported a Camden Lock restoration initiative in 2026; the archive records historic configuration without assuming current operational status.
- Two former graving docks and a separate southern dock converted to a slipway around 1850 are catalogued as distinct NIAH entries.
- McKenny Bridge and its neighbouring lock are separately registered. The bridge is a c.1790 granite single-arch canal bridge; the lock has its own limestone chamber and gate fittings.
- Sir John Rogerson's Quay retains documented granite walling, iron mooring fittings, steps and inset rails.
- The Grand Canal Quay railway bridge, c.1835, is part of the Dublin and Kingstown Railway, Ireland's first railway line.
