## New catalogue objects

### DUB-BLD-0118 — Redbrick Electricity Substation
NIAH Registration 50011170, at the corner of East Wall Road and Alexandra Road. The NIAH describes a c.1900 two-storey red-brick electricity substation with a breakfronted entrance, gauged brick arches, granite coping and decorative brick detailing. Dublin Port confirms its role in early port electrification and its restoration as a museum.

The NIAH Irish Grid coordinate 318206, 234875 was transformed from TM65 / Irish Grid (EPSG:29902) to WGS 84: **53.351119174701, -6.225622095176**. This is a source-derived site point, not founder GPS or field-verified. The 1728 sea wall discovered below the building is a separate buried archaeological feature and is not merged into the substation record.

### DUB-INF-0032 — Crane 292
A Stothert and Pitt crane from Dublin Port's 1960s cargo-handling era. Dublin Port records that it worked at Alexandra Quay from 1964 until retirement in 1997, was 115 feet tall and was restored beside Port Centre in 2017.

The mapped point **53.34998, -6.22645** is an approximate third-party map-derived locator, not an official survey coordinate or founder GPS. The record preserves the distinction between the crane's former working location at Alexandra Quay and its present landmark location beside Port Centre.

## Further evidence recorded without speculative map pins

A new research note records four separate subjects for spatial follow-up:

- **Pumphouse No. 2:** Dublin Port's Conservation Strategy describes a 1950s building with electrically driven pumps and substantial underground supporting structure, associated with Graving Dock No. 2.
- **Electric capstan:** the strategy records an electric capstan associated with Graving Dock No. 2 during redevelopment works; present disposition and exact position remain unconfirmed.
- **1826 patent slip:** uncovered and recovered during works, with stone samples taken into care. The original alignment and current storage location are not established by the consulted source.
- **1728 sea wall:** part of the original wall was found beneath the Substation during refurbishment. Dublin Port says facing stones were likely reused in the visible boundary wall; this remains a qualified statement, not proof that the whole wall survives there.

No separate point is created for these features until the relevant plans and archaeological records establish their footprint, current state and provenance.
