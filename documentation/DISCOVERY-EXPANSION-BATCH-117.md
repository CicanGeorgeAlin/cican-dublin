## New catalogue objects

Four distinct National Inventory of Architectural Heritage records were added as separate map objects:

- **DUB-BLD-0119 — R. & H. Hall — monumental reinforced-concrete grain silo** (NIAH 50060589). Built 1915–1920, with later steel-bin and concrete additions. NIAH rates it National and identifies Frederick G. Hicks as its designer. Its description records loading arches, granite wheel-guards and diorite setts.
- **DUB-BLD-0120 — Odlum's Mills — c.1920 reinforced-concrete grain silo** (NIAH 50060590). Six-storey, eleven-bay silo with panelled pilasters, crown cornice and glazed breakfront.
- **DUB-BLD-0121 — Odlum's Mills — c.1935 grain tower and steel silo drums** (NIAH 50060591). Reinforced-concrete tower with riveted cylindrical steel grain-silo drums and a loading block.
- **DUB-BLD-0122 — Alexandra Road — early steel-frame redbrick industrial building** (NIAH 50060592). c.1920 multi-storey building with redbrick walls on a riveted iron frame.

## Research and provenance

Dublin Port's Conservation Strategy explicitly includes the former Odlums Flour Mills among the port's heritage assets. The NIAH records separately identify the monumental R. & H. Hall silo and the two Odlum's Mills silo structures. The archive preserves their individual registration identities rather than collapsing them into one generic mill pin.

One research note records the complex as a group and flags open questions about historical ownership phases, the relationship between individual buildings, current status and access. One investigation task was added to compare the NIAH footprints with historic port plans and to establish lawful public viewpoints.

## Visual discovery and access

Each new building receives its own visual record and founder-photography brief. No third-party photograph was copied. No record is marked as founder-photographed, illustrated or published.

Dublin Port is an operational industrial estate. The archive does not imply public access, interior access or permission to approach the structures. Photography must be limited to lawful public viewpoints or carried out with explicit authorisation.
