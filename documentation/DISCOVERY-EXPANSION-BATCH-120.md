## New catalogue object

### DUB-INF-0033 — Dublin Port Diving Bell — Stoney's compressed-air quay-construction caisson
NIAH Registration **50020468**, Sir John Rogerson's Quay, Dublin 2.

The NIAH describes a cast-iron and riveted plate-iron diving bell fabricated c.1870, with a chamber 23 feet square by 6.5 feet high, a vertical access shaft with iron rungs and an air lock. The bell was designed by Dublin Port engineer Bindon Blood Stoney and used in construction of deep-water quay walls. Dublin Port records that it was delivered in 1866, entered service in 1871 and remained in use until 1958. It is now mounted on a modern display structure at Sir John Rogerson's Quay and forms part of Dublin Port's Distributed Museum.

The NIAH Irish Grid reference 317365, 234293 was transformed from TM65 / Irish Grid (EPSG:29902) to WGS 84: **53.346079478839, -6.238460516703**. This is a source-derived NIAH locator, not founder GPS or field-verified.

## Chronology and provenance

The archive deliberately preserves differences between official sources:
- NIAH describes fabrication c.1870 and use from 1872.
- Dublin Port states delivery in 1866, entry into service in 1871 and use until 1958.
- Dublin Port Archive describes its compressed-air operation, six-person crew and role in constructing deep-water quay walls.

These may reflect different stages (construction, delivery, commissioning and operational use); no unsupported single date has been imposed.
