## Evidence notes

- **Griffith Avenue post box (50130259):** NIAH records a c.1900 cast-iron pillar box with a VR monogram, Post Office lettering and no visible maker's mark. The VR monogram identifies it as pre-1901.
- **Whitehall Delivery Office (50130140):** The c.1935 postal sorting office retains a copper-sheeted north-light roof, red-brick elevations, timber sash windows and a recessed porch.
- **Former Whitehall Garda Station / Office of the State Pathologist (50130143):** The 1934 corner building retains a geometric entrance portal, raised date, pink brickwork and granite detailing. Its current use is recorded as the Office of the State Pathologist.
- **45 Home Farm Road (50130148):** NIAH records a c.1930 corner building with a canted shopfront, geometric detailing and stained-glass overlights.
- **Drumcondra Railway Station (50130208) and bridge (50120207):** The station opened in 1901; the bridge is a c.1900 box-truss steel-girder structure with rusticated limestone abutments. The NIAH appraisal records the railway's interrupted early history and the station's 1998 reopening.
- **Holy Cross / Clonliffe College (50120175):** The quadrangular seminary was built in 1860–61 and is now used as administrative headquarters for the Catholic Archdiocese of Dublin.
- **DCU St Patrick's Campus (50130152):** The former St Patrick's Training College building dates from 1885–95. NIAH records the original quadrangle, Italianate campanile and cast-iron weather vane.
- **Old St Patrick's College Chapel (50130151):** The 1899 Gothic Revival chapel, now used as a university library, has an ornate Portland-stone entrance, rose window, Evangelist frieze, statue of St Patrick, belfry and stained glass.
- **New St Patrick's College Chapel (50130154):** The modernist chapel was built in 1964 to designs by Robinson, Keefe and Devane; NIAH records a campanile, cloister arcade and dalle-de-verre glazing.
- **Drumcondra Castle (50120019):** The building's core dates to 1561, with later additions. NIAH records the Regency-style cast-iron and timber porch, ironwork and entrance bootscrapes.
- **Drumcondra Library (50130157):** The 1937 Art Deco library is attributed by NIAH to Robert Sorley Lawrie and retains its geometric entrance portal and wrought-iron boundary treatment.
- **National Botanic Gardens entrance (50130059):** The c.1890 gate lodges and entrance screen retain documented bilingual lettering and original cast-iron turnstiles.

## Deduplication and modelling

All thirteen new NIAH registration numbers were checked against the complete existing object catalogue before new primary IDs were assigned. None was already present as a primary NIAH-backed object. Ten micro-details are intentionally linked to their host buildings and reuse the host NIAH reference; these are not duplicate sites or duplicate primary registrations.

## Coordinates and fieldwork

The published Irish Grid coordinates were transformed from EPSG:29902 to WGS 84 using the project's established operation 1641. Coordinates are source-derived site or host-building locators, not founder GPS. For micro-details, the pin deliberately identifies the host building and does not claim exact feature-level GPS.

All 23 new records remain awaiting founder-captured original photographs. No third-party photographs were imported; no record was marked photographed, illustrated or published. Photograph from lawful public viewpoints only. Campus, school, office, garden and interior access restrictions must be respected. A4 line art must be created only from the corresponding founder-captured original photograph.
