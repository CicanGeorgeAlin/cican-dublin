## Evidence summary

- **Botanic View, 176 Botanic Road (50130063):** Corner-sited end-of-terrace three-bay two-storey house over raised basement, built c.1820, with full-height rear return. The front elevation retains a round-headed doorway with moulded reveals, engaged Ionic columns, frieze and cornice, anthemion fanlight and timber panelled door, approached by granite steps and bounded by cast-iron railings. An early nineteenth-century late-Georgian house and part of a short terrace beside the National Botanic Gardens. Its surviving doorcase and fanlight are notable artistic and architectural features.

- **170 Botanic Road — late-Georgian villa (50130066):** Terraced three-bay single-storey villa over raised basement, built c.1830, with a hipped slate roof behind a rendered parapet. NIAH records painted stucco walling with banded quoins, moulded window architraves, timber sliding-sash windows, a recessed Doric portico, a six-panel timber door and a distinctive peacock-tail fanlight. Granite steps and wrought-iron railings frame the entrance. A well-preserved villa-style house representing the classical proportions and detailing of late-Georgian domestic architecture in Glasnevin. The doorcase and fanlight retain particular artistic interest.

- **Three Sisters Craft, 187 Botanic Road (50130109):** End-of-terrace three-bay two-storey house and shop dated 1901. NIAH describes a cranked façade, two angled northern bays, a curvilinear sandstone parapet inscribed with the date, domestic-revival gables, red-brick walling and a shopfront framed by brick piers with foliate capitals. A distinctive early twentieth-century commercial and domestic building within Glasnevin village. Its dated parapet, cranked façade and crafted brick shopfront contribute to the architectural character of the historic village centre.

- **Hart's Buildings, 2–6 Botanic Road (50130107):** Corner-sited group of two-storey shops with residential accommodation above, dated 1924–25. The symmetrical composition has east and west wings terminating at a canted southern corner, a mansard slate roof, decorative gablets and dormers, red-brick walling, granite lintels and sills, and varied shopfronts. NIAH notes surviving original upper-floor doorways and stained and leaded transom and sidelights. Known locally as Hart's Corner, this purpose-built commercial group marks a historic village junction and records the development of suburban retail premises in the 1920s.

- **The Brian Boru, 5 Prospect Road (50130022):** Detached three-bay two-storey public house built c.1855, with a traditional pubfront and later rear extensions. NIAH records a rendered parapet with gablet, lettering reading 'The Brian Boru', moulded window surrounds, a central first-floor glazed painting depicting Brian Boru, and a pubfront with Doric pilasters, glazed and gilded signage and timber doors. The interior survey records a tiled floor, Lincrusta-papered ceiling, cast-iron columns, wainscoting and a timber bar counter. A long-established Glasnevin public house with strong social and commercial-history associations. The surviving pubfront and painted Brian Boru panel are distinctive elements of the Prospect Road streetscape.

- **The Porterhouse, 22 Prospect Road — former Iona Airways garage (50130199):** Detached single-storey trapezoidal-plan former garage built c.1930, now in use as a public house. NIAH records a shallow-pitched seamed zinc roof with a raised glazed central lantern, stepped rendered parapets and gables, and full-height window openings. The building served as the headquarters of Iona Airways and aircraft were serviced there, while the aerodrome was at Kildonan House. An unusual surviving interwar garage associated with early Irish commercial aviation and entrepreneur Hugh Cahill. Despite later alterations, its original form and massing remain legible.

- **National Botanic Gardens — Great Palm House (50130057):** Freestanding timber-and-wrought-iron glasshouse erected in 1884, comprising a triple-height central house with a domed lantern, curved roof wings, and a substantial glazed range. A masonry boiler house adjoins the rear. The NIAH describes a complex iron-and-timber framework, cast-iron columns and walkways, historic glazing patterns, ventilation winch system and original heating infrastructure. The structure was prefabricated by James Boyd in Paisley and erected in Dublin in nine months. A nationally rated landmark of Victorian engineering and horticultural architecture within the gardens established in 1795. Its scale, ironwork, glazing and faithful conservation make it a major component of the historic botanic-garden landscape.

## Deduplication

The complete existing object catalogue (393 records) was parsed before this batch. All seven NIAH registration references were checked against every existing record; none was already present. The object names and addresses were also checked against the catalogue, with no matching existing record found. Seven new primary IDs were assigned consecutively from DUB-BLD-0157 through DUB-BLD-0163.
