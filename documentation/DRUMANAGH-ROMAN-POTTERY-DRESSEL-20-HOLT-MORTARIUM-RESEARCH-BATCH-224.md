# Drumanagh Roman Pottery and Amphorae — Imports, Production Origins and Context Integrity (Research Batch 224)

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Site:** Drumanagh Promontory Fort, Loughshinny, County Dublin  
**Recorded monument:** DU008-006001  
**Excavation project:** Digging Drumanagh, Seasons I–V  
**Consent / excavation reference:** C786 / E004805  
**Subject:** Dressel 20 amphorae, Holt mortarium, Samian ware and other imported pottery  
**Research status:** Documentation-only; no production catalogue or map changes  
**Research date:** 20 November 2026

## Executive finding

The excavation reports document repeated Roman-world ceramic imports at Drumanagh across multiple seasons:

- **Season I (2018):** two Dressel 20 amphora sherds from what the report describes as the original ground level south of the Martello road, disturbed by ploughing.
- **Season II (2019):** 87 pottery sherds in total, mostly prehistoric. Two thick rim sherds and an amphora base were compared with Dressel 20 fragments from Season I. A later specialist discussion identifies a separate mortarium sherd from Season II as typical of the Holt pottery industry near Chester, with production mainly in the first half of the 2nd century AD. The published discussion does not give its find number or context.
- **Season III (2022):** 39 sherds, mostly prehistoric. Two rim sherds were compared with Dressel 20 fragments from Seasons I and II. The report also records Roman-world pottery among the finds, but the two rim sherds are described as comparisons, not an independently quantified amphora assemblage.
- **Season IV (2023):** 53 mainly imported pottery sherds, including substantial amphora fragments comparable with Dressel 20, five preliminary Samian identifications (including two rimsherds), two sherds of possible Gaulish ware and at least two tiny fragments of prehistoric pottery.
- **Season V (2025):** 127 sherds of imported pottery; most are amphora fragments comparable with Dressel 20. Four burnt Samian sherds were identified as Central Gaulish, probably from one late Antonine dish, and three further rimsherds were preliminarily identified as Samian.

These imports are important evidence for Drumanagh's connections with the Roman world. They do not by themselves prove Roman occupation, a Roman military fort, or a single organised trading-post function. Context disturbance is significant, particularly in Season V where a large proportion of pottery was recovered from topsoil.

## 1. Dressel 20 amphorae — what the type means

Dressel 20 was a large globular transport amphora used primarily for olive oil. The Drumanagh excavation reports identify the production region as Baetica in southern Spain, within the Roman Empire, and give a production span from the 1st to 3rd centuries AD (later reports phrase this as late 1st to 3rd centuries AD).

This is the production date and provenance of the vessel type, not a direct date for every Drumanagh deposit containing a sherd. A sherd may be residual, redeposited, reused or present in a later context.

The Season III report discusses Drumanagh's Dressel 20 finds as evidence of imported olive oil. The Season IV and V reports continue to identify amphora sherds comparable with Dressel 20.

**Source:** Christine Baker, Season I–V preliminary reports; Season III report cites Williams & Peacock 1983 for the type and production origin.

## 2. Season I (2018) — two Dressel 20 sherds

The Season II preliminary report's historical synthesis of Season I states that:
- two Dressel 20 pottery sherds were recovered;
- they came from what would have been the original ground level south of the Martello road;
- that area had been disturbed by ploughing;
- the sherds came from amphorae used to transport olive oil;
- Dressel 20 was produced in Baetica, southern Spain, between the 1st and 3rd centuries AD.

The report does not provide individual object numbers for these two sherds in the passage reviewed. Their context is disturbed, so the finds cannot be used as a sealed deposit date.

## 3. Season II (2019) — amphora sherds and Holt mortarium

### Pottery count and amphora comparison

The Season II report registers **87 pottery sherds**, mostly prehistoric. It notes that two thick rim sherds and an amphora base recovered during Season II are comparable to the two Dressel 20 sherds identified from Season I.

The report does not publish a separate count of confirmed Dressel 20 vessels, minimum vessel count or a complete amphora catalogue in the passage reviewed. The two thick rims and base should be kept as sherd-level evidence; they should not automatically be counted as three vessels.

### Holt mortarium

The Season III report states that a mortarium sherd was recovered during Season II. It identifies the fabric/type as typical of mortaria made at **Holt**, the Roman works depot that supplied pottery and ceramic building materials to the Twentieth Legion at Chester.

The report states:
- mortaria were multipurpose mixing bowls, commonly used in food preparation;
- this sherd was confirmed as typical of Holt production;
- the main production period for this Holt pottery was the first half of the 2nd century AD;
- Holt pottery distribution beyond Chester and its dependent civilian settlements had not been comprehensively studied;
- a small number of Holt-attributable mortaria had been recorded at Segontium (Caernarfon).

**Critical limitation:** The published Season III discussion does not provide the Drumanagh mortarium's object number, context, exact fabric code, rim form, dimensions, photograph or specialist report. Do not infer that it came from F54, F60, the working platform or a particular structure.

Source: Baker, *Season III Preliminary Report* (December 2022), p. 59:
https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2023-02/Prelim%20Report%20III%20Drumanagh%20E4805%20NDec%202022.pdf

## 4. Season III (2022) — Roman-world ceramics in Trench 4

The Season III report records **39 pottery sherds**, mostly very small and abraded prehistoric wares. It notes:
- two rim sherds comparable to Dressel 20 amphora fragments from Seasons I and II;
- Dressel 20 was used for olive-oil transport and produced in Baetica, southern Spain;
- four sherds are mentioned in the pottery discussion after the amphora comparison, but the report's passage should be checked against the complete finds register before assigning them all to a Roman type.

The excavation's broader discussion reports Roman-world tableware, imported cups and jars, glass vessels and beads. The official Fingal project summary says pottery from at least four Roman-world types was recovered in Season III.

The trench was testing a suspected lane/trackway, but the excavated area instead revealed a structure-like feature and animal-bone-rich deposit. The presence of imported pottery in this trench is not proof that every sherd came from a primary occupation layer.

## 5. Season IV (2023) — amphora, Samian and possible Gaulish ware

The Season IV report records **53 sherds of mainly imported pottery**:
- substantial amphora sherds comparable with Dressel 20 fragments from Seasons I–III;
- five sherds preliminarily identified as Samian ware (terra sigillata), including two rimsherds;
- two sherds of the same vessel, possibly Gaulish ware;
- at least two tiny fragments of prehistoric, potentially Bronze Age, pottery.

The report describes Samian as red-gloss pottery mass-produced from the 1st to 3rd centuries AD, initially in northern Italy and later in Gaul and Colchester in Roman Britain.

These are preliminary identifications. The report does not provide a full vessel count or complete specialist catalogue in the section reviewed. The two possible Gaulish sherds are not a secure fabric or production-centre attribution until specialist confirmation.

Source: Baker, *Season IV Preliminary Report* (November 2023), p. 60:
https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2025-03/prelim-report-iv-drumanagh-e4805-2023.pdf

## 6. Season V (2025) — large amphora and Samian assemblage

The Season V report records:
- **127 sherds of imported pottery**;
- the vast majority described as amphora fragments comparable with Dressel 20 from Seasons I–IV;
- approximately two-thirds of the imported pottery sherds reported as recovered from topsoil, indicating substantial disturbance;
- four burnt Samian sherds, three apparently from the same vessel;
- the burnt Samian identified by Alex Croom as Central Gaulish, from a heavily burnt dish of late Antonine form;
- three further rimsherds preliminarily identified as Samian ware.

The report describes Samian (terra sigillata) as red-gloss pottery mass-produced from the 1st to 3rd centuries AD.

### Count and context caution

The report also states that 203 artefacts were recovered from Trench 6 topsoil, including 93 amphora sherds. This topsoil count is important evidence of disturbance and redeposition. The complete finds register is needed to reconcile the 93 topsoil amphora sherds with the wider 127 imported pottery count and the report's statement that roughly two-thirds of imported sherds came from topsoil.

Do not treat all 127 sherds as 127 amphorae or 127 individual vessels. Sherd count is not vessel count.

Source: Baker, *Season V Preliminary Report* (August 2025), pp. 50–53:
https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2025-09/prelim-report-v-drumanagh-e4805-2025.pdf

## 7. The “intact Roman pot” reported in May 2025 — a provenance and identification issue

News reports in May 2025 described an apparently intact Roman pot recovered during the Drumanagh excavation and said it would be sent to the National Museum of Ireland for analysis and conservation.

However, the Season V preliminary report documents an object group from pit **F163** that was initially thought to include an amphora rim/neck and a complete pot. After X-ray at the National Museum of Ireland, CT scanning at UCD and excavation under laboratory conditions, the report states that both objects were in fact **stone**, not ceramic.

This creates an important identification question:
- The May 2025 media reports describe an intact Roman pot.
- The Season V report later says a complete pot-like object from F163 was identified as stone.
- The published passages do not provide an object number or explicitly confirm whether the F163 object is the same object shown in the media coverage.

It would be unsafe either to keep calling the F163 object a Roman pot or to assert that it is definitely the same object in the media reports without matching the photograph, object ID and excavation register. The discrepancy should be resolved with Fingal and the National Museum of Ireland.

## 8. What the pottery demonstrates — and what it does not

### Supported by the published evidence
1. Drumanagh received amphorae of the Dressel 20 type associated with olive-oil transport from Baetica in southern Spain.
2. A Season II mortarium sherd was identified as typical of Holt production near Chester, mainly in the first half of the 2nd century AD.
3. Samian ware and other Roman-world pottery were recovered across later seasons.
4. The pottery record spans multiple seasons and contexts, including disturbed layers and topsoil.
5. The assemblage demonstrates material connections with Roman provinces and production centres in the western Roman world.

### Not established
- the number of complete amphorae or minimum number of vessels;
- the exact contents of every amphora sherd;
- the exact find context of the Holt mortarium;
- the identity of the people who imported, traded, used or discarded the vessels;
- whether the goods arrived directly from Baetica or through intermediate ports and traders;
- whether the goods were consumed by local inhabitants, visiting traders, migrants or people with Roman backgrounds;
- a Roman garrison, fort or formal Roman administration at Drumanagh;
- a single continuous trading-post function for the promontory.

Create separate evidence-linked subrecords only when object IDs and contexts are confirmed:
1. Season I Dressel 20 sherd group — two sherds; disturbed ground south of Martello road.
2. Season II Dressel 20-comparable sherds — two thick rims and one amphora base; individual object/context IDs pending.
3. Season II Holt mortarium sherd — production attribution supported by Season III specialist discussion; object ID/context pending.
4. Season III Dressel 20-comparable rimsherds — two rims; exact object/context details pending.
5. Season IV imported pottery — 53 sherds; amphora, Samian, possible Gaulish and prehistoric wares distinguished.
6. Season V imported pottery — 127 sherds; amphora-dominated, with topsoil disturbance explicitly flagged.
7. Season V F163 stone objects — keep separate from ceramic imports; record the later CT identification as stone.

Do not create separate monument pins for individual sherds. Link the finds to the existing Drumanagh monument and the relevant excavation season/trench/context.

---

**Batch 224 conclusion:** Drumanagh's Roman-world ceramic evidence includes Dressel 20 amphorae associated with Baetican olive-oil transport, a Season II mortarium attributed to the Holt industry near Chester, and Samian and other imported wares in Seasons IV and V. The evidence supports long-distance connections and imported food preparation/consumption, but the exact find contexts and vessel counts remain incomplete in public preliminary reports. The apparent 2025 “intact Roman pot” must be checked against the Season V report's later identification of two F163 pot-like objects as stone before any object record is published.
