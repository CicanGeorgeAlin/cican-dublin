# Drumanagh Season I Human Remains — Context, Osteology and Radiocarbon Evidence (Research Batch 221)

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Site:** Drumanagh Promontory Fort, Loughshinny, County Dublin  
**Recorded monument:** DU008-006001  
**Excavation:** Digging Drumanagh, Season I, 2018  
**Consent / excavation reference:** C786 / E004805  
**Subject:** Two disarticulated human bone fragments from Trench 2  
**Research status:** Documentation-only; no production catalogue or map changes  
**Research date:** 20 November 2026

## Executive finding

Season I (2018) recovered two **disarticulated human bone fragments** from separate contexts in Trench 2 near the Martello road:
1. An adult femur, find number **E4805:11:26**, from layer/Feature F11, described as a probable original ground-level deposit disturbed by later activity and the Martello road.
2. A cranial vault fragment, find number **E4805:16:2**, from F16, a later deposit associated with the possible interior of a structure and containing 19th-century material.

The 2019 Season II preliminary report publishes radiocarbon results:
- Cranial fragment, laboratory code **UBA-38844**: 2042 ± 44 BP; calibrated 95% probability **170 BC–cal AD 52**.
- Adult femur, laboratory code **UBA-38843**: 1976 ± 35 BP; calibrated 95% probability **49 BC–cal AD 118**.

These are two distinct dated bone samples, recovered from separate contexts. The reports state that they indicate at least two individuals and that the remains were disturbed by the Martello road and subsequent ploughing. The available reports do not describe articulated skeletons, intact graves, grave cuts, burial orientation or grave goods. Therefore, the safest archive description is **two disarticulated human bone finds with radiocarbon dates**, rather than two excavated in-situ burials.

## 1. Excavation and find identities

Christine Baker's July 2018 *Season I Preliminary Report* documents two trenches excavated between 21 and 31 May 2018. The human remains were recovered from Trench 2.

### Find A — adult femur

- Find number: **E4805:11:26**
- Bone: diaphyseal fragment of an adult left femur.
- Context: **F11**, described as a dark brown, moderately compact, friable clayey silt layer south of the Martello road.
- F11 interpretation: probable original ground surface / ground layer disturbed by later activity and insertion of the Martello road.
- Other finds in F11 included 19th-century pottery, iron nails and glass, as well as a decorated antler comb, partial blue glass bead and incomplete bone pin.
- The report says the femur was retrieved from the probable original ground level.

Source: Season I preliminary report, excavation stratigraphy and finds:
https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2020-01/season-i-preliminary-report-drumanagh-e4805.pdf

### Find B — cranial fragment

- Find number: **E4805:16:2**
- Bone: right frontal cranial vault fragment.
- Osteological description in the excavation report: possibly female adult.
- Context: **F16**, a compact dark-brown silty clay deposit with small stone, mortar and animal bone inclusions.
- F16 was possibly associated with the interior of a structure (F3/F7), directly overlaying pit F19 and natural subsoil.
- F16 also contained clay pipe, bottle glass and 19th-century pottery.
- The fragment was disarticulated.

Source: Season I preliminary report, Trench 2 Feature 16:
https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2020-01/season-i-preliminary-report-drumanagh-e4805.pdf

**Context implication:** Neither fragment came from a documented intact grave. Both occurred in deposits affected by later activity, and the cranial fragment's context contained modern-period material. The bone dates the human remains themselves, not the formation date of the mixed archaeological deposits.

## 2. Osteological identification

The Season I report states that Dr Linda Lynch was examining the disarticulated remains. The 2019 Season II report subsequently describes:
- the cranial fragment as belonging to a female individual aged between 18 and 45 years at death;
- the second fragment as an adult femur.

The 2018 preliminary report gives the cranial assessment as “possibly female adult”; the 2019 report gives the more developed estimate of female, 18–45 years. This should be represented as an osteological assessment, not a certainty about biological sex or exact age.

The available excavation reports do not provide a complete osteological catalogue, measurements, pathological observations, ancestry assessment, DNA results, stable isotope results, or a detailed minimum-number-of-individuals analysis beyond the report's conclusion that the two distinct fragments represent at least two individuals.

## 3. Radiocarbon results

The 2019 Season II preliminary report publishes both laboratory codes and radiocarbon measurements:

| Find | Laboratory code | Conventional radiocarbon age | Calibrated range, 95% probability |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Cranial fragment E4805:16:2 | UBA-38844 | 2042 ± 44 BP | 170 BC–cal AD 52 |
| Femur E4805:11:26 | UBA-38843 | 1976 ± 35 BP | 49 BC–cal AD 118 |

Source: Baker, *Digging Drumanagh, Season II: Preliminary Report* (November 2019), p. 20:
https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2020-01/prelim-report-season-ii-drumanagh-e4805-nov-2019.pdf

The published ranges overlap in the late first century BC to early first century AD. They do not establish that the two people died at the same time, were related, were buried together, or belonged to one burial event. Their separate contexts and different laboratory ages must remain visible.

The 2019 report describes the dates as 95% probability ranges. The available text does not give a more detailed multi-modal calibration distribution or calibration curve version. Do not recalculate or narrow the dates without the original laboratory certificates and calibration details.

## 4. What the reports say about disturbance

The Season I report interprets both fragments as indicative of disturbance caused by the construction of the Martello road. The 2019 report adds that subsequent ploughing south of the road also disturbed the remains.

The contexts are important:
- F11 is interpreted as a probable original ground layer but contains 19th-century material and artefacts.
- F16 is a later deposit with clay pipe, bottle glass and 19th-century pottery.
- Neither context is an intact grave.
- The femur and cranial fragment were recovered separately.

This evidence supports the conclusion that human remains from earlier activity were disturbed and redeposited or incorporated into later deposits. It does not reveal the original grave locations or burial form.

## 5. “At least two burials” — wording caution

Fingal County Council's public project page summarises the radiocarbon results as “indicating at least two burials in this area”. The excavation reports, however, describe **disarticulated fragments** in separate contexts and explicitly discuss disturbance. No intact burial feature is documented for these two fragments in the material reviewed.

For the archive:
- It is acceptable to report that the public project summary interprets the dates as indicating at least two burials.
- The detailed object record should use “disarticulated human remains” or “human bone fragments”.
- Do not assign grave numbers, burial orientations, grave cuts, burial positions or a cemetery boundary.
- Do not state that the two people were interred simultaneously.
- Do not infer a Roman military burial ground.

Public summary: https://www.fingal.ie/digging-drumanagh

## 6. Relationship to other Drumanagh evidence

The two radiocarbon results are consistent with human activity in the broad late Iron Age / early Roman-period horizon represented at Drumanagh. They are not dates for:
- the decorated antler combs;
- the Dressel 20 amphora sherds;
- the bone pins or glass bead;
- the structures excavated in Seasons II–V;
- the promontory ramparts as a whole.

The 2019 report notes that the dates fall within a similar broad period to other site evidence, but the human remains are from disturbed contexts. Chronological overlap does not prove direct association.

The Discovery Programme's Late Iron Age and Roman Ireland project investigated wider questions about interaction with the Roman world and used scientific analyses of human remains from Ireland. The project page does not identify these two Drumanagh samples as having been included in its isotope study; do not assume they were.

Source: https://discoveryprogramme.ie/projects/past-projects/late-iron-age-roman-ireland-project/

---

**Batch 221 conclusion:** Two disarticulated human bone fragments from separate Trench 2 contexts have radiocarbon dates spanning 170 BC–AD 118. The cranial fragment is from a later mixed deposit containing 19th-century material; the femur came from a probable original ground layer also affected by later disturbance. These findings support evidence for human remains of late Iron Age/early Roman-period date at Drumanagh, but do not document two intact graves or a defined cemetery. Keep the two finds separate, retain their laboratory codes and context histories, and seek the original osteological and radiocarbon records before making stronger claims.
