# Drumanagh Promontory Fort — Season V Trench 6 Finds and Assemblage (Research Batch 219)

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentation-only; no production catalogue or map changes  
**Site:** Drumanagh Promontory Fort, Loughshinny, County Dublin  
**Monument reference:** DU008-006001  
**2025 consent / excavation reference:** C786 / E004805  
**Season:** V, 14–28 May 2025  
**Research date:** 20 November 2026

## Executive finding

The 2025 Season V excavation at Drumanagh opened one trench (Trench 6, 20m north–south by 5m east–west) on relatively high ground of the promontory. The preliminary report identifies shallow pits, postholes/stakeholes, occupation deposits and stone deposits associated with substantial prehistoric activity. The excavator interprets the main evidence as an area of waste clearance, potentially from nearby habitation, while emphasising that further specialist work and radiocarbon dating are required.

The most striking assemblage is the **32,903 sea shells** registered from 104 samples, including material from a large shell-rich pit (F161). The finds register records 484 artefacts, including pottery, bone and antler, metal, flint, glass, stone and possible enamel. The report describes dice, gaming pieces, combs, bone tools, stone counters and unusual stone vessels/lamps.

This is an unusually rich evidence set, but it is still a preliminary report. No final chronology for Trench 6 is established by the Season V report; AMS dating material was still to be selected and specialist analysis was ongoing.

## 2. Excavated features and deposits

Trench 6 exposed a series of shallow pits, postholes/stakeholes, depressions and occupation layers. The report records eight principal shallow pits in the area delineated by large stone deposits. These were generally cut through a rich cultural layer into natural subsoil.

### Pit F161 — shell-rich pit

F161 was a large oval pit cut into natural subsoil:
- approximately 1.6m north–south × 2.18m east–west;
- depth approximately 0.7m;
- it had been recut and deliberately capped;
- one fill contained a very large quantity of shell, described as roughly 75% of the deposit;
- shell included limpets, mussels and periwinkles;
- animal bone was also recovered;
- finds from the pit included a bone-pin fragment (161:1), bone pin (161:2), bone tool (161:3) and possible struck flint (161:4).

The report suggests the shells may reflect food consumption, but the detailed specialist analysis remained pending. The feature should be described as a shell-rich pit, not definitively as a midden or feasting deposit.

### Pit F163 — unusual stone-lined feature

F163 was interpreted as a stone-lined storage pit, with a void in its eastern half suggesting possible organic contents. It contained several distinct fills, stone packing, tracked/rubbing stones, a bone pin and remains of a copper-alloy object. At least four stone vessels/lamps were identified in or associated with the pit. Their exact function remains unresolved; comparisons are made with Iron Age Scottish sites, but this is not proof that the objects were lamps or that they had a particular imported origin.

### Other features

The report records additional shallow pits, stakeholes/postholes and depressions. Some contained animal bone, shell, charcoal, iron objects and possible struck flint. The stakeholes were shallow and no clear pattern was discerned. The excavator notes that the deposits and features are consistent with activity involving waste deposition, but their precise function and relationship to nearby habitation are not resolved.

## 3. Shell assemblage

The preliminary report registers:
- **104 shell samples**;
- **32,903 shells** from layers and features;
- material from shell-rich pit F161 and deposits within layer F153;
- approximately one quarter of the shell assemblage came from topsoil.

The report notes periwinkles and limpets during retrieval and suggests that edible shellfish may reflect diet. The exact species composition, quantification by context and taphonomic analysis require specialist work.

The count is a registered shell total, not 32,903 separate archaeological features or sites. It belongs to the Trench 6 assemblage.

## 4. Artefact assemblage and an internal count discrepancy

The preliminary report states that **484 artefacts** were registered, with just over 40% retrieved from topsoil. It then lists the following category counts:

| Material/category | Count |
|---|---:|
| Antler | 35 |
| Bone | 97 |
| Pottery | 140 |
| Metal | 67 |
| Possible enamel | 5 |
| Flint | 26 |
| Glass | 25 |
| Stone | 72 |
| **Sum of listed categories** | **467** |
| **Report's stated total** | **484** |
| **Difference not explained in this summary** | **17** |

The eight category counts add to 467, not 484. The preliminary report does not explain the difference in the summary passage. It may reflect an omitted or unclassified category, later registration changes, or a reporting error; no explanation should be invented. This discrepancy should be checked against the final finds register.

The report separately states that 132 antler/bone artefacts were registered (35 antler + 97 bone), which reconciles internally.

## 5. Dice and gaming pieces — count discrepancy between sources

The Season V preliminary report says **six dice** and a range of gaming pieces and pegs were recovered. It identifies two complete parallelopiped dice as finds **150:2** and **152:68**; the remainder were partial, including one burnt example (**151:10**). It also records at least 12 pegged gaming pieces, plus additional heads/fragments of gaming pieces.

The Excavations.ie summary for 2025:715 says **seven dice**, as well as gaming pieces and pegs.

This is a clear discrepancy:
- Preliminary report: six dice.
- Excavations.ie annual summary: seven dice.

Do not silently choose one count. The final specialist catalogue or finds register should resolve it. The report also compares the forms with Iron Age parallelopiped dice known in Ireland, Scotland and the Western Isles, while noting possible influence from Roman gaming technology. The 2025 dice themselves had not yet been analysed sufficiently to confirm that interpretation.

### Stone gaming pieces

Of 72 stone objects, at least 24 were described as stone counters, although the report cautions that some may result from natural processes. Six conical gaming pieces were identified. Comparisons are made with examples from Knowth, Freestone Hill and the Rath of the Synods at Tara. These are comparative parallels, not proof that all the Drumanagh objects had the same function or date.

## 6. Other notable finds

### Bone and antler
The report records:
- 132 antler/bone artefacts in total;
- ten fragments of bone/antler combs;
- two I-shaped bone objects that may relate to textile production;
- a range of bone tools, pins and gaming equipment;
- a hilt guard (153:63) compared by the report with La Tène sword fittings.

The report notes that the bone/antler material's known comparisons are largely with Ireland, Scotland and pre-Roman Britain, unlike some pottery, metalwork and glass finds with Roman-world connections. The Trench 6 material still required specialist assessment.

### Pottery and amphora
The report registers 140 pottery artefacts and describes 127 sherds of imported pottery. Most are amphora fragments, comparable with Dressel 20 amphorae used to transport olive oil and produced in the Roman province of Baetica in southern Spain between the late 1st and 3rd centuries AD. Approximately two thirds of the amphora sherds came from topsoil, indicating disturbance.

Four sherds of burnt Samian ware were also identified; three may belong to the same vessel. Preliminary identification describes a heavily burnt Central Gaulish dish of late Antonine form. Three additional rimsherds were preliminarily identified as Samian ware. These identifications are provisional and must be attributed to the report's specialists.

### Stone vessels / possible lamps
At least four unusual stone vessels or possible lamps were associated with F163. Their function is not settled. The report describes two forms, including taller column-like examples and rounder vessel-like examples, and notes that at least one retains a possible handle. Comparisons with stone vessels from Clachtoll Broch in Scotland are research parallels only. The report explicitly leaves open whether these were lamps, vessels for processing/storing substances, or multi-use objects.

## 7. Dating and interpretation limits

The 2025 report states that:
- specialist analysis was ongoing;
- dating material would be selected for AMS dating after consultation with specialists;
- the post-excavation programme included archaeobotanical, animal bone, pottery, small-find, conservation and radiocarbon work;
- the conclusion was that evidence of Iron Age activity lay only centimetres below the surface and had been severely affected by ploughing.

The report's broad site context and earlier seasons include Iron Age and Romano-British evidence. The Season V preliminary report does not provide a new final radiocarbon date for Trench 6. Dates from earlier trenches must not be transferred to the Season V deposits or objects.

The excavator interprets the Season V evidence as an area of waste clearance, potentially from nearby habitation, but explicitly raises unresolved alternatives: waste from nearby habitation, feasting/food-related activity, or an area where bone artefacts were processed and produced. The evidence does not yet distinguish these possibilities.

## 8. Archive treatment

**Recommended archive record:** Excavation event / Trench 6 / Drumanagh Promontory Fort / Season V (2025).

This is an excavation record within an already recognised National Monument, not a new independent monument or a new place-name POI.

- Preserve the consent and excavation references: C786 / E004805.
- Preserve the Excavations.ie reference 2025:715 and the trench location as the report's published ITM coordinate.
- Keep the shell total, artefact catalogue and feature descriptions linked to Trench 6.
- Flag the 484-versus-467 artefact category discrepancy.
- Flag the six-versus-seven dice discrepancy between the preliminary report and Excavations.ie summary.
- Keep all provisional object identifications provisional.
- Do not label the shell-rich pit definitively as a midden or feasting deposit.
- Do not assign a final date to Trench 6 before specialist analysis and radiocarbon results.
- Do not create separate map pins for each artefact or for the pits inside the trench.
- Do not imply that the 2025 work establishes a Roman fort, Roman military occupation or a single settled interpretation of Drumanagh.

---

**Batch 219 conclusion:** Season V at Drumanagh produced a substantial Trench 6 assemblage, including 32,903 shells, gaming equipment, pottery, bone/antler objects, flint, metal, glass and unusual stone vessels. The published sources contain two count discrepancies (484 total artefacts versus 467 across the listed categories; six versus seven dice). The excavation's interpretation and chronology remain provisional pending final specialist work and radiocarbon dating.
