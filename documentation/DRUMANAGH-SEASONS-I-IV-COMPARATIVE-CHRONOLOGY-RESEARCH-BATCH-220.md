# Drumanagh Excavation Seasons I–IV — Comparative Chronology and Evidence Synthesis (Research Batch 220)

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Site:** Drumanagh Promontory Fort, Loughshinny, County Dublin  
**Recorded monument:** DU008-006001  
**National Monument:** Preservation Order No. 13/177  
**Protected Structure:** No. 252  
**Project:** Digging Drumanagh — Fingal Community Excavation Project  
**Research status:** Documentation-only; no production catalogue or map changes  
**Research date:** 20 November 2026

## Executive summary

The first four scientific excavation seasons at Drumanagh (2018, 2019, 2022 and 2023) show a developing picture of prehistoric activity, structures and craft-working, with imported Roman-world objects occurring in deposits associated with local activity. The trenches were not one continuous excavation: each season addressed a different question and sampled a different part of the promontory.

The clearest cumulative pattern is:
- **Season I (2018), Trenches 1–2:** examined the Martello road and its impact on earlier deposits; recovered flint, bone/antler objects, Roman-world pottery, human bone and later military material.
- **Season II (2019), Trench 3:** identified post- and stakehole evidence for a structure and an adjacent flagged/metalled working surface, with a weaving comb, bone points/tools, needle and Roman-world material.
- **Season III (2022), Trench 4:** tested a suspected trackway; no convincing trackway was found in the trench. Instead, a structure-like sunken area with metalled surface and large flags, bone-rich occupation/waste deposits, imported pottery, glass, bone tools, pins and spindle whorl were identified.
- **Season IV (2023), Trenches 5A and 5B:** tested geophysical and topographical anomalies. Trench 5B revealed another structure comparable to Trenches 3 and 4; Trench 5A revealed shallow pit activity, stone deposits and two large bell-shaped storage pits later reused as waste pits.

The preliminary reports repeatedly state that specialist post-excavation work was ongoing. This synthesis therefore preserves each report's interpretations as preliminary and does not impose one final chronology across all seasons.

## 1. Site identity and research framework

Drumanagh is a coastal headland between Loughshinny and Rush, County Dublin. The promontory is approximately 46 acres and is defended on its western side by closely spaced banks and ditches. The site is recorded as DU008-006001 and is protected as a National Monument and Protected Structure.

The Digging Drumanagh project was designed to address research gaps identified in the *Drumanagh Conservation Study and Management Plan 2018–2023*, improve understanding of the monument and involve the local community. Fingal County Council describes these as the first scientific excavations undertaken at the site.

Official project overview: https://www.fingal.ie/digging-drumanagh

## 2. Season I — 2018: Martello road and earlier deposits

**Excavations.ie:** 2018:137  
**Trenches:** 1 and 2  
**Dates:** 21–31 May 2018 (10 days)  
**Focus:** the original approach road to the early 19th-century Martello Tower and the impact of its construction on underlying archaeology.

Sources:
- Preliminary report: https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2020-01/season-i-preliminary-report-drumanagh-e4805.pdf
- Excavations.ie: https://excavations.ie/report/2018/Dublin/0026579/

### Excavated evidence

Trench 1 measured 15m north–south by 4m; Trench 2 measured 10m by 5m. The report describes natural subsoil overlain by disturbed loamy deposits truncated by construction of the Martello roadway.

Earlier activity was present but disturbed:
- Five fragments of struck flint and one animal bone were recovered from interface layer F15 in Trench 1.
- A shell-rich deposit (F12) occurred south of the road in Trench 1.
- A possible structure corner (F7) was identified in Trench 1.
- Evidence of the road included banks, footings and compacted roadway layers.

The Season I report's finds and discussion identify antler comb material, including a decorated comb fragment, Dressel 20 amphora sherds, a bone hilt associated with a small La Tène-style sword, glass bead/bone pins and human skeletal fragments. These are finds from disturbed or varied contexts and should not be treated as one closed assemblage.

### Human remains and dating

The official project summary reports:
- a cranial fragment from an adult female, estimated age 18–45, dated to 170 BC–cal AD 52;
- an adult femur from south of the Martello road dated to cal BC 49–cal AD 118.

The 2019 Season II report reproduces the radiocarbon results and notes that the bones came from distinct features/locations, indicating at least two individuals. The dates do not establish a single burial event or a cemetery plan. The cranial fragment's context was disturbed.

### Later activity

The excavation also recovered material associated with the Martello Tower and its occupants, including a Royal Downshire Militia belt plate dating to 1794–1800, a Royal Artillery shako plate and two buttons. These are later military-period finds and must remain distinct from the prehistoric and Roman-period evidence.

## 3. Season II — 2019: Trench 3, structure and working platform

**Excavations.ie:** 2019:299  
**Trench:** 3  
**Dates:** 15–29 May 2019 (13 days)  
**Focus:** the original Martello road at the western end of the promontory and the impact of its construction on earlier deposits.

Sources:
- Preliminary report: https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2020-01/prelim-report-season-ii-drumanagh-e4805-nov-2019.pdf
- Excavations.ie: https://excavations.ie/report/2019/Dublin/0028923/

### Excavated evidence

Trench 3 was approximately 20m north–south by 5m, with a 14m² extension. It lay about 350m west-south-west of the Season I trenches.

The excavation identified:
- an occupation layer (F49/F50) cut by pits, postholes and stakeholes;
- a series of post/stakeholes interpreted as a structure extending beyond the trench;
- a metalled surface (F46);
- a stone platform/large flags (F26);
- gravel deposits and finds-rich deposits including F45 and F51.

The report interprets the metalled and flagged area as a possible working platform. It distinguishes material that may have been redeposited for levelling from material east of the flagged surface that may represent primary activity.

### Craft-working evidence

The finds include:
- a decorated antler weaving comb (E4805:51:2);
- numerous bone points;
- possible bone tools;
- a bone needle;
- a stone counter;
- a fragment of flint;
- a bone bead or ring.

The report interprets the combination as evidence for processing/craft-working and textile production in the vicinity. This is a functional interpretation based on the assemblage and context, not proof that every object was used at the platform.

### Roman-world material

Almost 80 Dressel 20 amphora sherds are reported in the official Fingal project overview for Season II, from vessels used to transport olive oil and produced in Baetica, southern Spain, during the 1st–3rd centuries AD. Other Romano-British artefacts were also present. The presence of imported pottery is evidence for contact and exchange, but by itself does not establish Roman military occupation or direct political control.

## 4. Season III — 2022: Trench 4, trackway question and structure

**Excavations.ie:** 2022:584  
**Trench:** 4  
**Dates:** 17–31 August 2022 (13 days)  
**Focus:** test a possible lane/trackway identified through aerial photography, geophysical survey and hillshade analysis.

Sources:
- Preliminary report: https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2023-02/Prelim%20Report%20III%20Drumanagh%20E4805%20NDec%202022.pdf
- Excavations.ie: https://excavations.ie/report/2022/Dublin/0032608/

### Trackway result

The trench crossed the western limit of a sub-rectangular hollow. The report found no convincing evidence of the proposed trackway in the excavated area. The hollow instead contained a structure-like feature.

### Trench 4 evidence

The northern half included pits, possible postholes, stone sockets and uneven depressions. A bone-rich layer contained an amphora base, worked bone tools, copper-alloy pins and a spindle whorl.

The southern half contained a rectilinear cut interpreted as a structure, with:
- a basal metalled surface;
- packed soil and possible entrance/posthole evidence;
- large stone flags;
- internal occupation deposits rich in animal bone and artefacts.

The report compares this arrangement with the Season II working platform in Trench 3. It notes similarities in the metalled surface, flags, posthole and bone-/artefact-rich deposits. The comparison supports a possible recurring structure type, but the report still treats interpretation and post-excavation analysis as ongoing.

### Finds

The Trench 4 report records Roman-world pottery, glass vessel fragments, glass beads, copper-alloy pins, worked bone, a spindle whorl, struck flint and other objects. A human bone was also recovered from pit F80. The available report does not establish that this represents a burial; it should be recorded simply as human bone from that feature unless further osteological/contextual evidence becomes available.

## 5. Season IV — 2023: Trenches 5A and 5B, geophysical anomalies and storage pits

**Excavations.ie:** 2023:282  
**Trenches:** 5A and 5B  
**Dates:** 19 July–2 August 2023 (13 days)  
**Focus:** investigate differences between geophysical survey results and topographic features.

Sources:
- Preliminary report: https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2025-03/prelim-report-iv-drumanagh-e4805-2023.pdf
- Excavations.ie: https://excavations.ie/report/2023/Dublin/0033151/

### Trench 5A

Trench 5A measured 15m east–west by 4m and targeted geophysical anomalies interpreted as a possible kiln and a series of pits.

The excavation identified:
- shallow pit activity on higher ground;
- a layer of large stones and a stone deposit;
- two large bell-/funnel-shaped pits;
- evidence that the pits had been cut/re-cut and later reused as waste pits.

The geophysical anomalies had initially been interpreted as possible kilns, but the excavated features were interpreted as storage pits. The report discusses the possibility of grain storage, while acknowledging that this is an interpretation based on their morphology and parallels. Do not label them kilns.

The two pits were not fully excavated. Their bases were not reached in the investigated portions, and their later fills included animal bone, charcoal and artefacts. Finds included bone comb, pins, scoop, spindle whorl, antler offcuts, ceramic weight and a glass ball.

### Trench 5B

Trench 5B measured 7m east–west by 4m and was placed in a hollow 9m west of Trench 5A. Excavation showed that the hollow contained another structure. The report compares it with the structures/working areas in Trenches 3 and 4.

The report notes repeated elements:
- basal metalled surface;
- possible northern entrance with attendant posthole;
- internal slope;
- large flags;
- Romano-British artefacts in internal deposits.

The report says a specific structure type may be emerging across Trenches 3, 4 and 5B. This is a working archaeological interpretation, not a fully established site-wide building typology.

## 6. Cross-season synthesis

| Season | Year | Excavated area | Main research question | Principal result |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| I | 2018 | Trenches 1–2 | Martello road impact | Road disturbed earlier deposits; prehistoric/Roman-world finds and human bone; later tower-period material |
| II | 2019 | Trench 3 | Road impact and earlier stratigraphy | Post/stakehole structure and flagged/metalled working area; weaving/craft-working evidence |
| III | 2022 | Trench 4 | Suspected trackway | No clear trackway in trench; structure-like hollow with bone-rich occupation deposits |
| IV | 2023 | Trenches 5A–5B | Geophysical/topographic anomalies | Repeated structure-like form in 5B; shallow pits and two large storage-pit-like features in 5A |

### Emerging patterns

1. **Repeated structural activity:** Trenches 3, 4 and 5B share features interpreted as structures or working areas, including metalled surfaces, stone flags, possible entrances and artefact-/bone-rich internal deposits.
2. **Craft-working:** Season II's weaving comb, bone points and needle, and Season IV's comb, pins, scoop, spindle whorl and antler offcuts, reinforce evidence for textile and bone/antler working.
3. **Food and waste deposition:** Animal-bone-rich deposits recur in Seasons III and IV; Season IV's bell-shaped pits appear to have been reused for waste. The 2025 Season V shell-rich deposits expand this theme but require separate analysis.
4. **Roman-world contact:** Imported pottery, amphorae, Samian ware and glass recur across multiple seasons. These finds demonstrate connections with the Romanised world but do not establish the presence of a Roman fort or garrison.
5. **Landscape disturbance:** Martello road construction, later agriculture and ploughing affected archaeological deposits. Some finds are from disturbed or redeposited contexts.
6. **Chronological complexity:** The promontory contains prehistoric, Iron Age, Roman-period, early medieval, medieval and post-medieval evidence. These cannot be compressed into one continuous occupation phase.

## 7. Chronology: what can and cannot be said

- The Season I human bone radiocarbon results fall broadly around the late centuries BC to early centuries AD. They relate to two separate human bone finds and their respective contexts, not to every Season I artefact.
- Dressel 20 amphorae were produced in Baetica in the 1st–3rd centuries AD; this is the production range of the vessel type, not a direct radiocarbon date for each Drumanagh context.
- The reports describe some structures and craft-working as probable Iron Age, with Roman-world material present in deposits. The exact relationship between all features, objects and dates remains under specialist study.
- The 2024 post-excavation programme identified a charred fig from earlier excavations, reported by Fingal as approximately 2,000 years old and without parallel in Ireland. It is a site-wide post-excavation result; the project page does not identify it as a Season V object.
- No single definitive chronology for all Seasons I–IV should be published from these preliminary reports alone.

## 8. Archive treatment

**Recommended record type:** One excavation project with separate season/trench event records, all linked to the existing monument DU008-006001.

Do not create four new monument pins. The seasons are archaeological investigations within the same recognised monument.

For each season, preserve:
- season number and year;
- Excavations.ie report number;
- consent/excavation reference;
- trench numbers, dimensions and published coordinates where supplied;
- excavation dates;
- features and finds linked to their own contexts;
- the report's interpretive confidence and post-excavation status.

Keep later Martello Tower material, human bone, imported pottery, craft objects, flint and storage pits as separate evidence categories. Do not transfer dates from one trench to another.

## 9. Next research priorities

1. **Season I human remains:** obtain the osteological report and radiocarbon laboratory certificates, preserving the two individuals and find contexts separately.
2. **Season II comb and textile assemblage:** investigate the decorated antler weaving comb E4805:51:2, its context and specialist identification.
3. **Seasons III–IV structure comparison:** compare the original plans/sections and feature matrices for Trenches 3, 4 and 5B before asserting a common building type.
4. **Season IV storage pits:** obtain final specialist interpretation and any dating for F108 and F142; do not call them kilns.
5. **Roman imports:** consolidate amphora, Samian and glass identifications by context and season, keeping vessel-type production dates separate from context dates.
6. **Final reports:** update this synthesis only when final specialist reports and dating results are published.

---

**Batch 220 conclusion:** Seasons I–IV reveal a recurring pattern of prehistoric structural activity, craft-working, animal-bone-rich deposits and Roman-world imports, alongside later disturbance and Martello Tower-period material. The most defensible synthesis is a multi-period promontory landscape with repeated activity zones—not a single proven Roman fort or one uninterrupted settlement phase. All interpretations remain tied to the relevant season, trench and preliminary source until final post-excavation work is published.
