# Drumanagh Spelt Wheat — Archaeobotanical Evidence, Contexts and Dating (Research Batch 223)

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Site:** Drumanagh Promontory Fort, Loughshinny, County Dublin  
**Recorded monument:** DU008-006001  
**Excavation project:** Digging Drumanagh, Ministerial Consent C786 / Excavation E004805  
**Principal finds:** Spelt wheat (*Triticum spelta*) reported from Season II, Trench 3 pits F54 and F60  
**Research status:** Documentation-only; no production catalogue or map changes  
**Research date:** 20 November 2026

## Executive finding

The strongest published context-specific evidence for spelt wheat at Drumanagh comes from **two pits in Season II (2019), Trench 3**:
- **F54:** a circular pit, 0.7m in diameter, cut into natural subsoil. Its upper fill contained articulated pig bone and a possible flint core. Soil sample **#11** was taken from the pit.
- **F60:** an irregular, possibly sub-circular pit extending beyond the north-eastern limit of the trench. It contained two fills with frequent animal bone and a possible worked-bone object. Samples **#9** (upper fill) and **#10** (lower fill) were taken.

The 2022 Season III preliminary report states that archaeobotanical analysis identified spelt wheat in both pits and gives associated date ranges of **AD 17–139 for F54** and **AD 121–344 for F60**, respectively. The report does not publish the number or weight of spelt grains in either pit, nor does it provide the underlying laboratory certificates or sample-by-sample archaeobotanical table.

The 2023 Season IV report states that archaeobotanical evidence from Seasons II and III indicates spelt was present at Drumanagh. It argues that this, together with mortaria and bell-shaped storage pits, suggests grain growing, processing, storage and consumption with parallels in Roman Britain. This is a reasoned interpretation, not proof that all Drumanagh agriculture followed a Roman British model or that the site was a Roman military installation.

## 1. Primary context evidence — Season II, Trench 3

Christine Baker's *Digging Drumanagh, Season II: Preliminary Report* (November 2019) documents six soil samples, each with a maximum volume of 20 litres, selected from early features cut into subsoil and sent for environmental analysis.

The sample register identifies:
- **Sample #9:** upper fill of pit F60;
- **Sample #10:** lower fill of pit F60;
- **Sample #11:** pit F54;
- **Samples #12 and #13:** stakehole fills in the possible structure in the north-east of Trench 3;
- **Sample #14:** fill of posthole F67, sealed by artefact-rich layer F45.

The report says these samples were to undergo analysis for archaeobotanical remains. The 2019 report itself does not yet name spelt; that identification is reported in the later 2022 Season III discussion.

### Pit F54

The 2019 excavation report describes F54 as:
- located less than 0.2m from the eastern baulk and 1.3m south of a concentration of stakeholes;
- circular in plan, approximately **0.7m diameter**;
- cut into natural subsoil;
- concave in profile, with two distinct fills;
- basal fill F54:1: mid-brown silt with charcoal flecks, maximum depth 0.12m;
- upper fill: mid-dark brown clayey silt with sub-angular stone inclusions, maximum depth 0.22m;
- articulated pig bone in the upper fill;
- a possible flint core also recovered;
- sample #11 taken from the pit for environmental analysis.

The report notes that the ground level around the pit rose relative to the areas immediately north and south, and that its stratigraphic relationship with nearby stakeholes may indicate contemporary use.

### Pit F60

The 2019 report describes F60 as:
- in the north-eastern corner of Trench 3;
- extending beyond the limits of excavation;
- irregular and possibly sub-circular;
- approximately **1.19m east–west**, with the recorded north–south width varying from 0.13m to 0.41m;
- relatively shallow, with two fills measuring 0.1–0.35m in depth;
- basal fill F60:2: dark-brown soft silt with rare charcoal flecks and small sub-angular stones;
- upper fill F60:1: dark-brown loose matrix with frequent angular and sub-angular stones;
- frequent animal-bone inclusions and a possible worked-bone object;
- sample #9 from the upper fill and sample #10 from the lower fill.

Because the pit continued beyond the trench, its complete plan and dimensions were not exposed.

**Sources for both feature descriptions and sample numbers:** Baker 2019, pp. 30 and 39; Appendix 1 feature list.  
Report: https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2020-01/prelim-report-season-ii-drumanagh-e4805-nov-2019.pdf

## 2. Spelt identification and associated dates

The Season III preliminary report (December 2022), in its discussion of the emerging structure type and Roman-world connections, states:

- two Season II pits, F54 and F60, yielded spelt wheat;
- F54 is associated with a date range of **AD 17–139**;
- F60 is associated with a date range of **AD 121–344**;
- spelt was not common in Ireland's past but was strongly associated with farming strategies in Roman Britain, citing archaeobotanist Meriel McClatchie.

Source: Baker 2022, *Digging Drumanagh, Season III Preliminary Report*, p. 59:
https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2023-02/Prelim%20Report%20III%20Drumanagh%20E4805%20NDec%202022.pdf

### Dating caution

The 2022 report presents these ranges alongside the pit identifications, but the public passage does not supply the laboratory codes, dated material description, conventional radiocarbon ages, calibration curve, probability distributions or direct link between a specific grain and the dated sample.

Therefore, the archive should say **“pit-associated date range reported as…”**, not “each spelt grain was directly radiocarbon dated”. The underlying laboratory report and sample register are required before making a stronger statement.

The Season II 2019 report originally listed AMS dating as planned and stated that datable material would be selected in consultation with specialists. The later 2022 report provides the date ranges in its discussion, but the primary certificates remain necessary for independent verification.

## 3. Season III and Season IV archaeobotanical evidence

The 2022 Season III report's post-excavation programme lists archaeobotanical analysis by Dr Meriel McClatchie as ongoing. It says dating material would be selected for AMS dating after consultation with specialists. Its public discussion explicitly names the two Season II pits F54 and F60 as the spelt contexts.

The 2023 Season IV report later states that the archaeobotanical evidence from **Seasons II and III** indicates spelt was present at Drumanagh. However, the report does not identify the Season III context numbers, sample IDs, grain counts or weights in the passage reviewed for this batch.

The Season IV report also records 23 soil samples from Trench 5A and 5B sent for environmental analysis, including samples from pits, postholes, bell-shaped pits, recuts and layers. The report says these samples were to undergo archaeobotanical analysis. This sampling list should not be confused with proof that spelt was found in each of those Season IV features.

## 4. Quantity and preservation: what is not yet published

The sources reviewed for this batch do not provide:
- total spelt grain count from Drumanagh;
- weight or volume of spelt recovered;
- counts per pit, sample or context;
- proportion of whole grains, fragments, chaff or glume bases;
- whether the grains were charred, waterlogged or preserved by another process in each context;
- weed-seed and other crop composition;
- evidence for crop-cleaning waste, malting, sprouting, milling or baking from the F54/F60 samples;
- full archaeobotanical tables and identification criteria.

Popular summaries describe “significant quantities” or “copious amounts” of burned spelt across the excavations. Those phrases are not a substitute for quantified specialist data. The archive should not invent a count or assign the general site-wide description to F54 or F60 individually.

## 5. Interpretation — agriculture, storage, food and exchange

The evidence can be placed within a broader Drumanagh food and exchange assemblage:
- spelt wheat in Season II pits F54 and F60;
- imported Dressel 20 amphora fragments associated with olive-oil transport from Baetica in southern Spain;
- a mortarium sherd in the Season II assemblage, identified in the 2022 report as typical of production at Holt, associated with Chester;
- bell-shaped pits in Season IV Trench 5A, interpreted as originally intended for grain storage and later reused as waste pits;
- the charred fig identified during 2024 post-excavation analysis, with its context date reported separately in the fig research record.

The 2023 Season IV report interprets the combination of spelt, mortaria and bell-shaped pits as evidence that growing, processing, storing and consuming grain at Drumanagh may have followed practices more closely comparable with Roman Britain than with the usual evidence for Irish Iron Age agriculture.

This is an important interpretive model, but the archive should retain its evidential limits:
- the presence of spelt does not alone identify the farmers' origin;
- the cereal does not prove that Roman merchants or soldiers consumed it;
- storage-pit morphology does not prove every pit held grain throughout its use;
- the later reuse of pits for waste is a separate phase from their proposed original storage function;
- Roman-style material culture and foodways do not prove Roman conquest or occupation of Ireland.

## 6. Comparative significance

The Season III report notes that spelt is rare in Ireland's archaeobotanical record but strongly associated with farming strategies in Roman Britain. It compares Drumanagh with an undated posthole at an Iron Age settlement at Baysrath, County Kilkenny, where spelt grains were also recovered. The report discusses the possibility of settlers or traders from Roman Britain in that separate context, but this cannot be transferred as an established explanation for Drumanagh.

The correct conclusion is narrower: Drumanagh has archaeobotanical evidence for spelt in two identified Season II pits, with later reports referring to evidence from Seasons II and III. This adds food production and cereal use to the site's evidence for contact with the Romanised world.

---

**Batch 223 conclusion:** Published evidence securely identifies spelt wheat in Season II Trench 3 pits F54 and F60, with pit-associated date ranges of AD 17–139 and AD 121–344 respectively. The original 2019 sample register identifies the relevant environmental samples (#11 for F54; #9 and #10 for F60). Quantities, grain counts, preservation details and laboratory dating certificates are not included in the public preliminary reports reviewed. Later reports refer to spelt from Seasons II and III, but the Season III contexts remain unlocated in the public text. The next decisive step is the specialist archaeobotanical dataset, not further speculation about trade or diet.
