# Dublin 3 Discovery Batch 003 — Clontarf micro-details

**Research date:** 2026-10-26  
**Scope:** Clontarf Road and Castle Avenue, Dublin 3  
**Method:** Individual NIAH register records, catalogue duplicate checks, and separate records for independently photographable inscriptions and carved details.

## Confirmed catalogue additions

### DUB-BLD-0046 — Central Hall, 71 Clontarf Road
- **NIAH:** [50030188](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030188/central-hall-71-clontarf-road-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin)
- **Irish Grid:** 318790, 236135
- **WGS 84:** 53.3623040, -6.2163841
- **Date:** 1906
- **Documented features:** Sandstone date plaque, cut-granite verge stones and carved kneelers, crenellated parapet, granite plinth and walling, pointed-arch stained-glass windows, granite steps and threshold.
- **History:** NIAH says the hall was built in 1906 on the site of an earlier church of 1867, extended in 1881; it attributes the hall to Collen Brothers and designs by W.M. Mitchell and Sons.
- **Fieldwork:** Exterior from public footpath where visible. Managed access; obtain permission before entering grounds or interior.

### DUB-INS-0003 — Central Hall date plaque
- **Parent:** DUB-BLD-0046
- **NIAH:** 50030188
- **Inscription recorded by NIAH:** “Central Hall 1906”
- **Material/location:** Sandstone plaque on the front (south) elevation.
- **Coordinates:** Reuses the NIAH host-building point, 53.3623040, -6.2163841. This is not an exact plaque coordinate; the façade position must be confirmed visually during fieldwork.
- **Photography:** Square-on lettering, complete inscription, weathering and wider context.

### DUB-BLD-0047 — Clontarf Castle gate lodge
- **NIAH:** [50030163](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030163/clontarf-castle-castle-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Irish Grid:** 319498, 236289
- **WGS 84:** 53.3635277, -6.2056968
- **Date:** 1836–37
- **Documented features:** Three-stage round tower, carved limestone spire, crenellations, carved stringcourses, corbel table with sculpted animal heads, stepped buttresses, shield over former carriage arch, pointed-arch openings, hood-mouldings, limestone mullions, granite bollards and steel chains.
- **Attribution:** NIAH says the gate lodge appears to have been rebuilt with the castle to designs by William Vitruvius Morrison.
- **Fieldwork:** At the entrance to Clontarf Castle Hotel, beside a road roundabout and private grounds. Use only a lawful, safe public viewpoint; do not enter private grounds or stand on the carriageway/roundabout.

### DUB-STR-0039 — Sculpted animal-head corbels
- **Parent:** DUB-BLD-0047
- **NIAH:** 50030163
- **Evidence:** NIAH explicitly records a corbel table with sculpted animal heads and describes the carved stonework as evidence of nineteenth-century stonemasons' craft.
- **Coordinates:** Reuses the gate-lodge building point, 53.3635277, -6.2056968. Exact carving positions and the number of heads are not supplied by NIAH and must be established on site.
- **Photography:** Each visible head separately, wider context, orientation and condition; do not touch the stone.

## Additional research lead — not yet a mapped catalogue record

### 86 Clontarf Road — NIAH 50030182
The NIAH search result describes historic entrance fabric including a petal-motif fanlight, cut-granite entrance steps and platform, cast-iron balustrade, boundary railings and pedestrian gate, associated former gate lodge and outbuilding. The result also describes a terrace built in 1847 by Isaac Warren on ground obtained from the Vernon estate.

**Why not yet mapped:** the exact Irish Grid coordinate and full record could not be retrieved in this pass. No address-derived pin has been invented. This remains a research lead until the underlying point and full record can be inspected.

## Wider source findings and future micro-object work

1. Dublin City Council's *Historic Street Surfaces Conservation Study and Guidance Manual* review records a 2004 city inventory of historic street furniture and surfaces and a 2010 supplementary survey identifying 78 setted streets/lanes. It describes the value of granite flags, kerbs, granite/diorite setts, coal-hole covers and gratings. This is a city-wide methodology/source lead; it does not identify a specific Clontarf pavement object.
2. Dublin City Development Plan 2022–2028 Policy BHA18 lists historic ground surfaces and public-realm details including milestones, jostle stones, ward stones, bollards, coal-hole covers, gratings, boot scrapers, basement lights, street skylights, water troughs, post boxes, lampposts, railings, stone kerbs, pavement flags and setts. This policy applies to Dublin City Council's administrative area only.

## Coordinate method and limits

Original NIAH Irish Grid references are preserved. WGS 84 points were transformed from TM65 / Irish Grid (EPSG:29902) using the project's established transformation operation 1641. The published transformation accuracy does not establish the accuracy of the source NIAH point. For the two micro-details, the point is explicitly inherited from the host building and does not locate the plaque/corbels precisely. All four records remain SOURCE_DERIVED and NOT_FIELD_VERIFIED.
