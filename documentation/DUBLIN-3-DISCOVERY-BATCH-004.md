# Dublin 3 Discovery Batch 004 — Clontarf seafront and civic recreation heritage

**Research date:** 2026-10-26  
**Scope:** Clontarf Promenade, Clontarf Road and Strandville Avenue East, Dublin 3  
**Method:** Individual National Inventory of Architectural Heritage (NIAH) records, duplicate checks against the current catalogue, and source-derived coordinate transformation using the project's established EPSG operation 1641.

## Confirmed catalogue additions

### DUB-BLD-0051 — Clontarf Promenade modernist concrete shelter
- **NIAH:** [50030145](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030145/clontarf-promenade-clontarf-road-dublin-3-co-dublin)
- **Irish Grid:** 320336, 235670
- **WGS 84:** 53.3577789, -6.1933508
- **Date:** NIAH range 1950–1960; built circa 1955–1958
- **Documented features:** Openwork reinforced-concrete construction, canted front corners, bowed rear elevation, flat roof with oversailing eaves, pebbledash platband and integral concrete benches.
- **Significance:** One of a series of precast-concrete shelters built by Dublin Corporation along the promenade; NIAH identifies it as a strong example of Modernist seafront architecture.
- **Fieldwork:** Public promenade viewpoint. Record current condition and any alterations; avoid obstructing pedestrians and cyclists.

### DUB-BLD-0052 — Clontarf handball alley
- **NIAH:** [50030337](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030337/strandville-road-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin)
- **Irish Grid:** 318530, 236292
- **WGS 84:** 53.3637723, -6.2202287
- **Date:** NIAH range 1930–1940; built circa 1935
- **Documented features:** Freestanding three-sided alley with sloping side walls, yellow Flemish-bond brickwork, yellow-brick pilasters, smooth-rendered internal walls and remains of steel railings.
- **Significance:** NIAH says it probably served as an amenity for officers of the adjacent Garda station and is a well-preserved example of a standardised early twentieth-century handball alley.
- **Fieldwork:** Associated with Garda station grounds. Do not enter restricted property or photograph sensitive security details; use a lawful public viewpoint only.

### DUB-BLD-0053 — Seaview Terrace, 77–81 Clontarf Road
- **NIAH:** [50030051](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030051/seaview-terrace-77-81-clontarf-road-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Irish Grid:** 318953, 236051
- **WGS 84:** 53.3615131, -6.2139685
- **Date:** NIAH range 1840–1850; built circa 1845
- **Documented features:** Five single-storey houses over raised basements; M-profile roofs, brown-brick façades, granite stringcourses, raised entrance platforms, cobweb fanlights, console brackets, panelled doors, cast-iron balustrades, gates and boundary walls.
- **Significance:** NIAH describes the terrace as retaining its early suburban form and important entrance, door, window and boundary fabric.
- **Fieldwork:** Private residences. Photograph only from public footpaths and viewpoints; respect residents' privacy.

## Coordinate method and limits

The original NIAH Irish Grid references are preserved. WGS 84 points were transformed from TM65 / Irish Grid (EPSG:29902) using the project's established transformation operation 1641. The transformation's published accuracy does not establish the positional accuracy of the source NIAH points. All three records remain SOURCE_DERIVED and NOT_FIELD_VERIFIED.

The handball alley's present survival and public visibility require field confirmation. The Seaview Terrace coordinate represents the registered terrace, not the exact position of each doorway or small detail. No photographs or line art were fabricated or added.
