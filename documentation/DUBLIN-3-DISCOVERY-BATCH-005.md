# Dublin 3 Discovery Batch 005 — Clontarf Road and seafront heritage

**Research date:** 2026-10-26  
**Scope:** Clontarf Road, Conquer Hill Road, Clontarf Promenade and the historic coastal-resort corridor, Dublin 3.  
**Method:** Individual NIAH registrations checked against the existing catalogue before addition. Irish Grid coordinates are preserved; WGS 84 points use the project's established TM65 transformation operation 1641 and remain source-derived pending founder field verification.

## Eight catalogue additions

### DUB-BLD-0054 — Dublin United Tramways cast-iron transformer — Conquer Hill Road
- **NIAH:** [50030115](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030115/clontarf-road-conquer-hill-road-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1900
- **Irish Grid:** 320552, 235776
- **WGS 84:** 53.3586816, -6.1900681
- **Description:** Freestanding finial-topped cast-iron electrical transformer of circular plan, mounted on a repointed cut-granite plinth at a road-fronted corner site. NIAH records crisp castings incorporating the Dublin City arms and the Latin motto “OBEDIENTIA CIVIUM URBIS FELICITAS”.
- **Significance:** Installed by the Dublin United Tramways Company when the horse-drawn tram service between Dollymount and Nelson's Pillar was converted to electric traction on 19 March 1899. The surviving transformer is an unusually legible piece of early urban electrical and tramway infrastructure, with civic heraldry cast into its ironwork.
- **Fieldwork:** Roadside public-realm object at a junction. Photograph from the public footpath without entering the carriageway or obstructing pedestrians. Take care around traffic and do not touch the electrical structure.

### DUB-BLD-0055 — Clontarf Promenade reinforced-concrete bandstand
- **NIAH:** [50030149](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030149/clontarf-road-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1955
- **Irish Grid:** 319461, 235868
- **WGS 84:** 53.3597552, -6.2064111
- **Description:** Round-plan reinforced-concrete bandstand attached to the promenade retaining wall. NIAH describes a flat roof supported by seven round-plan piers, including one central pier, a plinth wall with an integral interior bench, and a limestone footing with areas of concrete render.
- **Significance:** Built as an entertainment facility for users of the adjoining Clontarf Baths, while also providing shelter and views when not used as a bandstand. NIAH identifies it as a strong example of Modernist seafront architecture whose geometric form projects into the seafront and creates a distinctive silhouette.
- **Fieldwork:** Promenade structure. Photograph from public promenade viewpoints and avoid obstructing the path or entering unsafe areas around the retaining wall.

### DUB-BLD-0056 — Victoria Terrace, 163–173 Clontarf Road
- **NIAH:** [50030144](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030144/163-173-clontarf-road-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1865
- **Irish Grid:** 319880, 235909
- **WGS 84:** 53.3600287, -6.2001055
- **Description:** Terrace of eleven houses built circa 1865, composed of projecting two-bay end houses over raised basements, intervening two-bay houses and a three-bay central house. NIAH records canted bay windows, M-profile slate roofs, red-brick Flemish-bond façades, granite plinths and sills, round-headed entrances, fanlights, timber-panelled doors, cast-iron handrails and wrought-iron gates.
- **Significance:** Developed by George Tickell to designs by architect J.J. Lyons. NIAH describes the terrace as a carefully composed seafront streetscape, with taller projecting end houses and a central breakfront creating a focal point. Historic maps show an earlier post office and police station occupied the site.
- **Fieldwork:** Private residential properties. Photograph only from lawful public footpaths and viewpoints; respect residents' privacy and do not enter gardens or shared private areas.

### DUB-BLD-0057 — Clontarf Promenade — group of eight Modernist concrete shelters
- **NIAH:** [50030146](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030146/clontarf-promenade-clontarf-road-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1955–1958
- **Irish Grid:** 318854, 236003
- **WGS 84:** 53.3611042, -6.2154728
- **Description:** Group of eight freestanding three-sided reinforced-concrete shelters with curved roofs and rear walls, built circa 1955–1958. NIAH records concrete roofs and walls with integral benches, round openings to the east and west elevations, and two diamond-shaped or round openings to the rear. The group is set back from the seafront on the north side of the promenade footpath.
- **Significance:** A coherent group of Modernist public-amenity structures whose repeated forms provide shelter and views along the promenade. NIAH notes the geometric openings and the material qualities of poured concrete, and relates the promenade's construction to flood protection and public amenity from the 1930s onward.
- **Fieldwork:** Public promenade setting. Photograph from public paths and safe viewpoints. Keep clear of cyclists and pedestrians; do not climb on structures or retaining walls.

### DUB-BLD-0058 — St Anthony's Hall — former Clontarf Town Hall
- **NIAH:** [50030192](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030192/st-anthonys-hall-61-clontarf-road-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** 1896; refaced and converted in 1926
- **Irish Grid:** 318723, 236179
- **WGS 84:** 53.3627142, -6.2173735
- **Description:** Detached gable-fronted building erected in 1896 as Clontarf Town Hall, refaced and converted to a Catholic church in 1926, and later used as a parish hall. NIAH records a pitched slate roof, carved granite verge stones, kneelers and finial, rusticated granite front walling, red-brick side elevations, buttresses and cut-stone detailing.
- **Significance:** Designed by township surveyor William G. Perrott and built by Robert Farquharson on land granted by Colonel Edward Vernon. Its changing uses include town hall, church, parish hall and, for a period in the 1920s, cinema. NIAH notes a reported association with meetings of Easter Rising rebels, but that claim should remain attributed to the register rather than presented as independently established fact.
- **Fieldwork:** Parish hall / managed property. Photograph the exterior from the public footpath. Obtain permission before entering grounds or interiors; respect worship, parish activities and private access.

### DUB-BLD-0059 — Clontarf Garda Station — former Dublin Metropolitan Police Station
- **NIAH:** [50030274](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030274/clontarf-garda-station-clontarf-road-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** 1909
- **Irish Grid:** 318533, 236256
- **WGS 84:** 53.3634483, -6.2201972
- **Description:** Detached nine-bay two-storey police station dated 1909, with a pedimented breakfront to the south elevation and a U-plan flat-roofed single-storey block to the rear. NIAH records a hipped natural-slate roof, carved eaves brackets, red-brick and roughcast-rendered elevations, granite detailing, timber sash windows, a carved granite doorcase and a cartouche displaying the construction date.
- **Significance:** Built by J.W. Scott to designs by Robert Cochrane for the Dublin Metropolitan Police after Clontarf was absorbed into Dublin City in 1900. The station continued in use as a Garda station after Independence. NIAH describes it as an important part of Clontarf's social history and built heritage.
- **Fieldwork:** Active Garda station and controlled premises. Do not enter the station, yard or restricted grounds. Photograph only from lawful public footpaths and avoid sensitive security details, staff, visitors, access controls or operational activity.

### DUB-BLD-0060 — Clontarf Baths — historic seafront swimming pool
- **NIAH:** [50030148](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030148/clontarf-baths-clontarf-road-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** 1881
- **Irish Grid:** 319535, 235844
- **WGS 84:** 53.359523, -6.2053093
- **Description:** Rectangular-plan outdoor swimming pool with chamfered south corners, built in 1881, with single-storey structures along the north shore. NIAH records concrete retaining walls to the seafront and metal railings and gates enclosing the north boundary. The overall pool form and much of the outer wall remain legible in the register's description.
- **Significance:** Established in 1881 as part of Clontarf's late nineteenth-century development as a suburban coastal resort, backed by J.E.V. Vernon and Lord Ardilaun. The baths included separate swimming areas for male and female bathers, a bridge from the seafront, and a boiler and pump operated by a steam engine. NIAH records that the baths have been disused since 1996, while historic fabric remains.
- **Fieldwork:** Historic pool and promenade edge. Do not enter the pool basin, climb retaining walls or cross barriers. Photograph from public promenade viewpoints only; check current safety and access conditions.

### DUB-BLD-0061 — Hillview Terrace — 204 Clontarf Road
- **NIAH:** [50030124](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030124/hillview-terrace-204-clontarf-road-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** 1846
- **Irish Grid:** 320219, 235785
- **WGS 84:** 53.3609455, -6.1995261
- **Description:** Terraced two-bay two-storey house over a basement, built in 1846, with a two-storey rear return. NIAH records an M-profile roof, rendered parapet and cornice, smooth-rendered walls with stringcourses, moulded window surrounds, a round-headed entrance with pilasters and cornice, panelled timber door, granite-capped plinth walls and cast-iron railings and pedestrian gate. Remnants of an early coach house survive to the rear lane according to the register.
- **Significance:** One of a terrace of five houses built by Joseph Mannin on land leased from J.E.V. Vernon. NIAH identifies the raised entrance, fanlight, cast-iron railings and early suburban seafront setting as contributing to its architectural interest. The locality was historically associated with drying sheds used by the fishing industry before Clontarf Road's designation after 1912.
- **Fieldwork:** Private residence. Photograph only from public footpaths and viewpoints; do not enter the garden, rear lane or private property. Respect residents' privacy.
