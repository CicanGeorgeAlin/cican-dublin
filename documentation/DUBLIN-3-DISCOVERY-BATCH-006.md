# Dublin 3 Discovery Batch 006 — Castle Avenue heritage and village connections

**Research date:** 2026-10-26  
**Scope:** Castle Avenue and adjoining Clontarf heritage corridor, Dublin 3.  
**Method:** Eight NIAH registrations were checked against the existing catalogue. Existing entries for Clontarf Castle, its gate lodge and Central Hall were excluded to avoid duplication. Original Irish Grid references are preserved; WGS 84 points are source-derived using the project's established TM65 transformation and await founder field verification.

## Eight additions

### DUB-BLD-0062 — Edward VII cast-iron postbox — Castle Avenue
- **NIAH:** [50030162](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030162/castle-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1905
- **Irish Grid:** 319489, 236260
- **WGS 84:** 53.3632693, -6.2058428
- **Description:** Wall-mounted cast-iron postbox dating to circa 1905, set in a pier in front of the Clontarf telephone exchange. It carries the royal monogram of Edward VII and raised 'Post Office' lettering; the foundry mark at its base is illegible.
- **Significance:** A surviving piece of early twentieth-century communications infrastructure that remains in active postal use according to the NIAH record. Its royal insignia and cast lettering make an ordinary street utility an unusually legible historic object.
- **Fieldwork:** Public street furniture beside a telephone exchange. Photograph from the public footpath without obstructing the postbox or postal users. Avoid handling or interfering with postal operations.

### DUB-BLD-0063 — Rathmore — former pair of houses, 8–9 Castle Avenue
- **NIAH:** [50030050](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030050/rathmore-8-9-castle-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1915
- **Irish Grid:** 319264, 236076
- **WGS 84:** 53.3616676, -6.2092902
- **Description:** Former semi-detached pair of three-bay, two-storey houses built circa 1915, now used as one house. NIAH records a gabled full-height box-bay window, shared recessed entrance, slate roof, red-brick Flemish-bond elevations, half-timbered gables, timber sash windows, granite steps and wrought-iron gates.
- **Significance:** The building retains the form and character of two early twentieth-century houses despite their later amalgamation. Its brickwork, decorative gables, bay windows and surviving front boundary contribute to the early suburban character of Castle Avenue, one of Clontarf's earliest laid-out streets.
- **Fieldwork:** Private residence. Photograph only from the public footpath; do not enter the garden or private property. Respect residents' privacy.

### DUB-BLD-0064 — Balmoral Lodge — 17 Castle Avenue
- **NIAH:** [50030157](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030157/balmoral-lodge-17-castle-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1825
- **Irish Grid:** 319305, 236186
- **WGS 84:** 53.3626462, -6.2086332
- **Description:** Formerly detached three-bay, two-storey house built circa 1825, with a later two-storey house attached to the south. NIAH records a pitched slate roof, brown-brick stepped chimneystacks, lined-and-ruled rendered walls with quoins, timber sliding-sash windows, a round-headed entrance with ornate petal fanlight and panelled door, and cast-iron railings and gates.
- **Significance:** One of the earlier surviving houses on Castle Avenue, an early route linking Clontarf Castle with the seafront. The building retains substantial historic fabric and illustrates the avenue's early nineteenth-century suburban development.
- **Fieldwork:** Private residence. Photograph only from the public footpath; do not enter the garden or property. Respect residents' privacy.

### DUB-BLD-0065 — Clontarf Telephone Exchange
- **NIAH:** [50030161](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030161/clontarf-telephone-exchange-castle-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin)
- **Date:** 1926; extended 1954
- **Irish Grid:** 319479, 236242
- **WGS 84:** 53.3631099, -6.2059998
- **Description:** Freestanding three-bay, two-storey telephone exchange built in 1926 and extended to the rear in 1954. NIAH records flat roofs, rendered parapets with frieze and cornice, red-brick corner pavilions, concrete-block and roughcast-rendered elevations, a cast-iron harp plaque, recessed windows and a rubble-stone front boundary.
- **Significance:** A distinctive piece of communications infrastructure associated with the transition to automatic telephone working in 1937. The building was designed by William Cooke and built by J. & R. Thompson of Fairview; its corner pavilions and contrasting brick, concrete and render make it a notable interwar streetscape feature.
- **Fieldwork:** Operational or managed utility property. Photograph the exterior only from the public footpath. Do not enter the site or photograph access controls, staff, or sensitive operational details.

### DUB-BLD-0066 — Ivy Lodge — 18 Castle Avenue
- **NIAH:** [50030158](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030158/ivy-lodge-18-castle-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1820
- **Irish Grid:** 319314, 236210
- **WGS 84:** 53.3628597, -6.208489
- **Description:** End-of-terrace three-bay, two-storey house built circa 1820, with a two-storey rear return and single-storey rear extension. NIAH records a pitched slate roof, rendered chimneystacks, lined-and-ruled rendered walls with quoins, timber sliding-sash windows, a round-headed entrance with petal fanlight and panelled door, granite entrance platform and cast-iron garden railings.
- **Significance:** A relatively intact early house on one of the first streets laid out in Clontarf, linking Clontarf Castle to the seafront. Its fanlight, moulded details and iron railings contribute to the historic suburban character of Castle Avenue.
- **Fieldwork:** Private residence. Photograph only from the public footpath; do not enter the garden or property. Respect residents' privacy.

### DUB-BLD-0067 — Clontarf Hospital — former nurses' home
- **NIAH:** [50030332](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030332/clontarf-hospital-castle-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin)
- **Date:** 1949–1952; dated 1951
- **Irish Grid:** 319712, 236729
- **WGS 84:** 53.3674307, -6.2023175
- **Description:** Attached L-plan nurses' home built 1949–1952 and dated 1951. NIAH records a four-bay, three-storey-over-basement front block, a two-storey rear block, a curved-corner single-storey element, a linking corridor to the adjoining hospital building, flat roofs, smooth-rendered walls, a date stone, porthole windows and recessed granite panels.
- **Significance:** An example of the International Style applied to Irish hospital architecture. Its horizontal emphasis, flat roofs, concrete canopies, curved elements and porthole windows distinguish it from the ornate nineteenth-century buildings nearby and document post-war healthcare architecture.
- **Fieldwork:** Hospital grounds and managed healthcare property. Photograph only from a lawful public viewpoint or with permission. Do not enter grounds, interfere with care, photograph patients or staff, or capture sensitive operational activity.

### DUB-BLD-0068 — Church of St John the Baptist — historic graveyard and ruined church
- **NIAH:** [50030312](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030312/church-of-st-john-the-baptist-castle-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** Graveyard circa 1300; ruined church circa 1610; entrance circa 1840
- **Irish Grid:** 319506, 236459
- **WGS 84:** 53.3650526, -6.2055125
- **Description:** Irregular-plan graveyard established circa 1300, containing a ruined church dating to circa 1610 and inscribed gravestones and markers from the eighteenth century to the present. NIAH records rubble-limestone boundary walls, curved coping stones, paths and a gable-fronted limestone pedestrian entrance of circa 1840 with a Tudor arch, slate roof, carved stone details and decorative cast-iron double gates.
- **Significance:** The burial ground predates the early seventeenth-century church and remains in use by families with established plots. NIAH notes Catholic burials from before Emancipation within this Church of Ireland graveyard, and identifies the grave markers as objects of artistic interest. It forms part of a wider historic group with Clontarf Castle and the nineteenth-century church.
- **Fieldwork:** Active historic graveyard and sensitive burial place. Enter only if public access is permitted and behave respectfully. Do not touch, lean on, clean, move or make rubbings of gravestones; avoid photographing mourners or private family activity. Confirm access arrangements before fieldwork.

### DUB-BLD-0069 — Brian Boróimhe's Well — 1850 commemorative drinking fountain
- **NIAH:** [50030154](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030154/brian-boroimhes-well-castle-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** 1850
- **Irish Grid:** 319311, 236116
- **WGS 84:** 53.3620162, -6.2085695
- **Description:** Former well and drinking-fountain feature with a decorative metal plaque dated 1850, set within a smooth-rendered wall. The plaque carries a shamrock motif and raised inscription: 'Erected over Brian Boróimhe's Well by subscription AD 1850'. NIAH records that it was placed over the remains of an earlier drinking fountain.
- **Significance:** The memorial feature records a nineteenth-century public subscription and local association with Brian Boru and the Battle of Clontarf. NIAH states that the well is at or close to an ancient well; the precise archaeological relationship should not be asserted beyond that wording without further archaeological evidence.
- **Fieldwork:** Street-side heritage feature. Photograph from the public footpath without touching the plaque or obstructing pedestrians. Do not scrape, clean or apply substances to the metal or wall.


## Important research boundaries

- The postbox and telephone exchange are separately registered objects in the same streetscape; keep both records, but field photography should capture their relationship without duplication.
- The graveyard is a sensitive active burial place. No touching, cleaning, rubbing or moving gravestones.
- The Brian Boróimhe's Well inscription is transcribed from the NIAH description. Its location is described by NIAH as at or close to an ancient well; no stronger archaeological claim is made.
- Private residences are photographed only from lawful public viewpoints.
- The nurses' home is not assumed to retain its historic use; the catalogue records the NIAH description and access cautions.
- All eight locations await founder photography and field coordinate verification. No photos or line art have been fabricated.
