# Dublin 3 Discovery Batch 007 — Seafield Road and Vernon Avenue

**Research date:** 2026-10-26  
**Scope:** Seafield Road West, Vernon Avenue and the historic Clontarf Sheds corridor.  
**Method:** Six NIAH registrations checked against the live catalogue before insertion. No duplicate registration numbers were found. Irish Grid references are preserved; WGS 84 coordinates are transformed using the project's established EPSG:29902 to WGS 84 operation 1641 and remain source-derived pending founder field verification.

## Six additions

### DUB-BLD-0070 — Seafield Road West — Jessop Davis cast-iron postbox
- **NIAH:** [50030322](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030322/seafield-road-west-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1940
- **Irish Grid:** 319748, 236383
- **WGS 84:** 53.3643153, -6.2019079
- **Description:** Wall-mounted cast-iron postbox erected circa 1940, with a hooded letter slot, Department of Posts and Telegraphs insignia and maker's mark 'Jessop Davis Enniscorthy'. It is set in a boundary wall beside the street.
- **Significance:** A locally manufactured example of post-Independence Irish street furniture. NIAH notes that the 1907 Ordnance Survey map already indicated a letter box at this location, suggesting the surviving box replaced an earlier one. Jessop Davis was also known as St John's Foundry until it closed in 1964.
- **Fieldwork/access:** Street-side object. Photograph from the public footpath without obstructing pedestrians or postal users. Do not touch, open or interfere with the postbox.

### DUB-BLD-0071 — St John the Baptist Church — First and Second World War Celtic cross memorial
- **NIAH:** [50030324](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030324/church-of-st-john-the-baptist-16-seafield-road-west-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin)
- **Date:** 1920; rededicated after 1945
- **Irish Grid:** 319715, 236380
- **WGS 84:** 53.3642959, -6.2024044
- **Description:** Carved limestone Celtic cross memorial dated 1920, later rededicated and inscribed following the Second World War. The cross has interlace carving to its head, panels to the shaft, rope-work at the corners, a sloped base and cut-limestone plinth. Memorial inscriptions appear on the base and south face of the shaft.
- **Significance:** The memorial commemorates thirty-three men of the parish who died in the First World War and was subsequently inscribed in memory of nine men and one woman lost in the Second World War, as recorded by NIAH. It is a significant example of a church-based local war memorial and Celtic Revival stone carving.
- **Fieldwork/access:** Located in church grounds. Photograph only where access is permitted; be respectful of worship, funerals and visitors. Do not touch, clean, chalk, rub or apply materials to the memorial.

### DUB-BLD-0072 — 54 Vernon Avenue — 1913 detached villa
- **NIAH:** [50030345](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030345/54-vernon-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** 1913
- **Irish Grid:** 320137, 236127
- **WGS 84:** 53.3619282, -6.1961647
- **Description:** Detached three-bay single-storey house dated 1913, with box-bay windows, pier buttresses and a recessed entrance. NIAH records a flat roof with rendered round chimneystacks and crenellated parapet, smooth-rendered walls, a date plaque, pointed-arch doorway and crenellated front boundary piers.
- **Significance:** One of four decorative villas built in succession by John Ryan, builder and plasterer, and his architect son Fergus Ryan on land leased from Col. Edward Vernon. NIAH identifies Nos. 50 and 54 as the most intact of the four. Vernon Avenue developed from a route to the Clontarf Sheds fishing village, where locally caught herring was cured.
- **Fieldwork/access:** Private residence. Photograph only from the public footpath; do not enter the garden or property. Respect residents' privacy.

### DUB-BLD-0073 — Vernon Avenue — Carron cast-iron pillar postbox
- **NIAH:** [50030098](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030098/vernon-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1940
- **Irish Grid:** 320181, 236430
- **WGS 84:** 53.3646393, -6.1953891
- **Description:** Freestanding cast-iron pillar postbox erected circa 1940. It has a shallow domed cap with fluted rim, moulded neck and plinth, raised 'Post Office' lettering on the front door and the maker's insignia 'Carron Company Stirlingshire' on the rear of the plinth.
- **Significance:** A surviving example of early twentieth-century mass-produced cast-iron street furniture. NIAH notes that Carron Ltd of Stirlingshire cast many postboxes in the area and that an earlier Victorian or Edwardian letter box was likely present near the Seafield Road corner on the 1907 Ordnance Survey map.
- **Fieldwork/access:** Public street furniture. Photograph from the public footpath, keeping the footpath clear. Do not touch, open or interfere with the postbox.

### DUB-BLD-0074 — Woodside Villa — 70–72 Vernon Avenue
- **NIAH:** [50030099](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030099/70-72-vernon-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1870
- **Irish Grid:** 320165, 236257
- **WGS 84:** 53.3630893, -6.195695
- **Description:** Pair of semi-detached houses, three bays and two bays respectively, built circa 1870 over basements. NIAH records flat-roofed canted bays, shared pitched slate roof, rendered parapet with moulded cornice, varied rendered wall finishes, decorative doorcases, granite entrance platforms and wrought-iron railings with cast-iron palmettes.
- **Significance:** The pair illustrates the nineteenth-century suburban development of Vernon Avenue, historically part of the route to the Clontarf Sheds fishing village. No. 70 was known as Woodside Villa and was recorded as the residence of William Barton Esq. in 1908. The paired bays and ironwork retain streetscape and craft interest.
- **Fieldwork/access:** Private residences. Photograph only from the public footpath; do not enter gardens or private property. Respect residents' privacy.

### DUB-BLD-0075 — Verville — early Georgian house and former private asylum
- **NIAH:** [50030334](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030334/verville-vernon-avenue-clontarf-sibylhill-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** Circa 1730; major additions circa 1800 and circa 1870
- **Irish Grid:** 319916, 236972
- **WGS 84:** 53.3695668, -6.1991624
- **Description:** Detached two-storey house over basement, centred on a pedimented five-bay house of circa 1730 with dormer attic and later additions, including a bowed bay and wing circa 1800 and further return blocks circa 1870. NIAH records red-brick and rendered elevations, timber sash windows, a cast-iron balcony, pedimented timber doorcase, granite steps and wrought-iron railings. Many openings are now boarded up according to the register description.
- **Significance:** One of the oldest houses in Dublin's northern suburbs, appearing on John Rocque's 1760 map of County Dublin. NIAH records its later use from 1859 as a private asylum for women under Dr William Lynch and its continued use as an asylum into the twentieth century. The building is important for both architectural and social history.
- **Fieldwork/access:** Private or managed property; current access and use must be confirmed before fieldwork. Photograph only from a lawful public viewpoint or with permission. Do not enter grounds, approach boarded openings or interfere with the site.


## Historical observations

- Two different postbox forms are recorded on the corridor: a Jessop Davis wall box on Seafield Road West and a Carron pillar box on Vernon Avenue. The NIAH appraisal connects the local postal landscape with earlier map evidence.
- The 1920 Celtic cross at St John the Baptist Church commemorates local First World War losses and was rededicated to include Second World War losses.
- NIAH describes Vernon Avenue as part of the historic route to the Clontarf Sheds, a fishing village associated with curing locally caught herring.
- Verville is a major early house with a documented later institutional history as a private asylum. Its current access and use must be independently checked before fieldwork.
