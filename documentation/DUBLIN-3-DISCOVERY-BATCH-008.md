**Research date:** 2026-10-26  
**Scope:** Seafield Road West and the northern edge of the former Clontarf Sheds fishing-village landscape.

## Two additions

### DUB-BLD-0076 — Church of St John the Baptist — Seafield Road West
- **NIAH:** [50030323](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030323/church-of-st-john-the-baptist-seafield-road-west-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** 1864–1866; additions 1897–1899
- **Irish Grid:** 319712, 236358
- **WGS 84:** 53.3640989629783, -6.202457805982534
- **Description:** Cruciform-plan Church of Ireland church built 1864–1866, with porch and vestry added 1897–1899. The building has a gable-fronted nave and transepts, lower chancel, three-stage belfry with limestone spire, slate roofs, rubble-granite walls and dressed-limestone detailing. Its windows include trefoil-headed lancets and a large pointed-arch east window with stained glass. The site also retains historic gates and boundary walls.
- **Significance:** Designed by Welland & Gillespie and built by Waldron Brothers, the church was consecrated on 14 May 1866. The Vernon family provided the site and commissioned a twelve-light chancel window depicting the apostles from Michael O'Connor of London. The church remains a prominent architectural and social landmark in Clontarf.
- **Fieldwork/access:** Active place of worship. Photograph only from public viewpoints or with permission. Do not enter during services, funerals or private events without permission; avoid photographing worshippers and respect church staff and visitors.

### DUB-BLD-0077 — Hillview Terrace / Rutland Terrace — 205–206 Clontarf Road
- **NIAH:** [50030123](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030123/205-206-clontarf-road-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin)
- **Date:** 1846 and circa 1860
- **Irish Grid:** 320227, 235778
- **WGS 84:** 53.35877352344337, -6.194946073034553
- **Description:** Attached pair of two-bay, two-storey houses over basements, built in 1846 and circa 1860. The pair has full-height bow windows, recessed entrance bays, hipped roofs concealed behind parapets, moulded cornices, timber sliding-sash windows, round-headed doorways, decorative fanlights, granite entrance steps and cast-iron railings. Remnants of early coach houses survive to the rear lane.
- **Significance:** No. 205 originally formed the end of a symmetrical five-house Hillview Terrace built by Joseph Mannin on land leased from J.E.V. Vernon in 1846. NIAH identifies No. 206 as a later neighbouring house, likely part of Rutland Terrace, despite its similar appearance. These buildings are among the earlier surviving seafront developments and occupy an area historically known as the Clontarf Sheds, associated with fish-drying sheds.
- **Fieldwork/access:** Private residences. Photograph only from the public footpath; do not enter gardens or rear lanes without permission. Respect residents' privacy.


## Historic landscape context

NIAH appraisals identify the Vernon Avenue route to the Clontarf Sheds and describe the seafront area as historically associated with fish-drying sheds. The 1837 Topographical Dictionary of Ireland independently describes wooden sheds along the water's edge used for curing fish and an oyster bank offshore. A modern historical account in History Ireland describes Clontarf as a small fishing base in the late eighteenth century, with fishermen's cottages at the Sheds. These contextual sources are leads for landscape research, not substitutes for archaeological evidence or proof of the exact footprint of individual sheds.
