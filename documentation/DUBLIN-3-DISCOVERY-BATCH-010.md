# Dublin 3 Discovery Batch 010 — three additional Victorian postboxes

**Research date:** 26 October 2026  
**Scope:** Clontarf Road, Howth Road and Haddon Road, Clontarf, Dublin 3.  
**Method:** Search of indexed National Inventory of Architectural Heritage (NIAH) records, duplicate check against the 127-record catalogue immediately before this batch, and transformation of published Irish Grid references using the project's established EPSG:29902 to WGS 84 operation 1641.

## Three new street-detail records

### DUB-STR-0043 — Clontarf Road Victorian cast-iron pillar postbox
- **NIAH:** [50030114](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030114/clontarf-road-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** circa 1890
- **Irish Grid:** 320568, 235779
- **WGS 84:** 53.3587049403907, -6.189826723248944
- **Indexed register description:** Freestanding cast-iron pillar postbox with shallow domed cap, moulded necking and dentillated frieze, moulded plinth, raised VR royal cipher and raised “Post Office” lettering. The indexed description records an A. Handyside & Co. Ltd, Derby & London maker's stamp on the front of the plinth.
- **Significance:** A surviving Victorian postal object with an identifiable foundry mark and royal cipher.
- **Fieldwork:** Photograph the complete box, cipher, lettering, cap and foundry mark from the public footpath. Do not touch or open it.

### DUB-STR-0044 — Howth Road Victorian wall-mounted cast-iron postbox
- **NIAH:** [50030284](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030284/howth-road-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin)
- **Date:** circa 1890
- **Irish Grid:** 318626, 236625
- **WGS 84:** 53.36674133576238, -6.218662638657769
- **Indexed register description:** Wall-mounted cast-iron postbox set into an uncoursed limestone wall south of Howth Road. The indexed description records Queen Victoria's raised royal cipher and crown motif, cast “Post Office” lettering on the letter flap and an illegible foundry mark.
- **Significance:** A distinct wall-box form documenting suburban postal infrastructure and the visual language of Victorian public communications.
- **Fieldwork:** Photograph the cipher, flap lettering, base and host masonry from the public footpath. Do not touch the box or enter adjoining property.

### DUB-STR-0045 — Haddon Road W.T. Allen Victorian wall-mounted postbox
- **NIAH:** [50030046](https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030046/haddon-road-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin)
- **Date:** circa 1895
- **Irish Grid:** 319115, 236001
- **WGS 84:** 53.36102760436781, -6.211555288778266
- **Indexed register description:** Wall-mounted cast-iron postbox set into a rubble-granite wall, with VR royal cipher, raised “Post Office” lettering on the hood and a W.T. Allen maker's mark on the frame.
- **Significance:** The maker's mark provides a tangible link to late-Victorian postal infrastructure and cast-iron manufacture.
- **Fieldwork:** Photograph the full box and host wall, cipher, lettering, maker's mark and wider streetscape from the public footpath.

## Evidence and coordinate limitations

The indexed official NIAH records provide the descriptions, dates and Irish Grid references. Direct opening of the individual NIAH pages returned HTTP 403 during this research pass. The three additions are therefore marked **PARTIALLY_VERIFIED**, not fully verified. Their source records explicitly preserve this limitation.

WGS 84 coordinates are transformations of published NIAH Irish Grid references, not founder-recorded GPS fixes. Exact on-site condition, current presence, access and maker marks still require field confirmation. No founder photographs, line art or publication images have been created.
