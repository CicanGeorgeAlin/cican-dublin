## Evidence and findings

### Dublin 3 — historic postal furniture

- **Ballybough Road / O'Sullivan Avenue (NIAH 50120227):** A freestanding cast-iron pillar box dated c.1905, with Edward VII's raised insignia and crown, raised 'Post Office' lettering and a McDowall Steven & Co. maker's mark. The official survey places it at the junction.
- **Philipsburgh Avenue / Morroch Terrace (NIAH 50120017):** A freestanding cast-iron pillar box dated c.1935, with P&T (Poist agus Teileagrafa) insignia, at the west side of Philipsburgh Avenue at the junction with Morroch Terrace.

The complete catalogue was checked before registration. Other promising Clontarf and North Strand post boxes discovered in the same search were already present and were deliberately not duplicated: NIAH 50120259, 50030098, 50030333, 50030322, 50030162, 50030114, 50030284, 50030046, 50030342 and 50030286.

### Dublin 1 — James Joyce Centre micro-details

NIAH 50010980 records the 1784 end-of-terrace townhouse now used as the James Joyce Centre. Its survey description specifically records cast-iron air vents below the windows, a Victorian bell push beside the entrance, and a cast-iron corner lamp standard on a moulded granite plinth wall. These were separated into individual visual capture records rather than left buried in the building description.

The repeated air vents are provisionally grouped as one record. Fieldwork may later separate them if distinct design or condition differences warrant separate IDs.

## Coordinate limitations

The published Irish Grid references were transformed from EPSG:29902 to WGS 84 using EPSG transformation operation 1641:

- Ballybough Road / O'Sullivan Avenue: 316914, 235828 → 53.35996471, -6.24466240
- Philipsburgh Avenue / Morroch Terrace: 317281, 237037 → 53.37074120, -6.23870486
- James Joyce Centre: 315908, 235158 → 53.35416881, -6.26001029

The two post-box points are source-derived NIAH feature locators, not founder GPS. The James Joyce Centre point identifies the host building, not the exact position of each architectural detail. Current survival, condition and precise positions require field verification.
