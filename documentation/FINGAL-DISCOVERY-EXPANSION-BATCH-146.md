## Significant overlooked discoveries

- **Clay Cottage's 1754 boulder:** a dated inscription embedded in the boundary wall of a c.1750 thatched cottage.
- **Mill Bridge:** benchmark-inscribed parapets and a memorial plaque to Peter Wilson, who died in the 1916 Rising.
- **Lusk water pump:** a c.1925 Tonge and Taggart cow-tail pump with Balrothery Rural District Council and “Déanta I nÉirinn” marks.
- **Swords water pump:** a c.1890 cast-iron pump with Tonge and Taggart plaque and a separate stone trough; NIAH records missing parts.
- **Lissenhall crossings:** two distinct bridge registrations, one built in 1895 and one enlarged in 1903, plus the separate five-arch Lissen Hall bridge dated c.1760.
- **Newbridge House:** two separately registered historic entrance-pier groups and a c.1800 single-arch bridge.

## Coordinate and fieldwork controls

Usable NIAH Irish Grid references were transformed from EPSG:29902 to WGS 84 using the project's established operation 1641. The transformed points are source-derived site locators, not founder GPS. The three micro-detail records deliberately reuse their host building/bridge locator and do not claim exact feature-level coordinates.

NIAH registration 11329015 (The Thatch Cottage, Ballisk) publishes -1,-1 rather than a usable coordinate. The object is catalogued but intentionally has no latitude, longitude or map pin until a defensible location is established.

All new objects await original founder photographs. Photograph only from lawful public viewpoints or permitted visitor routes. Do not enter private yards, climb bridges or monuments, stand in carriageways, enter watercourses, touch or clean historic fabric, or photograph interiors without permission. Check current visitor access for Saint MacCullin's Church and the Newbridge House demesne.
