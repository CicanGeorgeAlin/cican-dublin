**Date:** 17 November 2026

**Method:** Individual National Inventory of Architectural Heritage (NIAH) record inspection; candidate extraction focused on small, photographable details and village-scale heritage; registration numbers and source-derived Irish Grid points retained exactly as published.

No source, visual record, map pin, photograph or founder field verification was added.

## Priority detail targets

1. **Clay Cottage inscribed boulder:** establish whether the inscribed stone remains visible in the boundary wall, capture the exact inscription and record a founder GPS fix only after on-site matching. Do not touch, clean or disturb the stone.
2. **Mill Bridge benchmark and Peter Wilson plaque:** photograph the parapet benchmark(s), limestone coping and commemorative plaque as distinct details only if their exact positions and independent significance are established in the field. The NIAH point locates the bridge, not each detail.
3. **Rathbeale Road post box:** capture the full object, maker/insignia details if present, and its relationship to the road without entering the carriageway.
4. **Hamilton Monument:** record each inscribed tablet and the surrounding iron railings from a lawful public viewpoint; do not infer the full wording from the summary description.

## Evidence and limitations

- The NIAH record for Clay Cottage identifies the inscribed boulder and gives the estimated house date as c.1750. The published grid point is a site locator, not a measured coordinate for the boulder.
- The Mill Bridge NIAH record describes the bridge as c.1875 and transcribes the Peter Wilson commemorative plaque. Its published grid point locates the bridge.
- The two Rathbeale Road records are separate registrations: the post box (11335013) and the thatched house (11335014). Their close proximity does not make them one object.
- Saint MacCullin's Church is described by NIAH as built in 1847, closed in 1959 and now in use as a heritage/interpretative centre. Current visitor access and any interior photography permissions must be checked before fieldwork.
- The Hamilton Monument record describes its form, memorial subject and railings; the NIAH record does not by itself establish current condition or public access arrangements.
- All coordinates above are reproduced as Irish Grid values from NIAH. No WGS 84 conversion is claimed in this report. No coordinate is founder GPS.
