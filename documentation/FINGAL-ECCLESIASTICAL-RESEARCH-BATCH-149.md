## 1. St Doulagh's ecclesiastical complex — multiple separately registered features

A 2024 Conservation Management Plan prepared by Archaeological Management Solutions for the Select Vestry and Friends of St Doulagh's, with Fingal County Council support, provides unusually detailed evidence for a group of distinct monuments.

### Officially identified components

- **St Doulagh's Church** — NIAH 11350016; RMP DU015-009001-. The report describes a multi-period church, including an eastern portion dating to the mid-twelfth century with a vaulted stone roof and croft, a fifteenth-century central tower, and an adjacent nineteenth-century church built in 1864.
- **St Doulagh's Well / Baptistry** — NIAH 11350017; RMP DU015-009004-. A circular stone-lined well below ground level, enclosed by an octagonal building with a conical roof. The report records interior frescoes painted in 1609 as still visible in the late nineteenth century; this does not establish their present survival.
- **St Catherine's Well** — RMP DU015-009003-. A separate underground well adjoining the north wall of the Baptistry, enclosed by a rectangular vaulted building.
- **Medieval stone cross** — RMP DU015-009002-. An undecorated granite Latin cross which marks the site entrance; the report says it was originally located in the graveyard.
- **Former ecclesiastical enclosure** — RMP DU015-009005-. A former enclosure whose circuit is partly suggested by the curving graveyard wall and supported by geophysical survey and archaeological excavation.
- **Graveyard** — RMP DU015-009006-. No longer used for burials according to the plan; contains eighteenth- to twentieth-century headstones and medieval architectural fragments.
- **Medieval architectural fragments** — SMR DU015-009007-. A separately identified group within the graveyard.
- **Medieval field system** — SMR DU015-009008-. Sub-rectangular network of ditches south of the graveyard, identified through geophysical survey.
- **Subcircular enclosure** — SMR DU015-009009-. Approximately 60m in diameter, west of the church and graveyard, identified through geophysical survey.

### Coordinate and access controls

The conservation plan's title-page ITM coordinate is printed as 721051, 742104, while a church footnote gives 721051, 741104. This internal discrepancy must be resolved against the official NMS/NIAH spatial records before any coordinate is adopted. No coordinate or map pin is assigned in this research note.

The plan documents the wells, cross, church and graveyard as distinct archaeological assets, but current access, condition and public photography permissions must be checked. Do not enter enclosed wells, climb structures, disturb stonework, or enter restricted areas.

## 2. Grallagh historic graveyard — Garristown

Fingal County Council's Buried in Fingal site identifies Grallagh as the location of an ancient monastic establishment, with a few remnants remaining, on a small hillock approximately 4km north-west of Oldtown.

The council's cemetery description records:
- A sub-rectangular graveyard.
- A limestone boundary wall with coping on the east side.
- A ditch around the other sides, curving around the south and possibly reflecting an ecclesiastical enclosure.
- An eastern cast-iron gate and stone stile.
- Undulating ground rising towards the church.
- Headstones and free-standing crosses from the eighteenth to twenty-first centuries.
- An earliest inscribed marker dated 1768, commemorating Thomas Mangan and erected by Anna Doran.

The council burial-ground page publishes a locator at latitude 53.54645726, longitude -6.33015776. Treat this as a council-published site locator, not a founder GPS fix or exact coordinate for any individual grave marker.

No new grave marker is assigned a permanent CICAN ID in this pass. The 1768 stone is a strong candidate for a separately documented micro-detail after a duplicate check and review of the graveyard survey volume.
