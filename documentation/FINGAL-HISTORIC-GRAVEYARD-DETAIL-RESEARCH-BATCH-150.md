## 1. Holmpatrick, Skerries — medieval priory memorials and shipwreck graves

**Survey:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project, FHG 51  
**NIAH:** 11311041 (graveyard)  
**RMP:** DU005-03105  
**Survey grid reference:** 325585, 259834 (published in the 2008 survey; retain as source-derived, not founder GPS)  
**Address:** Miller's Lane, Skerries, County Dublin.

The Fingal survey documents several highly specific candidates:

1. **Peter Manne memorial slabs** — two sixteenth-century slabs dedicated to Peter Manne, last prior of the Augustinian Priory of Holmpatrick, who died in 1520. One is transcribed in the survey with a Latin inscription translated as: “Here lies Peter Manne formerly Prior of the House on who soul God have mercy. He died in the year of Christ 1520.” The survey says the Manne stone is near the south face of the bell tower. Keep the two slabs distinct only if the survey photographs or later inspection establish their separate forms and positions.
2. **Richard de la Hoyde / Delahoyde memorial** — late sixteenth-century stone at the base of the tower beside the Manne stone, commemorating Richard de la Hoyde of Loughshinny, who died in 1587. The survey records a hole in the stone and a local claim that it was used to cure warts. Record the healing association as folklore, not fact.
3. **Elizabeth Finglas slab** — dated 1577; incorporated into a reused table tomb north of the tower, with crest and Latin inscription.
4. **Thomas McClerey shipwreck memorial** — commemorates a 16-year-old member of the crew of the Savage of Portaferry, wrecked at Skerries on 12 February 1756. The survey transcribes the inscription and describes a curved-top stone with a winged cherub head and floriated roundels.
5. **Royal Navy First World War memorial marker** — standard military-style headstone to an unnamed “Seaman of the Great War, Royal Navy 18th March 1918”.
6. **Medieval line-impressed floor tiles** — seven fragmentary tiles were reportedly found in a grave and considered probably associated with the former priory. These are archaeological finds, not automatically surviving display objects; no separate map point is justified without locating them.

The former priory's medieval buildings are no longer extant. The standing square tower dates to 1819 and was an addition to a church building now gone. The graveyard is recorded as well kept and no longer in use in the 2008 survey. Current condition and access still require field verification.

## 2. St Mary's Abbey, Howth — cross slabs, effigy tomb and holy spout

**Survey:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project, FHG 28  
**NIAH:** 11359010 (graveyard)  
**RMP:** DU015-02906  
**Survey grid reference:** 328718, 239185  
**Address:** Church Street, Howth, County Dublin.

The survey identifies:

1. **Two medieval grave slabs with carved crosses** — attached to the east and south walls of the south aisle. Both bear an inscribed cross with a stepped base.
2. **A third medieval grave slab** — sandstone, with a stepped base and thin cross shaft, reused in the kerbing of a flowerbed west of the church.
3. **Christopher St Lawrence and Anna Plunkett effigy tomb** — a c.1470 tomb in the chantry chapel, commemorating Christopher St Lawrence, 13th Baron of Howth (d.1462), and his wife Anna Plunkett of Ratoath. The survey records effigies of the knight and his wife.
4. **Holy spout / “eye spout”** — a water outlet in the retaining wall of the abbey graveyard. The survey says the current water issues from a short length of pipe said to have come from a holy well dedicated to the Virgin Mary. The claimed healing of sore eyes is local tradition, not medical evidence.
5. **Late medieval college remains** — ruins south of the abbey, identified as a fifteenth- or sixteenth-century college where members of the community lived. Treat as a site component unless discrete surviving features are separately described.
6. **Reused architectural fragments** — loose stone is reported across much of the southern part of the graveyard. No individual fragment is assigned an ID until it can be identified and located.

The graveyard is recorded as closed to burials in 1973. The abbey is a National Monument in State ownership; the graveyard is maintained by Fingal County Council. Any photography must respect current access rules and conservation restrictions.

## 3. St Columba's (Church of Ireland), Swords — early medieval grave slab

**Survey:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project, FHG 49  
**NIAH:** 11343007 (church)  
**RMP:** DU011-03403; medieval tower DU011-03404  
**Survey grid reference:** 318047, 246733  
**Address:** Church Street, Swords, County Dublin.

The survey identifies:

1. **Early medieval inscribed grave-slab fragment** — built into the base of the south wall of the medieval tower, northwest of the modern church. The report calls it an early medieval grave slab and notes it is inscribed. The precise inscription is not transcribed in the survey excerpt. It is a strong micro-detail candidate, but remains attached to its host tower and is not a separate free-standing object.
2. **Reused window-moulding fragment** — visible within the graveyard. Exact position and identity require field verification before separate registration.
3. **Charles McAlister memorial** — seventeenth-century sandstone headstone with IHS and sunburst decoration, and the phrase “Gloria in Excelsis Deo”; the survey transcribes a commemoration erected by Charles McAlister of Turvey for his wife Anne, who died in June 1690. The full day is not legible in the survey transcription.
4. **Andrew Kettle memorial pillar** — tall granite pillar with lead plaque in the twentieth-century extension, dedicated to Andrew Kettle, Land League participant, who died in 1916. Confirm exact inscription and current survival before separate catalogue promotion.

The 2008 survey also notes a sheela-na-gig formerly near St Columba's Church but transferred to the National Museum in 1945 (NMI 1945:18). It must not be mapped as if it remains at Swords.
