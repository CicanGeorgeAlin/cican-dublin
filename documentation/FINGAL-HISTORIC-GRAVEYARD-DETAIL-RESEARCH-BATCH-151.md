## 1. Baldongan — FHG 2

**RMP:** DU005-03703702 (graveyard); church DU005-03702 / DU005-03701 in survey text  
**NIAH:** 11318008 (church)  
**Survey grid:** 324059, 257534  
**Location:** Baldongan, Skerries, County Dublin.

The late medieval church retains pointed-arched openings and recesses at the east end. The southern chancel recess contains a carved stone with a coat of arms. A three-stage tower survives at the west end. The graveyard survey records one tall pillar stone with remnants of an inscription east of the church, a 19th-century family plot south of the church with two IHS-decorated headstones, and a southern slab carved with a Crucifixion and angel heads. Broken stones were collected at the base of the church's south wall; the survey specifically recommends investigating and recording their positions before any movement.

**Candidate details for later object-level review:**
- Heraldic carved stone in the southern chancel recess (fixed architectural feature).
- Inscription-remnant pillar stone east of church (exact reading not established).
- IHS family-plot headstones and carved Crucifixion/angel-head slab (one plot, multiple possible components; inspect survey photographs to avoid splitting a single monument incorrectly).
- Unidentified broken stone fragments at south wall (not individual objects until identifiable).

The historical association with the Knights Templar and the Blessed Virgin Mary is documented in the survey. The dramatic 1642 siege narrative is explicitly attributed to local tradition / historical sources in the report; do not turn uncorroborated details into established fact.

## 2. Balrothery — FHG 5

**RMP:** DU005-05705 (graveyard); medieval tower DU005-05703; carved head DU005-05704  
**NIAH:** 11309002 (church)  
**Survey grid:** 319954, 261139  
**Location:** Balrothery village, County Dublin.

The graveyard contains a significant group of early eighteenth-century inscribed headstones, box and table tombs, and cast-iron railed plots. The survey identifies several distinctive candidates:

- **Patrick Farrell headstone, 1727:** commemorates a local blacksmith; carved pincers and hammer are recorded. The story that he shod King William's horse is reported as tradition and must be labelled accordingly.
- **Belle Hill shipwreck memorial:** commemorates victims of the wreck north of Balbriggan in February 1875.
- **Reused millstone / memorial fragment:** a large square central hole; inscription begins “Here lyeth ye body of Frances Davis first wife to Samuel Brown who dyed Apl 30th 1715” and continues with Sarah Brown. The survey calls it a significant reused architectural fragment, possibly a millstone.
- **Robert Telling headstone, 1710:** inscription transcribed as “Here let the body of Robert Telling who dyed 9br ye 8th 1710”.
- **Medieval carved limestone head:** reinserted under the east window of the current church; distinct from the headstone memorials.
- **Stoup or font:** on the ground near the entrance to the rectory north of the church.
- **Inscribed headstone reused in southern entrance pillar:** location and inscription require close review of survey photographs.

The present church dates to 1816 and incorporates a medieval tower. Excavations found burials beyond the current boundary and evidence for a possible enclosure. The survey records a medieval carved head as RMP DU005-05704.

## 3. Bremore / St Molaga's — FHG 6

**RMP:** DU002-00203 (graveyard), DU002-00202 (church), DU002-00201 (fortified house)  
**Survey grid:** 319767, 264488  
**Location:** Bremore, Balbriggan, County Dublin. No NIAH number is listed in the 2008 graveyard survey.

This is a particularly strong micro-detail site. The survey describes:
- A late medieval carved Crucifixion plaque above a chimneypiece on the north side of the surviving church wall.
- A late seventeenth-century chimneypiece with a carved date plaque **1689**, plus carved symbols including a cross, ladder and nails above the opening.
- A surviving church wall approximately 2m high.
- An early nineteenth-century sandstone headstone to Andrew Herbert, erected by Anne Herbert alias Grimes; the visible transcription includes his death on 2 November 1806 and six children, but the remainder was obscured by vegetation in 2008.
- A broken late nineteenth-century headstone northwest of the church.
- A damaged, overgrown railed plot along the northern boundary.

The graveyard was disused and in very poor condition in the 2008 survey. It was overgrown and tree roots were affecting the church ruins. Current condition must be checked before any fieldwork. The Crucifixion plaque and 1689 chimneypiece are architectural details of the church ruin, not free-standing grave markers. Preserve their parent relationship if catalogued.

The reported seventh-century foundation by St Molaga is presented in the survey as historical tradition / cited historical sources, not as a securely demonstrated surviving early medieval structure.

## 4. St Marnock's, Portmarnock — FHG 7

**RMP:** DU015-00704 (graveyard); church DU015-00701; interior plaque DU015-00705  
**NIAH:** 11351005 (graveyard)  
**Survey grid:** 324503, 243496  
**Location:** Strand Road, Portmarnock, County Dublin.

The survey provides several important leads:
- **Oliver Barnewall memorial plaque, 1690:** RMP DU015-00705, inside the church. It commemorates Oliver Barnewall, his wife Mary Galtrim and their children. It was inaccessible during the 2008 survey because the church interior was overgrown. A related memorial stone near the north wall of the chancel is described in the historical context.
- **Two First World War military headstones:** 8462 Sergeant J Lynch, Royal Irish Rifles, 3 November 1918; and T4/263994 Driver C J Lynch, Royal Army Service Corps, 16 November 1918.
- **1735 grave marker:** located immediately north of a path under a large tree; the survey identifies it as one of the earliest markers but does not transcribe its inscription in the cited passage.
- **Marble Celtic Revival cross:** described as well executed, east of the church.
- **Cast-iron crosses and grave railings**, plus broken slabs along the path and western boundary.

**Important negative finding:** the survey says St Marnock's holy well in the dunes southwest of the church is no longer visible. It also says an ogham stone was reportedly broken at the well in 1854 and that no trace was visible in 2008. Neither the vanished well nor the unlocated broken ogham stone should be represented as a currently existing map object. They remain historical research leads only.

The late medieval church walls survive, with the gables almost full height and side walls around 1m. The graveyard was heavily overgrown in 2008; the interior was inaccessible due to tree growth. Current access and conservation conditions need checking.
