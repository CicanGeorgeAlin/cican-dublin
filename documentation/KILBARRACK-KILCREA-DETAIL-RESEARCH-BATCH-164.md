# CICAN Dublin Archive — Kilbarrack & Kilcrea Detail Research (Batch 164)

**Status:** Desk research only. No permanent archive IDs, catalogue records or production map pins created.  
**Scope:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project FHG 30 Kilbarrack and FHG 31 Kilcrea.  
**Primary source:** Fingal County Council, *Fingal Historic Graveyards Project, Volume 2*, survey entries of 3 August 2008 and 4 November 2008.  
**Key principle:** The 2008 survey is a dated baseline. For Kilcrea, later site inspection in 2021 confirms severe overgrowth and provides measured church dimensions.

## 1. Kilbarrack — FHG 30

### Site identity and designations
- Survey date: 3 August 2008.
- Townland: Kilbarrack Lower; parish: Kilbarrack.
- Address: Dublin Road, Sutton, Dublin 13, County Dublin, Ireland.
- Survey grid reference: ITM 324240, 239063.
- RMP: DU015-02102 (graveyard); associated church DU015-02101; grave-slab component DU015-02103.
- RPS 549; no NIAH number recorded in the FHG survey.
- Ownership: Fingal County Council.
- Setting: north side of the R106, overlooking the strand and Bull Island. The graveyard is on a rise and has been extended northwards.
- The graveyard remains in use according to later Kilbarrack heritage material, while the 2008 FHG survey described the historic graveyard as no longer in use. This likely reflects later cemetery use/extension or different definitions; do not repeat the 2008 status as current.

### Early ecclesiastical and maritime history
- Local historical tradition identifies Kilbarrack as a 6th-century foundation of St Berach. The FHG survey phrases this as “reported”; do not state the foundation date as archaeologically proven.
- The surviving Chapel of Mone, commonly called the Abbey of Kilbarrack, has fabric dated by the survey to the late 12th/early 13th century. It is described as a votive chapel used by mariners frequenting Dublin Bay.
- The site is associated with the burial of drowned sailors washed ashore; this is a historical tradition requiring attribution to the cited antiquarian literature.
- The graveyard was formerly on the strand near the North Bull sandbank and apparently lacked a boundary wall at an earlier period. A roughly three-acre area is now enclosed by a later limestone wall.
- St Berach's Well is reported north of the church and was noted for eye-related cures. The well is a separate feature outside the graveyard; its exact current position and survival status need confirmation before any location pin.

### Church fabric and associated structures
- The remains of the church (DU015-02101) stand on a prominent rise in the south-east corner of the graveyard.
- The surviving fabric consists of the nave and chancel walls, except for the west gable. The walls are limestone and sandstone with evidence of rebuilding.
- A pointed-arched window survives at the east end.
- The interior has been extensively used for burial. Record interior burials/markers separately from the church fabric and do not disturb them.
- A small two-bay, single-storey cottage stands in the north-east corner of the graveyard. The FHG extract is truncated after describing its hipped roof; its original function and later history need a full source review. Keep it separate from the church.
- The graveyard boundary consists of squared rubble limestone and sandstone with coping; cast-iron gates are on the southern boundary.

### Memorials and distinctive micro-details
1. **Howard headstone, 1654** — the earliest memorial visible today according to the later Buried in Fingal transcription and Kilbarrack heritage map. The 2008 FHG survey says an earlier memorial recorded historically as dating to 1654 may have sunk and is no longer visible. This is a direct source discrepancy: a later source says the 1654 Howard memorial is visible. Fieldwork must locate and photograph it before a definitive current-status statement.
2. **Private M. E. Brady, Canadian Infantry** — standard military headstone; transcription in FHG: “20 Private M. E. Brady 8th BN. Canadian Inf. 17th November 1915”. Record the exact service number and punctuation from the stone itself.
3. **Private F. E. Brady, Leinster Regiment** — standard military headstone; FHG transcription: “350X Private F. E. Brady Leinster Regiment 1st November 1915”. Preserve “350X” exactly as source transcription until checked against the headstone.
4. **Bernard “Bernie” Brennan memorial** — late-20th-century limestone headstone east of the church, curved top and foliage decoration. FHG transcription: “Remember Bernard ‘Bernie’ Brennan 22nd October 1938 - 1 October 1991”. It is a distinct memorial and useful example of later craft.
5. **John Sweetman memorial plaque** — commemorates John Sweetman (1752–1826), United Irishman of 1798, with wording transcribed by FHG as: “John Sweetman / United Irishman / 1798 / is interred by / RIP / Erected by the National / Graves Association. In his memory”. Confirm exact punctuation and layout in situ.
6. **Francis Higgins (1746–1802)** — notable burial identified by Fingal's later cemetery information; exact grave-marker identity and inscription require location verification.
7. **Eoin MacNeill (1867–1945) and James MacNeill (1869–1938)** — notable burials listed by the later Kilbarrack heritage map. Identify the actual markers and distinguish memorials from burial locations before creating records.
8. **Sweetman family burials** — the heritage map identifies several members of the brewing family, including John Sweetman. Do not create a family-group memorial record without confirming each distinct stone.
9. **Celtic Revival cross and railed plot** — a railed plot with a Celtic Revival cross lies northwest of the church; similar plots occur inside the church. Photograph the cross, rails, plinth and associated inscriptions as distinct but spatially related components.
10. **IHS and mortality carvings** — older limestone slabs around the church commonly have IHS motifs in sunbursts; one example has a skull carved on either side of the IHS. This is a priority micro-detail, but the exact stone is not named in the survey.
11. **Convent burial enclosure** — a square enclosure in the north-west corner contains burials from a convent. Treat the enclosure, its boundary and each identifiable memorial as separate candidates.
12. **War of Independence burials** — the 2025 Kilbarrack heritage booklet notes Daniel Head, killed during the burning of the Custom House in May 1921 and buried with full military honours. The Kilbarrack heritage map also names Frank Flood. Locate exact memorials and verify identities through the booklet's cited sources before publishing individual object records.

### Conservation and fieldwork priorities
- FHG 2008 records broken headstones, damaged railings, fragments reinserted into the ground and subsidence in 20th-century plots.
- The survey recommends checking stability before straightening, recording fragments before moving them, repairing railings, improving the entrance gates and footpath, and consolidating the church walls.
- Fieldwork priority: locate the Howard 1654 marker; photograph both Brady military headstones; locate Sweetman and Bernie Brennan memorials; identify Higgins, MacNeill and Daniel Head/Frank Flood markers; record the Celtic Revival cross, skull/IHS motif, convent enclosure and north-east cottage.
- The survey's Plate 30.2 shows a military headstone; compare it with the two Brady markers before assuming which stone is depicted.
- Do not straighten stones, remove ivy, lift fragments or undertake repairs without conservation guidance.

## 2. Kilcrea — FHG 31

### Site identity and designations
- Survey date: 4 November 2008.
- Townland: Kilcrea; parish: Donabate.
- Address: Kilcreagh Road, Donabate, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG survey grid reference: ITM 321768, 248796.
- RMP: DU012-01602 (graveyard); associated church DU012-01601.
- RPS 499; no NIAH number recorded in the FHG survey.
- Ownership was undetermined in 2008. Confirm landowner and permission before any field visit.
- The graveyard lies south of the R126, east of a local road leading toward Malahide Estuary.
- Fingal's 2019 Broadmeadow Way EIAR appendix gives ITM 721676, 748831 for Kilcrea Cemetery; this appears to be a different coordinate reference system or an alternative record. Do not substitute it for the 2008 FHG ITM without GIS conversion and confirmation.

### Church remains
- A plain rectangular church survives within the graveyard.
- FHG 2008 describes the east gable as surviving to full height, with north and south walls also surviving. It is built of uncoursed limestone.
- A single-light window in the east gable is splayed internally. An opening survives at the west end of the north wall.
- A later archaeological audit (site visit 22 September 2021) gives measured dimensions: approximately 8.75m long, 5.40m wide and maximum 3.50m high. It describes conglomerate and limestone blocks, splayed single-light windows in the east gable and at the east end of the south wall, and a surviving door rebate on the south side.
- The 2021 auditor could only just see the church gable over the wall because the site was extremely overgrown; access involved a scramble up the roadside bank. This is a strong warning against unplanned field entry.
- The FHG survey says there are no upstanding archaeological remains in the site-setting description, but immediately documents the standing church walls in the architectural section. Interpret the phrase as referring to no wider visible remains outside the church; do not quote it as meaning the church has vanished.

### Graveyard boundary and setting
- The graveyard is no longer used for burial.
- It is enclosed by a modern concrete wall erected in the early 1990s, approximately 45m by 30m according to the archaeological audit.
- The entrance is an opening in the east wall, reached across a small drainage ditch; a stile-type entrance is reported.
- The 2008 survey describes the site as completely overgrown and in poor condition, with animal burrows.
- The 2021 audit independently confirms the overgrown state and difficult visibility.
- The graveyard has a partial rectangular church, but the FHG entry does not enumerate individual named headstones or provide inscription transcriptions. Do not invent a memorial inventory from the two survey plates.

### Historical tradition
- Local tradition recorded in the Schools' Manuscript Collection says monks at Kilcrea and Ballymadrough supported themselves by tilling fields and spinning/weaving their own clothes.
- This is folklore collected in the 20th century, not direct evidence of the medieval community's economic organisation.
- Fingal's 2019 protected-structures schedule separately lists a possible motte off Kilcrea Road, a possible ringfort, the ruined church and graveyard (RPS 499), and a possible tidal mill (RPS 501) in the wider Kilcrea townland. These are distinct landscape features, not parts of the cemetery record.
- A separate 2019 EIAR refers to the fragmentary remains of a 17th-century tidal mill, Baltray Corn Mill, in the Kilcrea townland context. Do not assume the mill is within the cemetery or use its location as a graveyard pin.

### Kilcrea fieldwork priorities
1. Obtain landowner permission and an access/safety plan before attempting a visit; the 2021 survey indicates difficult access and heavy vegetation.
2. Photograph the east gable, single-light window, south-wall window and door rebate from safe accessible positions.
3. Measure church length, width and wall heights to test the 2021 dimensions and compare them with the 2008 survey.
4. Record the drainage ditch, concrete boundary wall, east entrance opening and roadside bank without climbing unstable walls.
5. Only if vegetation has been safely and lawfully managed by qualified personnel, identify individual grave markers and document them in situ.
6. Investigate the wider possible motte, ringfort and tidal mill as separate heritage research items; do not add them to the cemetery without distinct site evidence.

## 3. Cross-checks and archive decisions

- **Kilbarrack early foundation:** St Berach's 6th-century foundation is a reported tradition; the standing chapel is dated to the late 12th/early 13th century by the FHG survey.
- **Kilbarrack 1654 Howard stone:** FHG 2008 says an earlier 1654 memorial may have sunk and is no longer visible; later official and local heritage sources identify a visible Howard memorial dated 1654. Current field verification is essential.
- **Kilbarrack status:** FHG 2008 says no longer in use; more recent heritage sources say the cemetery remains in use. The old burial ground and modern extension may be described differently. Use current cemetery management information for present status.
- **Kilcrea coordinate:** FHG ITM 321768,248796 differs numerically from the 2019 EIAR ITM 721676,748831. Retain both source records and resolve the grid system before creating canonical geometry.
- **Kilcrea condition:** 2008 and 2021 sources both describe severe overgrowth; 2021 confirms poor visibility and access difficulty.
- No permanent archive IDs assigned.
- No production catalogue records or map pins created.
- Site, church, graveyard, cottage, each named memorial, military headstone, enclosure, St Berach's Well, and nearby landscape monuments remain separate candidates.
- Master catalogue access and duplicate checks remain prerequisites for permanent IDs and publication.
