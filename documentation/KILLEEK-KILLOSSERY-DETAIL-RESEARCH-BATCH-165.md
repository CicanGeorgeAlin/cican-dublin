# CICAN Dublin Archive — Killeek & Killossery Detail Research (Batch 165)

**Status:** Desk research only. No permanent archive IDs, catalogue records or production map pins created.  
**Scope:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project FHG 32 Killeek and FHG 33 Killossery (also called Rolestown Cemetery in current council burial records).  
**Primary source:** Fingal County Council, *Fingal Historic Graveyards Project, Volume 2*, surveys dated 30 July 2008 and 4 August 2008.  
**Archive principle:** Church, graveyard, enclosure, each memorial, reused architectural fragment, and nearby landscape feature are separate candidates. Folklore is attributed, not treated as archaeological fact.

## 1. Killeek — FHG 32

### Site identity and designations
- Survey date: 30 July 2008.
- Townland/parish: Killeek / Killeek.
- Address: Killeek, Swords, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG survey grid reference: ITM 314411, 246153.
- RMP: DU011-03103 (graveyard); associated ecclesiastical enclosure DU011-03101 and church DU011-03102.
- RPS 633; NIAH 11342010 (graveyard).
- Ownership: Fingal County Council.
- Official Buried in Fingal locator: 53.45337804, -6.27841753. Retain the survey ITM and current web locator as separate source values until GIS conversion and field GPS confirmation.
- The site is in tillage land west of a local road and west of the R106. It is an oval, raised burial ground.

### Ecclesiastical enclosure and church
- A curved rubble-limestone boundary and earthen bank on the south-west, west and north sides are interpreted by the 2008 survey as indicating an earlier enclosure.
- The earthen bank outside the graveyard wall is considered probably to be the remains of an early ecclesiastical enclosure. This remains an archaeological interpretation, not a fully excavated fact.
- The original church was in ruins by 1630 according to Walsh (1888). The current simple church remains within the graveyard, and its interior appears to have been used for burials.
- The standing church is a two-bay nave of roughly coursed rubble limestone. Opposed pointed-arched openings occur at the west ends of the north and south walls; a segmental-headed chancel arch survives in the east wall.
- An earlier photograph showed a wall extending from the north-east corner of the chancel, but this wall had been removed by the 2008 survey. Do not depict it as extant.
- The ground level within the graveyard is substantially raised. This is a separate topographic characteristic from the external bank/enclosure.

### Memorials and micro-details
1. **Earliest dated inscription: 1701** — the FHG survey says the earliest dated inscription appears to be 1701, but does not identify the stone in its descriptive text. Locate and transcribe in the field; do not assign an identity from date alone.
2. **Private J. McCormack, Labour Corps** — standard First World War military-style headstone. FHG transcription: “275893 Private J. McCormack Labour Corps. 25th December 1918 age 46”. The official Buried in Fingal page gives the epitaph “Labour omnia vincit”. Photograph the whole stone, regimental/service number, epitaph and base.
3. **Margaret Mulligan memorial, 1797** — the official Buried in Fingal page quotes a verse attributed to her husband: “Here lies from strife, a faithful virtuous and loving wife, / A tender parent, a faithful friend, / Happy was her beginning and I hope no doubt her end.” Locate the physical stone and verify the complete inscription and line breaks.
4. **18th-century headstone illustrated in Plate 32.2** — the survey identifies it as an 18th-century headstone but does not provide a transcription in the entry text. Treat as an unidentified photographic candidate pending plate-to-field matching.
5. **Timber and cast-iron crosses** — both types are recorded. Distinguish individual examples by exact location, construction, condition and inscription, rather than making one aggregate object.
6. **Reused architectural fragments** — several fragments lie at the base of the church wall. Photograph each in situ, with scale and position, before attempting any interpretation or relocation.
7. **Church interior burials** — the survey indicates the interior has been used for burial. Record visible markers separately from the church fabric; do not enter unstable areas or disturb burials.

### Condition and fieldwork
- The 2008 survey rated the graveyard good and well maintained, with generally tidy plots; it was no longer in use at that time.
- No significant conservation issues were recorded. The survey recommended continued regular maintenance and aerial photographic/geophysical analysis around the graveyard to identify possible further enclosures or archaeological features.
- The current Buried in Fingal page describes the cemetery as maintained by Fingal County Council and provides the 1701/1754-era burial history, McCormack and Mulligan memorial details.
- Fieldwork priorities: document the church openings and chancel arch; map the enclosure bank and curved wall; locate the 1701 marker, J. McCormack stone and Margaret Mulligan stone; photograph Plate 32.2 candidate; record each timber/cast-iron cross and every reused fragment.
- Do not clear vegetation, lift fragments or carry out repairs without archaeological/conservation advice.

## 2. Killossery — FHG 33 / Rolestown Cemetery

### Site identity and designations
- Survey date: 4 August 2008.
- Townland/parish: Killossery / Killossery.
- Address: Killossery, Rowlestown, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG survey grid reference: ITM 312586, 249998.
- RMP: DU011-00502 (graveyard); associated church DU011-00501 and font site DU011-00503.
- RPS 335; NIAH 11327005 (graveyard).
- Ownership: Fingal County Council. Denomination is marked “Roman Catholic?” in the FHG survey; retain the question mark rather than assert a denomination.
- Current official name in Buried in Fingal: Rolestown Cemetery, Killossery, Rolestown, Co. Dublin.
- Official WGS84 locator: 53.49077597, -6.29893381. Retain separately from the FHG ITM reference pending coordinate conversion and field confirmation.
- The graveyard lies north of the R125, immediately south of the Broadmeadow River. It is sub-oval, raised above surrounding ground, with a northern earthen embankment that may represent an early ecclesiastical enclosure.

### Church history and standing fabric
- The church is dedicated to St Brigid. The FHG survey says there was probably a church on the site from the early medieval period; the visible stone church was probably built around the Anglo-Norman invasion and appears to have fallen into ruin between 1630 and 1650.
- Walsh (1888) suggested Vikings may have had a rath here. This is an antiquarian hypothesis, not a confirmed archaeological identification.
- The graveyard is substantially raised above the immediate ground level, suggesting that the church may have been built on an earlier monument. The 2012 Fingal archaeological-resource report also describes the graveyard as artificially raised by about 2m and of medieval significance on an earlier monument.
- The gable walls of the church survive to approximately 1.5m; the north wall survives to approximately 0.6m.
- A door opening survives in the north wall and a window opening in the east wall.
- The church and graveyard must remain separate parent/child components; the nearby Broadmeadow River, embankment and possible earlier monument are distinct landscape features.

### Graveyard layout and boundary details
- Sub-oval enclosure with an earthen boundary showing evidence of stone facing; rubble limestone wall survives to the south and west.
- Entrance at the south-east has mild-steel gates and a stile. A separate archaeological-resource description notes a graveslab cemented at an angle at the top of the stile. This is a highly distinctive micro-detail and a priority for in-situ photography; do not assume its original position or function.
- Many plots have low stone plinths and cast-iron railings; a large railed plot lies north of the church.
- A tall pillar stone stands north-west of the church, west of a yew tree according to the 2012 archaeological-resource description. Its surface is laminated and may once have had a cross inscribed on one face. Record the uncertainty and do not call it a cross slab without evidence.
- A large concentration of older markers lies east of the church.
- Several undecorated stones occur east and west of the church; one cast-iron marker is at the east end.
- The 2008 survey describes the graveyard as well maintained and no longer in use. Buried in Fingal says it is closed and maintained by Fingal County Council.

### Distinct memorials
1. **Will Connell and Agnis Connell alias Kenedy memorial** — one of the earliest inscribed stones, dated by the inscription to Will Connell's death on 8 December 1717, aged 55; it also commemorates his wife Agnis Connell alias Kenedy. The inscription says it was erected by their son Richard Connell, who died 15 April 1748, aged 41. FHG transcription: “IHS. This burial place of Will Connell who departed this life the 8th of December 1717 aged 55 years. Also his wife Agnis Connell alias Kenedy. The same being erected by his son Richard Connell who departed this life ye 15th day of April 1748 aged 41 years”. The stone has an unusual wave pattern to the top. Treat all three people as commemorated persons on one physical memorial unless inspection proves otherwise.
2. **O'Crionagain / John Crenigan memorial cross** — early-20th-century cross dedicated to “O'Crionagain, 1916 Óglaigh Na hÉireann”. Fingal's Rowlestown Local Area Plan identifies John Crenigan, a local volunteer killed at Ashbourne on 28 April 1916, as the person commemorated. Photograph and transcribe the cross before linking the name to the historical person in the catalogue.
3. **Tall laminated pillar stone** — located north-west of the church. The survey says it may once have carried an incised cross. It is a distinct object, not a confirmed cross.
4. **Francis P. Murphy memorial** — Buried in Fingal describes a headstone erected by the North Dublin Executive of the United Irish League to Francis P. Murphy, who died in 1912. Locate and verify its exact wording and position.
5. **Lawless family and First World War burials** — the official Rolestown cemetery page notes members of the Lawless family and WWI war dead. These are research leads; identify individual stones and inscriptions before separate records.
6. **Cast-iron marker east of church** — distinct from the iron railings around plots; photograph the marker's form, inscription, fixing and current condition.
7. **Grave slab set at an angle atop the south-east stile** — the 2012 report's description should be field checked. It may be a reused grave slab and must not be conflated with the tall pillar stone.
8. **Annie Maguire headstone** — the 2008 survey specifically warns that abrasive cleaning techniques have been used on some headstones, giving Annie Maguire as an example. Locate the stone and document surface damage without further cleaning.

### Funeral custom and folklore
- The Schools' Manuscript Collection records a custom in which a coffin was carried three times around a patch of grass in the middle of the crossroads, then brought into the graveyard and carried three times around it. The account says the custom had been practised since penal times.
- Record this as a collected local tradition, not a currently verified practice. The crossroads grass plot is a separate landscape feature and its precise historical location requires map comparison and local evidence.
- A separate Schools' Collection account says monks at Killossery and Ballymadrough supported themselves by tilling fields and spinning/weaving clothes. This is folklore and must not be presented as direct evidence for medieval economic life.

### Conservation and fieldwork
- The 2008 survey recorded broken 19th-century stones, broken railings and plinths, worn stones inside the church and broken markers on sloping ground. It specifically cautions against abrasive cleaning.
- The 2012 archaeological-resource report notes significant potential for slippage/collapse at the eastern graveyard wall, exacerbated by tree-root growth and extensive weedkiller use above the wall. It also reports inappropriate cement repointing that may damage the stonework.
- Fieldwork priorities: photograph the 1717 Connell memorial from all sides; locate the 1916 O'Crionagain cross; record the tall pillar stone; photograph the graveslab built into the stile; identify Francis P. Murphy and Lawless/WWI memorials; document the cast-iron marker, railed plots and Annie Maguire stone; assess the east wall from safe ground-level viewpoints.
- Do not use abrasive cleaning, apply weedkiller, move fragments, reset stones or undertake wall repairs without qualified conservation advice.

## 3. Important cross-checks

- **Killeek earliest memorial:** the FHG survey gives 1701 as the earliest dated inscription, while Buried in Fingal says Dr Egan records the earliest headstone as 15 August 1754. The discrepancy may reflect different surveys or a distinction between a visible marker and Egan's transcription. Preserve both claims and identify the 1701 stone in situ before deciding.
- **Killeek Margaret Mulligan:** the official Buried in Fingal page supplies a detailed verse for a 1797 memorial. The exact physical stone and full inscription must be checked against the source.
- **Killossery naming:** the 2008 survey calls the site Killossery; the current official burial database calls it Rolestown Cemetery, Killossery, Rolestown. Keep “Killossery” as historic site name and “Rolestown Cemetery” as current administrative label/alias.
- **Killossery denomination:** FHG's “Roman Catholic?” is uncertain; do not silently upgrade it to confirmed.
- **Killossery St Brigid:** the church dedication is reported by the FHG survey. It is not a surviving inscription unless field evidence establishes one.
- **Killossery earlier monument/rath:** raised ground and Walsh's Viking-rath suggestion are different levels of interpretation. Do not label the site a Viking rath.
- No permanent archive IDs assigned.
- No production catalogue records or map pins created.
- The master catalogue still needs to be fully accessible for duplicate checks before permanent publication.
