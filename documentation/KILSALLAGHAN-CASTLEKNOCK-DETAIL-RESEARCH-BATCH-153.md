**Date:** 17 November 2026  
**Scope:** St David's, Kilsallaghan (FHG 9); St Brigid's, Castleknock (FHG 10)  
**Status:** Research-only. No permanent catalogue objects, IDs, source-registry entries, visual records or map pins created.

## Method and limitations

Primary evidence is Fingal County Council's 2008 *Fingal Historic Graveyards Project, Volume 2*, entries FHG 9 and FHG 10. The council's later Community Monuments Fund report provides an update on the Kilsallaghan boundary-wall repair. Repository searches for the principal RMP/NIAH identifiers and named monuments below returned no matches, but the canonical data/objects.json was not available for complete duplicate reconciliation. Therefore no IDs are allocated and no candidate is promoted to the public catalogue.

## 1. St David's, Kilsallaghan — FHG 9

**Address:** Kilsallaghan, County Dublin, Ireland  
**RMP:** DU011-01102 (graveyard); DU011-01101 (possible medieval church); DU011-01103 (possible ecclesiastical enclosure)  
**NIAH:** 11333001 (church)  
**RPS:** 653  
**2008 survey grid reference:** 311709, 248680 (site-level reference only; not founder GPS)

### Historical and archaeological features

- The current Church of Ireland building was constructed in the early nineteenth century by the Board of First Fruits near the site of an earlier church.
- The possible medieval church is represented by a line of grassed-over wall footings extending from the southeast corner of the nineteenth-century church.
- A small fragment of wall footing south of the present church may belong to the earlier church.
- A wide, flat embankment outside the graveyard to the northwest, with traces continuing around the north/east and southwest, is interpreted in the survey as a possible ecclesiastical enclosure. The 2025/26 council account says the enclosure evidence suggests a pre-Norman origin, but the interpretation remains archaeological inference rather than proof of a precise foundation date.
- In 1197 St David's church was gifted to St Thomas' Abbey in Dublin, according to Fingal County Council's Community Monuments Fund account.

### Individually described memorial candidates

1. **Richard Corbley table tomb, 1730** — limestone slab on granite legs, decorated with an IHS marker. The survey transcribes: “Here lieth the body of Richard Corbley of Strablee who departed this life august the 24th 1730 aged 70 years. Also Mary Corbley wife to the above RC who departed this life December the 17th 17...aged 90 years”. The second date is incomplete in the survey transcription; do not complete it by guesswork.
2. **Lamb family upright slab, 1740** — described near the church door; the survey identifies a member of the Lamb family but does not give a complete name in the entry.
3. **Mid-eighteenth-century headstone** — shown in Plate 9.2; the 2008 plate caption provides the date range but not a readable transcription in the text extract. Inspect the original survey photograph before assigning a separate record.
4. **Other table tombs** — the survey describes multiple inscribed limestone slabs with granite legs. Individual tombs require photographic and inscription-level identification before separate records.
5. **Possible grass-covered grave slabs** — numerous mounds in the southwest section may conceal flat slabs. These are not visible individual objects and must remain archaeological research leads.

### Later conservation update

Fingal County Council reports that the graveyard retaining wall collapsed after heavy rain following harsh winters. The wall was rebuilt and a bulge in the other retaining wall repaired under archaeological supervision. A small number of bone fragments were recovered and analysed; they came from at least one adult and one juvenile and were assessed as no more than a few centuries old. This is a conservation and archaeological works record, not evidence for a specific named burial.

The 2008 survey described the graveyard as well maintained but somewhat overgrown, and still in use. That is a dated condition statement; the later wall works do not establish the present condition of every headstone.

## 2. St Brigid's, Castleknock — FHG 10

**Address:** Castleknock Road, Castleknock, Dublin 15, County Dublin, Ireland  
**RMP:** DU017-00802 (graveyard); DU017-00801 (site of thirteenth-century monastery)  
**NIAH:** 11362003 (church)  
**RPS:** 765  
**2008 survey grid reference:** 308982, 237141 (site-level reference only; not founder GPS)

### Historical and architectural context

The present Church of Ireland church was built in 1806 on the site of a late twelfth- or thirteenth-century monastery attached to the Benedictine Priory of Little Malvern. Side aisles were added around 1860. The current building has a three-stage western tower, rubble granite walls, ashlar plinth course and pointed-arched aisle windows with hood mouldings and stone sills.

The survey says there are no upstanding early medieval archaeological features within the graveyard, while associating the site with a nearby St Brigid's Well. Do not assume the well is within the graveyard or use the graveyard coordinate as its location.

### Individually described monument candidates

1. **Large Meade / Dunn family box tomb** — west of the church, within a cast-iron-railed plot. The north panel is dedicated to the Meade family; east and south panels to the Dunn family; west panel is uncarved. Plate 10.2 depicts this large stone box tomb. Treat the whole structure as one tomb with multiple inscribed panels unless detailed survey evidence demonstrates separate monuments.
2. **Late eighteenth-century box tomb, dated 1780** — separately described in the conservation section as having damage to its sides and needing repair. The survey does not explicitly establish whether this is the same as the Meade/Dunn tomb. Do not merge the two until the original plate and site plan confirm identity.
3. **Barons of Holmchapel burial plot, erected 1912** — north of the church, surrounded by a low plinth wall and containing three grave markers. The plot is a parent feature; its three markers may be individual children only after visual inspection.
4. **Damaged table tomb in a cast-iron-railed plot** — near the western boundary. The survey calls it badly damaged; exact inscription and family identity are not given in the text.
5. **Broken crosses and railings** — west of the church. These are conservation observations, not separate catalogue objects until individual pieces can be identified and located.
6. **Other eighteenth- to twenty-first-century headstones and crosses** — multiple markers are recorded, but individual inscriptions and distinctiveness need the survey's photographs or current field photography before separate entries.

The graveyard was recorded as well maintained and still in use in 2008. That dated observation does not prove present condition.

## 3. St Brigid's Well — separate location research lead

The FHG 10 survey says a holy well known as St Brigid's Well is associated with the church, but it does not provide a precise location or separate RMP/NIAH number in the graveyard entry. Keep it as a separate research lead. Do not place it at the church's site coordinate or make a map pin until its own location and monument identity are confirmed.

## 5. Fieldwork and image priorities

1. Kilsallaghan: current photographs of Richard Corbley's table tomb and inscription, Lamb family slab, mid-eighteenth-century headstone, possible medieval church footings and repaired retaining wall.
2. Castleknock: all four faces of the Meade/Dunn box tomb, separate 1780 box tomb if confirmed distinct, Barons of Holmchapel plot and its three markers, damaged western table tomb, broken crosses and railings.
3. St Brigid's Well: establish exact location and official monument identity before photography or mapping.
4. No ivy removal, cleaning, movement, straightening or entry into unstable structures. Both sites are protected heritage places; any intervention requires specialist advice and relevant permissions.
