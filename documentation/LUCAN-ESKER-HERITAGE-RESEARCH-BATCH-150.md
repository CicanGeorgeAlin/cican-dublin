## A. King John's Bridge, Esker South

- **RMP:** DU017-078.
- **Date:** late 12th / early 13th century.
- **Location locator:** ITM E 703901, N 734276; Excavations.ie publishes 53.348489, -6.439481. This is a source-derived site locator, not founder GPS.
- **Evidence:** Excavations.ie report 2021:715 records a surviving small arch between two piers on the River Griffeen. It was once the central arch of a three-arch bridge. Archaeological monitoring in 2021 exposed the south face of the west pier, confirmed it was a central pier rather than an abutment, found evidence for the spring of a further arch and identified a cutwater hidden by vegetation.
- **Conservation caution:** The bridge was considered at risk from tree-root activity and remedial works were undertaken under National Monuments Service consent. The report did not identify survival of the previously reported wicker-centering imprint. Do not state that the wicker imprint survives.
- **Nearby relationship:** Report places the bridge approximately 100m south-west of Esker Church and Graveyard (DU017-02201 / DU017-02202).
- **Potential visual record:** extant stone arch, piers, cutwater and masonry repairs as separate views within one bridge record, not separate objects unless evidence establishes independent significance.

## B. St Finian's Church and Graveyard (Esker Church)

- **Heritage Council identification:** St Finian's Church & Graveyard, Lucan, County Dublin.
- **Period:** Heritage Council states the church and graveyard date from the 11th century and have been ruined since the 17th century.
- **Community conservation:** Society for Old Lucan adopted the monument. An architectural assessment and geophysical survey in 2019 identified possible structural remains of an earlier church within the grounds.
- **Caution:** Geophysical anomalies are not exposed architectural fabric. Photograph visible remains and grave markers only from lawful access; do not infer buried structures from the survey.
- **Potential visual subjects:** standing church masonry, graveyard boundary and individually inscribed grave markers where legible and appropriate. Grave markers need their own evidence and privacy/cultural sensitivity review before separate records.

## C. Vesey Bridge, Lucan village

- **NIAH:** 11201046.
- **Date:** 1770–1775; NIAH describes it as built in 1773.
- **Location:** Main Street, Lucan Demesne, Lucan. NIAH grid locator 303251, 235213.
- **Fabric:** Single-arch road bridge with ashlar limestone parapets, raised voussoirs, radial spandrel stonework, blind niches in northern quadrant walls, Coade stone plaques and panels on the north side, and an inscribed limestone commemorative block facing south on the north parapet.
- **Strong micro-detail leads:** Coade stone plaques/panels; commemorative inscription; blind niches; parapet masonry and voussoirs. Treat these as parent-linked details of Vesey Bridge, not separate structures.
- **Source note:** NIAH rates the bridge Regional and records it as substantially in original condition. This is a survey appraisal, not a fresh condition inspection.

## D. Former Coldblow Bridge spring arch

- **NIAH:** 11201145.
- **Date:** c.1765 according to the 2021 archaeological impact assessment.
- **Survival described:** The spring of the southernmost arch of the former road bridge survives on the south bank of the Liffey, with steel railings above it. First-edition OS mapping (1836–37) labels surviving remains as “Piers of Br.”
- **Caution:** This is a surviving remnant of a former bridge, not a complete bridge. Need confirm NIAH record details and exact public viewpoint before catalogue promotion.

## E. Lucan Bridge and bridge-related details

- **RMP:** DU017-019006.
- **Current bridge:** The archaeological assessment states the present single-span masonry-arch bridge was constructed in 1814, possibly on the site of an earlier medieval bridge. No upstanding remains of the earlier bridge were identified at the present bridge location.
- **Visible architectural detail described:** cut limestone construction, parapet balustrade with cast-iron balusters, and a millrace arch north of the main bridge.
- **Caution:** Do not describe the present bridge itself as medieval. The medieval bridge is an earlier site/history association; the surviving bridge is 19th century.
- **Potential parent-linked details:** cast-iron balusters and the millrace arch, subject to complete-catalogue deduplication.

## F. River, mill and industrial traces

The 2021 archaeological impact assessment identifies several distinct traces:
- **13th/14th-century fish-weir site:** DU017-019005, located approximately 60m west of Lucan Bridge. The assessment warns that later 19th-century weir/sluice works and bridge construction likely caused significant disturbance. It is an archaeological site, not necessarily a visible medieval structure.
- **Late 19th-century sluice and waterworks:** the assessment describes waterworks on the north side of the river and a mass-concrete weir. These are later industrial/river-engineering fabric and should not be conflated with the medieval fish weir.
- **Early 20th-century turbine house:** on an island west of Lucan Bridge; single-storey structure with slated roof, cast-iron lattice windows, sand-and-cement render and a steep metal stairway to a gated structure. The assessment does not establish public access. Photograph only from lawful public viewpoints; do not enter the island or approach restricted infrastructure.
- **Mill race:** shown in the assessment with the turbine house; map the visible channel only if its present form and accessible viewpoint can be field-confirmed.

## G. River-edge and demesne micro-details

The same assessment's 13 April 2021 field inspection recorded:
- **CH1:** linear boulder revetment along parts of the southern river bank, age unknown.
- **CH2:** surviving former property-boundary wall, roughly coursed limestone rubble about 1.2m high, with concrete capping and some red brick.
- **CH3:** Lucan Demesne boundary wall, altered and repaired in places.
- **CH4:** single-arch stone footbridge over Tobermaclugg Stream, about 2m wide; quarry-faced keystones.
- **CH5:** irregular stone steps leading to the river, likely associated with the former demesne.
- The report says these field-identified features had no protected status at the time of assessment but were considered of local historical interest.
- The report's figure/plate list labels the river steps CH5, although one figure caption elsewhere calls them CH6; preserve the table's CH5 label but flag this minor internal inconsistency.
- These features are excellent visual-discovery candidates, but their present condition and access must be rechecked. The 2021 inspection is not a 2026 condition statement.

## H. Lucan medieval church, tower house and castle — access restriction

A South Dublin County Council meeting response states that the medieval Church of the Blessed Virgin Mary and adjacent castle ruins are within the grounds of the Italian Ambassador's residence. The area is not generally open to the public; managed access may be facilitated for special events. Do not plan an unscheduled visit or imply public access.

The 2021 archaeological assessment also distinguishes the tower house attached to the medieval parish church (RMP DU017-019001) from Lucan Castle; the report says the tower house is often mistaken for Lucan Castle and that the actual castle site's location is uncertain. The distinction must be maintained.
