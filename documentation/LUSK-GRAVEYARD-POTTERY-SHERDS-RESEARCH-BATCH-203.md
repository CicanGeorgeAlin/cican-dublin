# CICAN Dublin Archive — Lusk Graveyard Pottery Sherds Research Batch 203

**Date:** 17 November 2026  
**Scope:** Investigate the pottery component of National Museum of Ireland Topographical Files range 1999:151–173, recorded from the surface of Lusk Graveyard; establish what can be said about number, fabric, glaze, form, date, context, imagery and custody. Compare only as contextual evidence with separately excavated pottery from Lusk.  
**Status:** The 2012 published summary confirms a pottery component described as glazed pottery sherds, with the find field listing three sherds. No diagnostic fabric, form, date, photograph, accession-level breakdown or present custody has been established. No production object record or map pin created.

## 1. Principal finding — the published record is very limited

The Greater Dublin Drainage Phase Two cultural heritage assessment (May 2012), Appendix 6.14.3, reproduces National Museum of Ireland Topographical Files information for Lusk museum numbers **1999:151–173**.

The entry records:
- Townland: Lusk.
- Find place: “Found on surface at Lusk Graveyard”.
- Find field: clay floor tiles, roof tiles, brick, baked-clay fragments and **3 pottery sherds**.
- Description field: clay floor tiles, roof tiles, six unclassifiable pieces of baked clay and **glazed pottery sherds**.

The report does not state the pottery's:
- fabric or clay composition;
- colour or inclusions;
- glaze colour, finish or distribution;
- vessel form or function;
- rim, base, handle or body-fragment status;
- decoration;
- dimensions or thickness;
- archaeological period or date;
- exact point within the graveyard;
- individual museum numbers within the 1999:151–173 range;
- current physical repository or display status.

The only defensible object-level description at present is **three pottery sherds, described in the summary as glazed pottery, found on the surface at Lusk Graveyard**.

## 2. Preserve the discrepancy in the published table

The table's two fields do not use identical wording:
- “Find” says **3 pottery sherds**.
- “Description” says **Glazed pottery sherds**, without repeating the count.

The report does not explain whether “glazed” applies to all three sherds or whether the description is a broad note for the group. Until the original Topographical Files are checked, record the count and glaze description as separate source statements, not as a fully reconciled inventory.

The museum-number range 1999:151–173 is a range of 23 consecutive-looking identifiers, but the report's table does not provide an item-by-item list or say that every number represents a pottery sherd. It is unsafe to infer 23 pottery objects from the range. The same row includes multiple material categories.

## 3. Context — surface collection, not a stratified deposit

“Found on surface at Lusk Graveyard” is a broad surface-find description. It does not establish:
- a sealed archaeological layer;
- a burial association;
- a medieval church floor context;
- a grave cut or grave fill;
- a specific grave, wall or building;
- whether the finds were collected together in one visit;
- whether they were exposed by erosion, maintenance, gardening or another activity.

A surface collection can combine material from different periods and sources. The pottery should not be dated by assuming it belongs to the medieval church or by treating the entire 1999:151–173 group as one chronological assemblage.

The National Museum Topographical Files are described in the same 2012 assessment as the national archive of finds recorded by the Museum, including artefacts and references to monuments and previous excavations. The 2012 report is a secondary summary of the files, not a substitute for the original individual records.

Source:

## 4. Do not confuse pottery with the baked-clay fragments

The same museum-number range includes six “unclassifiable pieces of baked clay” in the description field. These must remain distinct from the pottery sherds.

The source differentiates:
- clay floor tiles;
- roof tiles;
- brick;
- six unclassifiable baked-clay pieces;
- glazed pottery sherds.

No basis is provided for relabelling the six unclassifiable baked-clay pieces as pottery, tile, daub, kiln furniture or another object type. Equally, no basis exists to treat the three pottery sherds as part of the floor-tile group.

## 5. Comparison with other pottery recorded at Lusk — separate sites and contexts

Other archaeological reports document pottery from Lusk, but these are not evidence that the graveyard surface sherds have the same date or origin.

### 5.1 Skerries Road, 2004 — medieval pottery in pits

Excavations.ie report 2004:0622, licence 04E0751, records monitoring at a residential development on Skerries Road, Lusk. One sherd of medieval pottery was recovered during preliminary investigation of archaeological features, including circular pits. The site was subsequently excavated under licence 04E0903.

Excavations.ie report 2004:0623 records that, with the exception of sherds of medieval pottery, no finds were retrieved from the pits. The same report says sherds of post-medieval pottery were recovered from seven linear features, likely drains or field boundaries.

These finds are from a separate development site at Skerries Road, not the Lusk Graveyard surface collection. They show that medieval and post-medieval pottery was present in the wider Lusk settlement landscape, but they do not date the 1999:151–173 sherds.

Sources:
- https://excavations.ie/report/2004/Dublin/0011746/
- https://excavations.ie/report/2004/Dublin/0011747/

### 5.2 Church Road, 2008 — 13th–14th-century pottery in a ditch

A separate Excavations.ie report for archaeological testing at Church Road records sherds of 13th–14th-century pottery in shell-rich deposits interpreted as probable domestic waste within a possible outer ecclesiastical enclosure. This is another separate context and cannot be used to date the graveyard surface finds.

Source:
https://excavations.ie/report/2008/Dublin/0019489/

## 6. Dating — unknown for the graveyard sherds

The 2012 Topographical Files summary gives no date for the pottery. The description “glazed pottery” is not enough to assign a medieval or post-medieval date without examining fabric, glaze, vessel form and diagnostic features.

The separately excavated pottery from Skerries Road and Church Road demonstrates that Lusk has produced pottery from different periods and archaeological contexts. It is not valid to transfer those dates to the graveyard sherds.

**Current dating field:** unknown / not stated in published summary.

## 7. Visual evidence — no authenticated image identified

No photograph or drawing of the three Lusk Graveyard pottery sherds was identified in the sources examined for this batch. The 2012 appendix is a text summary of Topographical Files entries.

Do not use generic images of medieval Irish pottery as if they depict the Lusk objects. Do not create a reconstructed vessel from the phrase “glazed pottery sherds”. A valid visual record requires an image of the actual sherds with source attribution and rights status.

## 8. Custody and access — not confirmed

The 2012 report attributes the information to National Museum of Ireland Topographical Files and gives the museum-number range 1999:151–173. It does not establish whether the physical sherds are held by the National Museum today, whether all materials in the range remain together, or whether photographs or drawings are available.

The original files should be requested to establish:
1. the exact number of pottery sherds;
2. whether all three are glazed;
3. the individual object or file numbers within the range;
4. fabric, glaze, vessel form and diagnostic features;
5. any specialist dating or identification;
6. finder, collection date and circumstances;
7. any photographs, drawings or correspondence;
8. current custody and access conditions.

## 12. Conclusion and next target

The Lusk Graveyard pottery evidence is currently a short Topographical Files summary: three pottery sherds are listed, and the description calls them glazed pottery. No fabric, vessel form, glaze colour, dimensions, date, photograph or precise find location is supplied. The wider 1999:151–173 museum-number range includes floor tiles, roof tiles, brick and six unclassifiable baked-clay pieces as well, so the range must not be interpreted as 23 pottery objects.

Separate excavations at Skerries Road and Church Road recovered medieval and post-medieval pottery in their own contexts. Those reports establish a wider pottery record for Lusk but do not identify or date the graveyard sherds.

**Next research target:** investigate the six unclassifiable baked-clay pieces in the same NMI range 1999:151–173. Determine whether the original Topographical Files, any archaeological report or surviving photographs clarify whether these are daub, kiln-related material, building fragments or another category. Preserve the source's “unclassifiable” label unless specialist evidence permits a better identification.
