# CICAN Dublin Archive — Octagonal Font, Lusk Belfry Research Batch 188

**Date:** 17 November 2026  
**Scope:** Research the octagonal limestone font documented on the ground floor of the historic belfry at St MacCullin's, Lusk; record its form, dimensions, dating confidence, current published setting, and distinction from the bullaun stone and fireplace fragment.  
**Status:** Research-only. Existing parent record DUB-BLD-0177 remains unchanged. No permanent child ID, source-registry entry, visual record or production map pin created because the complete canonical object and component catalogues cannot be validated.

## 1. Object identity and parent relationship

- **Working name:** Lusk Octagonal Limestone Font.
- **Object type:** Stone font, described as octagonal, with pedestal and base.
- **Material:** Limestone, according to the 1988 archaeological survey.
- **Parent:** DUB-BLD-0177 — Saint MacCullin's Church, Lusk.
- **Published setting:** Ground floor of the adjacent square belfry / tower complex.
- **Survey date attribution:** “?17th cent.” in the 1988 survey. The question mark is significant and must be retained.
- **Primary source:** Bradley & King, *County Dublin Urban Archaeology Survey*, Part VIII(iv), 1988, Lusk, p. 264.
- **Dimensions published:** Height 92 cm; diameter 82 cm.

Heritage Ireland's current visitor description also identifies a seventeenth-century font within the adjacent square belfry. This supports the presence of a font as part of the tower's object assemblage, but does not independently describe the carved panels or provide measurements.

## 2. Physical description

The 1988 survey describes the object as:
- Limestone.
- Octagonal in plan.
- Supported by a pedestal and base.
- Eight sides/panels, each with a trefoil-headed chamfered recess.
- Base with both concave mouldings and chamfers.
- Published height: 92 cm.
- Published diameter: 82 cm.

These measurements are historical survey data, not measurements taken by CICAN. The report does not provide a separate measurement for the bowl, pedestal or base, nor does the cited description establish the exact profile of the bowl interior, drainage hole, lead lining, surface finish or tool marks.

**Do not add details such as a central drain, lead lining, water basin depth, lid, carved figures or liturgical marks unless a photograph, conservation report or direct permitted examination confirms them.**

## 3. Dating — probable, not proven

The 1988 survey dates the font “?17th cent.”, expressing uncertainty. Heritage Ireland's current visitor page describes a “17th century font” in the belfry. The visitor page is useful corroboration of the broad attribution, but does not provide the reasoning or a specialist dating assessment.

**Recommended archive wording:** “Probably seventeenth century; dated ‘?17th cent.’ by Bradley & King (1988), with Heritage Ireland describing it as seventeenth century. Exact date not established.”

Do not give a specific year or decade. Do not call it medieval simply because it is housed within a medieval ecclesiastical complex.

## 4. Function and interpretation

The archaeological survey identifies it as a font. Its form and ecclesiastical setting are consistent with a baptismal font, but the sources consulted in this batch do not state:
- Which church or chapel originally commissioned it.
- Whether it was originally installed in the medieval church at Lusk.
- Whether it was made for the fifteenth-century belfry, an earlier church, or another ecclesiastical location.
- Whether it remained in continuous liturgical use.
- When it was moved to its present recorded position.

**Archive rule:** use “font” as the source-defined object type. “Baptismal font” may be used as a functional gloss, but do not invent an original location, donor, maker, date of use or movement history.

It lists 2026 tour dates and times, with limited capacity and first-come-first-served admission.

## 6. Distinction from the bullaun stone and fireplace plaque

The belfry assemblage includes separate objects:
- **Font:** limestone, octagonal, pedestal and base, trefoil-headed chamfered recesses; H. 92 cm, Diam. 82 cm.
- **Bullaun stone:** pink granite, described in the 1988 survey as beside the south entrance.
- **Fireplace / chimney-breast fragment:** limestone plaque with six heraldic shields surrounding an Annunciation; reported removed from Bremore Castle; researched in Batch 187.

The font and bullaun are not the same object. A 2023 heritage profile's revised entry for the Lusk bullaun says it is now outside the entrance to the modern Roman Catholic church and formerly lay inside the medieval church tower. It quotes Hunt (1974) and Roe (1979) as recording a seventeenth-century font, a bullaun stone and a portion of a fireplace inside the tower. This is a reported movement history for the bullaun, not for the font.

Do not relocate the font in the catalogue based on the bullaun's separate movement history.

## 7. Comparative context — avoid typological overdating

Irish antiquarian writing on fonts notes that octagonal forms became common after the twelfth century and that octagonal fonts are often difficult to date because ornament is not always diagnostic. This broad context does not independently date the Lusk font. The Lusk font's trefoil-headed recessed panels and moulded base are useful descriptive traits, but no specialist comparative typology or conservation assessment has been identified in this batch.

**Conclusion on chronology:** keep the local survey's tentative seventeenth-century date as the principal archive statement. Do not infer an earlier medieval date from octagonal form alone.

Background reading:
- *Irish Baptismal Fonts*, Antiquities of Ireland: https://libraryireland.booksulster.com/Antiquities/III-II.php

## 8. Founder photography brief — guided access only

If the font is visible during a permitted guided visit and photography is allowed:
1. Photograph the complete font in its architectural context.
2. Take a straight-on view of each of the eight panels, numbered consistently clockwise from a chosen reference side.
3. Photograph the bowl rim and interior from above only if this can be done without leaning on or touching the object.
4. Capture each trefoil-headed recess and chamfer in close-up.
5. Photograph the pedestal, base, concave mouldings and chamfered edges.
6. Record any visible cracks, losses, repairs, biological growth or deposits neutrally; do not infer structural instability.
7. Record orientation, image date, access conditions and permission details.
8. If permitted, use a scale placed nearby without contact; otherwise rely on the published dimensions and do not improvise measurements.

Do not touch, wet, chalk, rub, trace, clean or place equipment on the font. Do not insert anything into the bowl or any possible drain. Do not use flash or additional lighting if prohibited.

## 11. Conclusion and next steps

The Lusk octagonal font is a distinct limestone ecclesiastical object recorded on the ground floor of the belfry. Its eight trefoil-headed recessed panels, pedestal, moulded base and published dimensions make it a strong micro-heritage subject. The 1988 survey's date is explicitly tentative (“?17th cent.”), while Heritage Ireland gives a broad seventeenth-century attribution. Its original commissioning church and movement history remain unknown.
