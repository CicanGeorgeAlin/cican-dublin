# Lusk Polished Stone Axehead — NMI 1999:63 Attribution Conflict
## Research Batch 207

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentary comparison; object not independently examined  
**Target:** NMI registration 1999:63, described as a polished stone axehead  
**Key outcome:** Published sources disagree on the townland. The detailed discovery description is associated with Causestown in one report, while a later Lusk assessment assigns the same registration to Lusk.

## 1. The Lusk reference (2021)

Martin E. Byrne’s *Kilhedge Lane, Lusk* Archaeological Heritage Assessment (August 2021), §5.3, lists the NMI Topographical Files entry:

| Field | Published entry |
|---|---|
| NMI registration | 1999:63 |
| Description | Polished Stone Axe Head |
| Find spot | Lusk |
| Period | Neolithic (possible) |

The table is explicitly introduced as stray finds recovered from the townland of Lusk. It provides no find circumstances, dimensions, material identification, image, or custody information for this entry.

Source: Byrne 2021, §5.3, p.12.  
https://consult.fingal.ie/ga/system/files/materials/22195/KILHEDGE%20LANE%20LUSK%20-%20Archaeological%20AIR%20-%20FINAL%20-%2011-08-2021.pdf

## 2. The conflicting record (2012)

The Greater Dublin Drainage *Alternative Sites Assessment – Phase Two* (May 2012), Appendix 6.15.3, lists **Museum No. 1999:63** as:

- **Townland:** Causestown
- **Find:** Polished stone axehead
- **Find place:** Found on the surface of a field while picking potatoes
- **Description:** Fine polished stone axehead with a thin pointed butt and broad cutting edge.

This is not merely a similar object with a nearby number: the registration number is identical. The townland attribution conflicts directly with the 2021 Lusk assessment.

Source: GDD 2012, Cultural Heritage Appendix, Appendix 6.15.3, pp.44–45.  
https://www.water.ie/sites/default/files/docs/greater-drainage-dublin/alternative-sites-assessment-phase2/Appendix-6-text.pdf

## 3. What can and cannot be concluded

The two sources agree on the museum registration and general object class, but disagree on the townland:

| Source | Registration | Townland | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Byrne 2021 | 1999:63 | Lusk | Polished stone axe head; Neolithic possible |
| GDD 2012 | 1999:63 | Causestown | Polished stone axehead; detailed form and potato-field discovery |

The 2012 description is valuable, but it cannot safely be attributed to a Lusk find while the townland conflict remains unresolved. Likewise, “Neolithic (possible)” is the dating assessment in the 2021 table, not a confirmed date or independent scientific result.

No accessible source in this review establishes dimensions, weight, exact stone type, precise field location, date of discovery, finder, photograph, current collection location, or custody.

## 4. Do not conflate with other Lusk axeheads

Earlier Lusk research (Batch 200) documented other axehead references:
- NMI 1980:119, listed in the 2012 GDD Lusk Topographical Files summary as an axehead from topsoil in “commons”.
- A separate “Record only” axehead entry associated with foundations of a thatched cottage about 200 yards from the round tower, potentially relevant to Dan Sherry’s newspaper account but not proven to be the same find.

Those are not registration 1999:63. The 1988 County Dublin Urban Archaeology Survey also lists a **flat bronze axehead** from Whitestown, NMI R.1695, found seven feet below ground while digging a ditch. That is a different object and material class.

Source: Bradley & King 1988, Lusk stray finds, p.268.  
https://www.archaeology.ie/app/uploads/2025/05/County_Dublin_Urban_Archaeology_Survey_1988-1.pdf

## 5. Primary-file verification required

Request the original NMI Topographical File for **1999:63**, asking the National Museum / National Monuments Service archive to confirm:
1. the correct townland and county;
2. the original find report and exact wording of the findspot;
3. whether the potato-field discovery description belongs to this registration;
4. finder, landowner, discovery date and circumstances, if recorded;
5. dimensions, weight, stone identification, condition and photographs/drawings;
6. whether the object is held by the NMI and its current collection status;
7. whether a correction, cross-reference or duplicate record exists.

The NMS explains how researchers can access its collections and archive material:  
https://www.archaeology.ie/collections-and-publications/collections/access-and-deposit-of-collections/

## 6. Archive decision

**Research outcome:** NMI registration 1999:63 is described as a polished stone axehead in both reports. Its townland attribution is unresolved: Lusk in Byrne 2021, Causestown in GDD 2012. The detailed form and potato-field discovery account occur in the Causestown entry and must not be transferred to Lusk without primary confirmation.

**Production decision:** Documentation only. Do not create a permanent Lusk object record, map pin, precise coordinates, image asset, confirmed Neolithic date, or custody statement. Do not merge this entry with the separate Lusk axehead records discussed in Batch 200.

**Next research step:** Seek primary-file clarification for NMI 1999:63 and, if possible, 1999:41–44 in the same archive enquiry. Meanwhile, continue with a separate Lusk archaeological record that does not depend on the disputed 1999 registration sequence.
