# Lusk Stone Ball — NMI 1999:41
## Research Batch 205

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentary research; object not independently examined  
**Target:** National Museum of Ireland (NMI) Topographical Files registration 1999:41  
**Scope:** Identify what can be established about the recorded stone ball, test for corroborating published descriptions or images, and define the next evidence step.

## 1. Confirmed catalogue reference

The 2021 *Kilhedge Lane, Lusk* Archaeological Heritage Assessment reproduces a list of stray finds recorded in the NMI Topographical Files. It gives:

| Field | Recorded information |
|---|---|
| NMI registration | 1999:41 |
| Description | Stone Ball |
| Find spot | Lusk |
| Period | Unknown |

The same table separately lists **1999:43, Stone Rubber**, also from Lusk and also with period unknown. These are two distinct registration numbers and must remain separate archive research subjects. The report does not provide a more precise findspot for either object.

**Source:** Martin E. Byrne, *Kilhedge Lane, Lusk, Co. Dublin: Archaeological Heritage Assessment Report* (August 2021), §5.3, printed p.12 / PDF lines 424–463.  
https://consult.fingal.ie/ga/system/files/materials/22195/KILHEDGE%20LANE%20LUSK%20-%20Archaeological%20AIR%20-%20FINAL%20-%2011-08-2021.pdf

## 2. What the evidence does not establish

The accessible published summary supplies no:
- material or geological identification beyond the generic word “stone”;
- diameter, weight, shape details, surface treatment or manufacturing marks;
- precise discovery location, land parcel, depth or archaeological context;
- date or cultural attribution;
- proposed function;
- photograph, drawing or object condition;
- present collection location or custody confirmation.

No independently accessible published source located in this research adds these details or reproduces an image that can securely be matched to registration 1999:41.

Accordingly, the object must not be described as a prehistoric weapon, slingstone, mace head, gaming piece, cannonball, or any other functional type without examination or a source explicitly identifying it. “Stone ball” is the recorded description; “period unknown” is the recorded dating status.

## 3. Topographical Files access and next verification step

The National Monuments Service (NMS) states that its Topographical Files are held in original format but are not listed as digitally available in the catalogued-collections table. The NMS says most catalogued collections can be consulted by researchers by prior appointment and directs enquiries about relevance to **nmarchive@housing.gov.ie**.

Official collection information:  
https://www.archaeology.ie/collections-and-publications/collections/catalogued-collections/

A targeted archive enquiry should request the complete file for **NMI Topographical Files, County Dublin, Lusk, registration 1999:41**, asking specifically for:
1. the original find report or correspondence;
2. exact findspot and circumstances of discovery;
3. any dimensions, weight, material identification or condition notes;
4. photographs, drawings or associated documentation;
5. accession/collection status and current location, if recorded;
6. whether the record has been revised or cross-referenced.

This is the most direct route to move beyond the short secondary summary. Until the file is inspected or the NMS confirms its contents, these details remain unknown.

## 4. Separation from related Lusk finds

Registration **1999:43** is listed as a “Stone Rubber”. It is not an alternative name for 1999:41 and should not be merged with the stone ball. It is reserved as a separate follow-up research batch.

The Lusk stone ball is also not to be conflated with the polished stone axeheads or other stray finds listed in the same 2021 assessment.

## 5. Archive decision

**Research outcome:** A discrete NMI-recorded stray find is confirmed, but only at the catalogue-summary level.

**Production decision:** Documentation only. Do not create a permanent object record, map pin, precise coordinates, date, function, image asset or custody statement from the current evidence. The findspot is townland-level (“Lusk”), not a verified point.

**Recommended next target:** NMI Topographical Files registration **1999:43 — Stone Rubber**, keeping the same strict separation between the two records. A direct NMS archive enquiry covering both records may be efficient, but each object must retain its own evidence trail.
