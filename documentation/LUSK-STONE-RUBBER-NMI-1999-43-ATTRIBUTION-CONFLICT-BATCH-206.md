# Lusk “Stone Rubber” — NMI 1999:43: Attribution Conflict
## Research Batch 206

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentary comparison; object not independently examined  
**Target label:** NMI 1999:43, described as “Stone Rubber” in the 2021 Lusk assessment  
**Key outcome:** Conflicting published townland attribution. Do not treat the Lusk attribution as settled.

## 1. The Lusk reference

Martin E. Byrne’s 2021 *Kilhedge Lane, Lusk* Archaeological Heritage Assessment, §5.3, says the NMI Topographical Files record the following stray finds from the townland of Lusk:

- **1999:41** — Stone Ball — Lusk — period unknown.
- **1999:43** — Stone Rubber — Lusk — period unknown.

This is a clear published statement in a report specifically about Lusk. It gives no dimensions, material specification, discovery circumstances, photograph, precise findspot, function, or current custody for 1999:43.

Source: Byrne, *Kilhedge Lane, Lusk, Co. Dublin: Archaeological Heritage Assessment Report* (August 2021), §5.3, p.12.  
https://consult.fingal.ie/ga/system/files/materials/22195/KILHEDGE%20LANE%20LUSK%20-%20Archaeological%20AIR%20-%20FINAL%20-%2011-08-2021.pdf

## 2. A conflicting NMI Topographical Files summary: Causestown

A separate source, the May 2012 Greater Dublin Drainage Alternative Sites Assessment, Appendix 6.15.3 (“NMI Topographical Files”), records a group under:

- **Museum numbers:** 1999:41–44
- **Townland:** Causestown
- **Finds:** Stone balls, rubbing stone and counter-sunk pebble
- **Find place:** Found after ploughing
- **Description:** Stone balls of approximately oval form; stone rubber of truncated conical form; fragment of a counter-sunk pebble, consisting of over half of the original.

The source groups the four registrations together; it does not map individual objects to each number in the extracted table. It does not explicitly identify which of 1999:41, :42, :43 or :44 is the rubbing stone.

Source: Greater Dublin Drainage, *Alternative Sites Assessment – Phase Two: Sites Assessment and Route Selection Report*, Cultural Heritage Appendix, May 2012, Appendix 6.15.3, printed p.44.  
PDF: https://www.gddapplication.ie/sites/default/files/docs/greater-drainage-dublin/alternative-sites-assessment-phase2/Appendix-6_Cultural-Heritage-Assessment.pdf  
Text appendix: https://www.water.ie/sites/default/files/docs/greater-drainage-dublin/alternative-sites-assessment-phase2/Appendix-6-text.pdf

## 3. Why the two references cannot yet be merged

The two reports give incompatible townland attributions for the same apparent registration-number sequence:

| Source | Registration | Townland | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Byrne 2021 | 1999:43 | Lusk | Stone Rubber |
| GDD 2012 | 1999:41–44 (group) | Causestown | Includes a stone rubber |

The GDD table's grouped range does not identify the individual number belonging to the rubbing stone. It is therefore not possible to prove from that report alone that its rubbing stone is specifically NMI 1999:43. However, the overlap is a significant warning: the two descriptions may reflect a transcription error, a mistaken townland attribution, a reused/aggregated number range in a secondary summary, or another catalogue complication. None of these explanations can be selected without primary-file confirmation.

The 2012 report's more detailed description — truncated-conical form and found after ploughing — must **not** be transferred to the alleged Lusk 1999:43 object unless the NMI file confirms that they are the same record.

## 4. What “stone rubber” means here

The GDD report uses the term “stone rubber” and describes a truncated-conical form, but it does not provide a functional explanation, dimensions, geological identification, use-wear analysis or date. The word should be retained as the source's object label. Do not silently modernise it to “rubber”, “grinding stone”, “quern rubber” or another interpretation.

The Causestown group includes oval stone balls and a counter-sunk pebble. Those are separate descriptions within the same grouped entry, not evidence that the Lusk-labelled stone rubber belongs to that group.

## 5. Primary-source verification required

The next decisive step is a direct check of the National Museum of Ireland Topographical Files. Request the complete records for **1999:41, 1999:42, 1999:43 and 1999:44**, asking the NMI to confirm:
1. the townland and county attached to each individual registration;
2. which number, if any, is the “stone rubber”;
3. whether the Causestown grouped entry is correctly transcribed in the 2012 GDD appendix;
4. whether a separate Lusk 1999:43 record exists;
5. original find circumstances, dimensions, material, photographs/drawings and current custody;
6. whether any correction, cross-reference or duplicate record is held.

National Monuments Service, “Catalogued Collections” (collection access information):  
https://www.archaeology.ie/collections-and-publications/collections/catalogued-collections/

## 6. Archive decision

**Research outcome:** The 2021 Lusk assessment does list a “Stone Rubber” as NMI 1999:43 from Lusk. A 2012 NMI-files appendix independently describes a stone rubber within registrations 1999:41–44 but attributes that grouped find assemblage to Causestown. The identity and townland attribution of the individual 1999:43 object remain unresolved.

**Production decision:** Documentation only. Do not create a permanent Lusk object record, map pin, coordinates, date, functional interpretation, image asset or custody statement. Do not merge the Causestown group with the Lusk stray-find list.

**Next target:** Resolve the NMI 1999:41–44 sequence through the primary Topographical Files before proceeding to another object in that sequence. If direct archive access is not immediately possible, continue with independent Lusk finds whose registration and provenance are not entangled in this conflict.
