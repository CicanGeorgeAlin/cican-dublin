## 2. Physical description — cross-checked sources

The NIAH describes a single-arch humpback road bridge, c.1780, with:
- Carved limestone archivolt and keystones.
- Random-rubble stone parapet walls.
- Wrought-iron railings.
- Ashlar piers.

The 2008 Fingal Historic Bridges Survey gives a more detailed physical description:
- Single arch carrying an internal road within Newbridge Demesne over a small watercourse near the park's south-eastern entrance.
- Coursed rubble limestone construction.
- Ashlar sandstone piers to the parapet, holding wrought-iron railings between stone-built abutment parapets.
- Cut-stone coping.
- Smooth-rendered elevations with moulded limestone impost moulding, archivolt and keystone on both sides.
- Approximate arch span: 4.0 metres.
- The surveyed stream was approximately 4.0 metres wide and 0.4 metres deep at the bridge in 2008.

The survey's technical description is valuable for future close-up photography and for distinguishing the bridge from other estate bridges.

## 3. Chronology — retain the source disagreement

There are two date formulations:

1. **NIAH:** 1760–1800, with a c.1780 description.
2. **Fingal Historic Bridges Survey (2008):** says the bridge probably dates from the construction of Newbridge House in 1737 for Dr Charles Cobbe, later Archbishop of Dublin.

Fingal County Council's Newbridge House heritage page says the original house was built between 1698 and 1705, while the current house was completed in 1752. This differs from the bridge survey's shorthand association with 1737. The bridge survey also notes that the bridge is visible at the site of Mack's Bridge on John Rocque's 1760 map, and is present but unnamed on the 1843 and 1908 Ordnance Survey maps.

**Archive wording:** “Late eighteenth-century bridge, conventionally dated c.1780 by NIAH; a 2008 Fingal survey suggests a possible earlier association with the estate's development. Present evidence does not establish an exact construction year.” Do not present 1737 as a proven bridge construction date.

## 4. Historic condition and ecology — date-stamped evidence only

The Fingal Historic Bridges Survey inspection took place on 29 October 2008. At that time it recorded:
- Structural condition: good.
- Parapets: good, with some light surface rust to iron railings.
- Matrix/mortar: good.
- Arch soffit: good.
- Accessible on all sides, although thick vegetation obstructed the north bank on the west side.
- A semi-mature ash tree rooted beneath the western arch on the upstream side.
- Ivy cover recorded at approximately 35%.
- The stream was described as heavily silted with detritus and woody material; water quality appeared eutrophic to the surveyors.

The report recommended monitoring trees rooted within or adjacent to the structure, as roots could interfere with the bridge. It described the bridge as well maintained and mainly used as a pedestrian bridge in a public park.

**Important:** These are observations from 2008, not a statement of current condition or current access. No present-day structural condition, vegetation status, stream condition or visitor access is asserted in this batch.

## 5. Setting and designation

The 2008 bridge inventory records Mack's Bridge as:
- Within the Newbridge Demesne Architectural Conservation Area.
- Within the curtilage of Recorded Monument DU012-004 (Newbridge House).
- Owned by Fingal County Council at the time of that survey.

The report places the bridge near the south-eastern entrance of the park. Fingal County Council's heritage page describes Newbridge Demesne as an intact eighteenth-century demesne landscape with a Georgian house, designed landscape, farmyard and walled garden. This context is relevant to the bridge's role as estate infrastructure, but should not be used to invent a specific designer or patron for the bridge.

## 6. Coordinate provenance and precision

Two independent published coordinate pairs are nearly identical:
- NIAH: 322039, 249413.
- Fingal Historic Bridges Survey (FHBS09): 322038, 249413.

The one-unit easting difference is consistent with a small difference in published locator precision or point placement; it does not establish survey accuracy. Both sources label these as grid coordinates but the extracted entries do not state the CRS/datum in the cited fields.

**Do not convert these values to WGS 84 or label them GPS / field-verified in this batch.** Preserve the source values and their source attribution until the coordinate reference system is confirmed and a documented transformation is applied. Do not silently substitute the ITM coordinates for the NIAH coordinates.

## 7. Distinction from the nearby c.1800 bridge

Mack's Bridge is NIAH 11336004. It must remain distinct from the separately registered NIAH 11336005:
- 11336004: single-arch humpback bridge, c.1780, carved limestone archivolt and keystones, wrought-iron railings and ashlar piers.
- 11336005: separate single-arch road bridge, c.1800, random-rubble parapets with cut limestone coping, ending in square piers on vermiculated plinths.

The NIAH search results list both registrations separately. The earlier catalogue already associates 11336005 with CICAN ID DUB-INF-0151. Do not duplicate or absorb that record into Mack's Bridge.

NIAH 11336005: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11336005/newbridge-house-newbridge-demesne-donabate-co-dublin

These historic photographs are strong research references, but they are not founder photographs and are not automatically licensed for reuse. No photograph was imported or registered in this batch.

If present access is confirmed, the founder's new photographs should prioritise:
1. Full west/downstream elevation.
2. Full east/upstream elevation.
3. Both parapets and railings from the public path.
4. Close-up of carved archivolt and keystone on each elevation.
5. Moulded impost and arch springing points.
6. Arch soffit, photographed only from a safe lawful viewpoint.
7. Ashlar sandstone piers, cut-stone coping and any visible repair interfaces.
8. Wider view showing the bridge in its estate landscape and the watercourse.
9. Any present vegetation or damage, recorded neutrally and without touching the structure.

Do not climb the parapets, enter the watercourse, remove vegetation or touch historic masonry. Respect park rules and any temporary closures.
