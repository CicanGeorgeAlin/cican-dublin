# CICAN DUBLIN — Master Project Blueprint

**Founder:** CICAN GEORGE ALIN  
**Project type:** Evidence-first discovery, investigation, fieldwork and founder-photographed visual platform  
**Geographic scope:** Dublin City and County Dublin  
**Working principle:** Discover every detail. Verify every claim. Locate responsibly. Photograph in person. Preserve faithfully.

## 1. Mission

Build the most comprehensive evidence-led discovery and visual record possible of Dublin and County Dublin's historically meaningful physical details, from famous landmarks to overlooked micro-details. The archive must include major landmarks and overlooked details: buildings, monuments, memorials, inscriptions, street furniture, lamps, gates, bridges, paving patterns, cobblestones, boundary markers, shopfronts, signs, architectural details, industrial remains, archaeological features and other documentable objects.

CICAN DUBLIN has three connected purposes:

1. **Historical discovery and investigation:** identify overlooked details, inspect sources, verify claims and preserve uncertainty.
2. **Fieldwork application:** locate field-ready discoveries and guide the founder's visits and original photography.
3. **Public visual collection:** publish the founder's photographs and faithful derivative A4 line art, then develop separate colouring-book publications.

This is not a list of famous attractions. It is a systematic attempt to document Dublin's visible physical record, including small details that ordinary heritage guides overlook.

## 2. Geographic strategy

CICAN DUBLIN is designed for all of Dublin from the beginning. Research and fieldwork will proceed in manageable geographic phases, beginning with Dublin 1 and then expanding district by district.

Postal districts are useful working zones, but they are not a complete administrative map of County Dublin. The long-term geographic model must also accommodate neighbourhoods, townlands, local authority areas, estates, parks, transport corridors, coastline and rural locations.

No area is to be labelled complete merely because its major landmarks have been listed. Each zone needs explicit coverage tracking and a record of what has and has not been surveyed.

## 3. Discovery model

Every candidate is recorded at the correct physical level:

- **Site:** a large place or complex, such as a square, cemetery, park, bridge or institutional campus.
- **Primary object:** a building, monument, memorial, structure or identifiable historic feature.
- **Micro-detail:** a separately identifiable element such as a lamp standard, plaque, carved date, gate hinge, bollard, paving feature or maker's mark.
- **Surface or sequence:** a larger area containing many repeated elements, such as a cobbled street or patterned pavement. Record the parent area first; add individual child records only when a feature can be identified and located reliably.

Use parent-child relationships to avoid both duplicate records and misleading claims of completeness.

## 4. Evidence and coordinate rules

- Every historical or attribution claim must be linked to a source.
- Prefer official registers, public authorities, archival catalogues, conservation records, primary documents and reputable specialist research.
- Clearly separate RESEARCH_LEAD, PARTIALLY_VERIFIED and VERIFIED records.
- A source record does not prove that the founder has visited or photographed an object.
- Never invent latitude, longitude, Irish Grid conversion, address, inscription, date, attribution, image or access permission.
- Preserve Irish Grid references separately from decimal GPS coordinates.
- Store coordinate provenance and accuracy. Coordinates may be marked approximate only when the source and uncertainty are stated; field-verified coordinates must be distinguished from address geocoding.
- A map pin is a navigation aid, not proof of the exact position of a small object.

## 5. Founder photography and image integrity

Only photographs taken by the founder during field visits qualify as archive originals. Do not use stock photography, borrowed photographs or generated images as substitutes for the founder's documentation.

For each visit, capture as appropriate:
- a contextual view showing where the object sits;
- a complete view of the object;
- close-ups of inscriptions, construction, materials and distinctive details;
- multiple angles where access and safety allow.

Preserve the original file separately and do not overwrite or silently retouch it. Create line art as a separate derivative. The line art must faithfully represent only what is visible in the founder's photograph; it must not invent missing features or reconstruct unseen sides.

Do not publish a founder photograph until it has been supplied and its archive association confirmed.

## 6. Field mission system

Each field-ready record should provide:
- a clear mission title and permanent archive ID;
- verified address and GPS status;
- map/navigation link;
- access and photography notes;
- a concise shot checklist;
- parent site or nearby mission links;
- current research, visit, image and illustration status.

The experience may feel like a real-world exploration game, but its progress indicators must reflect actual work. No points, badges or completion states may imply that a visit or photograph happened when it did not.

## 7. Public website principles

The website should be image-first, minimal and practical. Its main job is to help a person discover a feature, understand where it is, and plan a field visit.

Core experience:
1. **Explore:** map and geographic browsing.
2. **Missions:** nearby or grouped field assignments, with navigation and shot lists.
3. **Archive:** visual catalogue with search and category filters.
4. **Object detail:** photographs first; concise facts, location, source evidence and fieldwork checklist on opening.
5. **Progress:** transparent coverage and production states.

Visual direction: black background, bright white typography, restrained interface, clear image presentation and a consistent CICAN identity. Avoid text-heavy landing pages. Empty image states must be clearly labelled as awaiting the founder's photograph.

## 8. Record lifecycle

DISCOVERED → RESEARCHED → VERIFIED (or PARTIALLY_VERIFIED) → GPS_LOCATED → FIELD_READY → VISITED → ORIGINAL_PHOTO_ARCHIVED → LINE_ART_CREATED → QUALITY_CHECKED → PUBLISHED

These stages are separate. A record may be research-verified while still lacking exact GPS. A visit does not count as photographed until the original file is archived. A line-art file does not count as approved until it passes comparison against the original.

## 9. Ten-stage delivery roadmap

1. **Project foundation:** lock mission, scope, evidence rules, geography, image integrity and workflow. *(This document.)*
2. **Data architecture:** audit and standardise object, site, micro-detail, source, coordinate, image and status fields.
3. **Discovery taxonomy:** create a complete category/subcategory system that captures both major heritage and everyday physical details.
4. **Geographic coverage system:** define Dublin-wide zones, district boundaries, coverage states and a phased research order.
5. **Research inventory:** systematically discover candidates from authoritative sources; verify, deduplicate and register them with source trails.
6. **Coordinate and navigation layer:** resolve locations responsibly, preserve coordinate provenance and generate field-ready map links.
7. **Field mission planner:** group records into practical visits and routes with access notes and shot checklists.
8. **Website redesign:** build the map-led, image-first discovery and fieldwork interface on top of the standardised data.
9. **Founder image pipeline:** establish upload, original-file preservation, naming, metadata, line-art conversion and quality-control procedures.
10. **Publication and expansion:** prepare approved A4 pages and themed books, publish only verified outputs, then repeat the research and fieldwork cycle across Dublin.

Each stage must have a defined deliverable and a short acceptance test before moving on. Do not redesign the same component repeatedly once it passes its test.

## 10. Initial baseline

The repository currently contains a 15-record starter catalogue, 15 source entries, 22 postal-district working zones and nine broad categories. These are useful starting assets, not a complete Dublin inventory. The current records are not evidence that the areas have been comprehensively surveyed. Existing fieldwork and record-schema documentation remain in force unless superseded by a later, explicit version.

## 11. Definition of success

The archive succeeds when a user can discover a documented physical feature, understand what is known and how it is evidenced, navigate to its location, follow a safe and useful photography brief, and later see the founder's original photograph alongside faithful line art.

**Core rule:** No invented evidence. No substitute imagery. No false completion. Every record must help the archive become more accurate and more useful.
