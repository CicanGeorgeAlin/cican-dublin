## 1. Lusk — St MacCullin's historic church complex

**Existing parent record:** DUB-BLD-0177 — Saint MacCullin's Church, Lusk (Batch 146). Do not create another parent building.

The 1988 *County Dublin Urban Archaeology Survey*, commissioned by the Office of Public Works, identifies several distinct monuments and architectural details within the church complex. These are candidates for parent-linked micro-detail records, subject to full master-catalogue duplicate checks:

1. **Walter Dermot tomb, 1535x8** — rectangular limestone slab with a Holy Trinity relief and marginal inscription. Survey pp. 264–265.
2. **Barnewall and Sharl table tomb, dated 1575 in the survey's monument heading** — Renaissance table tomb with knight and lady effigies, heraldic end panels and a long commemorative inscription. Fingal County Council's visitor page identifies the 16th-century effigy tomb of Sir Christopher Barnewall and Marion Sharle. Survey pp. 265–266.
3. **James Bermingham memorial, 1637** — limestone slab with relief effigy of an armoured knight and heraldic details. Survey p. 267.
4. **Seventeenth-century chimney-breast plaque** — limestone plaque with six shields surrounding an Annunciation and an inscription beginning “MAREARET SAINTE LAWRENS MADE THIS WORK DOND”. Survey pp. 264–265. The exact present position and accessibility need confirmation.
5. **Octagonal limestone font** — described in the survey as possibly seventeenth century, with trefoil-headed chamfered panels. Survey p. 264.
6. **Bullaun stone** — pink granite, recorded beside the south entrance. Survey p. 264. Confirm whether it remains in situ and whether it is the same stone associated in historical writing with St MacCuillin's Well; do not conflate the two without evidence.
7. **Small piscina in the west entrance porch** — the survey says it may have been salvaged from the medieval church. Its origin is therefore provisional, not certain.
8. **Carved stone head at the south-east corner of the tower** — recorded high on the exterior. Survey p. 263.

Fingal County Council describes Lusk Heritage Centre as housed in the historic St Macculin's Church complex and identifies the Barnewall/Sharle effigy tomb. The council visitor information gives the site as a public heritage destination, but opening arrangements and photography permission should be checked before a visit.

## 2. Swords — St Columba's ecclesiastical complex

The Fingal Development Plan's Record of Protected Structures and Architectural Conservation Areas identifies the Church Road Historic Ecclesiastical Core (DF-ACA-33). It includes the round tower, medieval square/belfry tower, early nineteenth-century church and surrounding historically associated buildings.

A 2024 archaeological monitoring and recording report by Trim Archaeology Projects Ltd, published by Fingal County Council, records previously unrecorded internal architectural evidence from conservation works:

- Round tower: a well-made stone-and-mortar floor was exposed in the basement, 1.4m below entrance level.
- Round tower: original or historic beam holes on floors 3–6 helped interpret the internal floor layout.
- Round tower: a ring of corbelled stones supports the interpretation of former floors; some corbels are damaged or truncated.
- Belfry / medieval square tower: probable original joist sockets, projecting stone corbels, putlog holes and possible evidence of an infilled window were recorded.
- The 2024 conservation works were completed, including round-tower repointing and reinstatement of belfry floors.

These are **architectural features within existing tower records**, not automatically separate visitor-accessible objects. The report documents interior features through conservation scaffolding and specialist access. Do not plan public photography or entry into restricted interiors without explicit permission.

The 1988 OPW survey also illustrates an early Christian cross on top of the Swords round tower and identifies tomb monuments including James Blakeney et al. (1587) and Christopher Hewetson (1694). The illustration is historical evidence only; the present position and survival of the cross and tomb features must be checked against current official records and on-site conditions before creating new entries.

## 3. Evidence and uncertainty controls

- The Lusk church complex is already represented by DUB-BLD-0177; all proposed details must be linked to that parent if promoted.
- The Lusk survey uses differing historical dating conventions. Preserve the source's wording (for example, “1535x8” and “1575”) rather than silently normalising dates.
- The Barnewall/Sharl monument is described as 1575 in the OPW survey, while the Fingal visitor page calls it a sixteenth-century tomb. Both support a sixteenth-century attribution; do not claim a more exact date without further evidence.
- The piscina is only possibly reused from the medieval church.
- The bullaun stone's relationship to the historic well is not established by the survey.
- The Swords 2024 report describes internal construction evidence, not unrestricted visitor access.
- The historic 1988 illustration of the cross does not prove that it remains in the same position today.
- No founder field observations or photographs were supplied. No feature is marked as currently surviving or field-verified by this research note.
