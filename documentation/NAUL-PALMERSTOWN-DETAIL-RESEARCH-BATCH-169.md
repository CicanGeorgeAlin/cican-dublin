# CICAN Dublin Archive — The Naul & Palmerstown (Batch 169)

**Status:** Evidence-led research report; saved as documentation only. No permanent CICAN object IDs, production catalogue records or map pins created.  
**Scope:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project FHG 40 The Naul and FHG 41 Palmerstown (Oldtown area).  
**Critical geographic distinction:** This FHG 41 is rural Palmerstown near Oldtown in north-west Fingal. It is not Palmerstown Lower beside Mill Lane in South Dublin. Do not merge these separate places or their RMP/NIAH records.

## 1. The Naul — FHG 40

### Identity and location
- FHG 40; survey dated 24 July 2008.
- Address: The Naul, County Dublin, Ireland; northeast of Naul village in tillage fields.
- FHG source coordinates: X 313340, Y 261021. The survey table does not state the coordinate reference system; retain the raw values and do not label them ITM until confirmed.
- Current Buried in Fingal locator: 53.58693589, -6.28906096; public entry calls it Naul Cemetery.
- RMP DU004-04505 (graveyard), within the wider DU004-045 village complex; church DU004-04504; tower house DU004-04502; holy well DU004-04503; date stone DU004-04506; watermill site DU004-04501; bridge site DU004-04508.
- RPS 104; NIAH 11308004 (graveyard).
- Ownership: Fingal County Council according to FHG 2008 and Buried in Fingal.
- The graveyard surrounds the ruined 1710 chantry chapel and is close to the ruins of the so-called Black Castle / Norman tower house. Keep the graveyard, chapel, castle, holy well and other village components as distinct archive entities.

### Historical and architectural sequence
- The original parish church was reportedly burnt and desecrated in the mid-16th century. The Civil Survey of 1654–56 described it as ruinous, with only the walls standing.
- The surviving building was erected in 1710 as a chantry chapel for the Hussey family, using architectural elements from an earlier church.
- FHG 2008 records three standing walls and the footings of the fourth. The church contains a later-medieval ogee-headed window and punched dressed stones reused in window and door surrounds.
- The wall plaque above the doorway records the erection of the chapel and monument in 1710.
- A 19th-century cross is associated with the chapel and is included in the protected-structure record; distinguish it from the medieval/reused fabric.
- The graveyard is sub-rectangular, bounded by squared rubble limestone wall. The raised and gently undulating ground contains burial markers around all sides of the chapel. The west-wall concrete steps replaced the former southeast entrance.
- The wider village is an Area of Archaeological Potential. The Naul Community Heritage Audit lists the village complex and its individual RMP components. The 2019 community dig in the field north of the graveyard found evidence of medieval cultivation and dumping of building material, not evidence that the field itself is a burial plot.

### High-priority memorials and architectural details
1. **Hussey 1710 dedication plaque** — Diamond-shaped mural tablet above the chapel doorway, with roll moulding and a carved crest. FHG transcription: “This chapel and monument were erected by Hon. Edward Hussey of Westowne and His Lady Madam Mable Hussey alias Barnewall for their prosperity in the year of Our Lord God 1710”. The Heritage Audit transcribes a closely related form using “Honourable Edward Hussed” and “Mable Hussed als Barnwell”. Preserve source-specific transcriptions; photograph the entire plaque and letterforms before choosing a normalized reading.
2. **Hussey family underground vault** — Buried in Fingal states that the chapel contains an underground Hussey family vault and that Anthony Strong Hussey is said to have been the last buried there. Treat the burial detail and the claimed last interment as a cemetery-history source statement; do not assume the vault can be accessed or that its internal layout is known.
3. **Dickson headstone (1717)** — Buried in Fingal identifies a stone dated 1717 as the earliest recorded by Dr Egan. The 2021 Naul Heritage Audit describes the wider cemetery as containing cut-stone markers from c.1680 to the present. These are not necessarily contradictory: Egan's “earliest recorded” is a transcription corpus claim, whereas the audit's c.1680 is a broad visual/date assessment. Photograph and independently transcribe the Dickson stone; investigate any earlier-dated stones.
4. **Daniel Macken headstone** — FHG describes a well-carved early-19th-century stone with Crucifixion plaque, winged angels, IHS motif, skull and crossbones above the inscription, and incised marginal decoration. High-priority close-up object.
5. **Blacksmith-tools headstone** — At the east end of the church, with tools of a blacksmith carved at the top. FHG does not provide the name in the quoted description. Record as an unidentified memorial pending field transcription.
6. **Seamus Ennis family memorial** — Late-19th-century family headstone west of the church. FHG transcription records James Ennis (d. April 1919), wife Mary née Macken (d. November 1890), daughter Angela (d. June 1904), son Michael (d. May 1964, aged 94), Jack Ennis (1888–1965), James A. Ennis (1885–1964), Marie Ennis née McCabe (1890–1977), Séamus Ennis (1919–1982) and Gertrude Clement née Ennis (1883–1983). Buried in Fingal independently identifies traditional musician Séamus Ennis as buried here. Preserve exact source transcription and verify punctuation/date formats on site.
7. **18th–21st-century markers** — FHG notes headstones and free-standing crosses around all four sides of the chapel, with individual table tombs and undecorated stones; many plots have low concrete kerbs.
8. **Reused medieval architectural details** — Ogee-headed window and punched dressed stones in the 1710 chapel. These are architectural fabric, not memorials; record their current position and likely reused context separately from the chapel building.
9. **Nearby Black Castle** — A separate late-medieval tower house / castle site near the graveyard. The Naul Heritage Audit describes it as a probable 15th-century tower house, despite the “Norman Castle” wording in some public cemetery descriptions. Use “Black Castle / tower house” as a name and keep date interpretation qualified.

### Condition and conservation leads
- FHG 2008 rated the graveyard good, well kept and clear of vegetation; it noted root damage to graves, a badly leaning table tomb north of the church and cracks in the Hussey memorial.
- FHG recommended investigation of leaning markers, recording and retention of loose architectural fragments, cutting back sycamore growth where damaging, and lime-mortar pointing of cracks in the Hussey memorial.
- Reassess current condition; do not describe 2008 condition as present-day condition.
- The graveyard extension is still in use according to FHG 2008; the historic chapel enclosure and modern extension should be mapped as separate zones only after field boundary verification.
- Fingal's 2019 community dig in the field north of the graveyard documented medieval cultivation and building-material dumping. The field is not automatically an extension of the burial ground.
- The Naul Community Heritage Audit places the chapel and graveyard in the village's archaeological and protected-structure context; it identifies the church and graveyard as separate RMP components.

### Fieldwork priorities
- Photograph the 1710 Hussey plaque in full and at high resolution; use raking light to reveal the worn lettering and crest.
- Locate and document the 1717 Dickson stone; survey all stones that could pre-date it, without cleaning or moving them.
- Record Daniel Macken's complete inscription and the iconography in separate detail frames.
- Identify the blacksmith-tools memorial and transcribe it exactly.
- Record the Seamus Ennis family stone, with individual named persons treated as inscription subjects, not separate physical memorial objects unless independent markers are located.
- Photograph reused ogee window and punched stones; document the chapel wall sequence and former southeast entrance.
- Do not enter the Hussey vault or attempt any ground disturbance. Confirm permission and safe access before field photography.

## 2. Palmerstown (Oldtown) — FHG 41

### Identity and location
- FHG 41; survey dated 4 August 2008.
- Address: Palmerstown, Oldtown, County Dublin, Ireland.
- This is the Palmerstown cemetery about 4 km southwest of Oldtown in north-west Fingal, located in a corner of a large field off Robertstown Road and surrounded by a circular wall.
- FHG source coordinates: X 309266, Y 252513. Coordinate reference system is not specified in the survey; preserve the raw pair and do not assume ITM.
- Current Buried in Fingal locator: 53.51156235, -6.35366491.
- RMP DU007-01303 (graveyard); DU007-01301 (church); DU007-01302 (holy well).
- RPS 135; no NIAH reference in the FHG survey.
- Fingal County Council owns/manages the cemetery and its records.
- Do not confuse this site with Palmerstown Lower, Mill Lane, South Dublin, whose NIAH graveyard record 11203013 and RMP DU017-026002 belong to a completely different site.

### Church and landscape
- The circular walled graveyard contains the foundations/remains of a medieval church dedicated to St James, which was a possession of the Priory of St John the Baptist, Dublin.
- FHG describes the church foundations as surviving at the east and west gables, with north and south walls to approximately 0.5m high; coursed masonry and loose stone remain around the walls.
- The present boundary wall does not appear to be early, according to Walsh (1888), despite its circular form.
- The road and field boundaries curve around the south side of the site and may preserve the line of an earlier ecclesiastical enclosure. This is an interpretation, not a confirmed enclosure.
- The ground within the graveyard is considerably higher than the surrounding land.
- A holy well known as St James's Well is recorded in the field to the south of the graveyard. It is separately designated DU007-01302. Do not merge the well with the graveyard or assume the well is currently visible.
- Buried in Fingal describes the site as 4 km southwest of Oldtown, in a field corner off Robertstown Road, surrounded by a circular wall, with church foundations inside.

### High-priority memorials
1. **Peter Duff / Catherine Duff headstone (1774)** — FHG describes this as one of the earliest stones. Inscription: “IHS This stone and burial place belongeth to Peter Duff and his posterity. Here lieth the body of Catherine Duff who departed this life May ye 17th in the 56th year of her age, 1774”. Record the stone's physical identity and the inscription as a separate transcription. Confirm whether 1774 refers to Catherine's death and whether the year is part of the original inscription.
2. **Celtic Revival headstone** — Shown in FHG Plate 41.2 as a tall Celtic Revival headstone with cross-shaped top. Photograph its full form, base, inscription and any maker's mark; do not assign a person without reading the inscription.
3. **18th- and 19th-century markers** — FHG notes a smaller group of 18th- and 19th-century stones among predominantly 20th-century headstones, plus undecorated stone markers west of the church.
4. **Interior burials** — FHG reports burials inside the church remains. Treat this as a burial context, not as a visible monument or individually locatable grave unless a marker or archaeological record establishes it.
5. **Cracked and broken crosses** — FHG 2008 identifies several cracked and broken crosses. Their present condition and exact positions require a new survey.
6. **Split headstone at foot of west wall** — Specifically recorded by FHG; check whether it remains in place and photograph its fracture and inscription without moving it.
7. **Loose stone around church foundations** — Retain as archaeological fabric; do not reassemble or assign fragments to a wall without specialist evidence.

### Condition and conservation leads
- FHG 2008 rated the graveyard good and well maintained, but recorded cracked/broken crosses, loose stone around the church, surface lamination on many older stones and loss of inscription legibility, plus a split headstone at the west wall.
- FHG recommended recording and retaining loose cut-stone fragments, investigating the church walls and carrying out remedial works where required.
- FHG recommended aerial-photographic and geophysical analysis immediately around the graveyard to investigate possible additional enclosures or related archaeological features.
- Buried in Fingal confirms Fingal County Council responsibility for the graveyard and records.
- Recheck current access, vegetation, wall stability and memorial condition before any visit. Do not clean, straighten, move or repair stones.

### Fieldwork priorities
- Locate Peter Duff/Catherine Duff 1774 stone and make a full transcription plus iconographic details.
- Photograph the Celtic Revival cross-shaped headstone in FHG Plate 41.2 and match it to the current marker.
- Conduct a systematic photographic walkover of the predominantly 20th-century markers and earlier stones, with special attention to the lamination and loss of inscriptions.
- Photograph the split headstone and cracked/broken crosses with scales and contextual frames.
- Record the church foundations, loose masonry and raised ground level without moving any stone.
- Locate St James's Well in the southern field only with permission and after verifying the source location; record it as a separate object/site from the graveyard.
- Investigate the southern boundary curve through historic mapping and, if appropriate, licensed geophysical survey; do not call it a confirmed early enclosure.

## 3. Duplicate prevention and interpretation controls
- The Naul graveyard is DU004-04505; its 1710 Hussey chantry chapel is DU004-04504; the Black Castle is DU004-04502; the nearby Lady Well is DU004-04503. Keep these separate.
- Palmerstown (Oldtown) FHG 41 is DU007-01303. Its church is DU007-01301 and St James's Well is DU007-01302.
- Palmerstown Lower in South Dublin is not FHG 41; it has a different church, cemetery, NIAH entry and RMP reference. Never merge.
- The Naul 1717 Dickson stone is “earliest recorded by Dr Egan” according to Buried in Fingal; the heritage audit's broad “c.1680 to present” marker date range does not identify a specific c.1680 stone. Keep both claims qualified.
- No permanent CICAN archive IDs, catalogue objects or production map pins created in this research batch. Complete master catalogue access, duplicate search and schema validation remain prerequisites.
