# CICAN DUBLIN — NIAH and Specialist Research Batch 004

**Date:** 20 November 2026  
**Autopilot:** CICAN HERITAGE AUTOPILOT  
**Research cycle:** Four NIAH records + one specialist archaeological source  
**Scope:** Neillstown / Clondalkin / Cloverhill Road corridor, South Dublin  
**Status:** Research-only. No catalogue objects, source-registry entries, visual records, map pins or website code changed.

## Executive summary

Four individual official NIAH records were checked: Coolevin House (11205035), Neilstown Lodge (11205036), the former Cloverhill Road railway station building (11205037), and the single-arch railway bridge (11205038). The four records form a useful small heritage corridor and should remain separate registered structures. A specialist National Monuments Service archaeological survey was also reviewed for Clondalkin's early Christian precinct.

South Dublin County Council's 2025 environmental report for Proposed Variation No. 1 to the 2022–2028 Development Plan identifies both Coolevin House and Neilstown Lodge among notable protected structures in the Clondalkin planning area. The Council's 2016–2022 Record of Protected Structures schedule separately lists Neilstown Lodge and Coolevin House at Neilstown Road / Lucan-Newlands Road. These council sources strongly support the South Dublin / Clondalkin context for those two properties. The Cloverhill station and bridge are described by NIAH as being near Raheen / Clondalkin; their local-authority assignment remains provisional pending an official point-in-polygon check.

## 1. Four NIAH records checked

### 1. Coolevin House — NIAH 11205035

- Official record: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11205035/coolevin-house-lucan-newlands-road-neillstown-dublin
- NIAH rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural, Technical.
- Date range: 1880–1890; described as c.1885.
- Published ITM: E 306690, N 232684.
- Description: detached, multiple-bay, single-storey house on a U-shaped plan, with diagonally set corner projections and a central projecting gabled glazed porch. The survey notes rendered ruled-and-lined walls, timber sash windows, slate roof, perforated cresting and finials.
- Council corroboration: SDCC's 2025 SEA report names Coolevin House as a notable protected structure in the Clondalkin planning area. The Council's 2016–2022 RPS schedule lists Coolevin House at Lucan-Newlands Road, Clondalkin.
- Caution: the NIAH point is a property locator, not founder GPS. The NIAH date is an approximate range, not an exact construction year.

### 2. Neilstown Lodge — NIAH 11205036

- Official record: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11205036/neilstown-lodge-neilstown-road-neillstown-co-dublin
- NIAH rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural.
- Date range: 1810–1830; described as c.1820.
- Original use: Gate lodge; current NIAH use field: House.
- Published ITM: E 306537, N 232812.
- Description: detached single-storey former gate lodge. The survey describes two fixed-light timber windows in lancet openings in the pebble-dashed southern wall, forming a flanking screen wall to the gateway; a single-bay lodge with roughcast render, brick door surround and slate roof with decorative bands; and later additions.
- Council corroboration: SDCC's 2025 SEA report names Neilstown Lodge as a notable protected structure in the Clondalkin planning area. The Council's 2016–2022 RPS schedule separately identifies Neilstown Lodge, Neilstown Road, Clondalkin.
- Detail worth photographing: the lancet-window screen-wall composition and its relationship to the former entrance. This is a photographic priority only after safe, lawful public-viewpoint assessment; the building is now in residential use.

### 3. Former railway station building, Cloverhill Road — NIAH 11205037

- Official record: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11205037/cloverhill-road-raheen-ne-by-co-dublin
- NIAH rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural, Technical.
- Date range: 1840–1860; described as c.1850.
- Original use: Railway station; current NIAH use field: House.
- Published ITM: E 306928, N 232590.
- Description: detached three-bay, single-storey with attic former station building on an L-plan. The survey describes ruled-and-lined render, chamfered reveals and drip mouldings, timber casements, four-centred arched doorways, a canted bay window, raised gables and a central brick chimney. It notes platform-access doorways in the brick wall on each side.
- Caution: the building is now a private house. No entry, platform access or photography from railway property is assumed. Exact local-authority assignment remains pending spatial verification.

### 4. Single-arch railway bridge, Cloverhill Road — NIAH 11205038

- Official record: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11205038/cloverhill-road-raheen-ne-by-clondalkin-co-dublin
- NIAH rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural, Technical.
- Date range: 1850–1870; described as c.1860.
- Original / current use: Bridge / Bridge.
- Published ITM: E 306993, N 232581.
- Description: single-arch road bridge over the railway, with a three-centred arch and dressed voussoir stones. The NIAH describes coursed squared limestone parapets raised with coursed granite rubble, and limestone rubble retaining walls to either side.
- Relationship: NIAH's appraisal says it complements the adjacent station building. Preserve the two NIAH registrations as separate structures; do not merge them into one record.
- Caution: inspect and photograph only from safe public road/pavement locations, without entering railway land or standing in the carriageway. Exact local-authority assignment remains pending spatial verification.

## 2. Specialist archaeological source — Clondalkin early Christian precinct

**Source:** John Bradley and Heather A. King, *County Dublin Urban Archaeology Survey*, Part VIII(iv), report commissioned by the Office of Public Works, released 1988. Official NMS-hosted PDF:
https://www.archaeology.ie/app/uploads/2025/05/County_Dublin_Urban_Archaeology_Survey_1988-1.pdf

The Clondalkin section records:
- The medieval parish church's historical sequence and the 1787 gunpowder-mill explosion, after which the old church collapsed.
- A surviving fragment of old-church masonry southeast of the modern church, as described in the 1988 survey.
- The round tower's conical cap, bulging base, steps to the doorway, calp-limestone masonry with some granite, lintelled granite doorway and upper-storey windows.
- Two granite crosses and a granite font in the churchyard, which the survey describes as apparently Early Christian in date.
- Clondalkin's long-lived early Christian monastic and later Anglo-Norman borough context.

**Archive decision:** This is a valuable specialist evidence source for the existing Clondalkin monastic-precinct research, not authority to create three new object records automatically. The crosses and font require individual current Historic Environment Viewer / SMR record checks, present-location confirmation and full-catalogue identity reconciliation. The 1988 description does not prove current survival, exact position or public access. No micro-object coordinates or map pins are inferred.
