### 11201008 — Gate piers and boundary wall

The official page describes a pair of ashlar limestone gate piers with capping stones, c.1765, a replacement tubular metal gate and a random-rubble limestone wall to the north. Its appraisal says the piers were probably a formal entrance to former estate land west of the lane.

### 11201009 — Former farm outbuilding

The official page describes a former detached farm outbuilding, c.1775, partly converted for domestic use and extended south c.1930. It records coursed rubble limestone, three slit openings, a later window, slate roofs, a chimney, a northern rubble-stone outbuilding and a northern wall with capped gate piers. The appraisal identifies a significant survival of farm-estate vernacular architecture.

**Relationship caution:** 11201008 separately records gate piers and a north wall in the same Cooldrinagh Lane area. 11201009 also mentions capped gate piers in its northern wall. The exact physical overlap or relationship has not been established. Both official registration records are retained, but the archive does not claim that the piers are two separate physical features. Founder field review must resolve the relationship before any component-level visual counting.

### 11201013 — Former walled garden

The official page describes an enclosed former walled garden, c.1765, with limestone rubble boundary walls, a west entrance with replacement iron gates, and remains of a two-storey house and lean-to outbuildings. Its appraisal identifies it as an intact garden and an important element of the former Cooldrinagh demesne.

### 11201015 — Former stables and coach-house range

The official page describes a group of former domestic outbuildings, including a five-bay two-storey stable block c.1850, flanking single-storey buildings including a coach house, limestone rubble walls, carriage arches, replacement casements and later roof materials. Its appraisal identifies the range as a valuable estate-building group that largely retains its original form.

### 11201016 — T & T cast-iron water pump

The official page describes a freestanding cast-iron water pump, c.1880, with a mass-concrete boundary wall to three sides and a 'T & T' logo. Its appraisal identifies it as increasingly rare suburban street furniture and a remnant of local social history.

## 4. Duplicate, access and image-rights controls

- The five exact registration numbers, new CICAN IDs, public NIAH references, source IDs and visual record IDs were checked against the full pre-batch files. No exact collisions were found.
- All five records are marked PARTIALLY_VERIFIED. This means their official register identities and source fields were checked; it does not establish current survival, condition, precise on-site position or field verification.
- Access remains NOT ASSESSED. Buildings, estate structures and the walled garden may be private. Do not enter private property without permission. The pump should only be photographed from a lawful public vantage point once its location and public boundary are confirmed.
- No photograph from the NIAH pages or CSV IMAGE_LINK field was reused. The NIAH dataset's CC BY 4.0 licence applies to record data; linked photographs are not assumed to share that licence.
- Five visual inventory entries were added. Founder photographs, A4 line art and public galleries remain outstanding.
