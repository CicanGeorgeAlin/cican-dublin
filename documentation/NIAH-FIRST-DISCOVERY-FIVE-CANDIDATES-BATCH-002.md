## 1. Five candidate records

### Candidate 1 — Lucan House cemetery and medieval remains

- **NIAH registration:** 11201140
- **Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11201140/lucan-house-lucan-demesne-lucan-dublin
- **Locality:** Lucan Demesne, Lucan, County Dublin
- **Provisional local authority:** South Dublin County Council
- **NIAH rating:** Regional
- **Special-interest categories:** Archaeological, Architectural, Artistic, Historical, Social
- **NIAH date range:** 1700–1740
- **Original / current use:** Graveyard/cemetery
- **Official ITM coordinate:** E 303172, N 235187
- **Date recorded:** 24 April 2002

The NIAH describes an enclosed cemetery containing the unroofed remains of a church rebuilt around 1730, ruins of a medieval tower house, and carved headstones dating from the early eighteenth century. Its appraisal identifies the group as including the original Lucan Estate tower house and headstones associated with the Vesey and Sarsfield families.

**Archive caution:** this is a composite heritage group, not a single isolated gravestone or a newly discovered medieval building. Any later catalogue record should preserve the ensemble nature and separately document individual components only where there is defensible evidence.

### Candidate 2 — Coolevin House

- **NIAH registration:** 11205035
- **Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11205035/coolevin-house-lucan-newlands-road-neillstown-dublin
- **Locality:** Lucan-Newlands Road, Neillstown, County Dublin
- **Provisional local authority:** South Dublin County Council
- **NIAH rating:** Regional
- **Special-interest categories:** Architectural, Technical
- **NIAH date range:** 1880–1890
- **Original / current use:** House / House
- **Official ITM coordinate:** E 306690, N 232684
- **Date recorded:** 24 May 2002

The official description identifies a detached, multiple-bay, single-storey house dating to around 1885, on a U-shaped plan. It has diagonally set corner projections, a central projecting gabled glazed porch, rendered ruled-and-lined walls, timber sash windows and a pitched slate roof with perforated cresting and finials. The appraisal calls attention to its unusual design, original proportions and materials, and its south-facing setting across substantial grounds.

**Archive caution:** the NIAH date is an approximate date range; do not turn it into a precise construction year. The address and ITM location should be checked on the ground before any field-verified status is assigned.

### Candidate 3 — Rathfarnham weir on the River Dodder

- **NIAH registration:** 11211029
- **Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11211029/rathfarnham-rathfarnham-dublin
- **Locality:** Rathfarnham, Dublin
- **Provisional local authority:** Dublin City Council
- **NIAH rating:** Regional
- **Special-interest categories:** Architectural, Technical
- **NIAH date range:** 1780–1820
- **Original / current use:** Weir / Weir
- **Official ITM coordinate:** E 314761, N 229659
- **Date recorded:** 30 May 2002

The NIAH describes a two-stage weir in the River Dodder, around 1800, originally supplying water to a mill stream. The first tier is described as cobbled slabs, cobbles and cement, with a curving profile about 10 metres long and a drop of about 3 metres. The second tier is about 5 metres long with a vertical drop of about 2 metres. The overall width is given as about 25 metres, leading to a millstream, sluice and overflow on the north bank; substantial revetment walls are noted downstream.

**Archive caution:** this is an industrial-water-management structure. Do not confuse the NIAH description with a claim that a mill building survives at the weir. The official description's dimensions are recorded as approximate.

### Candidate 4 — Saint Matthias' Church, Killiney-Ballybrack

- **NIAH registration:** 60260255
- **Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/60260255/saint-matthias-church-killiney-ballybrack-church-road-loughlinstown-dublin
- **Locality:** Church Road, Loughlinstown, County Dublin
- **Provisional local authority:** Dún Laoghaire-Rathdown County Council
- **NIAH rating:** Regional
- **Special-interest categories:** Architectural, Artistic, Historical, Scientific, Social, Technical
- **NIAH date range:** 1830–1840
- **Original / current use:** Church/chapel / Church/chapel
- **Official ITM coordinate:** E 324923, N 224732
- **Date recorded:** 31 January 2017

The official record identifies a detached five-bay, double-height Church of Ireland church designed in 1834 and built in 1835, with later improvement works in 1879–80. It describes a cruciform plan, granite construction, a three-stage tower, lancet openings, stained glass, a spiral staircase, timber choir gallery, exposed timber roof construction and landscaped grounds. The appraisal attributes the design to Frederick Darley Junior and the later improvements to William John Welland. It also notes stained glass by Heaton, Butler and Bayne, Catherine O'Brien and Franz Mayer and Company.

**Archive caution:** retain the sequence of original design/building and later alteration. Do not describe the present church as entirely an 1835 composition without noting the 1879–80 changes. The church is part of an ecclesiastical group with adjacent rectory and parochial hall records; those should be treated as separate records only after their identities and NIAH references are checked.

### Candidate 5 — Pastorville, Swords

- **NIAH registration:** 11335003
- **Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11335003/swords-parochial-house-swords-demesne-fingal
- **Locality:** Seatown Road, Swords, County Dublin
- **Provisional local authority:** Fingal County Council
- **NIAH rating:** Regional
- **Special-interest categories:** Architectural, Artistic
- **NIAH date range:** 1890–1900
- **Original / current use:** Presbytery/parochial/curate's house
- **Official ITM coordinate:** E 318548, N 246989
- **Date recorded:** 26 July 2000

The official record names the building Pastorville and describes a detached three-bay, two-storey red-brick house, around 1895, with recessed entrance porch, projecting full-height bays and a two-storey rear return. The record attributes the design to J. Kelly Freeman and notes that he exhibited the design at the Royal Hibernian Academy of Arts exhibition in 1894. Architectural details include red-brick Flemish bond, rubble-stone plinth, yellow-brick coping and decorative brick string courses.

**Archive caution:** preserve the official building name Pastorville and its historic use as a parochial house. Do not infer a continuing religious use or current ownership from the historical survey entry.

## 3. Geographic coverage and coordinate policy

The five candidates provide locality coverage across all four Dublin local-authority areas in the project scope. ## 4. Source version and date correction

The previous Batch 001 report's description of the data.gov.ie dataset update date has been corrected.

The sources distinguish three different metadata dates:
- The data.gov.ie dataset-level metadata reports **11 January 2024** as the last update.
- The national CSV bulk snapshot is labelled with version date **11 October 2023**.
- The separately listed NIAH ArcGIS Feature Service resource reports data and metadata last updated **16 September 2026**.

These dates must not be conflated. The ArcGIS Feature Service is the freshest currently documented official source in the reviewed metadata. The CSV should be treated as a historical snapshot unless its downloaded file metadata demonstrates a newer version. The service's live feature layer and the Historic Environment Viewer are the appropriate next routes for full-scale extraction and current verification.
