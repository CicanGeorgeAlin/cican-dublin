# CICAN Dublin Archive — Kilcrea and Corballis Heritage Reconciliation Batch 012

**Research date:** 20 November 2026  
**Scope:** Reconcile the Kilcrea and Corballis archaeological inventory, graveyard survey, local plan protected structures and individual NIAH building records.  
**Status:** Research-only. No production objects, map pins or website changes.

## 1. Kilcrea ecclesiastical site — two separate SMR records

Fingal's Donabate–Portrane Heritage Assets Audit identifies two separate records within the Kilcrea graveyard:

### 1.1 Kilcrea Church — DU012-016001

- Classification: Church.
- Townland: Kilcrea.
- ITM coordinate pair in the 2022 audit: E 721676, N 748839.
- Access classification in the audit: Partially accessible.
- The audit describes a plain rectangular building with the east gable and side walls still standing; approximately 5.40 m wide, 8.75 m long and up to 3.50 m high.
- Construction described as roughly built blocks of conglomerate and limestone, with splayed single-light windows and a surviving south-side entrance rebate.
- Surveyor in September 2021 described it as extremely overgrown and only just visible over the wall, requiring a scramble up a roadside bank to see it.

The wider 2022 heritage report says the church ruins remain overgrown and cites an 1888 description of an ivy-clad ruin. This is a historical condition record, not proof of the church's condition today.

### 1.2 Kilcrea Graveyard — DU012-016002

- Classification: Graveyard.
- Townland: Kilcrea.
- ITM coordinate pair in the 2022 audit: E 721679, N 748831.
- Irish Grid coordinate pair in the same table: E 321754, N 248808.
- Fingal Historic Graveyards Project survey reference: FHG 31.
- Historic graveyard survey coordinate fields: X 321768, Y 248796 (the survey does not identify the CRS in the record).
- RMP reference: DU012-01602.
- RPS reference: 499.
- NIAH: none recorded in the graveyard survey.
- The survey says the graveyard is no longer in use, is bounded by a modern concrete wall, and contains the partial remains of the plain rectangular church.
- The 2021 heritage audit notes it is extremely overgrown and only partially accessible.

Keep the church and graveyard as separate but related archaeological objects. Do not merge the church into the graveyard record or interpret the RPS 499 reference as a separate NIAH building registration.

## 2. Kilcrea enclosure — DU012-017

- Classification: Enclosure.
- Townland: Kilcrea.
- ITM coordinate pair: E 721855, N 749059.
- Access classification: N–AP (not visible at ground level; evidence from aerial photography).
- The 2021 survey describes cropmark evidence for a roughly circular enclosure approximately 30 m in diameter, with an irregularly shaped annexe attached.
- It is situated under tillage on a gentle north-facing slope.
- The surveyor's field note says no feature was visible at ground level.

This is an archaeological cropmark, not a standing ringfort or visible enclosure wall. The audit's ITM coordinate is a source location for the monument, not a surveyed boundary polygon.

## 3. Kilcrea tidal mill — DU012-018

- Classification: Tide mill, unclassified.
- Townland: Kilcrea.
- ITM coordinate pair: E 722197, N 748671.
- Access classification in the audit: Not accessible.
- The 2022 audit describes a site identified through historical mapping and surviving millrace evidence.
- The 1837 Ordnance Survey six-inch map shows the site of Baltray Corn Mill.
- The 1937 six-inch map marks the highest point to which medium tides flow along a millrace east of Kilcrea House.
- The audit records stone collapse at the location and says the north side of the millrace is stone-walled.
- It suggests this is probably the tidal mill shown on Rocque's 1756 map of County Dublin.
- The 2022 report's historical narrative says two watermills were present on the Kilcrea lands by the late sixteenth century, and that the mill operated by a tenant of the Cobbe family closed after the railway viaduct and sluice works altered tidal conditions in the 1840s.

The attribution to the historic Baltray Corn Mill / Rocque map is presented as a probable identification by the audit, not a proven surviving mill building. The report includes a photograph captioned “Bridge at site of tidal mill”; this does not establish that the bridge is a separate NIAH bridge record or that all visible masonry is part of the mill.

Do not encourage access to this site: the audit marks it inaccessible. Do not create a public route or field-photography instruction without permission.

## 5. Kilcrea House — NIAH 11336025

Official NIAH individual record:
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11336025/kilcrea-house-fingal

- Rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural, Artistic.
- Original/current use: Farm house.
- NIAH date range: 1780–1820; description dates it c.1800.
- NIAH coordinate pair: E 321712, N 248739.
- Recorded 1 August 2000.
- Detached five-bay, two-storey farmhouse with a parallel range to the southeast and a return to the northeast.
- Renovated and refenestrated c.1985, with a projecting entrance porch added to the return.
- Several outbuildings and entrance gateways are associated with the site.

The 2022 heritage audit says the townland of Kilcrea is documented from the fourteenth century and that the current dwelling house dates to the 1800s. It also states that the house and mill appear on Rocque's 1760 map. Keep the NIAH c.1800 building date distinct from the earlier historical reference to the estate/house site.

## 6. Kilcrea railway bridge — NIAH 11336027; possible RPS 502 relationship

The official NIAH county search result identifies NIAH 11336027 at Kilcrea as a metal railway bridge spanning between stone piers, c.1860, with alterations c.1960.

The Donabate Local Area Plan 2016 lists RPS 502 as “Railway Bridge”, Corballis Road, townland Kilcrea, described as a stone rail bridge over road.

These two descriptions are consistent with a railway bridge at Kilcrea, but the source pages reviewed do not explicitly cross-reference NIAH 11336027 and RPS 502 in one record. Treat the relationship as a strong candidate match requiring verification against the current RPS map/schedule and the NIAH record map. Do not state the cross-reference as proven until that check is completed.

The bridge is distinct from NIAH 11336014 at Turvey Avenue, which the NIAH county search page describes as a single-arch ashlar limestone road bridge over the railway line. The two bridge records must not be merged.

## 8. Hearse Road vernacular house and former forge — NIAH 11336026; RPS 505 candidate

The official NIAH county search result identifies NIAH 11336026 at Hearse Road, Donabate:
- Four-bay single-storey house, c.1850.
- Gable-fronted projecting porch.
- Single-storey outbuilding attached to the east gable, attached to a former forge to the north.
- Rubble-stone outbuildings on the site.

The Donabate Local Area Plan 2016 lists RPS 505 as “Vernacular House and Former Forge”, Hearse Road, Donabate, describing a house, forge, wheel shoeing-stone and stone trough.

The NIAH and RPS descriptions appear to refer to the same house/forge group, but the individual NIAH page could not be retrieved successfully in this session and the RPS/NIAH cross-reference was not directly confirmed. Retain the candidate match with this limitation. Do not split the forge, wheel shoeing-stone and trough into independent objects without feature-specific survey evidence and distinct positions.

## 9. Corballis archaeological landscape: additional records

The 2022 Donabate–Portrane Heritage Assets Audit and Donabate Local Area Plan identify these additional archaeological records:

| SMR reference | Class | ITM Easting | ITM Northing | Evidence/status |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| DU012-019 | Earthwork | 723050 | 749124 | Recorded monument; detailed record still to retrieve |
| DU012-020 | House, 16th/17th century | 723406 | 748700 | Recorded monument; detailed record still to retrieve |
| DU012-070 | Burial | 723488 | 747478 | Isolated burial on Malahide Island sandbar; partially accessible |
| DU012-086 | Burnt mound | 723496 | 749419 | Identified by geophysical survey/test excavation; subsequently excavated |
| DU012-087 | Burnt mound | 723385 | 749419 | Identified by geophysical survey/test excavation; subsequently excavated |
| DU012-097 | Ring-ditch | 723499 | 749530 | Fully excavated in 2017 before Donabate Distributor Road construction |

The audit's coordinate table explicitly labels these as ITM. For DU012-086 and DU012-087, the survey records burnt mound material of approximately 4 m and 7.5 m diameter respectively. Both were investigated under licence 06E0027 and later excavated with other sites in the vicinity.

DU012-097 was identified in a 2005 geophysical survey, test excavated in 2006 and fully excavated in 2017 under licence 17E0407 in advance of the Donabate Distributor Road. The report describes a c.16 m circular ring-ditch with an enclosing fosse that was recut. Archaeological finds included a stone bead, lithics and burnt bone; charcoal from a lower deposit returned a date of 20–210 cal AD (95% 2σ) in the report's summary.

These records should be treated as excavated or subsurface archaeological evidence, not as extant monuments that a visitor can see. Their original positions may now lie within developed road or housing lands. Do not create visitor directions or suggest field access.

DU012-070 is an isolated inhumation burial excavated in 1950 on the west side of Malahide Island, a sandbar approximately 400 m east of the Malahide coastline. The heritage audit describes an extended burial and associated rounded limestone pebble. This is a burial findspot, not a standing tomb or public attraction.

## 11. Coordinate, condition and access controls

The 2022 Donabate–Portrane Heritage Audit explicitly labels its first coordinate pair as ITM Easting/Northing and its second pair as Irish Grid Easting/Northing. Where both are supplied above, preserve them as separate source-labelled coordinate values. Where the audit supplies only ITM, do not fabricate Irish Grid values.

The FHG 31 graveyard survey gives X/Y coordinate values but does not state the CRS in the record. Keep these raw and separate from the audit's explicit ITM coordinate pair.

No WGS84 conversion was performed in this batch. No map pins are approved. No field verification, image registration or present-condition claim was made.

Access categories in the heritage audit are historical survey observations from 2021. Kilcrea church and graveyard were assessed as partially accessible but overgrown; the tidal mill was inaccessible; cropmark sites were not visible at ground level. Current access must be checked before any visit.
