**Research date:** 20 November 2026

**Status:** Research-only. No production objects, pins or website changes.

## 1. Lanistown Castle — DU012-004

Lanistown (also called Lanestown) Castle is a medieval tower house in Newbridge Demesne, Donabate. Fingal County Council dates it to the mid-15th century and says it was probably built by the de Bathe family, who held the manor from 1376. The castle was later used as a dining hall by the Cobbe family in the eighteenth century and as a cattle shelter in the 1960s.

The 2025 archaeological monitoring report identifies:
- Recorded Monument / SMR: DU012-004.
- Record of Protected Structures: RPS 493.
- Townland: Newbridge Demesne.
- Site coordinate supplied by the report: ITM 720918, 749661.
- Ownership and care: Fingal County Council.
- Conservation monitoring: works began September 2024 and final inspection was 5 February 2025.

The conservation works addressed structural failure, mortar and masonry loss, water ingress and vegetation. The report describes stabilisation and repair to the projecting southeast masonry, eastern arch, first-floor vault and internal arches. This is important for the archive's condition timeline: use the 2025 report as a dated conservation record, not as proof of the castle's current condition after that inspection.

The castle is a separate archaeological monument from Newbridge House (NIAH 11329001), the farmyard (NIAH 11329002) and the surrounding demesne. It should be classified as an archaeological tower house / recorded monument, not as an NIAH building unless a distinct NIAH registration is established.

## 2. Associated early medieval enclosure — DU012-101

The 2025 archaeological monitoring report states that a substantial double-ditched enclosure, DU012-101, was identified approximately 45 metres north of Lanistown Castle during the Fingal Community Geophysical Survey Project in 2018. The report interprets this as evidence that the castle stands within a broader early medieval settlement/ecclesiastical foundation.

Important limits:
- The enclosure is a distinct recorded monument, not part of the castle's masonry.
- The report does not supply an exact coordinate pair for DU012-101 in the text examined.
- Do not assign the castle's coordinate to the enclosure.
- Do not describe the interpretation as a proven ecclesiastical site; the report uses it as an interpretation of the wider landscape.
- Do not create a separate map pin until the official SMR geometry or a properly referenced survey coordinate is obtained.

## 3. Lanestown cropmark landscape — five separate SMR records

The Fingal Donabate–Portrane Heritage Assets Audit provides a detailed table with both ITM and Irish Grid coordinate columns. The five records below are individually identified as cropmarks in Lanestown. They have no visible surface remains according to the survey; their archaeological evidence is aerial-photo cropmarks. The report marks them N–AP (not visible at ground level / aerial-photograph evidence).

### 3.1 DU012-073 — enclosure

A sub-rectangular enclosure visible as a cropmark on an aerial photograph. The report places it in relatively flat farmland near a field boundary, under crop, with no visible remains at ground level. The general area is visible from the road, but the feature itself is not a standing structure.

### 3.2 DU012-074 — ring-ditch

A circular ring-ditch visible as a cropmark on an aerial photograph. The report states that it is in relatively flat farmland near a field boundary, under crop, with no visible remains at ground level. The report corrects the townland attribution to Lanestown (rather than Newbridge Demesne).

### 3.3 DU012-075 — enclosure

A sub-circular enclosure visible as an aerial cropmark, in the same field as DU012-076 and DU012-077. The report describes the location as the western end of a large, relatively flat open field. The general area is visible from the road.

### 3.4 DU012-076 — enclosure

A second sub-circular enclosure visible as an aerial cropmark in the same field as DU012-075 and the DU012-077 field system. It is a separate SMR reference and should not be merged with the adjacent enclosure.

### 3.5 DU012-077 — field system

A field system visible as an aerial cropmark in the same field as DU012-075 and DU012-076. The report states that the area is visible from the road but has no surface expression.

These five records are archaeological landscape features, not conventional buildings or visible monuments. Their catalogue entries should explain that the source evidence is aerial imagery and that no surface remains were recorded by the 2021 survey. Use cautious map styling and do not present a point marker as an exact archaeological feature footprint.

## 4. Possible earlier house site — DU012-060

The same audit identifies DU012-060 as “House - 18th/19th century” in Newbridge Demesne. It records that a 1705 survey shows a schematic large single-storey rectangular house belonging to John Forde, and suggests it may have been incorporated into the present building on the site. The audit's site-visit note asks whether it could have been incorporated into the single-storey building beside the main house.

This is a tentative archaeological/historical interpretation. It is not evidence of a separately surviving building. Do not create a visible object representing a standing house or assign it a separate point without further archaeological and architectural evidence. Keep it as a research lead linked to Newbridge House.

## 5. Newbridge Demesne landscape context

Fingal County Council's current visitor page describes the regional park as approximately 370 acres and lists woodland walks, deer park, lime kiln, ruins of Lanistown Castle, watercourses and pleasure grounds. It also identifies the Walled Garden, orchards, historic Irish apple varieties, restored nineteenth-century glasshouses, farmyard and outbuildings.

This verifies that these landscape components are part of the demesne visitor experience, but it does not by itself establish a separate NIAH or SMR registration for each item. Future research should seek individual records and source evidence for:
- The lime kiln.
- The walled garden and its walls/gates.
- The two restored nineteenth-century glasshouses.
- Individual farmyard outbuildings.
- Watercourses, bridges and designed landscape features.
- The deer park and historic boundaries.

Do not create duplicate component records merely because a general park description names these attractions.

## 7. Coordinate and publication controls

The Donabate–Portrane Heritage Assets Audit explicitly labels its coordinate columns ITM Easting / ITM Northing and Irish Grid Easting / Irish Grid Northing. Therefore the five Lanestown cropmark coordinates above can be preserved in both source-labelled coordinate systems.

The 2025 Lanistown Castle monitoring report labels its castle coordinate as ITM 720918, 749661.

No coordinate was derived for DU012-101; its only location statement in the examined report is approximately 45 metres north of the castle. Do not copy the castle coordinates to it.

No WGS84 transformation, map pin creation, field verification, image registration or production catalogue update was performed in this batch.

## 8. Access and interpretation

The Council identifies Newbridge Demesne as a public regional park. The 2025 monitoring report describes the castle within the publicly owned amenity. This does not establish public access to every adjacent field or archaeological cropmark. The 2021 heritage audit specifically notes that the Lanestown cropmark fields are under crop and not visible at ground level; access should be treated as road-side viewing only unless landowner permission or a public route is confirmed.

Never encourage entering cultivated fields, crossing fences, disturbing archaeology or attempting to expose buried features.
