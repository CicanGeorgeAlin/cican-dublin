## 2. Specialist historical context — South Dublin County Council archaeological assessment

Source: Siobhán Deery, Courtney Deery Heritage Consultancy Ltd, *Lucan Village Public Realm — The Demesne and Riverside: Archaeological Impact Assessment Report*, 12 December 2021, prepared for South Dublin County Council.

Official council-hosted PDF: https://consult.sdublincoco.ie/en/system/files/materials/7464/2%20RS%20%26%20DPE%20Archaeological%20Impact%20Assessment%20Report.pdf

Relevant findings in section 3.1.6 and cartographic analysis:

- The report states that the Lucan Spa Hotel had established itself as a health resort, first opening in 1758 after discovery of an iron spa near Chapel Hill.
- The spa was on Agmondisham Vesey's lands. A wall was built around it to protect it from the nearby River Liffey.
- The waters are described in the report as containing carbonate of lime and sulphurated nitrogen gas. This is a historical report of the spa waters, not a modern water-quality assessment.
- The report identifies a mid-eighteenth-century bath-house within Lucan Demesne, recorded in the RMP as an oratory (DU017-020). It notes that tradition associated the bath-house with St John of Jerusalem, while no obvious earlier fabric was extant according to the cited NIAH entry. The structure is screened from public view by the demesne wall.
- In 1795, the Old Lucan Spa Hotel (identified in the report as the present Lucan County Bar) was built and incorporated a ballroom.
- The report says the hotel closed to fashionable spa visitors by the mid-nineteenth century as other spas nearer Dublin became more popular; the 1843 first-edition OS map labels the former hotel as a school house for clergymen's sons.
- Rocque's 1760 map depicts the Spa Well and a small square bath-house structure in the demesne woodland. Taylor's 1816 map marks the spa well and hotel west of the village.
- The 1907–09 OS 25-inch map depicts a larger new Spa Hotel, a Spa Restaurant to its northwest, and Spa House at the river bank, accessed through an underpass to the demesne grounds.
- The report's 2021 archaeological field inspection concerned the Demesne Park and Liffey Promenade public-realm works, not a current condition or access survey of every Spa Hotel structure.

These statements are attributed to the 2021 assessment and its cited historic cartography. They must not be presented as proof of present-day survival, access, or condition.

## 3. Keep separate registrations and sites distinct

The evidence supports a historic Lucan spa complex with several distinct elements, but does not justify merging them:

1. **NIAH 11201019** — the 1795 Old Lucan Spa Hotel, formerly Spa Restaurant / later Lucan County Bar.
2. **NIAH 11201020** — the separate larger Spa Hotel built in 1891. Its individual NIAH record describes a distinct building with bow, half-timbered gables and tower. Official record: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11201020/lucan-spa-hotel-cooldrinagh-co-dublin
3. **RMP DU017-020 / NIAH 11204015 context** — the bath-house / oratory within Lucan Demesne, a separate site screened by the demesne wall. Do not assume that the bath-house, spa well, Spa House, hotel and restaurant are one building or one point.
4. **Spa Well** — a historic feature shown on Rocque's 1760 map and discussed in the council assessment. Exact present survival and precise location are not established by this desk review.

The 2021 report also distinguishes the spa's earlier eighteenth-century context from the 1907–09 map's later Spa Hotel, Spa Restaurant and Spa House arrangement. Do not infer that the later Spa Restaurant is necessarily the same physical building as NIAH 11201019 without documentary/plan-level confirmation.
