# CICAN Dublin Archive — Newbridge Demesne Component Evidence and Registration Audit Batch 011

**Research date:** 20 November 2026  
**Scope:** Audit the Newbridge Demesne lime kiln, walled garden, restored glasshouses and former lodge as potential separately catalogued heritage objects; distinguish registered assets from components documented only in estate-level sources.  
**Status:** Research-only. No production catalogue, map or website changes.

## 1. Key outcome

The official Fingal sources confirm several important Newbridge Demesne features, but the sources reviewed in this batch do not establish a separate NIAH registration number or SMR identifier for the lime kiln, walled garden, glasshouses or former lodge. The former lodge is separately listed as RPS 495. The other features are described as elements of the demesne or as restoration projects.

Do not invent individual NIAH/SMR IDs or promote a general visitor description into a standalone registered record. They can be tracked as provisional component candidates with estate-level provenance until an individual statutory record, survey plan or reliable feature-specific source is located.

## 2. Former Lodge to Newbridge House — RPS 495

Fingal County Council's Record of Protected Structures lists **Former Lodge to Newbridge House**, RPS 495, on Newbridge Avenue off The Square, Newbridge Demesne. The description identifies a three-bay, single-storey house at the gates on the western edge of the demesne.

An An Bord Pleanála Inspector's Report (ABP-313836-22) independently describes RPS 495 as an early nineteenth-century single-storey former gate lodge at the western edge of Newbridge Demesne, approximately 80 metres southwest of the appeal site. The report also notes nearby RPS 494 Newbridge House, RPS 507 water pump and RPS 508 St Patrick's Church of Ireland.

**Registration status:** RPS 495 confirmed. No separate NIAH registration number was established in the sources examined in this batch. It is therefore a valid protected-structure candidate, but must not be assigned a fabricated NIAH reference.

**Research action:** Obtain the current statutory RPS schedule/map entry and verify the exact lodge footprint and current address before assigning coordinates. The description and approximate relative location are not sufficient for a precise pin.

## 3. Newbridge Demesne lime kiln — component candidate

Fingal County Council's Newbridge House and Farm visitor information explicitly lists a lime kiln among the demesne's features. The 2022–2041 Woodland Management Plan describes an old lime kiln within the wider approximately 400-acre estate and lists it among surviving original designed-landscape features.

The sources establish the existence of an estate lime kiln as a named heritage feature. They do not, in the material reviewed here, provide a separate NIAH registration, SMR number, exact location, construction date, dimensions or current condition.

**Registration status:** Estate-level feature confirmed; individual statutory registration and exact location unresolved.

**Research action:** Check the National Monuments Service Historic Environment Viewer and the historic maps/estate surveys for a discrete lime-kiln record. Seek the Newbridge Demesne Conservation Plan and feature schedule. Do not assume the kiln is the same as any other Fingal lime kiln or industrial monument.

## 4. Walled garden and orchards — component candidate

Fingal County Council identifies the Walled Garden as a visitor attraction, with extensive orchards, old Irish apple varieties and replanted herbaceous borders. The Woodland Management Plan describes the walled garden as part of the historic demesne and its surviving designed landscape. A 2019 conservation-plan summary identifies the walled kitchen gardens among the original features of the designed landscape.

The sources establish a historic garden component but do not supply a separate NIAH registration or a precise garden-wall polygon in the material reviewed here.

**Registration status:** Estate component confirmed; separate statutory registration and surveyed extent unresolved.

**Research action:** Locate historic garden plans, conservation drawings and any individual NIAH garden survey entry. If the garden walls, gates, glasshouses and orchard are separate physical assets, record their relationships without duplicating the garden as an umbrella object.

## 5. Two restored nineteenth-century glasshouses — component candidates

Fingal County Council's current visitor page states that two nineteenth-century glasshouses have been restored at Newbridge Demesne. The Council also replanted sections of the walled garden. An official public procurement notice records a project titled “Conservation and Restoration of Heritage Glasshouses at Newbridge House Demesne, Donabate, North County Fingal” (FCC/053/24).

This establishes that there are two heritage glasshouses and that the Council commissioned conservation/restoration works. The source material reviewed does not provide individual NIAH references, precise coordinates, architect/builder, exact construction dates, detailed fabric descriptions or the restoration completion date.

**Registration status:** Two estate components confirmed; individual statutory registration and feature-specific survey detail unresolved.

**Research action:** Obtain the conservation specification, measured survey, heritage impact assessment, tender drawings and completion report associated with FCC/053/24. Those may establish whether the two glasshouses are separate structures, their historic fabric, date range and exact location. Do not use procurement notice metadata alone as evidence of completion or present condition.

## 6. Estate-wide context and evidence distinctions

Fingal's 2022–2041 Woodland Management Plan describes the estate as approximately 400 acres and identifies the house, outbuildings and yards, walled garden, Lanistown Castle, old lime kiln, River Pill, parkland and mature woodland. It describes surviving features of the mid-eighteenth-century designed landscape, including woodland, parkland, deer park, ornamental water, bridge, lime kiln, entrance gates and boundaries.

Fingal's visitor page describes the public regional park as approximately 370 acres. These two acreage figures are retained with their respective source attributions and are not reconciled in this batch; they may reflect different measurement boundaries or purposes.

The visitor page also confirms the estate's public-park status and visitor facilities. This does not establish unrestricted access to every structure, garden area or conservation worksite. Use official visitor routes and permission requirements for photography.

## 8. No pin / no invented registration policy

No exact coordinates for the former lodge, lime kiln, walled garden or glasshouses were established in the sources examined. No map pins or WGS84 coordinates should be created from broad estate-level location text.

The archive should distinguish:
- Individually registered NIAH buildings.
- Individually listed RPS structures.
- Recorded archaeological monuments (SMR/RMP).
- Designed-landscape components supported by estate conservation documents.
- Modern visitor amenities or restored features whose historic fabric and registration remain to be verified.
