## 1. Chapel Green windmill — NIAH 11324016

**Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11324016/chapel-green-rush-rush

- Rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural.
- Original use: Mill (wind).
- NIAH date range: 1780–1820; description dates the windmill c.1800.
- Raw coordinate pair displayed by NIAH: 323084, 250874.
- Date recorded: 10 August 2000.
- NIAH description: Windmill now in ruined condition, with random-rubble limestone walls, segmental-headed openings with limestone-rubble reveals and a mild-steel gate enclosing the entrance; narrow square-headed window openings.

**Fingal archaeological resource context:** Fingal County Council's archaeological resource management report identifies Rush Windmill as DU008-012 and RPS 276, located at Mill Bank, Rush. It describes a three-storey cylindrical tower on an artificial rise, with opposed doorways, slit windows and a basement feature extending east. Its site assessment notes the structure's former fair-green context and records access across the open space. The report also records historic issues such as graffiti, litter and denudation; these are observations from that report, not verified current conditions.

**Rush Urban Framework Plan:** The 2018 plan identifies the windmill at Chapel Green as a landmark and discusses its relationship to the civic quarter. A separate Fingal planning objective encouraged restoration of the former windmill as a tourist amenity. These are plan statements, not evidence that restoration has occurred.

**Coordinate anomaly:** The NIAH page displays 323084, 250874. This pair appears inconsistent with the other Rush NIAH pairs in the 326xxx, 254xxx range. Do not correct it by guesswork or pin it. Verify against the NIAH CSV/feature service and original map sheet; retain the raw NIAH value and record this discrepancy.

## 2. Millbank — NIAH 11324001

**Official NIAH record (search listing):** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/?county=&date_from=&date_to=&group=&location_type=building&query=chimney&search_page=38&type=

- NIAH registration: 11324001.
- Rating: Regional (verify on the individual record before production).
- NIAH vernacular/thatch search description: Terraced two-bay single-storey thatched house, c.1850, on an L-shaped plan with projecting entrance porch. The roof is double-pitched thatch with rendered coping and brick and rendered chimney stacks.

**Fingal Thatch Project 2022:** Record FCC-TS-25 identifies NIAH 11324001, RPS 277, name “Mill Bank”, address Chapel Green, townland Rush. The report provides ITM east 725652.7347 and ITM north 754017.9080. It describes an east-facing semi-detached L-plan, direct-entry, two-bay single-storey thatched house with rear extensions, a windbreak, oaten-straw roof, chimneystacks with terracotta pots and wire cowls, roughcast front elevation and random-coursed stonework to the south gable. It states the house was attached to a hall used by St Maur's Pipe Band and that it was one of the last five remaining buildings with intact early thatched roofs in Rush at the time of the 2022 survey.

**Identity caution:** The Fingal report describes Millbank as attached to a hall used by St Maur's Pipe Band. Preserve the report's relationship description; do not assume the hall is part of the NIAH object or create a second building record without separate evidence. Survey-era condition and use do not establish current condition or occupancy.

## 3. Saint Maur's Catholic Church — NIAH 11324004

**Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11324004/saint-maurs-catholic-church-chapel-green-rush-rush-co-dublin

- Rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural, Artistic, Social, Technical.
- Original use: Church/chapel.
- NIAH date range: 1755–1765.
- Raw coordinate pair displayed by NIAH: 325938, 254088.
- Date recorded: 3 August 2000.
- Description: Detached gable-fronted Catholic church, built c.1760, with six bays to the side elevation. Transepts were added c.1820; a square-profile bell tower and polygonal apse were added c.1860, together with several gabled single-bay additions. The NIAH describes a T-plan slate roof with cast-iron cresting and rainwater goods, coursed rubble walls, sandstone quoins to the tower, pointed-arched openings with sandstone reveals and leaded stained-glass windows. Interior features include rib-vaulted ceilings, stained glass by Early Bros. of Dublin, decorative apse painting, a marble altar and memorial plaques, a rear gallery with balustrade on a marble arcade, and terracotta porch tiling.

**Local context:** Fingal's Rush Urban Framework Plan identifies the former St Maur's Church as a civic-quarter building and lists Rush Library (former St Maur's Church) as RPS 272. The current library/community-centre use is a local-plan description; verify the present use and boundaries before asserting them as current.

**Separation rule:** The church is distinct from the separate NIAH presbytery registration 11324003 and from the former medieval church remains at Whitestown (RPS 279). Do not merge the historic church phases, later additions, presbytery or Whitestown ruins.
