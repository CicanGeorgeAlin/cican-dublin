## 1. NIAH 11324024 — The Thatched Cottage, Skerries Road

The official NIAH individual page identifies **The Thatched Cottage, Skerries Road, Rush**, registration **11324024**.

- Rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural, Social.
- Date range: 1740–1820; described as c.1780.
- NIAH coordinates: **326643, 254655** (CRS not stated beside the page coordinates).
- Recorded: 10 August 2000.
- Description: Detached four-bay, single-storey thatched house with projecting entrance porch; single-storey side extension c.1950.
- Roof: double-pitched thatch, raised rendered gable ends, three small rendered chimney stacks; clay-tiled extension and slate porch roof.
- Walls: nap-rendered house; rough-cast rendered extension.
- Openings: square-headed windows, rendered reveals, painted granite sills, single-pane sash windows, small timber casement in gable indicating a loft, and tongue-and-groove door.

This is not the same record as 11324027, the separate Skerries Road gate lodge. Do not confuse a shared road address with one heritage object.

**Fieldwork status:** NIAH source description only. Current roof condition, survival of original sash windows, exact entrance, ownership and access require field verification.

## 2. NIAH 11324010 — Sion House / former Rush Constabulary Barrack

The official NIAH individual page identifies **Sion House, Lower Main Street, Rush**, registration **11324010**.

- Rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural, Social.
- Previous name: Rush Constabulary Barrack.
- Original use: House; historical use: Garda station/constabulary barracks; current NIAH use: House.
- Date range: 1840–1850; described as c.1845.
- NIAH coordinates: **326893, 254122** (CRS not stated beside the page coordinates).
- Recorded: 3 August 2000.
- Description: End-of-terrace, three-bay, two-storey house with projecting entrance porch and two-storey rear extension.

The record describes an M-profile slate roof, terracotta pots and ridge tiles, rendered chimney stack, nap-rendered walls, lined-and-ruled quoins, rendered plinth, metal ventilation grilles, cornice to porch, square-headed windows, painted granite sills, timber panelled door and glass overlight, and 2/2 timber sash windows.

**Identity control:** The historical constabulary use is an attribute of this building’s record, not evidence of a separate surviving police station structure. Do not create a second object for the former barracks without separate evidence.

## 5. Coordinate handling — do not manufacture pins

The NIAH individual pages display coordinate pairs for the records above, but the page excerpts do not state a CRS beside those pairs. NIAH’s data-download documentation confirms that its downloadable CSV provides location information in multiple forms: ITM, latitude/longitude and National Grid. It is therefore necessary to obtain the official row and use the explicitly labelled coordinate field before importing coordinates into the archive.

**No coordinate transformation or map pin is approved by this batch.** Preserve the raw displayed pair and its source URL in research notes only; do not label it ITM, Irish Grid or WGS84 by inference.
