## 1. Saint Maur's Catholic Church presbytery — NIAH 11324003

**Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11324003/saint-maurs-catholic-church-chapel-green-rush-rush-co-dublin

- Rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural, Artistic.
- Original and current NIAH use: Presbytery/parochial/curate's house.
- Date range: 1840–1850; described c.1845.
- Raw NIAH coordinate pair: 325916, 254069.
- Date recorded: 3 August 2003.
- Description: Detached three-bay two-storey presbytery, c.1845, enclosed by wrought-iron railings. Hipped asbestos-tiled roof with terracotta ridge tiles, rendered chimney stacks, yellow terracotta chimney pots and cast-iron rainwater goods. Rendered lined-and-ruled walls with rendered quoins. Square-headed windows with rendered soffits, reveals and cills; elliptical-headed front door with reproduction doorcase and timber/stained-glass door retaining original console brackets supporting a moulded timber cornice.

**Relationship context:** This is a separately registered building from Saint Maur's Catholic Church (NIAH 11324004). Do not combine presbytery and church into one object. The nearby Rush Library/former St Maur's Church references relate to the former church, not automatically to this presbytery.

## 2. Rush Harbour — NIAH 11324013

**Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11324013/harbour-road-rush-rush-dublin

- Rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural, Technical.
- Original and current use: Harbour/dock/port.
- Date range: 1820–1850; described c.1835.
- Raw NIAH coordinate pair: 327237, 254311.
- Date recorded: 3 August 2000.
- Description: Harbour with dressed and rubble limestone pier and seawall.

**Fingal Coastal Architecture Study:** Site FCAS 014 identifies Rush Harbour, map sheet DU008/2791, Irish National Grid 327379, 254286, date range 1810–1850, condition recorded as good in the 2009 survey, and designation NIAH 11324013. It describes an L-shaped harbour pier with coursed and rubble limestone pier and walls, limestone steps to water level, remnants of early render, a curved limestone seawall to the west with base batter, buttresses and cut-stone coping, and painted stone mooring posts.

**Fingal RPS context:** RPS 0892 describes a mid-nineteenth-century L-shaped limestone pier at Rush Harbour, possibly containing elements of an earlier pier. A separate RPS 0891 entry describes the breakwater, a distinct structure. A proposed addition review for Rush Harbour (IH0383) identifies NIAH 11324013 and describes its fishing-industry role and historic harbour context. Do not merge the pier and breakwater.

**Coordinate caution:** NIAH displays 327237, 254311; the coastal study displays Irish National Grid 327379, 254286. These values differ. Preserve both exactly with their source labels. Do not choose one or average them. Resolve against NIAH data and source map before pinning.

## 3. Whitestown House — NIAH 11324014

**Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11324014/whitestown-house-whitestown-ba-e-by-lusk-ed-dublin

- Rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural, Artistic.
- Original/current use: Farmhouse.
- Date range: 1840–1860; described c.1850.
- Raw NIAH coordinate pair: 327303, 254329.
- Date recorded: 10 August 2000.
- Description: Detached three-bay two-storey farmhouse, c.1850, with a two-bay two-storey lean-to return to rear possibly incorporating fabric of an earlier house. A rubble-stone barn, c.1850, and conservatory, c.1920, are to the left; rubble-stone farm buildings, c.1850, are also on site. A corn mill, rebuilt 1852 and possibly incorporating earlier fabric, is described as derelict. Roofs include double-pitched slate with terracotta ridge tiles and rendered chimney stacks; the walls include roughcast render, rubble stone and limestone quoins to the mill. Openings include sash and casement windows, granite cills and a round-headed door with leaded fanlight and fluted timber Ionic columns.

**Research significance:** The NIAH description includes a corn mill on the site. It is described as a subsidiary structure within the site record, not a separately numbered NIAH object in the source reviewed. Do not split it into a separate production object without independent registration or evidence that it warrants a distinct record. Also do not assume that “Whitestown House” is part of Whitestown graveyard; they are separate places and separate NIAH records.

## 4. Desio, 60 Lower Main Street — NIAH 11324011

**Official NIAH record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11324011/desio-lower-main-street-rush-rush

- Rating: Regional.
- Special interest: Architectural, Social.
- Original/current use: House.
- Date range: 1850–1900; described c.1875.
- Raw NIAH coordinate pair: 326990, 254106.
- Date recorded: 3 August 2000.
- Description: Detached four-bay single-storey thatched house with attic storey, c.1875, with three-bay rear extension c.1990. Double-pitched thatched roof with decorative roped ridging and eaves, cement-rendered stack and concrete asbestos tiles to extension. Cement-rendered walls. Square-headed openings with rendered reveals and granite cills; timber sash windows and timber door, c.2000, with uPVC windows to extension.

**Research caution:** The record describes the property as a house in use as a house at survey time. It does not establish current occupancy, current roof material or present condition. No current photographic inspection was carried out in this batch.

## 5. Specialist sources and local context

### Fingal Coastal Architecture Study

https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2019-04/Fingal%20Coastal%20Architecture%20Study.pdf

The 2009 study's FCAS 014 record provides a detailed architectural description and photographs of Rush Harbour, labels the Irish National Grid coordinate pair 327379, 254286, and identifies NIAH 11324013. It is useful independent corroboration, but its condition assessment is a 2009 snapshot.

### Fingal Record of Protected Structures 2023–2029

https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2024-03/record-of-protected-structures-and-acas-2023-2029.pdf

The RPS schedule distinguishes Rush Harbour (0892) from its breakwater (0891). RPS inclusion is a local-authority designation and must not be inferred from NIAH inclusion alone.

### Whitestown graveyard cross-check — existing archive record

NIAH 11324015 is Whitestown graveyard, with St Maur's Church ruin. The Fingal Historic Graveyards Project Volume 2 identifies it as FHG 54, St Maur's, Whitestown, gives a survey date of 26 July 2008, and lists RPS 279 and NIAH 11324015. Its location is south of the R128 between Lusk and Rush; the old church ruin and later cemetery extension are part of the wider cemetery context. The GDD cultural heritage assessment also identifies NIAH 11324015 and NGR 324642, 253561.

A repository search found an existing document:
`documentation/ST-MAURS-WHITESTOWN-BRETON-MARINERS-ARCHBOLD-WELL-BATCH-177.md`.
Therefore 11324015 was not added as a new candidate in this batch. The old graveyard and St Maur's Church remains are related but distinguishable archaeological components (DU008-021001 and DU008-021002). St Maur's Well on Spout Road is a separate nearby feature (RPS 281 / DU008-020), not part of the graveyard.
