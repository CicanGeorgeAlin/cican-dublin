**Method:** Targeted indexed repository searches for the identifiers and place terms below returned no matches. This is not a full duplicate audit against the canonical catalogue and source files.

## 1. NIAH 11324020 — Lower Main Street water pump

The official NIAH Buildings of Ireland water-pump search result identifies 11324020 at Lower Main Street, Rush, as a cast-iron water pump, c.1900, square profile, with the note “Now missing parts.” It is distinct from NIAH 11324012, the Harbour Road pump documented in Batch 013.

**Evidence:** Official NIAH search-result listing.
**Outstanding:** Full individual record, exact coordinates/CRS, current survival, missing components, in-situ status and current photograph. Do not map-pin or mark field-verified.

## 3. Kenure Park — World War II coastal watching lookout post (Fingal RPS 0265 b)

The adopted Fingal Development Plan 2023–2029 RPS lists 0265 b, World War II Look Out Post, in the grounds of the Martello Tower, Tower Street, Rush. It describes a mid-20th-century concrete hut, built winter 1939/40, used as a Coastal Watching Look Out Post to record naval and aircraft activity off the Irish coast.

This is distinct from the Martello Tower (RPS 0265 a / NIAH 11324023), documented in Batch 011. Do not merge the hut into the tower record. No separate NIAH number is asserted.

**Outstanding:** Current condition, exact footprint, access, ownership/management and any separate NIAH registration.
**Fieldwork:** Confirm survival from a lawful, safe public viewpoint; do not enter private grounds.

## 4. Kenure Park — mid-19th-century road bridge (Fingal RPS 0890)

The current Fingal RPS lists 0890, Road Bridge, at Kenure Park, Rush, as a mid-19th-century single-arch masonry bridge carrying an extended avenue within Kenure Park Demesne over a river.

This is separate from the portico, gate lodges, entrance gates and mansion-related features documented in Batches 015/016. No NIAH registration is asserted.

**Outstanding:** Exact location, present condition, watercourse name, further construction details, access and any NIAH cross-reference.
**Fieldwork:** Locate from authoritative mapping or estate/heritage plans before visiting. Do not infer coordinates from the general park address.

## 5. Rush Harbour — breakwater (Fingal RPS 0891)

The adopted Fingal RPS lists 0891, Breakwater, at Rush Harbour, as a mid-19th-century limestone breakwater built in two sections. A separate Fingal proposed-addition assessment (review reference IH0386) describes two sections northwest of the harbour, squared random-coursed limestone walls, half-round coping and a central gap containing remains of wooden posts. The assessment recommends addition; the adopted 2023–2029 RPS independently confirms listing as RPS 0891.

**Critical distinction:** This is not the L-shaped harbour pier, RPS 0892, documented in Batch 014, nor Rogerstown Pier RPS 0893. Keep each structure separate.
**Outstanding:** Exact geometry, present condition, safe public access and survival of timber-post remnants.
**Fieldwork:** Visit only in safe tide/weather conditions and photograph from permitted public access. Do not walk on exposed or unstable harbour fabric.

## 6. Rogerstown Pier — Fingal RPS 0893

The adopted RPS lists 0893, Rogerstown Pier, at Rogerstown, Rush, as a turn-of-19th-century limestone pier, excluding derelict stone outbuildings. It is separate from Rush Harbour RPS 0891/0892 and the Rogerstown Estuary sluice/bridge features. No NIAH ID is asserted.

**Outstanding:** Exact extent, condition, relationship to the derelict outbuildings, access and any NIAH registration.
