## 2. Record findings

### 2.1 NIAH 11220002 — Saint Anne's Catholic Church, Friarstown Upper

The NIAH individual record identifies a detached, gable-fronted cruciform church built in 1876. It has a seven-bay nave, polygonal east apse, single-bay transepts and a north entrance porch. The record describes snecked rock-faced limestone walls, angle buttresses, lancet windows, quatrefoil or hexafoil tracery, a west wheel window, transept tracery windows, a pointed doorcase with roll mouldings and paired colonnettes, a banded slate roof and a bellcote to the south transept. The interior retains a timber scissors-truss roof.

NIAH rates the church Regional and records architectural, artistic, social and technical interest. The appraisal calls it substantially in original condition and notes its prominence at the road junction.

The NIAH page publishes coordinates **309417, 225019**. The individual page does not state the coordinate reference system or positional accuracy alongside the value. Preserve the raw source coordinate; do not convert or publish a map pin until reconciled with the official CSV/Feature Service row.

Official record: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11220002/saint-annes-catholic-church-bohernabreena-road-friarstown-upper-co-dublin

### 2.2 NIAH 11220007 — Ballycullen Road, Oldcourt — Murrain Cross

The NIAH individual record identifies a freestanding, square-section, rough-dressed granite cross erected in 1868, approximately three metres high and 30 centimetres in section. It has an equal-armed cross head, a worn incised date on the east face and an irregular sub-rectangular granite base.

NIAH rates it Regional and records artistic, historical and social interest. Its appraisal identifies it as a so-called Murrain Cross, a memorial associated with the end of cattle fever and a former local practice of blessing grazing fields. The record says this cross is one of only two surviving Murrain Crosses in the south Dublin landscape and identifies a sibling off Firhouse Road, NIAH 11215009.

The NIAH page publishes coordinates **311726, 225476**. The record's concise description and appraisal are not a present-day condition assessment. The incised date is described as worn; exact legibility and current condition require field photography.

Official record: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11220007/ballycullen-road-oldcourt-co-dublin

### 2.3 NIAH 11220008 — Orlagh House, Gunny Hill

The official NIAH record identifies Orlagh House, formerly Orlagh College, as a detached five-bay, three-storey-over-basement former country house dating to 1785–1795, now used as a friary. It describes a double staircase to the projecting entrance porch, two-storey wings and rear extension added c.1875, smooth-rendered walls, slate roofs, a central hexagonal bellcote and mature enclosed parkland.

NIAH rates it Regional and records architectural, artistic, historical, social and technical interest. Its appraisal distinguishes the eighteenth-century house from substantial late-nineteenth-century additions and notes that its original two-phase form and parkland setting survive despite alteration and modernisation.

The NIAH page publishes coordinates **311524, 225167**. The current use is stated as friary in the NIAH survey record; opening arrangements and public access are not confirmed by this research. Do not assume it is open to visitors.

Official record: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11220008/orlagh-house-gunny-hill-orlagh-dublin

### 2.4 NIAH 11220014 — Glenasmole Reservoir, Glassamucky — lower reservoir

The official NIAH record identifies this as the lower of two reservoirs serving the Ballyboden water treatment works, built c.1880. The described complex includes a turf-covered stone dam, stone-lined reservoir, spillway with a double-arched coursed-rubble stone bridge and a stone valve building approached by a steel lattice bridge.

NIAH rates it Regional and records architectural, scientific and technical interest. The appraisal describes a unified collection of structures representing Victorian engineering schemes and attributes the design to Richard Hassard in 1880.

The NIAH page publishes coordinates **309004, 222796**. A distinct upper reservoir is separately recorded as NIAH 11225016. The lower and upper reservoirs must remain distinct catalogue identities while their engineering relationship is recorded. Do not merge their coordinates or treat one point as a locator for the complete water-supply system.

The NIAH page does not establish present-day access, operational condition or public visiting permission. Reservoir and water-treatment infrastructure may be restricted; field photography must be limited to lawful public viewpoints and must not enter operational land.

Official record: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11220014/glenasmole-reservoir-glassamucky-co-dublin

### 2.5 NIAH 11220020 — Saint Colmcille's Well, Oldcourt

The NIAH record gives the previous name Saint Columkille's Well and identifies the original and current use as a holy well. It describes a coursed-rubble stone, gable-fronted statue niche erected in 1914 over the well, containing a statue of St Colmcille. The well opening has random paving and is set in a water garden approached by a small bridge; a memorial Celtic cross is also recorded within the grounds.

NIAH rates the site Regional and records archaeological, architectural, artistic and social interest. The appraisal says the well was recovered from a dilapidated state by people from nearby Orlagh Monastery and records annual religious use at the time of the survey. This is historical survey information, not confirmation of current religious use or present condition.

The NIAH page publishes coordinates **311680, 225424**. The well, 1914 niche, approach bridge and memorial cross are described as components of one NIAH site. Do not create separate public map pins for those components without exact independent evidence and a clear parent-child relationship.

Official record: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11220020/saint-colmcilles-well-ballycullen-road-oldcourt-co-dublin
