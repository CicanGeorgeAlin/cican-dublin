# CICAN Dublin Archive — St Catherine's Portrane & Kenure CofI (Batch 170)

**Status:** Evidence-led research report, committed as documentation and fetched back for verification. No permanent archive IDs, production catalogue records or pins created.  
**Scope:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project FHG 42 (St Catherine's, Portrane) and FHG 43 (Kenure Church of Ireland, Rush).  
**Critical distinction:** FHG 43 Kenure (CofI) is the 1866 Church of Ireland churchyard east of the R128. FHG 44 Kenure is the separate medieval ruined church/graveyard west of the R128. FHG 43 must not be merged with FHG 44 or with the nearby St Catherine's Church, Rush.

## 1. St Catherine's, Portrane — FHG 42

### Identity and location
- FHG reference 42; survey dated 4 August 2008.
- Address in FHG: Langston Park, Portrane, County Dublin, Ireland. Current Fingal burial database uses Portrane Cemetery, Burrow Road, Portrane.
- FHG coordinate fields: X 325352, Y 250960. Coordinate reference system is not stated in the FHG table; retain raw source values without relabelling as ITM.
- Buried in Fingal cemetery locator: 53.49392826, -6.11193807. This is a public cemetery locator, not a surveyed coordinate for each monument.
- RMP graveyard DU008-03102; church DU008-03101; RPS 522. FHG notes excavation 2002:0648.
- Fingal County Council ownership/management is recorded in FHG and the current burial-ground page.
- Site lies north of the R126, south of Portrane village, in a small walled graveyard with views toward the Dublin coastline and Lambay Island.

### Historical context and church sequence
- FHG associates the church with St Catherine and notes documentary evidence for an early church foundation. The upstanding remains are later medieval.
- The church was appropriated to the monastery of Grace Dieu by Archbishop John Comyn in the late 12th century; the monastery retained a portion of the tithes into the 16th century.
- The 1615 Regal Visitation Book described church and chancel as in good repair; by 1630 Archbishop Bulkeley recorded them as down.
- The current official Fingal heritage page describes the church as medieval and says it was founded by the Normans in the 14th century, while the FHG historical narrative points to late-12th-century ecclesiastical administration. Preserve this difference: documentary appropriation/rectory history is not necessarily the date of the standing building.
- Standing remains comprise a simple nave and chancel with a three-stage tower at the west end, with stepped battlements. Construction is sandstone and limestone rubble with dressed quoins.
- A segmental-headed opening at the west end of the south wall gives access to the nave. Square-headed openings with dressed stone surrounds and bar holes occur at the east end of the south wall.
- A scar of the pitched nave roof is visible on the east wall of the tower. The tower has a two-light ground-floor window and segmental-headed upper-floor openings.
- The graveyard has an unusual ornamental battlemented parapet and a rectangular plan with curved parapets along the northwest, east and west sides; other parapet sections have been rebuilt in similar style.
- The interior of the church was used for burial.

### Individual memorials and architectural details
1. **Patt Mackian headstone (1743)** — Simple stone pillar inscribed “1743 IHS Patt Mackian”. FHG calls this one of the earliest headstones. It was already leaning significantly in 2008 and was singled out for stability investigation. Photograph full stone, inscription, top and base; do not straighten or reset without conservation approval.
2. **Unfinished IHS-and-cross headstone** — FHG records another stone with an IHS motif and cross and an unfinished inscription. It should be located and documented as a separate object; do not infer the intended text.
3. **Mid-19th-century headstone in Plate 42.2** — FHG provides a photograph of a tall rounded-top stone with a carved emblem. Its inscription/name is not given in the text. Match the plate to the physical stone and transcribe it in full.
4. **Hampden, Margaret, George and Sophia Evans burials** — Fingal's heritage page identifies these members of the Evans family of Portrane Demesne as buried here. Locate individual markers and confirm inscriptions before associating any visible stone with a named person.
5. **Possible bullaun stone** — Identified within the ground floor of the tower in 1992. Fingal notes that its function is uncertain and records folklore concerning rolling stones for cures. Treat “bullaun” as a qualified interpretation; document its form, dimensions, location and surface wear.
6. **Small carved heads on tower walls** — Fingal's current heritage page specifically points out small carved heads on two walls of the tower. These are high-priority overlooked details: record each separately with wall orientation, height, scale and close-up images.
7. **Reused architectural features** — FHG notes reused architectural elements throughout the graveyard. Map and photograph each feature in context before assigning a source building or date.
8. **IHS and Lamb of God motifs** — Several headstones carry these motifs. Catalogue each stone individually and record motif type, position, carving technique and condition.
9. **Undecorated markers** — Many stones may be earlier in date, but their plain form alone cannot establish medieval or early-medieval age.

### Archaeology and conservation
- Archaeological excavation before installation of lighting uncovered medieval and post-medieval pottery and fragments of flint and shell. These are excavation finds, not objects proven to remain exposed in the graveyard.
- FHG 2008 rated the graveyard relatively good and no longer in use. It recorded broken stones south of the church, vegetation within the church, significant leaning slabs including the mid-18th-century Mackian stone, a broken table tomb inside the church and strap-pointing on the south wall.
- FHG recommended stability assessment, recording and retention of loose cut-stone fragments, appropriate repair of church wall tops and suitable pointing materials.
- Fingal County Council undertook conservation works at St Catherine's Church in 2021. Do not assume the 2008 defects remain unchanged; compare current condition against post-2021 documentation and field photography.
- The church and graveyard are separate archaeological components; keep the possible bullaun as a distinct architectural/archaeological object within the church, not as a grave marker.
- No cleaning, ivy removal, resetting, excavation or movement of stones by archive fieldworkers. Seek permissions and specialist advice.

### Fieldwork priorities
- Photograph both carved heads high on the tower walls, with orientation and scale.
- Locate the possible bullaun in the tower ground floor and record its exact form without assuming function.
- Record Patt Mackian's 1743 pillar stone and unfinished IHS/cross stone.
- Match the mid-19th-century FHG plate to the present headstone.
- Photograph parapet curves, battlements, roof scar, window openings, dressed quoins, reused stone and interior burial context from permitted positions.
- Locate and verify Evans family memorials against burial records.
- Compare with 2021 conservation photographs/reports before writing any current condition statement.

## 2. Kenure (Church of Ireland) — FHG 43

### Identity and location
- FHG reference 43; survey dated 26 July 2008.
- Address: Skerries Road, Rush, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG coordinate fields: X 326664, Y 254942. FHG does not state the coordinate reference system. Do not label this as ITM without verification.
- NIAH church reference 11324026; RPS 260. FHG records no RMP number for this CofI churchyard.
- Denomination Church of Ireland; ownership recorded as Representative Church Body.
- Church stands on the east side of the R128. FHG describes the graveyard as no longer in use and the burial area as a very small part of the overall churchyard.
- NIAH coordinate values 326663, 254952 are close to, but not identical with, FHG survey values. Preserve source and coordinate metadata separately.

### Church architecture and history
- Built in 1866 for Sir William Henry Roger Palmer / the Palmer family, designed by James Edward Rogers and consecrated 4 December 1866.
- Four-bay nave with single-bay east apse and adjoining vestry; gable-fronted entrance porch; single bellcote at west gable.
- Snecked limestone walls with sandstone string courses; pitched natural slate roof; lancet windows; rose window to west wall.
- The west-window stained glass was designed by Rogers in memory of Mary Ellen Peel and executed by James Powell & Sons.
- The Dictionary of Irish Architects confirms the 1866 date, patron Sir William Henry Roger Palmer and consecration date.
- FHG text inconsistently spells the architect's name as “James Edward Rogers” in the historical context and “John Edward Rogers” in the architectural section. The Dictionary of Irish Architects and NIAH support James Edward Rogers. Record the FHG typo/variant in provenance, not as a second architect.

### Churchyard and memorial objects
1. **Pedestal and urn monument** — FHG Plate 43.2 specifically depicts a pedestal with urn. It is a distinctive funerary monument and should be photographed from all sides, including base, inscription panels, urn profile and any maker's marks.
2. **Unusual rough-hewn granite marker** — FHG describes an unusual rough-hewn granite marker. It is a high-priority object; determine whether it is a headstone, boundary/plot marker or reused stone from its form and inscription/context.
3. **Celtic Revival cross plot** — FHG reports subsidence within the grave plot containing a Celtic Revival cross. Record the cross and plot separately if the archive schema permits parent/child grouping.
4. **Late-19th-century markers** — The oldest graves in this CofI churchyard are closest to the church. The burial zone is a small area at the east end; most markers are headstones, with individual free-standing crosses and one pedestal/urn.
5. **20th- and 21st-century memorials** — FHG records later markers in the small eastern burial area. Each should be transcribed and photographed individually.
6. **Boundary railings, gate and ashlar piers** — The west boundary has an ashlar limestone plinth wall, cast-iron railings, ashlar pillars and a cast-iron gate at the south end. These are designed landscape features worth recording separately.
7. **Mature boundary trees** — Mature trees define north, south and east boundaries; FHG notes encroachment onto burial plots. Photograph tree-to-plot relationships but do not cut back vegetation.

### Condition and conservation
- FHG 2008 rated the graveyard good, well kept and maintained, but no longer in use.
- It recorded subsidence in some graves, including the Celtic Revival cross plot, and trees encroaching on plots at the north and southwest boundaries.
- FHG recommended continued maintenance, re-erection of toppled headstones where appropriate, careful management of encroaching vegetation including sycamore, and maintenance of railings.
- Reassess current condition; do not assume that 2008 issues persist or have been resolved.
- The building remains listed as a Church of Ireland church in the NIAH. Confirm current parish use and access arrangements before a field visit; the burial ground's “no longer in use” status does not automatically mean the church is closed.

### Fieldwork priorities
- Photograph the 1866 church's west rose window, bellcote, lancets, porch, sandstone string courses, roof and stained glass.
- Photograph the pedestal/urn monument and rough-hewn granite marker in full and detail.
- Locate the Celtic Revival cross plot and document subsidence without touching the monument.
- Record every headstone in the eastern burial area, beginning with the oldest nearest the church.
- Photograph cast-iron railings, gate, ashlar piers and boundary wall; record tree encroachment.
- Verify current access with the parish/Representative Church Body.

## 3. Critical distinction: Kenure FHG 43 vs Kenure FHG 44
- **FHG 43 Kenure (CofI):** 1866 Church of Ireland church designed by James Edward Rogers, east side of the R128, RPS 260, NIAH 11324026, Representative Church Body ownership, no RMP number listed by FHG.
- **FHG 44 Kenure:** older medieval ruined church and graveyard west side of the R128, RMP DU008-00402 (graveyard), RPS 256, NIAH 11324029 (graveyard), Fingal County Council ownership.
- FHG 44 contains the late-medieval church with single bellcote, two 17th-century tombs (Baron of Strabane and Palmer family), the reputed grave of John Connors / “Jack the Bachelor”, a polished marble obelisk and a cholera memorial plaque. These are not objects in FHG 43 and are not included as FHG 43 records.
- FHG 44's nearby St Catherine's Well and the Rush tower house are separate monuments. The 2022 St Catherine's Church, Rush monitoring report is about FHG 44 / the medieval church and graveyard, not the 1866 CofI church FHG 43.
- The similar names “St Catherine's Portrane” (FHG 42) and “St Catherine's Rush/Kenure” are also separate sites.
