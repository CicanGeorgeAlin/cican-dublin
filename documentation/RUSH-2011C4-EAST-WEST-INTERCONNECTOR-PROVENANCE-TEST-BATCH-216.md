# Rush 2011C4 Flint Records — East–West Interconnector Provenance Test (Research Batch 216)

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentation-only; no production catalogue or map changes  
**Question:** Can the 149 NMI `2011C4` flint entries attributed to Rush be securely linked to archaeological monitoring for the East–West Interconnector (EWIP)?  
**Research date:** 20 November 2026

## Executive finding

The East–West Interconnector is a credible provenance lead for the Rush flint records, but the link is **not yet proven at accession level**.

The Excavations.ie entry **2010:234**, by Martin E. Byrne, documents archaeological monitoring for the High-Voltage East–West Interconnector Project under licence **10E0154**. It states that flint artefacts, including an arrowhead and possible blades, were recovered during topsoil stripping of fields immediately north of Rogerstown Estuary, east of the Dublin–Belfast railway line. It also records medieval and post-medieval pottery from those fields and post-medieval pottery/clay pipe from road trenching and Rush village.

The 2024 Park Road, Kenure assessment independently reproduces 149 NMI Topographical Files entries with prefix `2011C4`, all described as flint from townland RUSH. The published appendix does not name the East–West Interconnector, licence 10E0154, the field north of the estuary, a finder, or a recovery context for those entries.

The dates and subject matter make an EWIP connection plausible, but no located source explicitly maps any `2011C4:<suffix>` accession to licence 10E0154 or to the fields described in 2010:234.

## 1. What the East–West Interconnector report says

**Excavations.ie 2010:234 — HIGH-VOLTAGE EAST–WEST INTERCONNECTOR PROJECT, Various Townlands, Dublin**
- Archaeologist: Martin E. Byrne, Byrne Mullins & Associates.
- Licence: **10E0154**.
- Project: underground high-voltage direct-current cable from North Beach, Rush, to Woodland, County Meath.
- The Dublin works included Rush, Whitestown, Rogerstown and other townlands.
- County Dublin works began in late July 2010.
- Flint artefacts, specifically an arrowhead and possible blades, were recovered during topsoil stripping of fields immediately north of Rogerstown Estuary, east of the Dublin–Belfast railway.
- Cable-trench works were scheduled to continue until early summer 2011.

Source: https://excavations.ie/report/2010/Dublin/0021444/

**Important limitation:** This report describes flint finds in a broad project account. It does not list NMI registration numbers, object counts, exact field names, coordinates for the flint scatter, or a direct relationship to the `2011C4` sequence.

## 2. What the 2011C4 catalogue says

The 2024 *Park Road, Kenure, Rush, Co. Dublin Archaeological Impact Assessment* reproduces Appendix 3, “NMI Topographical Files”. It lists 149 registrations beginning `2011C4:`, all with:
- Name: Chunk, Flake or Sample;
- Material: Flint;
- Townland: RUSH;
- Notes: blank/dash.

The published list comprises 133 chunks, 15 flakes and one sample (`2011C4:1983`). The 148 object-labelled entries plus eight earlier Rush flint entries (1941:13–14 and 1946:438–443) reconcile with the assessment's section 4.6 summary of 156 Rush flint finds.

Source: https://consult.fingal.ie/en/system/files/materials/35680/Park%2520Road%252C%2520Kenure%252C%2520Rush%252C%2520Co.%2520Dublin%2520AIA%252024E0330.pdf

The appendix does not provide the original NMI registration cards or explain the `2011C4` prefix. It is not safe to decode that prefix as an excavation licence or to infer the collection's recovery circumstances from the year-like digits alone.

## 3. The likely lead — and what cannot be claimed

The following points support investigation of the EWIP connection:
1. The EWIP project is documented as having recovered flint in fields north of Rogerstown Estuary during 2010.
2. Its monitoring continued into 2011.
3. The NMI group is labelled `2011C4` and consists of numerous Rush-attributed flint entries.
4. The 2024 assessment includes the NMI entries in a list of Topographical Files records, not as finds made during its own Park Road test excavation.

However, the available documents do **not** establish:
- that the 2011C4 prefix means the objects were registered from EWIP;
- that all 149 entries came from licence 10E0154;
- that the entries came from the fields north of Rogerstown Estuary;
- that the arrowhead and possible blades mentioned in 2010:234 are among the 2011C4 entries;
- that the 149 entries represent one site, one field, one collection event or one archaeological context.

**Archive conclusion:** Record “possible association with EWIP monitoring (licence 10E0154), not confirmed against NMI accession records”. Do not state “found during the East–West Interconnector” as an established fact.

## 4. Do not confuse the separate Rogerstown burial-ground investigation

The 2011 Excavations.ie entry **2011:229**, licence **11E0235**, concerns test trenching at a previously unrecorded burial ground in Rogerstown, Rush. Human remains had been discovered during cable-trench excavations associated with the EWIP. Testing identified an early medieval cemetery, associated ditches and a roadway.

Source: https://excavations.ie/report/2011/Dublin/0022348/

This is a separate archaeological investigation/licence from the general EWIP monitoring record 10E0154. The 2011:229 report is focused on the burial ground and its associated features. It does not identify the 2011C4 flint entries.

The 2012 Excavations.ie entry for reinstatement under licence 11E0235 likewise concerns the burial-ground site and says no further excavation was carried out during reinstatement:
https://excavations.ie/report/2012/Dublin/0023209/

**No flint assemblage should be attributed to the burial ground on the basis of these reports.**

## 5. A secondary cross-check

The 2015 EirGrid *Evidence Based Studies: Study 2 — Cultural Heritage* mentions a collapsed cable trench revealing burials adjacent to Rogerstown Estuary, Rush, and cites licence “10E155”. This appears to be an abbreviated or inconsistent rendering when compared with the Excavations.ie project record, which gives **10E0154** for the East–West Interconnector project. The EirGrid study is useful as broader project context, but the licence should be taken from the specific Excavations.ie record unless primary licence documentation resolves the discrepancy.

Source: https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/EirGrid-Evidence-Based-Environmental-Study-2-Cultural-Heritage.pdf

A 2021 Fingal cultural heritage risk assessment independently summarises the Rogerstown cemetery discovery during construction of the underground cable, but does not connect it to the flint accessions:
https://www.fingal.ie/sites/default/files/2021-07/fingal-cultural-heritage-risk-assessment-report_1.pdf

## 6. Recommended primary-source request

The next decisive step is to obtain the original NMI Topographical Files records and the full archaeological monitoring archive for licence 10E0154.

Request:
- NMI registration cards or ledger pages for `2011C4:1614–1618`, `2011C4:1848–1920`, and `2011C4:1933–2003`;
- any field, townland, grid reference, collection date or finder information attached to each registration;
- the registration key or administrative explanation for the prefix `2011C4`;
- object photographs, drawings and specialist lithic identifications;
- the final monitoring report, site registers, finds register, context sheets, field plans and correspondence for licence 10E0154;
- confirmation whether the arrowhead and possible blades noted in 2010:234 were registered under the 2011C4 sequence;
- any accession schedule linking NMI numbers to individual fields or monitored cable sections.

If the NMI ledger confirms the relationship, update the provenance field object-by-object. Do not assume all 149 objects share a single findspot merely because they share a prefix.

## 7. Archive treatment

**Current classification:** Rush-attributed flint catalogue group; possible East–West Interconnector provenance lead, unconfirmed.

- Preserve all 149 NMI suffixes and the separate Sample classification.
- Keep the 1941 and 1946 NMI flint registrations distinct.
- Keep licence 10E0154 (EWIP monitoring) separate from licence 11E0235 (Rogerstown burial-ground testing).
- Do not place 149 map pins or assign the estuary field location to the group.
- Do not describe the group as a single knapping site, workshop, occupation site or dated prehistoric assemblage.
- Do not equate the arrowhead and possible blades in the 2010 project summary with any particular 2011C4 registration.

---

**Batch 216 conclusion:** The East–West Interconnector monitoring is now the leading provenance hypothesis for the Rush 2011C4 flint group because the project explicitly recovered flint in the wider Rogerstown area in 2010–2011. But no source currently ties the NMI accession numbers to licence 10E0154. The archive must preserve that distinction until the original museum and excavation records are checked.
