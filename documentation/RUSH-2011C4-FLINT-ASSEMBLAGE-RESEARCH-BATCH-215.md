# Rush 2011C4 Flint Assemblage — Research Batch 215

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentation-only; no production catalogue or map changes  
**Primary subject:** National Museum of Ireland Topographical Files references beginning `2011C4:`, attributed to the townland of Rush  
**Research date:** 20 November 2026

## Executive finding

The 2024 *Park Road, Kenure, Rush, Co. Dublin Archaeological Impact Assessment* reproduces 149 entries under the NMI registration prefix `2011C4` in its Appendix 3. Every entry is assigned to townland **RUSH** and material **Flint**. The catalogue labels are:

| Catalogue label | Number of entries |
|---|---:|
| Chunk | 133 |
| Flake | 15 |
| Sample | 1 |
| **Total** | **149** |

The single entry labelled **Sample** is `2011C4:1983`. It should remain a separate catalogue category and should not automatically be counted as an artefact. Excluding that sample leaves 148 entries labelled as flint objects.

The same 2024 assessment's section 4.6 reports 156 Rush flint finds, described as flakes, chunks, cores or debitage. Appendix 3 also lists eight earlier Rush flint records: `1941:13–14` (two) and `1946:438–443` (six). The arithmetic is consistent: 148 2011C4 object entries + 8 earlier entries = 156. This is a useful internal cross-check, not independent confirmation of the underlying NMI archive.

The report explains that the Topographical Files were searched for the environs of the proposed development and records 256 artefact entries in the wider area, including 162 assigned to Rush. It describes the overwhelming majority of the Rush finds (156) as flint fragments classified as flakes, chunks, cores or debitage. The report does **not** say that all the Rush objects were found within the 2024 Park Road development site. Indeed, it states that the Topographical Files were searched for the surrounding area and that no previous excavation had taken place within the subject area itself.

**Unlisted intervals:** `1619–1847` and `1921–1932` do not appear in the reproduced Rush list. The report does not explain whether these registration numbers belong to other sites, other material, or records not selected for this appendix. Their absence must not be treated as proof that the numbers were never issued.

The catalogue's “chunk”, “flake” and “sample” are reproduced as written. No attempt is made to reinterpret the pieces technologically or typologically.

## What the evidence establishes

1. **A substantial recorded flint collection is attributed to Rush.** The 2024 report transcribes 149 2011C4 entries, all marked as flint and Rush.
2. **The recorded object classes are heavily weighted towards chunks.** 133 of the 149 entries are called chunks; 15 are called flakes; one is a sample.
3. **The 2011C4 records are distinct catalogue entries.** Each suffix number should be retained as an individual NMI reference rather than collapsed into one undifferentiated object.
4. **The report's summary count can be reconciled.** Excluding the one sample entry, the 2011C4 list has 148 flint object entries. Together with the eight earlier Rush flint entries (1941:13–14 and 1946:438–443), this equals the 156 flint finds stated in §4.6.
5. **The 2024 test excavation is not the source of these artefacts.** The assessment says the Topographical Files were searched as a desk-based source and separately records that its own four test trenches under licence 24E0330 found no archaeological objects or features.

## What remains unproven

The available published transcription does not provide:
- the exact findspot, field, road corridor or coordinates for the 2011C4 pieces;
- the identity of the finder, excavator, collector or reporting archaeologist;
- the circumstances or date of recovery;
- whether all suffixes belong to one field collection, one excavation, multiple contexts or more than one collecting event;
- stratigraphic contexts, feature numbers, trench numbers or sample relationships;
- artefact dimensions, flint source, cortex details, reduction sequence, retouch, use-wear, chronology or specialist lithic analysis;
- photographs or drawings of the individual objects;
- current custody or display status.

The accession prefix `2011C4` is reproduced as an NMI registration string. The assessment does not explain its administrative meaning or identify a corresponding excavation licence. It must not be silently treated as an excavation licence number.

## Context check: nearby 2010–2011 works

The Excavations.ie summary for **2010:234**, the High-Voltage East–West Interconnector Project, reports recovery of flint artefacts (including an arrowhead and possible blades) during topsoil stripping of fields immediately north of Rogerstown Estuary, east of the Dublin–Belfast railway. It also notes finds from road-trench work in Rush. The project was scheduled to continue into 2011.

Public record: https://excavations.ie/report/2010/Dublin/0021444/

A separate Excavations.ie record, **2011:229 / licence 11E0235**, concerns test trenching at a burial-ground discovery in Rogerstown, Rush, beside the estuary. It documents human burials and associated features, not the 2011C4 flint catalogue.

Public record: https://excavations.ie/report/2011/Dublin/0022348/

These records demonstrate archaeological fieldwork and flint recovery in the broader Rush–Rogerstown area around the relevant period. However, neither source, as reviewed here, identifies the NMI 2011C4 suffixes. The 2010 interconnector flint finds are therefore a **possible research lead only**, not an established provenance for this assemblage. The 2011 burial-ground test excavation must not be conflated with the flint group.

## Interpretation limits

The quantity and predominance of chunks may justify a future specialist review of the collection, but the present catalogue transcription alone cannot establish a knapping floor, workshop, settlement, prehistoric activity phase, or single coherent assemblage. “Chunk” is a catalogue label, not a demonstrated technological diagnosis. The entries should remain described as **NMI-recorded flint objects from Rush** until primary archive records or specialist reports add context.

No period is assigned. No cultural attribution is made. No archaeological feature or site is inferred from the list.

## Archive treatment

**Recommended status:** Research lead / object-group record, not yet a mapped heritage place.

- Preserve each complete NMI reference exactly as transcribed.
- Retain the three catalogue classes separately.
- Keep `2011C4:1983` as “Sample”.
- Do not generate 149 public-facing pins: the published transcription supplies townland-level attribution only, not object coordinates.
- Do not merge these records with the 1941 or 1946 flint entries. Those are separate NMI registrations.
- Do not attribute the group to the 2010–2011 East–West Interconnector or to licence 11E0235 without primary evidence.
- Do not infer a prehistoric date or manufacturing activity from the generic labels.

## Priority next research action

Request the underlying NMI Topographical File records for the full `2011C4` sequence, especially the entries reproduced as Rush suffixes 1614–1618, 1848–1920 and 1933–2003. Ask the National Museum of Ireland / National Monuments Service archive for the registration ledger, original finder or site details, any associated excavation or collection report, object photographs, and the meaning of the `2011C4` prefix. In parallel, inspect the full 2010 East–West Interconnector archaeological report and archive records to test (not assume) whether any of those finds correspond to the NMI sequence.

---

**Batch 215 conclusion:** The published record securely establishes a large Rush-attributed NMI flint catalogue group, with 148 entries labelled as objects and one as a sample. It does not yet establish a single findspot, recovery event, date or archaeological interpretation. Further provenance work should begin with the original NMI file rather than map placement or typological speculation.
