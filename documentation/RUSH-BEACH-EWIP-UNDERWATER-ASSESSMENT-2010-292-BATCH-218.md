# Rush Beach Underwater Archaeological Assessment — EWIP Landfall (Research Batch 218)

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentation-only; no production catalogue or map changes  
**Subject:** Rush Beach foreshore, intertidal and sub-tidal survey for the East–West Interconnector landfall  
**Excavations.ie reference:** 2010:292  
**Licences:** 09E085, 09D014, 09R046  
**Author:** Rex Bangerter, The Archaeological Diving Company Ltd.  
**Research date:** 20 November 2026

## Executive finding

The 2010 underwater archaeological assessment for the East–West Interconnector (EWI) landfall at Rush Beach reports that **no archaeological material, deposits or structures of archaeological significance were encountered** in the foreshore, intertidal or sub-tidal areas assessed.

The investigation included interpretation of geophysical data, visual and metal-detection survey of the foreshore and intertidal zones, visual inspection of the sub-tidal cable route from IMO1 to chainage 600m, and assessment of 18 geophysical anomalies. Positive targets inspected were interpreted as modern or natural seabed features.

The report records bedrock beneath seabed silts at varying depths of 0.5–1.9m along the cable-route centreline, with exposed bedrock rising up to 0.2m above surrounding silts in places. Glacial till was recorded across a 133m section of the centreline. The author nevertheless cautions that archaeological material could remain in shallow silty-sand deposits above bedrock and till.

**Archive significance:** This is a documented archaeological investigation of the Rush Beach landfall landscape, not a confirmed archaeological site or object find. Its negative result must be preserved as survey evidence, not converted into a heritage object or monument.

## 2. Scope of the investigation

The report describes an underwater assessment undertaken as part of the EWI landfall contracts at Rush Beach:
1. Archaeological interpretation of geophysical survey data.
2. Visual and metal-detection survey of the foreshore and intertidal zones.
3. Visual inspection of the sub-tidal cable route from IMO1 to chainage 600m.
4. Assessment of 18 geophysical anomalies identified in the preceding interpretation.
5. Geotechnical assessment of sediment types and thickness across the site.

The assessed area is described as approximately 600m north of Rush village harbour, separated from the harbour by rocky shoals. The proposed cable landfall was on Rush Beach.

## 3. Results and geological observations

The report states:
- No known archaeological remains had been identified in the foreshore, intertidal or sub-tidal areas associated with the proposed landfall.
- All inspected geophysical anomalies with positive targets proved to be of modern origin or natural seabed features.
- No material, deposits or structures of archaeological significance were encountered.
- The seabed was largely free of surface man-made debris.
- The report attributes the relative absence of surface debris, in part, to the high-energy coastal environment.
- Bedrock occurred between 0.5m and 1.9m below the covering seabed silts along the cable-route centreline.
- In places, bedrock was exposed and rose as much as 0.2m above surrounding seabed silts.
- Glacial till was present across a 133m section of the cable-route centreline.
- The report notes that archaeological material could potentially be retained in shallow silty-sand deposits above bedrock and glacial till.

These are observations and interpretations from the 2010 assessment. They do not establish that archaeological remains are present; nor does the negative survey establish that the wider Rush coast contains no submerged heritage.

## 4. Nearby heritage context is not evidence of finds in the survey area

The report mentions the archaeological potential of the wider Rush area, including:
- Drumanagh Promontory Fort, approximately 1.6km north;
- Martello towers at Rush and Drumanagh headland;
- a significant number of reported shipwrecking incidents associated with Rush.

These are contextual references. The report does not identify any of those monuments or shipwrecks within the surveyed foreshore, intertidal or sub-tidal landfall area. It specifically reports no archaeological remains encountered there.

Do not infer that the cable survey located, exposed or investigated a particular wreck merely because shipwrecking is known in the broader Rush area.

## 5. Keep this survey separate from the Rush flint records

This underwater assessment is not the same operation as the 2010 general cable-route monitoring record **10E0154**:
- 2010:292 covers the underwater landfall assessment at Rush Beach under licences 09E085, 09D014 and 09R046.
- 2010:234 covers archaeological monitoring across multiple townlands under licence 10E0154 and reports flint artefacts from fields immediately north of Rogerstown Estuary, east of the railway.

The 2024 Park Road, Kenure assessment separately lists 149 NMI Topographical Files entries with prefix `2011C4`, all described as flint from Rush. No source reviewed for this batch connects those entries to the Rush Beach underwater assessment or its three licences.

## 6. Archive treatment

**Recommended classification:** Archaeological investigation / negative underwater assessment; Rush Beach EWI landfall.

- Preserve the three licence numbers exactly: 09E085, 09D014 and 09R046.
- Preserve the report's negative findings and its stated survey methods.
- Keep the geological observations separate from archaeological interpretation.
- Treat the published coordinate as the investigation's reference location only.
- Do not create a heritage object, wreck, monument or archaeological-site record from this negative assessment alone.
- Do not use the report coordinate as an artefact pin.
- Do not merge this assessment with the 10E0154 flint-monitoring record, 11E0090 Rush North Beach monitoring, 11E0235 Rogerstown cemetery investigation or NMI 2011C4 Topographical Files entries.

## 7. Research value and next action

This report helps define what was actually checked in the EWI landfall area and what the archaeologist concluded at the time. It also gives a useful baseline for any later coastal or marine-heritage investigation.

The next step for the 2011C4 flint provenance question remains separate: request the original NMI Topographical File registration records and the full 10E0154 finds register. For the coastal landfall itself, request the complete 09E085 / 09D014 / 09R046 report and its geophysical interpretation, anomaly schedule, survey track plots and final drawings if the archive's online summary is insufficient.

**Batch 218 conclusion:** The 2010 Rush Beach underwater assessment is a well-defined negative archaeological survey of the EWI landfall area. It records survey methods, 18 assessed geophysical anomalies and seabed geology, but no archaeological material or structures. It must remain an investigation record, not a claimed heritage find or wreck location. The unresolved 2011C4 flint group remains a separate archive-provenance question.
