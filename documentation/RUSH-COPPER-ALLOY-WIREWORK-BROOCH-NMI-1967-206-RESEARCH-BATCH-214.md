# Rush Copper-Alloy Wirework Brooch — NMI 1967:206
## Research Batch 214

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentary research from two assessments reproducing NMI Topographical Files data; brooch not independently examined  
**Townland:** Rush, County Dublin  
**NMI registration:** 1967:206  
**Subject:** Copper-alloy wirework brooch with interlocking circular / abstract spiral decoration

## 1. The 2024 NMI Topographical Files summary

Maeve McCormick’s *Park Road, Kenure, Rush* Archaeological Impact Assessment (2 April 2024), §4.6 and Appendix 3, identifies one copper-alloy brooch registration from the townland of Rush:

- **NMI number:** 1967:206
- **Object:** Brooch
- **Material:** Copper alloy
- **Townland:** Rush
- **Notes:** “Wirework brooch with abstract spiral pattern, some decoration visible on the surface.”

The assessment notes that 162 Topographical Files entries relate to the townland of Rush and that this is the one copper-alloy brooch entry in its list of Rush finds. The report does not give dimensions, a date, a typological class, a photograph, a finder, or a more precise findspot in Appendix 3.

Source: Maeve McCormick, *Park Road, Kenure, Rush, Co. Dublin: Archaeological Impact Assessment* (Archer Heritage Planning Ltd, 2 April 2024), §4.6, p.4 and Appendix 3, pp.15–16.

## 3. Comparing the two descriptions

The 2012 and 2024 descriptions are complementary at a broad level but use different wording:

- 2012: bronze wire formed into interlocking circles.
- 2024: copper-alloy wirework brooch with an abstract spiral pattern; some decoration remains visible on the surface.

Both refer to the same NMI registration, 1967:206, and both identify a brooch from Rush. The wording could describe the same overall construction and ornament from different levels of detail. It does not establish the exact number of loops, whether the circles are complete, the brooch's fastening mechanism, or whether the surviving decoration is original surface treatment, corrosion-related appearance, or another feature.

The 2024 report's “copper alloy” is a broad material category. The 2012 phrase “bronze wire” is the earlier description. Without material analysis, retain both source wordings and do not claim a laboratory-confirmed alloy composition.

## 4. Object type, date and function: what cannot yet be said

No accessible source in this review assigns the brooch a date or specialist typological classification. “Wirework brooch” and “interlocking circles” are descriptive terms, not sufficient by themselves to date the object.

Do not label it:
- Early Medieval or Viking Age;
- a penannular, annular, ring-headed or zoomorphic brooch;
- a dress-fastener of a particular regional type;
- a ritual or votive object.

Those interpretations would require a clear photograph or drawing, measurements, construction details (including pin, terminals and fastening arrangement), and specialist comparison. Abstract spiral ornament is not in itself a reliable chronological diagnostic.

## 5. The Cowhole field: locality versus exact findspot

The 2012 summary records the field name “Cowhole” and a distance of approximately 200 yards from the sea. This is valuable provenance information, but it is not an exact archaeological location.

Further work needed:
1. Search historic Ordnance Survey maps, estate maps and field-name sources for “Cowhole” or plausible spelling variants in Rush.
2. Establish whether the field name belongs to a historic parcel and whether boundaries can be reconstructed.
3. Compare the historic shoreline and field boundaries with modern mapping.
4. Only assign coordinates if the field can be identified from reliable cartographic or documentary evidence.

Until then, the archive locality should read: **Rush townland; field called Cowhole, reported c.200 yards from the sea (2012 reproduction of NMI Topographical Files)**. Do not map the brooch to a generic point along the coast.

## 6. Current custody and image status

The registration is recorded in the NMI Topographical Files, but the accessible sources do not establish:

- its current condition or completeness;
- whether the pin survives;

- whether an object photograph or drawing exists in the original file or museum records.

No securely matched image of NMI 1967:206 was located in the sources reviewed. Generic images of Celtic or wirework brooches must not be used as if they depict this Rush object.

## 8. Archive decision

**Research outcome:** NMI 1967:206 is a copper-alloy brooch from Rush. The 2024 assessment describes it as wirework with an abstract spiral pattern and some decoration visible on the surface. A 2012 reproduction of the NMI Topographical Files gives the findspot as a field called Cowhole, approximately 200 yards from the sea, and describes a brooch made from bronze wire formed into interlocking circles. No exact field location, date, dimensions, image, or current custody has been verified.

**Production decision:** Documentation only pending canonical catalogue duplicate checking and primary-file inspection. This is a promising candidate for a distinct object record because it has an NMI number, material, descriptive features and a named historical field. Do not assign a date, specialist brooch type, exact coordinates, or generic substitute image. Retain both published descriptions with their source attribution.

**Next research target:** Continue with Rush's 2011C4 flint finds. The 2024 assessment lists a large sequence of individually registered flakes and chunks. First identify the complete number ranges and count by object class; then determine which entries derive from archaeological test excavation 2011C4 and whether published reports give contexts, dates, plans or photographs. Do not merge all the entries into one scatter unless their field context is demonstrably shared.
