# Rush Flint Debitage and Flake — NMI 1941:13–14
## Research Batch 212

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentary comparison of two archaeological assessments reproducing NMI Topographical Files data; artefacts not examined  
**Townland:** Rush, County Dublin  
**Registrations:** 1941:13 and 1941:14  
**Subject:** One flint debitage entry and one flint flake entry

## 1. Individual registration breakdown

The 2024 *Park Road, Kenure, Rush* Archaeological Impact Assessment reproduces an Appendix 3 table headed “NMI Topographical Files”. It identifies the two records separately:

| NMI registration | Recorded name | Material | Townland | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1941:13 | Debitage | Flint | Rush | None |
| 1941:14 | Flake | Flint | Rush | None |

This is a more precise breakdown than the earlier grouped reference “two flints”. The two registrations should remain separate archive identities: one is recorded as debitage, the other as a flake.

Source: Maeve McCormick, *Park Road, Kenure, Rush, Co. Dublin: Archaeological Impact Assessment* (Archer Heritage Planning Ltd, 2 April 2024), Appendix 3, p.15.

## 2. Findspot detail in the 2012 NMI-files summary

The Greater Dublin Drainage *Alternative Sites Assessment – Phase Two* reproduces a grouped NMI Topographical Files entry:

- **Museum numbers:** 1941:13 and 14
- **Townland:** Rush
- **Find:** Two flints
- **Find place:** “Cliff underneath cairn on coast to north of Rush”
- **Description:** None

This gives a significantly more informative locality description than the 2024 table, whose Notes column contains only a dash. The 2024 table does not include a separate find-place column, so its omission should not be treated as a direct contradiction or correction of the 2012 location statement.

The 2012 entry attributes the cliff-and-cairn locality to the paired registrations collectively. It does not specify which of the two artefacts was found at which exact point, nor does it provide a map reference, named cairn, cliff name or coordinates.

Source: Greater Dublin Drainage, *Alternative Sites Assessment – Phase Two: Sites Assessment and Route Selection Report*, Cultural Heritage Appendix, Appendix 6.2.3, p.10 (printed appendix pagination); see also §6.3.3, p.16.

## 3. Relationship to the 1946 Rush flint group

The same 2012 assessment discusses both 1941:13–14 and 1946:438–443 as small quantities of worked flint recorded from Rush. However, it lists the two groups separately:
- 1941:13–14 — two flints; cliff underneath a cairn on the coast north of Rush.
- 1946:438–443 — flint flakes and chunks; find place “No detail”.

The 2024 assessment separately classifies the 1946 group as two chunks, three flakes and one core. It does not establish that those six objects came from the same cliff or cairn as the 1941 finds.

Therefore the 1941 pair has a published coastal-cairn locality, while the 1946 group remains without a detailed find place. Do not transfer the 1941 locality to the 1946 registrations.

## 4. Does the cairn have a known identity?

The accessible NMI-files summary does not name the cairn. Rush has other recorded prehistoric monuments, including the passage tomb/cairn complex known as Knocklea or Giant’s Hill, but the wording “cliff underneath cairn on coast to north of Rush” is not sufficient by itself to identify that cairn as Knocklea/Giant’s Hill.

Do not attach the finds to a named monument unless an original NMI file, historic map, contemporary account or other reliable source establishes the identification. The coastal description is retained verbatim as a historic findspot statement, not converted to a precise coordinate.

## 5. Archaeological interpretation and limits

“Debitage” is a general term for material resulting from stone-working. The NMI summary does not say whether 1941:13 comprises one fragment or multiple pieces, nor does it provide technological attributes, raw-material source, dimensions or evidence of retouch/use-wear.

The 1941:14 entry is simply “Flake”. No diagnostic features or typological date are given.

The two flints may be consistent with prehistoric activity in the wider Rush coastal landscape, but the accessible records do not establish:
- a date or archaeological period for either item;
- a knapping event or workshop;
- whether the objects were found together in one deposit;
- whether they are in situ or redeposited;
- their precise positions below the cliff or cairn;
- a direct relationship to a passage tomb or burial.

The 2012 assessment's broader discussion of prehistoric flint finds in the region is contextual, not an object-specific dating assessment.

## 6. What remains unknown

For each registration, the accessible summaries do not give:
- exact find date, finder or landowner;
- precise cliff, cairn or field location;
- map reference or coordinates;
- exact number of pieces within the debitage record;
- flake dimensions, morphology or technology;
- flint variety or geological source;
- condition, photographs or drawings;
- current collection location and custody.

The original NMI Topographical Files for 1941:13 and 1941:14 should be requested separately, asking whether the paired findspot description can be tied to a named cairn or historic map and whether the two records derive from a single recovery event.

## 7. Archive decision

**Research outcome:** Two distinct Rush flint registrations are documented: 1941:13 (debitage) and 1941:14 (flake). The 2012 reproduction of the NMI Topographical Files describes them collectively as two flints found at a cliff underneath a cairn on the coast north of Rush. The 2024 assessment confirms their individual object labels but does not repeat the findspot detail.

**Production decision:** Documentation only pending canonical catalogue duplicate checks and primary-file inspection. Preserve two separate object registrations and one shared historical locality statement. Do not identify the cairn as Knocklea/Giant’s Hill, assign precise coordinates, infer a workshop, or transfer this findspot to the 1946 Rush flint group.

**Next research target:** Investigate the Rush ribbed bowl NMI 1965:20 and its associated human remains NMI 1965:21. The 2012 NMI-files summary says the vessel and cremated bone were deposited with the museum in 1965, had been found at Kenure House, and most likely originally came from Giant’s Hill/Knocklea Passage Grave. Keep the recorded discovery at Kenure House distinct from the proposed original monument association.
