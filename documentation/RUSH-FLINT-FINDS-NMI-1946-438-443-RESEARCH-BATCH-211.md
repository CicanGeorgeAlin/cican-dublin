# Rush Flint Finds — NMI 1946:438–443
## Research Batch 211

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentary research using two archaeological assessments reproducing NMI Topographical Files data; no artefacts examined  
**Townland:** Rush, County Dublin  
**Registrations:** 1946:438, 1946:439, 1946:440, 1946:441, 1946:442, 1946:443  
**Subject:** Six individually registered flint artefacts

## 1. Individual registration breakdown

The 2024 *Park Road, Kenure, Rush* Archaeological Impact Assessment reproduces an Appendix 3 table headed “NMI Topographical Files”. It identifies the six registrations individually:

| NMI registration | Recorded name | Material | Townland | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1946:438 | Chunk | Flint | Rush | None |
| 1946:439 | Flake | Flint | Rush | None |
| 1946:440 | Core | Flint | Rush | None |
| 1946:441 | Chunk | Flint | Rush | None |
| 1946:442 | Flake | Flint | Rush | None |
| 1946:443 | Flake | Flint | Rush | None |

This resolves the grouped label “flint flakes and chunks” into six distinct museum registrations, including one core. The two “chunk” entries and three “flake” entries are not interchangeable with the core.

Source: Maeve McCormick, *Park Road, Kenure, Rush, Co. Dublin: Archaeological Impact Assessment* (Archer Heritage Planning Ltd, 2 April 2024), Appendix 3, pp.15–16.

## 3. Possible coastal origin — an assessment, not a findspot

The 2012 GDD report states in its archaeological discussion that the Rush flint records (1941:13–14 and 1946:438–443) “most likely originated near the coast c. 2km to the east”. This is an archaeological assessment of likely origin, not an exact location copied from the NMI record.

The same report's reproduced NMI table explicitly gives **“Find place: No detail”** for 1946:438–443. Therefore:
- Rush is the recorded townland.
- A coastal origin roughly 2km east is a published hypothesis.
- No beach, field, dune, cliff, or other exact findspot is identified in the accessible file summaries.
- Do not assign coordinates to the group or any individual registration.

Source: GDD 2012, §6.3.3, p.16 and Appendix 6.2.3, p.10.  
https://www.water.ie/sites/default/files/docs/greater-drainage-dublin/alternative-sites-assessment-phase2/Appendix-6-text.pdf

## 4. Chronology and archaeological interpretation

The accessible NMI summary reproduced in 2024 gives only the artefact labels and material. It supplies no period, typology, dimensions, retouch description, raw-material source, condition, use-wear, or technological analysis.

The 2012 GDD report discusses the flint finds in its wider account of prehistoric activity and coastal flint scatters in north County Dublin. That contextual discussion does not provide a date for each of these six objects. The core and flakes could represent knapping activity, but the records do not establish that they were found together, refit, or form an in-situ knapping scatter. Do not label the group a workshop, lithic production site, prehistoric settlement, or dated assemblage without primary-file or field evidence.

## 5. Nearby and related records — keep separate

The same 2024 NMI Topographical Files table lists separate Rush registrations:
- 1941:13 — flint debitage;
- 1941:14 — flint flake.

These are earlier-numbered registrations and should not be merged into 1946:438–443. The 2024 table also lists numerous later flint registrations from 2011C4, each separately numbered. Those are not part of this six-object group.

The 2012 GDD assessment groups 1941:13–14 and 1946:438–443 in a broader discussion of Rush flint records, but it does not establish that all the objects share one findspot or archaeological context.

## 6. Recent archaeological work at Kenure — no connection established

The 2024 Park Road assessment reports that archaeological test excavation was undertaken on 22 March 2024 and that no archaeological objects or features were discovered within that development site. It also notes that stray finds suggest prehistoric and medieval activity in the wider area.

This is useful modern context but does not locate the 1946 finds at Park Road or Kenure. The 2012 report's coastal-origin hypothesis points approximately 2km east of the GDD land parcel discussed there; neither assessment establishes an exact source location for the six artefacts.

Source: McCormick 2024, summary and §5.3, pp. iii and 14–15.

## 7. What remains unknown

For all six registrations, the accessible summaries leave unknown:
- exact findspot and coordinates;
- finder, landowner and date of discovery;
- whether the objects were found together;
- stratigraphic context and recovery method;
- flint variety or geological source;
- dimensions, weight, condition and photographs;
- technological attributes and any retouch/use-wear;
- archaeological period;
- current collection location and custody.

A primary-file request should seek each registration separately (1946:438 through 1946:443) and ask whether the six records share an original find report or locality description.

## 8. Archive decision

**Research outcome:** Six individual NMI registrations from Rush are confirmed in the 2024 reproduction of the Topographical Files: two flint chunks, three flint flakes and one flint core. The 2012 GDD summary groups them as flint flakes and chunks, states that the find place has no detail, and separately suggests a likely coastal origin about 2km east. That coastal attribution remains a hypothesis, not a mapped findspot.

**Production decision:** Documentation only pending canonical catalogue duplicate checks and primary-file inspection. If later promoted, maintain six separate registration identities linked by a shared research-group identifier only if the source files establish a meaningful relationship. Do not create a single “flint workshop” object, infer a date, or place map pins.

**Next research target:** NMI 1941:13 and 1941:14 — Rush flint debitage and flake. Compare their records with this 1946 group, but do not merge them unless the original NMI files confirm shared provenance.
