# Rush Ribbed Bowl and Cremated Bone — NMI 1965:20–21
## Research Batch 213

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentary research; artefacts and human remains not independently examined  
**Townland:** Rush, County Dublin  
**NMI registrations:** 1965:20 (ceramic bowl) and 1965:21 (human remains)  
**Key issue:** The vessel was found at Kenure House and is thought possibly to have originated at Giant's Hill/Knocklea Passage Tomb. The relationship between the bowl and cremated bone is questioned in the published excavation report, and a later assessment gives a conflicting context label for the bone.

## 1. What the NMI Topographical Files summary says

The 2012 Greater Dublin Drainage Cultural Heritage Assessment reproduces the NMI Topographical Files entry as follows:

| Field | Recorded information |
|---|---|
| Museum numbers | 1965:20–21 |
| Townland | Rush |
| Find | Food vessel and cremated human remains |
| Find place | Probably from Giants Hill cairn |
| Description | Vessel and cremated bone deposited with the museum in 1965; found in Kenure House, but most likely originally came from the cairn at Giants Hill/Knocklea Passage Grave |

This is explicitly a qualified provenance: the finds were in Kenure House when discovered/recovered, while the proposed original source is the passage-tomb cairn. The phrase “most likely” is an attribution, not proof of recovery from an archaeological deposit in the cairn.

Source: Greater Dublin Drainage, *Alternative Sites Assessment – Phase Two: Sites Assessment and Route Selection Report*, Cultural Heritage Appendix, Appendix 6.2.3, p.10.

## 2. The bowl: ribbed bowl 1965:20

The 2024 *Park Road, Kenure, Rush* Archaeological Impact Assessment lists 1965:20 as a ceramic bowl from Rush and describes it as a **ribbed bowl**.

The 1934 excavation report for Giant's Hill, reproduced on Excavations.ie, adds a historical note: a bowl found in Kenure House and acquired by the National Museum of Ireland in 1965 through the Irish Land Commission was a ribbed bowl with simple decoration arranged in vertical panels. Its footnote identifies the bowl as NMI 1965:20.

The source notes that the vessel was found in Kenure House; it does not document an archaeological excavation of the bowl from the cairn. The connection with Giant's Hill is therefore a proposed original provenance, not a recorded in-situ find context.

## 3. The cremated bone: registration 1965:21 and dating problem

The Excavations.ie report says the ribbed bowl contained a small quantity of cremated bone, identified in the footnote as NMI 1965:21. It reports a radiocarbon determination on a sample of that cremated bone:

- Laboratory reference: GrA-14071
- Conventional radiocarbon age: 3440 ± 35 BP
- Calibrated range reported: 1881–1666 BC

The same report states that this date is too late for a ribbed bowl of this type and that E. M. Brindley considered the cremated bone to be a **mis-association**. Brindley places the ribbed bowl in stage 3 of the bowl tradition, approximately 1980–1930/20 BC. The date on the bone therefore should not be presented as a secure date for the bowl or as proof that the vessel and bone were deposited together in prehistory.

This is an important interpretive warning: the museum registration numbers are paired in the NMI summary, but the published specialist discussion questions whether the bone originally belonged with the vessel.

Source: Excavations.ie 1934:015, comment and footnote 87; see also its discussion of Brindley 2007.  
https://excavations.ie/report/1934/Dublin/0028427/

## 4. A conflict in the 2024 assessment's summary table

The 2024 *Park Road, Kenure, Rush* assessment lists:
- 1965:20 — Bowl — Ceramic — Rush — ribbed bowl.
- 1965:21 — Human remains — Rush — Notes: “found in cist”.

This note conflicts with the 2012 NMI Topographical Files reproduction and with the Excavations.ie historical account, both of which associate the bone with the bowl found in Kenure House and discuss a possible, not proven, Giant's Hill origin. The Excavations.ie report does not say that 1965:21 was found in a cist; it explicitly questions whether the bone was correctly associated with the bowl.

The “found in cist” note may be a transcription or summary error, or could refer to a different piece of information in the underlying file. The accessible sources do not resolve it. It must not be used to create a cist-burial record for 1965:21 without checking the original NMI file.

Source: McCormick 2024, Appendix 3, p.15.  
https://consult.fingal.ie/en/system/files/materials/35680/Park%2520Road%252C%2520Kenure%252C%2520Rush%252C%2520Co.%2520Dublin%2520AIA%252024E0330.pdf

## 5. Distinguish this group from the 1934 Giant's Hill cist burials

The 1934 excavation at Giant's Hill/Knocklea documented separate cist burials:
- NMI 1934:432 — necked bipartite bowl.
- NMI 1934:433 — human skeletal remains from the associated inhumation.

The excavation report describes one cist with an inhumation and necked bipartite bowl, and a second cist at the northern end of the cairn containing a food vessel. These are not registrations 1965:20–21.

The 1965 bowl and cremated bone should not be merged with the excavated 1934 cist context. The 2012 assessment's proposed association with Giant's Hill is a separate provenance hypothesis.

Source: Excavations.ie 1934:015.  
https://excavations.ie/report/1934/Dublin/0028427/

## 6. Kenure House historical context

A Maynooth University study of Kenure Estate describes the estate's transfer to the Irish Land Commission after its sale in 1963. Ask About Ireland also records the 1964 auction of the house contents and the sale of the estate to the Land Commission. These sources provide context for the 1965 museum acquisition, but do not establish exactly where inside the house the bowl was found or how it arrived there.

## 7. What remains unknown

The accessible sources do not establish:
- the exact room, date or circumstances in which the bowl was found in Kenure House;
- when or by whom the vessel was first removed from Giant's Hill, if it did originate there;
- a secure archaeological context for the cremated bone;
- whether the bone was physically inside the vessel when recovered or associated later;
- the precise circumstances behind the 2024 “found in cist” note;
- full dimensions, fabric description, condition and current display/storage location of the ribbed bowl;
- whether the NMI file contains correspondence or earlier provenance notes.

The original Topographical Files for 1965:20 and 1965:21 should be inspected separately, with a specific request to resolve the 2024 “found in cist” note and the mis-association issue raised by the published radiocarbon discussion.

## 8. Archive decision

**Research outcome:** NMI 1965:20 is a ribbed ceramic bowl, and 1965:21 is cremated human bone. The bowl was found in Kenure House and acquired by the NMI in 1965 through the Irish Land Commission. A 2012 NMI-files summary proposes that the group most likely originated at Giant's Hill/Knocklea Passage Tomb. The published excavation report describes the bone's radiocarbon date (1881–1666 BC calibrated) as too late for the bowl and reports Brindley's view that the bone may be mis-associated. A 2024 assessment separately labels the bone “found in cist”, which conflicts with the earlier account and requires primary-file checking.

**Production decision:** Documentation only pending canonical catalogue duplicate checks and original NMI-file review. Keep bowl and bone as separate registrations, link them only as a reported association, and preserve the disputed provenance. Do not create a secure cist-burial object, claim the bowl was excavated from Giant's Hill, or assign the bone's radiocarbon date to the vessel.

**Next research target:** Investigate NMI 1967:206, the Rush copper-alloy wirework brooch with abstract spiral decoration. Determine its exact description, find context, date and whether any image or specialist catalogue reference survives.
