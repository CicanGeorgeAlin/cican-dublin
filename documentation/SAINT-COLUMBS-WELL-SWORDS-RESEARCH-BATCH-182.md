# CICAN Dublin Archive — Saint Columb's Well, Swords Research Batch 182

**Date:** 17 November 2026  
**Scope:** Resolve the physical components, coordinate provenance, historic condition reports and folklore associated with Saint Columb's Well, Well Road, Swords.  
**Status:** Evidence-backed research note only. No permanent CICAN object IDs, source-registry entries, visual records or production map pins created because the complete canonical catalogue cannot yet be fully inspected and reconciled.

## 1. Site identity

- **NIAH:** 11343013 — Saint Columb's Well, Well Road, Townparks, Swords.
- **NMS / SMR:** DU011-034013- — ritual site / holy well.
- **NIAH rating:** Regional.
- **NIAH categories:** Archaeological, architectural, artistic, social and technical.
- **NIAH use:** Original use and recorded use are both listed as holy well.
- **NIAH record date:** 26 July 2000.
- **NIAH stated date for the shelter:** 1980–1990; description dates the gable-fronted rubble-stone well house to c.1985.
- **NIAH description:** Freestanding single-bay, single-storey, gable-fronted rubble-stone building over the holy well, with a square-headed doorway approached by steps. The same NIAH record also describes a freestanding cast-iron water pump, c.1890, immediately associated with the well.

Official NIAH record: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/11343013/saint-columbs-well-well-road-townparks-ns-by-swords-co-dublin

## 2. Separate physical components — do not over-split the catalogue

The evidence identifies at least two distinct pieces of physical fabric:

1. **The well enclosure / well house:** an enclosed spring approached by a flight of steps, with a later rubble-stone shelter over the well.
2. **The cast-iron pump:** a freestanding pump, described by NIAH as c.1890 and located immediately north of the well in the archaeological assessment.

The NIAH groups both features within registration 11343013. That does not automatically establish that CICAN should create two unrelated primary objects. Keep them as separately describable components under one site-level research target until the complete catalogue, visual inventory and exact physical relationship have been checked. If a separate pump record is later justified, use a parent-child relationship and independent evidence; do not invent a second NIAH registration.

## 3. Coordinate discrepancy — retain both source values

Two published coordinate sets are present:

- **Archaeological Impact Assessment (AIA), Appendix 1:** ITM 718035, 746569 for SMR DU011-034013-.

## 4. Historic condition evidence and conflicts

The 2024 AIA reproduces earlier descriptions:

- **Ó Danachair (1958):** a flight of steps to the well; the vertical opening covered by a small iron door; the well partly filled with stones and apparently dry; no signs of devotion were observed at that time.
- **Henry A. Wheeler (29 July 1975):** seven stone steps, an iron gate at the top, a position about six feet below ground, water flowing through a shallow basin, and a dark chamber thought to be the original well chamber but apparently dry. Wheeler considered the structure difficult to date and probably not very old.
- **NIAH (recorded 26 July 2000):** lists the original and in-use function as holy well and describes the later stone well house and c.1890 pump.
- **AIA (2024):** repeats the historic observations and folklore; it is not a fresh condition survey of the well.

These accounts describe different dates and possibly different parts of the water system. Do not claim the well is currently dry, currently flowing, currently used for devotion, or restored. Current condition and access remain **NOT FIELD VERIFIED**.

## 5. Folklore and interpretation — attribute, do not state as fact

The AIA reproduces Schools' Collection accounts associating the spring with St Colmcille and the origin of the name Sord / Sord Cholmcille. Traditions include the saint stepping from the church area to the spring, a footprint, and cures for sore eyes or other ailments.

The National Monuments Service record describes the well as traditionally associated with St Columba and records former visits for sore-eye cures. These are traditions and historical accounts, not proof that a sixth-century event occurred at this exact spot and not medical evidence. Keep them in an attributed folklore field, separate from the physical description.

Relevant Schools' Collection references reproduced in the AIA:
- Volume 0788, page 110: https://www.duchas.ie/en/cbes/4428186/4384664
- Volume 0788, pages 123–124 and Volume 0789, page 150: https://www.duchas.ie/en/cbes/4428188/4384892

## 6. Access and photographic protocol

- Location is off Well Road, Swords, within view of St Columba's Church, according to the NMS/AIA description.
- The NMS record describes steps leading down from street level. This is not confirmation of unrestricted current access.
- Do not enter a locked enclosure, climb over gates, or assume the site is open.
- Photograph from a lawful public viewpoint only, or obtain permission from the relevant owner/manager.
- Do not open the well, remove stones, touch the water, clean the pump, scrape ironwork or disturb masonry.
- Respect nearby residents and avoid photographing private interiors or people without permission.

Suggested future field photographs, only if lawful access is confirmed:
1. Overall relationship between the well enclosure and the pump.
2. Gable-fronted rubble-stone shelter and roofline.
3. Entrance, steps and any visible gate or iron door.
4. Pump from all publicly accessible sides, including maker's marks or plaques if legible.
5. Any inscribed plaque, recording the exact wording and location without interpreting folklore as fact.
6. Wider Well Road context to support later coordinate verification.

No third-party photograph has been copied or registered in this batch. The local history page and Flickr result are leads only; no image licence has been established for reuse.
