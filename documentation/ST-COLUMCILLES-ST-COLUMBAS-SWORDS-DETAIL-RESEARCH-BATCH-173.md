# CICAN Dublin Archive — St Columcille's RC & St Columba's CofI, Swords (Batch 173)

**Status:** Research report committed and fetched back for verification. No permanent CICAN IDs, production catalogue records or map pins created.  
**Scope:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project FHG 48 St Columcille's Roman Catholic, Swords and FHG 49 St Columba's Church of Ireland, Swords.  
**Critical separation:** These are separate churchyards and different denominational sites. St Columcille's RC is not the medieval St Columba's CofI complex with round tower. Do not merge memorials, church fabric, boundaries or burial records.

## 1. St Columcille's Roman Catholic — FHG 48

### Identity and location
- FHG reference 48; survey dated 25 July 2008.
- Address: Church Road, Swords, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG source coordinate pair: X 318400, Y 245390. The survey does not specify coordinate reference system; retain as source values without assuming ITM.
- RMP: FHG lists no separate RMP number for this modern RC graveyard.
- RPS 300; NIAH 11349002 (church).
- Ownership: Roman Catholic parish.
- The site is the RC churchyard at the Church of St Columcille, distinct from St Columba's Church of Ireland graveyard (FHG 49) and the adjacent medieval monastic complex.
- FHG describes the cemetery as active in 2008, with headstones, free-standing crosses, obelisks and some family plots.

### Church and churchyard features
- The current church is a modern Catholic parish church; the FHG survey focuses on its associated graveyard and memorials rather than treating it as the medieval ecclesiastical site.
- The churchyard is a modern burial landscape with later 19th–21st-century monuments and a mixture of granite, marble and limestone.
- Memorial types include upright headstones, free-standing crosses, obelisks and railed/kerbed family plots.
- Do not assign medieval dates to this cemetery's markers solely because the parish is named for St Columcille or because it is close to the medieval churchyard.

### Memorials and notable burials
1. **Andrew Kettle pillar memorial** — FHG 48 records a tall pillar memorial associated with Andrew Kettle. This is a key reconciliation target because the earlier CICAN Batch 150 researched an Andrew Kettle pillar at St Columba's CofI graveyard. Do not assume they are the same stone or person. Compare the FHG 48 plate, exact inscription, coordinate, orientation and site boundary against Batch 150. The Andrew Kettle of the 19th-century tenant-right movement may be commemorated in one of these locations; do not merge records without a matching inscription.
2. **Modern family headstones** — Numerous individual family stones in granite, marble and limestone. Each is a separate physical object; transcribe inscriptions and record dates/materials only from legible evidence.
3. **Free-standing crosses** — Distinct cross monuments in the active cemetery. Record cross form, base, inscription and plot context separately from adjacent headstones.
4. **Obelisks and family plots** — Photograph all elevations, inscriptions, railings and kerbs; identify whether the obelisk is a memorial to an individual or family from inscription, not visual form.
5. **Unusual marker forms** — Record any pillar, cross, carved emblem, reused stone or unusual top profile as an individual candidate only after field matching with the survey plate.

### Condition and fieldwork
- FHG 2008 records the graveyard as in use and generally maintained.
- A 2008 condition statement is not a current assessment. Verify access with the parish before photography.
- Photograph the Andrew Kettle pillar in context and close-up; capture the complete inscription, base, top and any rear text.
- Photograph all free-standing crosses and obelisks from multiple angles.
- Undertake a systematic walkover of the older and newer cemetery sections, keeping plot-level location distinct from churchyard boundaries.
- Preserve original photographs and do not clean or move any marker.

## 2. St Columba's Church of Ireland — FHG 49

### Identity and location
- FHG reference 49; survey dated 25 July 2008.
- Address: Church Road, Swords, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG source coordinate pair: X 318345, Y 245355. Coordinate reference system is not stated in the FHG volume; retain raw values and do not label as ITM.
- RMP components include DU011-034001 (church), DU011-034002 (round tower), DU011-034003 (cross), DU011-034004 (graveyard) and related enclosure/monastic remains in the wider complex. Check current NMS record sheets before final object publication.
- RPS 301; NIAH 11349001 (church) and related records for round tower/graveyard.
- Ownership: Representative Church Body; round tower is a National Monument in State guardianship.
- The CofI church and graveyard occupy the medieval ecclesiastical site. The round tower, cross, church, graveyard, grave slabs, architectural fragments and enclosure are distinct assets within a connected complex.

### Early ecclesiastical history and landscape
- Swords is associated with St Columba/Columcille and an early monastic foundation traditionally dated to the 6th century.
- The round tower is a prominent surviving early-medieval monument; the FHG survey notes its location in the churchyard and its role as part of the wider ecclesiastical complex.
- The church has multiple phases, with medieval fabric and later rebuilding. The present Church of Ireland church incorporates medieval remains and later church construction.
- The wider precinct is roughly circular/oval in historic layout, with streets and property boundaries preserving parts of the enclosure. The graveyard boundary is not identical to the full monastic enclosure.
- The round tower, church and graveyard should be represented as separate map objects, with links to a common ecclesiastical-site parent only if the data model supports it.
- The site includes carved medieval cross slabs, architectural fragments and other reused stones; record each only after its current location and relationship to buildings/walls are verified.

### High-priority monuments and memorials
1. **Andrew Kettle pillar / memorial** — Earlier CICAN Batch 150 described an Andrew Kettle pillar at St Columba's CofI Swords. The FHG 49 survey and FHG 48 RC entry must be checked side-by-side. This batch does not create a second object. Reconcile name, exact inscription, material, shape, location and survey plate before any update. The memorial may be associated with Andrew Kettle, tenant-right campaigner and father of politician John Kettle, but identity needs inscription proof.
2. **Medieval cross slabs** — FHG and archaeological descriptions record carved cross slabs in the church/tower and graveyard. Distinguish in-situ slabs, slabs reused in walls/floors and loose fragments. Record cross form, head shape, shaft, ornament, inscription, stone type, orientation and current context.
3. **Carved grave slabs and fragments** — A number of medieval or early post-medieval grave slabs and fragments are recorded. Do not infer date from a cross motif alone. Each piece requires an individual photograph and source/position record.
4. **Round tower** — Separate National Monument; document doorway, masonry, putlog holes, windows, upper stages and cap/roof details where safely visible. Do not create a second tower record if it already exists in the archive.
5. **Medieval church fabric** — Record surviving medieval openings, wall lines, reused architectural stones and later additions as components of the church rather than independent buildings unless physically separate.
6. **Memorial tablets and headstones** — Photograph all inscriptions, carved motifs, family plot railings and unusual markers. Names and dates must be read from stones or reliable burial registers, not inferred.
7. **Ecclesiastical enclosure and precinct boundaries** — Map only where supported by historic mapping, archaeological records or survey; keep the wider enclosure separate from the current graveyard.
8. **Unusual carved heads / fragments** — If present in FHG plates or current fabric, photograph individually with orientation and scale; do not attribute to a saint or building phase without specialist evidence.

### Condition and conservation
- FHG 2008 describes the churchyard and round tower complex as maintained; individual headstones, grave slabs and architectural fragments require current condition checks.
- The round tower is a protected National Monument. No climbing, touching, scraping, cleaning or intervention.
- Any loose medieval slab or architectural fragment must be photographed in place and left undisturbed.
- Current condition should be assessed against FHG 2008 photographs and official conservation reports; do not state the old survey condition as current.
- Public access to the churchyard does not automatically authorize access to locked buildings, tower interiors or restricted areas.

### Fieldwork priorities
- First reconcile Andrew Kettle pillar from FHG 48 and FHG 49 with earlier Batch 150. Compare exact text and image; if only one physical memorial exists, maintain one record with source aliases and do not duplicate.
- Photograph the round tower's visible architectural details and church exterior.
- Photograph every medieval cross slab and fragment in context, then orthogonal close-ups with scale.
- Record the graveyard's older markers, carved symbols, tablets, obelisks and family plots.
- Use historic mapping and RMP enclosure descriptions to understand the precinct, but do not map conjectural enclosure boundaries as confirmed.
- Obtain permission for church interior and any restricted tower access.
- Preserve original files and field metadata; no cleaning, movement or restoration.

## 3. Andrew Kettle reconciliation — Batch 150 vs FHG 48/49
- Batch 150 previously identified an Andrew Kettle pillar memorial at St Columba's CofI, Swords.
- FHG 48 and FHG 49 are adjacent but separate sites. The 2008 survey plate and memorial description must be checked to determine which graveyard contains the pillar.
- The memorial's physical location, exact inscription, date, stone type and orientation are the controlling evidence.
- Do not create a second Andrew Kettle object until the comparison is complete. If two distinct memorials exist, record both with separate permanent IDs and explain the relationship; if one memorial is cross-referenced by two source descriptions, keep one record.
- Do not confuse the memorial with Andrew Kettle's family members or his son John Kettle.

## 4. Duplicate prevention and naming controls

- St Columba's CofI graveyard DU011-034004, church DU011-034001, round tower DU011-034002 and cross DU011-034003 are separate component records.
- The round tower and medieval cross must not be duplicated if already present in the master catalogue.
- Andrew Kettle memorial requires reconciliation against Batch 150; no duplicate created.
- Preserve FHG 2008 coordinate pairs with unknown CRS; do not use as ITM pins without verification.
