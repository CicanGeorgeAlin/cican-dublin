# CICAN Dublin Archive — St Movee's & St MacCullin's (RC), Lusk Detail Research (Batch 161)

**Status:** Desk research only. No permanent archive IDs, catalogue records or production map pins created.  
**Scope:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project (FHG) 24 and FHG 25.  
**Important sequence correction:** FHG 25 is **St MacCullin's (RC), Lusk**, not St Margaret's. St Margaret's is FHG 46 in the official Volume 2 contents. The earlier next-batch note naming St Margaret's was incorrect; this report follows the official FHG sequence.

## 1. St Movee's — FHG 24

### Site identity and location
- Surveyed 31 October 2008.
- Graveyard name: St Movee's; townland: Grange (ED Holmpatrick); parish: Holmpatrick.
- Address: Grange Road, Holmpatrick, Skerries, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG survey grid reference: ITM 322437, 259278.
- RMP: DU005-02404 (graveyard); associated church: DU005-02401. Volume 1 also lists DU005-02402 (holy well) and DU005-02403 (ecclesiastical enclosure).
- RPS 240; NIAH 11310003.
- Denomination: Church of Ireland. Ownership was recorded as private in 2008. The wider estate context is Milverton Demesne; permission must be obtained before visiting beyond public access.
- The graveyard lies in a larger field within a curving boundary interpreted as part of an ecclesiastical enclosure. Geophysical survey provided further evidence for the enclosure's extent.

### Chapel remains and archaeology
- The graveyard contains the remains of St Movee's chapel. Walls survive to a maximum height of about 0.5m and consist of coursed rubble masonry, with evidence of modern rebuilding.
- A large boulder forms part of the north wall of the church and has been faced on its exterior. An opening is present at the western end of the south wall; loose limestone rubble lies around the exterior.
- A sherd of 13th-century pottery was reportedly found in the rubble core of the church foundations during a cemetery clean-up. This is a reported find, not an object currently visible or available for photography.
- The wider ecclesiastical enclosure is demarcated in part by townland boundaries and a stream. Keep the enclosure as a separate landscape feature from the graveyard and chapel.

### The hollow and local tradition
- A hollow within the graveyard is described as approximately 14 feet long by 7 feet wide.
- Local tradition identifies it as the place where a ploughman, his plough and horses were swallowed after he ignored a warning not to plough sacred ground. The reported words include a variant of the saint's name, “St Movee or St Movo”.
- This is folklore, not an archaeological explanation for the hollow. Record the physical depression separately from the story and attribute the story to the Schools Manuscript Collection and later published accounts.

### St Mobhi / St Movee's Well and the bullaun
- A holy well traditionally associated with St Movee is located nearby, outside the graveyard. The well is a separate RMP component (DU005-02402) and must not be treated as an internal graveyard feature.
- The FHG survey describes a bullaun stone set into the boundary near the well. A covering stone is reported to bear an incised cross, possibly representing Christianisation of an earlier monument; this interpretation is tentative.
- A later specialist well entry calls it St Mobhi's Well, associates it with the old Milverton Demesne, and describes a large stone surround, flagstone at the stepped descent and a bullaun set into the wall beside the bottom step. The bullaun reportedly collects rainwater.
- Names encountered include St Movee's Well and St Mobhi's Well. Keep the names as source-attributed aliases. Do not assume the saint is identical to St Mobhi of Glasnevin; the well source explicitly distinguishes the local hermit tradition.
- The reported pilgrimage custom is to walk around the well three times clockwise, take water and make a wish. This is a recorded tradition, not a verified current practice.
- A secondary photographic account gives Discovery Map reference O 2248 5937 and warns that the well is on private estate land; treat this as an approximate locator only, not a canonical GPS coordinate.

### Small graveyard features and memorial types
- A small quernstone is located at the base of the stile in the north-eastern wall. This is a particularly valuable micro-object: photograph its position in the stile, dimensions, surface wear, and relationship to the wall without moving it.
- The graveyard has many small undecorated stone markers, including uninscribed markers; distinguish each only when it can be individually located and documented.
- A mixture of 18th-, 19th- and 20th-century headstones and free-standing crosses is recorded, along with three large kerbed plots and several broken headstones.
- Boundary features include squared rubble limestone walling with limestone coping, a lower roadside boundary with a stone stile, and a second stile in the eastern wall formerly giving access to the demesne.
- The FHG survey rated the graveyard good and regularly maintained in 2008, but no longer in use. It recommended consolidation repairs to stone wall remains and noted broken headstones.

### St Movee fieldwork priorities
1. Photograph the chapel footprint and surviving wall fabric, especially the faced boulder, south-wall opening and loose rubble.
2. Measure and photograph the hollow, documenting its shape and location independently of the folklore.
3. Locate the separate holy well and bullaun stone; photograph the well structure, steps, bullaun and any cross-marked covering stone in situ, subject to landowner permission.
4. Photograph the quernstone at the north-eastern stile in context and close-up.
5. Record the enclosure boundary and both stiles as separate landscape/architectural features.
6. Revisit the three kerbed plots, broken stones and individual early memorials; the 2008 report does not provide a full inscription transcription list.

## 2. St MacCullin's (Roman Catholic), Lusk — FHG 25

### Site identity and location
- Surveyed 27 July 2008.
- Official FHG name: St MacCullin's (RC), Lusk.
- Townland: Greatcommon; parish: Lusk.
- Address: Chapel Road, Lusk, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG survey grid reference: ITM 321384, 254823.
- RMP designation is recorded as “within DU008-010” in the 2008 survey; the graveyard itself was not given a separate RMP number in that entry.
- RPS 301; NIAH 11323003 (church).
- Denomination and ownership: Roman Catholic / Roman Catholic Church.
- The cemetery is at the northern edge of Lusk village and remains in use.

### Church and earlier burial ground
- The present Neo-Romanesque Roman Catholic church was built in 1924, according to the FHG survey. The church has a tower at the south end and a rounded apse to the east, snecked masonry, a pitched slate roof, round-headed openings and a west entrance with a pointed tympanum above.
- The west wall has a triple-light window and a round-headed entrance.
- The survey notes that grave markers pre-date the current church, suggesting an earlier church or burial place on the site. This is a possibility, not a confirmed surviving earlier church structure.
- The 2005 independent cemetery transcription says a previous church was built in 1809 and was declared unsafe in 1922 before replacement. Preserve this as a separate secondary-source claim; the 2008 FHG survey itself dates the present building to 1924 and does not document the 1809 building in detail.

### Bullaun stone
- The 2008 FHG survey identifies a bullaun stone as the only upstanding archaeological feature on the site and says it is likely to have been moved from its original position.
- This makes the stone a priority micro-object, but its present location is not necessarily its original archaeological context. Record current position, orientation, dimensions, basin shape, tool marks/weathering and any evidence of reuse.
- Do not attach an original location or ritual function without independent evidence.

### Late-18th-century IHS headstone
- Plate 25.2 of the FHG survey illustrates a tall, weathered late-18th-century headstone with an IHS emblem and engraved inscription.
- The survey text does not transcribe the inscription or name the deceased. The image should therefore be treated as a distinct, unidentified memorial candidate pending visual comparison and on-site transcription.
- Photograph the complete stone, emblem, inscription, sides and base. Use raking light and high-resolution close-ups; do not clean or chalk the surface.

### Other memorials and distinctive ornament
- The graveyard contains 18th-, 19th-, 20th- and 21st-century headstones and free-standing crosses, predominantly east-facing.
- The FHG survey records recurring IHS and Lamb of God emblems, Crucifixion plaques and winged angels on many 18th- and 19th-century stones. Each stone and each carving should be distinguished individually; the shared motif does not establish common ownership or date.
- A large concentration of Celtic Revival crosses lies east of the church. Record each cross separately where its form, inscription or setting is distinct.
- The survey identifies significant mid-19th-century obelisks, including one commemorating Elizabeth Cuffe, dated 1840. The survey gives no inscription transcription or further biographical details; do not infer family relationships.
- Some formerly standing headstones were lying on the ground in 2008. Their present state and any subsequent conservation work need current inspection.
- Boundary features include a squared rubble limestone wall with concrete coping and a northern extension enclosed by concrete block walling.

### Burial-record lead
- An independent 2005 online transcription lists 472 burial entries and gives an approximate GPS position of 53.529759, -6.170618. This is not an official survey coordinate and should be treated only as a locator lead.
- The National Library of Ireland catalogue confirms that Michael J. S. Egan's *Memorials of the Dead*, Volume 10, includes St MacCullin's (Catholic) Graveyard, Lusk. This is a key source to consult for complete inscriptions and ornamentation.
- Do not use the online burial list as a complete census or assume every listed person has a surviving marker.

### St MacCullin's fieldwork priorities
1. Locate and photograph the bullaun stone, noting that the survey says it may have been moved.
2. Identify the late-18th-century IHS headstone shown in Plate 25.2 and transcribe it fully.
3. Locate the Elizabeth Cuffe 1840 obelisk; photograph all faces, base, inscriptions and decorative details.
4. Photograph each distinct Lamb of God, IHS, Crucifixion and winged-angel carving.
5. Document the Celtic Revival crosses east of the church and the fallen headstones in situ.
6. Compare the visible memorials with Egan, *Memorials of the Dead*, Volume 10 and the 2005 transcription, preserving discrepancies rather than forcing a match.

## 3. Important correction: St Margaret's is FHG 46, not FHG 25

- The official Volume 2 contents list FHG 24 St Movee's, FHG 25 St MacCullin's (RC), Lusk, and FHG 46 St Margaret's.
- The modern St Margaret's Cemetery entry in Buried in Fingal gives coordinates 53.43312003, -6.30005406 and says it opened around 1930. This is a distinct later cemetery, not the next FHG 25 site.
- St Margaret's will be investigated at its correct place in the FHG sequence (FHG 46), rather than being substituted for St MacCullin's.
