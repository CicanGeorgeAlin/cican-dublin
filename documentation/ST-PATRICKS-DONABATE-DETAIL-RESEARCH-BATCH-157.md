# CICAN DUBLIN — St Patrick's, Donabate Detail Research Batch 157

**Date:** 17 November 2026  
**Scope:** FHG 17 — St Patrick's Church of Ireland and graveyard, Donabate  
**Status:** Research-only. No permanent catalogue objects, IDs, source-registry entries, visual records or map pins created.

## 1. Site identity and source references

**Address:** The Square, Donabate, County Dublin, Ireland  
**Fingal Historic Graveyards Project:** FHG 17, surveyed 4 August 2008  
**Survey grid reference:** 322563, 249950 (site-level reference only; not founder GPS)  
**RMP:** DU012-00503 (graveyard), DU012-00501 (church), DU012-00502 (tower house), DU012-00504 (Staffordstown / de la Hoyde memorial slab)  
**RPS:** 508  
**NIAH:** 11336013 (church)  
**Ownership:** Representative Church Body (per 2008 survey)

The current Church of Ireland building dates to c.1758 and occupies an early medieval ecclesiastical site. The 2008 survey notes a 1275 reference to a Grey Friars monastery and a 1532 reference to the church being appropriated to the nuns of Grany. Fingal's heritage page also records that archaeological excavation outside the present graveyard walls uncovered activity dated 661–778 AD, followed by medieval activity. The churchyard boundary therefore does not encompass the full archaeological landscape.

## 2. Distinct architectural objects

### A. Late medieval Gothic limestone doorway — c.15th century

The porch of the eighteenth-century church incorporates a pointed Gothic limestone doorway from the earlier ecclesiastical building. The survey notes an inscription and two coats of arms. This is a significant reused architectural feature, fixed within the church porch.

**Archive treatment:** record as a child/component of St Patrick's Church, not as a separate freestanding monument. Photograph full doorway, mouldings, inscription and each coat of arms separately, without rubbing or chalking.

### B. Tower house — late medieval, three storeys

A square tower house adjoins the northeast end of the church. The 2008 survey describes three storeys and stepped battlements. The official Buried in Fingal entry says it has a pointed-arched opening and original nail-studded door; the Fingal heritage page confirms the tower and private Cobbe family crypt.

**Archive treatment:** tower house is a distinct architectural structure within the ecclesiastical complex, not the same object as the church. Its door and architectural details are children/components of the tower. Do not infer a construction date more precise than late medieval without further NIAH/NMS evidence.

### C. Nail-studded timber door

The original nail-studded door survives in the pointed-arched opening associated with the tower house. This is a particularly strong visual detail.

**Archive treatment:** a component record linked to the tower house, only if current inspection confirms it remains original and survives in situ. Avoid touching or testing the door.

### D. Eighteenth-century sundial

An eighteenth-century sundial is positioned above the doorway at the church porch. The NIAH describes a timber sundial to the entrance porch.

**Archive treatment:** separate component/architectural detail attached to the church. Photograph the face and mounting; do not assume it is operational or give a precise date beyond the source's eighteenth-century attribution.

### E. Cobbe family private crypt and gallery pew

Fingal County Council's heritage page states that the Cobbe family of Newbridge House were the principal benefactors of the church and had a private crypt in the tower; when the church was extended, they added a private pew to the gallery decorated with ornate stucco plasterwork.

**Archive treatment:** crypt is an architectural funerary space, not a grave marker. The gallery pew and stucco are interior architectural details. Access may be restricted; do not imply public access or plan entry without permission.

## 3. Individual memorial objects

### A. Staffordstown / Begnet de la Hoyde slab — 1592

An early grave slab in the porch is dedicated to Patrick Barnewall of Staffordstown and his wife Begnet de la Hoyde, who died in 1592. The survey assigns the memorial RMP DU012-00504 and says it must have come from the old church. The later 2019 Broadmeadow Way EIAR independently describes the slab as an early eighteenth-century grave slab group and records the inscription and two coats of arms; this classification conflicts with the 1592 death date and should be resolved by inspecting the primary monument and NMS record. The memorial itself is a single slab with inscription and heraldry, not separate objects.

### B. Robert Nugent headstone — 1784

The 2008 survey transcribes: “Here lieth the body of Robert Nugent of the City of Dublin, Grocer, died September 16th 1784, Aged 30 years”. It describes the headstone as well carved, with a series of inscribed lines dressed on the back.

**Archive treatment:** strong standalone grave-marker candidate. Photograph front, reverse, edges and setting. Preserve the back inscription as a separate face of the same physical object.

### C. Abbot Cobbe mural memorial — died 1765

A memorial to Abbot Cobbe, who built the church, is located in the north wall of the nave. The survey does not provide a full inscription transcription in its summary.

**Archive treatment:** wall-mounted memorial component attached to the church, distinct from the family crypt. Inspect the original plate and current inscription before transcribing.

### D. Decorative family vault northeast of church

The survey describes a decorative vault northeast of the church, accessed by stone steps and surrounded by cast-iron railings. The official Buried in Fingal profile confirms the feature.

**Archive treatment:** one architectural funerary structure with its access steps and railings as associated components. Do not split the steps or railings into separate archive objects unless there is a clear independent historic significance.

### E. Earlier grave slabs and tower-wall slabs

The porch contains eighteenth-century and earlier grave slabs. The survey also records slabs built into the wall of the tower, eighteenth- and nineteenth-century slabs southeast of the church, and unmarked stones southwest of the church. Only the Staffordstown / de la Hoyde slab is individually identified in the survey summary. Other slabs require plate-by-plate identification and current verification before separate entries.

### F. Broken Heuston cross marker, 1943

The 2008 conservation section records a broken cross marker inscribed “Heuston, 1943” north of the church. This is a specific damaged memorial candidate. Its full inscription and present condition need verification.

### G. Cracked and laminated eighteenth-century headstone

The survey records a broken mid-eighteenth-century stone southwest of the church and a separate(?) mid-eighteenth-century stone described as painted, laminated and cracked. It is not clear from the summary whether these descriptions refer to one or two physical stones. Do not count them separately until the site plates and field evidence resolve the identity.

## 4. Holy wells — keep historical and surviving records separate

The 2008 survey records a holy well known as St Kenny's Well near the site, marked on the first-edition Ordnance Survey map, but says no visible remains were present at the time of the survey. It separately records the “Chink Well” at Donabate, said in local tradition to cure whooping cough.

**Archive treatment:** both are research leads only until their locations and identities are established. Do not place a pin at the church or conflate the two wells. The healing claim is folklore, not medical evidence.

## 5. Broader archaeological landscape

Fingal County Council's heritage page records excavation evidence outside the present graveyard: an early ditch dated 661–778 AD, later structures, drainage ditches and pits dated to the thirteenth–fifteenth centuries, and a post-medieval path and dovecote. The 2008 survey also notes burials may extend beyond the graveyard wall.

These are archaeological contexts and excavated features, not automatically visible public objects. They should be researched as separate monument/activity records only with exact archaeological report identifiers and locational data.

## 6. Duplicate and publication gate

Repository searches for DU012-00503, DU012-00502, DU012-00504, NIAH 11336013, Robert Nugent 1784, Staffordstown / Begnet de la Hoyde and St Kenny's Well returned no matches. This is a preliminary search only. The complete canonical data/objects.json was not available for full duplicate reconciliation.

No permanent CICAN IDs, source-registry entries, visual records or map pins have been created. Site-level 2008 grid reference is not founder GPS and must not be used to position individual memorials.

## 7. Field photography priorities

1. Full church exterior, porch and south entrance.
2. Gothic doorway: full view, mouldings, inscription and both coats of arms.
3. Tower house: all accessible elevations, stepped battlements and pointed doorway.
4. Nail-studded door: front, studs, hinges and frame, without touching.
5. Eighteenth-century sundial: face, mounting and context.
6. Staffordstown / Begnet de la Hoyde slab: full view, inscription, heraldry and any date/condition clues.
7. Robert Nugent headstone: front and reverse inscriptions.
8. Abbot Cobbe mural memorial in north nave wall.
9. Decorative vault, steps and cast-iron railings.
10. Heuston 1943 broken cross and other cracked/laminated slabs, photographed in situ.
11. Do not attempt to access restricted interiors, crypt or tower without explicit permission. No cleaning, chalking, rubbing, moving, straightening or disturbance.
