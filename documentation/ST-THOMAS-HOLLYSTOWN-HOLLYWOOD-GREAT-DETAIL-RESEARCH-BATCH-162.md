# CICAN Dublin Archive — St Thomas' Hollystown & Hollywood Great Detail Research (Batch 162)

**Status:** Desk research only. No permanent archive IDs, production catalogue records or map pins created.  
**Scope:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project FHG 26 and FHG 27.  
**Primary baseline:** Fingal County Council, *Fingal Historic Graveyards Project, Volume 2*, survey entries dated 30 July 2008 and 24 July 2008.

## 1. St Thomas' — Hollystown (FHG 26)

### Site identity
- Survey date: 30 July 2008.
- Townland: Hollystown; parish: Mulhuddart.
- Address: Ratoath Road, Hollystown, Dublin 15, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG survey grid reference: ITM 307883, 243732.
- RMP: none assigned in the FHG 2008 entry.
- RPS: 664; NIAH: 11346001 (church).
- Denomination: Church of Ireland; ownership recorded as Representative Church Body.
- The site is east of a minor road, northwest of the R121, in fields with a golf course to the west.
- Fingal's 2022 draft protected-structures appendix independently lists St Thomas Church (CofI), Ratoath Road, Hollystown, as a late-19th-century church and graveyard (item 0664). A separate 2017-2023 development-plan appendix also lists St Thomas Church (C of I) at Ratoath Road as a protected structure. The 2008 survey does not assign a separate RMP number to the graveyard.

### Church history and architecture
- On 4 March 1869 the Archbishop of Dublin licensed a house called Larch Grove as a temporary place of worship until a new parish church could be built; the earlier church was already in ruin.
- Architectural drawings for the replacement church were signed by Welland and Gillespie.
- Foundation stone laid 28 December 1870; consecration took place 19 December 1871.
- The church is a late-19th-century Church of Ireland building with a five-bay nave, pointed-arched openings, snecked limestone walls and dressed-stone quoins.
- A polygonal bell-tower occupies the south-west corner.
- A boot scraper survives at the entrance in the west wall. This is a small, potentially overlooked architectural detail and a good field-photography target.
- The graveyard contains the Pathfinders Millennium Garden. Treat the garden as a distinct commemorative landscape feature, not as a grave marker.

### Graveyard layout and memorials
- Sub-rectangular graveyard bounded by hedgerow, with a ditch in places.
- Large timber-and-iron gate with granite piers in the western boundary.
- The survey records 19th- and 20th-century grave markers, including headstones and free-standing crosses; many plots have stone kerbs.
- A double plot along the eastern boundary contains two 19th-century headstones with pointed tops. Keep the two headstones as separate candidate objects and the shared plot as their parent context.
- No detailed named inscription transcription is provided in the FHG entry.
- Plate 26.1 depicts a large stone cross monument on a stepped base in a wooded setting. The image is a survey-era reference; its precise location and whether it remains in situ require field confirmation.
- The 2008 survey describes the graveyard as well maintained, with some broken monuments and no major conservation issues.

### Hollystown fieldwork priorities
1. Photograph the church elevations, five-bay rhythm, pointed openings, dressed quoins and polygonal bell-tower.
2. Photograph the west entrance boot scraper in context and close-up with dimensions.
3. Locate the large cross shown in Plate 26.1; photograph base, shaft, head, inscriptions and orientation.
4. Document the paired pointed-top headstones on the eastern boundary individually, preserving their shared plot relationship.
5. Photograph the timber-and-iron entrance gate and granite piers, hedge/ditch boundary and Pathfinders Millennium Garden.
6. Confirm whether any earlier church remains or traces are visible; do not infer a precise predecessor location from the 1869 account alone.

## 2. Hollywood Great (FHG 27)

### Site identity
- Survey date: 24 July 2008.
- Townland: Hollywood Great; parish: Hollywood.
- Address: Hollywood, Naul, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG survey grid reference: ITM 315346, 257673.
- RMP: DU004-02302 (graveyard); associated medieval church DU004-02301; late-medieval holy-water stoup DU004-02303.
- RPS: 161; no NIAH reference recorded in the FHG survey.
- Ownership: Fingal County Council.
- The graveyard lies on the south side of a minor road east of the R106, on a slight rise in pasture.
- Official Buried in Fingal gives approximate WGS84 coordinates 53.55636181, -6.26001765 and describes the cemetery as surrounding the ruins of a large church dedicated to St Canice. Retain the survey ITM and official WGS84 values separately pending GIS and field verification.

### Early ecclesiastical history
- The place-name Hollywood has been interpreted as referring to wooded hills used for worship; this is a historical interpretation cited by Walsh (1888), not an established explanation.
- Documentary and antiquarian sources suggest an earlier church may have pre-dated the Anglo-Norman invasion. The standing remains are later medieval.
- The church was appropriated to the canons of Llanthony in Gloucester at an early date, according to D'Alton (1838).
- The church was in ruins by 1630 and the Civil Survey described the walls of the parish church.
- Walsh (1888) thought it may have been rebuilt after that period because a silver paten used at Naul Church bore the inscription “Holy Wood, 1754”. This is evidence cited by Walsh, not proof that the current ruins were in use in 1754.
- The church is said to have remained in use until the early 19th century.
- Walsh's 1887 description records chancel foundations measuring 30 by 20 feet, nave walls measuring 61 by 23 feet, and a triple-arched bell turret on the west gable.

### Standing medieval church fabric
- The west gable wall of the nave survives to full height; side walls survive to approximately 2m.
- A triple bellcote remains in the west gable.
- Grassed-over wall footings extend east of the nave and may represent the chancel footprint.
- The building is coursed limestone masonry with dressed limestone quoins.
- A segmental-headed opening survives in the east wall.
- Two opposing doorways are recorded at the west end of the nave.
- The late-medieval holy-water stoup (RMP DU004-02303) is on the interior of the doorway in the south wall. This is a high-priority micro-object: photograph its basin, stem/support, fixing, surface tooling, dimensions and exact doorway relationship without touching or wetting it.
- The 15th-century date is the survey's attribution. Verify stylistic dating through specialist review before presenting it as independently confirmed.

### Graveyard boundary and memorials
- Rectangular graveyard bounded by squared rubble limestone wall with coping.
- A laneway leads to the road at the north-west corner, with cast-iron gates at both ends.
- Grave markers date from the 18th, 19th and 20th centuries.
- Many 19th-century headstones have carved Lamb of God motifs; early-18th-century headstones commonly have IHS motifs.
- The earliest memorial described by the official Buried in Fingal entry is inscribed: “Here lies ye body of Peter Flinn who dyed May 3rd 1716 Aged 96 years. Michael Flinn dyed March 1st 1709 aged 33 years”.
- The two named commemorations are on one memorial according to the transcription; do not create two stone objects unless physical inspection establishes separate stones.
- An unfinished memorial bears the partial wording “This stone and burial”. The rest of the inscription and identity remain unknown; record only after identifying the stone in situ.
- A 19th-century headstone illustrated in Plate 27.2 leans against a wall. Its present position and whether it is still leaning require field verification.
- The official survey describes the graveyard as in good condition and still in use at the time of the 2008 inspection.

### Hollywood Great fieldwork priorities
1. Photograph the west gable and triple bellcote from multiple angles; capture the full surviving wall profile.
2. Document the east wall segmental-headed opening and grassed-over chancel footings without disturbing turf.
3. Photograph the late-medieval holy-water stoup in the south doorway, including scale and context.
4. Transcribe and photograph the Peter Flinn memorial, both inscriptions, letterforms, symbols, material and exact location.
5. Locate the unfinished “This stone and burial” memorial and photograph all faces and its setting.
6. Record each distinct Lamb of God and IHS carving, including headstone orientation and dimensions.
7. Photograph the north-west laneway, both cast-iron gates, boundary wall and coping.
8. Compare the 2008 survey plates with present conditions; do not assume the illustrated leaning headstone remains in the same position.

## 3. Cross-checks and archive integrity

- St Thomas' Hollystown has no separate RMP number in the 2008 FHG entry, but is listed as protected structure item 0664 in Fingal planning documentation. Keep the RPS and planning references distinct from an RMP designation.
- Hollywood Great's church, graveyard and holy-water stoup have separate RMP component references DU004-02301, DU004-02302 and DU004-02303. They must not be collapsed into a single object record.
- Hollywood Great's early foundation is based on documentary interpretation; distinguish it from the later medieval standing fabric.
- The silver paten inscription “Holy Wood, 1754” is a historical source claim about an ecclesiastical object used at Naul Church, not a surviving feature confirmed at the graveyard.
- No permanent archive IDs assigned; no catalogue records or map pins created.
- All present-day conditions, exact memorial positions and access arrangements require field verification.
- The complete master catalogue remains inaccessible for reliable duplicate clearance; permanent IDs and production publication remain on hold.
