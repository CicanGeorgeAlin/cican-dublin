# CICAN Dublin Archive — The Ward / St Brigid's historic graveyard (Batch 175)

**Status:** Research report committed and fetched back for verification.  
**Scope:** FHG 52, The Ward, Ward Lower, County Dublin.  
**Primary survey:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project, Volume 2, field survey 25 July 2008.  
**No permanent CICAN IDs, production catalogue records or map pins created.**

## 1. Site identity and location
- Official FHG sequence: 52 — The Ward.
- Address: The Ward, Ward Lower, County Dublin, Ireland; on the north side of the R121, east of the N2.
- FHG coordinate pair: X 309738, Y 244802. The 2008 survey does not state the coordinate reference system. Preserve the pair exactly as source data; do not label it GPS or ITM.
- Fingal County Council's Buried in Fingal portal gives latitude 53.44216593, longitude -6.34943472 for The Ward Cemetery. This is a separate published locator and is suitable as a locator cross-check, not proof of the precise position of every component.
- Townland: Ward Lower; parish: Ward.
- Ownership: Fingal County Council.
- Denomination: unknown.
- RMP: DU011-03902 (graveyard), with DU011-03901 (church). FHG Volume 1 lists the church and graveyard as separate components.
- RPS 660; no NIAH reference recorded in the FHG survey.
- FHG coordinate and Buried in Fingal GPS point appear to refer to the same historic graveyard. Confirm on the official SMR map before assigning final coordinates to the church mound, grotto, niche or any individual memorial.
- Important naming trap: do not confuse the historic Ward Lower churchyard with the separate modern Ward Cemetery burial-record page on Interment.net, which states that its cemetery opened around 1930. The Fingal Buried in Fingal page and FHG 52 refer to the older historic site; the Interment.net statement should not be used to date FHG 52.

## 2. Historical context
- The church was dedicated to St Brigid and is described by FHG as the original medieval church in the parish.
- The earlier history is unknown. FHG says the church appears first as a possession of St Patrick's Cathedral.
- The church probably fell into ruin between 1630 and 1650.
- The church at Ward Lower appears to have remained in use until c.1535, according to the historical account cited by FHG.
- In 1887 only a portion of the gable was said to remain.
- F.E. Ball recorded that by the time of John Rochfort in the 18th century the church was in ruins; Rochfort reportedly made a new entrance with stone-and-brick piers and planted two rows of elm trees in the churchyard.
- The 2008 FHG survey describes a roughly oval graveyard with raised ground, suggesting long-term use as a burial place.
- The Buried in Fingal portal says Dr Egan recorded the oldest headstone as dating to 1720. FHG's own general description identifies the oldest surviving headstone as an early-18th-century stone dedicated to Robert Lacy. Do not automatically equate the 1720 date with Robert Lacy until the original transcription or headstone is checked.

## 3. Church remains — DU011-03901
- The church remains are in the centre of the graveyard.
- FHG describes them as a low rectangular mound surrounded by a retaining wall of squared limestone masonry.
- The 2012 Fingal Field Monument Advisor (FMA) report gives the visible church mound as approximately 14m x 8–9m and 1m high, and says it was dedicated to St Brigid.
- The same 2012 FMA report describes the church as having been considerably “improved”: a rectilinear stone wall, grass ramp built into the south wall, return at the north wall and a stone-built, concrete-roofed alcove at the east end. The wall was 0.75–1m high and extensively ribbon-pointed. It was difficult for the assessor to determine how much masonry was original.
- The FMA report notes possible remnants or a stone dump, overgrown immediately from the southeast corner.
- The FHG and FMA descriptions represent different survey dates and different interpretations. Do not present the modernized enclosure as wholly medieval fabric. Record each visible wall segment and its material/repair phases separately.
- A limestone grotto containing a statue of the Blessed Virgin Mary is located at the east end of the mound. This is a distinct devotional feature, not part of the medieval church fabric by default.
- FHG says the church remains were in good general condition in 2008; the 2012 FMA describes significant modern alteration and ribbon pointing. Neither is a current 2026 condition survey.

## 4. Reused late-medieval window jamb — high-priority object
- FHG 2008 identifies a fragment of a limestone window jamb of late-medieval date reused as a headstone east of the church.
- This is an exceptional evidence item because it is both a funerary marker and a displaced architectural fragment from the earlier church or another medieval building.
- The 2012 FMA report explicitly says “No sign of reused window jamb” during its later assessment. This is a material discrepancy, not proof that the FHG record is wrong or that the stone has been destroyed.
- Possible explanations include burial by soil/grass, movement, misidentification, loss, or a different location/interpretation. These are hypotheses only.
- Field task: search the east side of the church mound using the FHG 2008 photographic plates and original survey position; compare the 2012 FMA report photographs and plan. Do not label any nearby stone as the jamb without a matching profile and material.
- If found, photograph the full headstone context and all faces, with scale; record the architectural profile, tooling, weathering and any inscription. Do not lift, turn or clean it.

## 5. Grotto and Sacred Heart niche — distinct devotional objects
1. **Limestone grotto with Blessed Virgin Mary statue** — FHG locates it at the east end of the church mound. Record grotto masonry, aperture, statue, base, any inscription and relationship to the church enclosure as a separate object.
2. **Marble and stone niche with Sacred Heart statue** — FHG locates this along the eastern boundary wall. It is distinct from the grotto and from the church remains. Record niche construction, statue, material transitions, inscriptions and wall junction.
3. These devotional features may reflect later Catholic use of a historic medieval church site. Do not attribute them to the medieval period without documentary evidence.

## 6. Holy well — separate archaeological lead
- FHG describes a holy well southwest of the church.
- RMP DU011-038 is a separate possible holy-well record in Ward Lower, with a different national-grid reference from the church/graveyard. The RMP list classifies it as “holy well possible”.
- Do not assume DU011-038 is definitively the well mentioned in FHG 52 until the exact location and source map are reconciled.
- Do not create a well pin from the vague “southwest of church” description alone.
- Fieldwork: use official SMR/RMP mapping and FHG plate/plan to locate the well; document aperture, stone surround, steps, water, associated vegetation and access, if it survives.
- No healing claims should be presented as medical fact. If historic devotional or healing traditions are discovered, attribute them explicitly as folklore.

## 7. Graveyard morphology and memorials
- FHG describes an oval graveyard enclosed by a limestone wall, with a gate and stile to the road.
- The interior ground is higher than the surrounding land.
- The oldest headstones are south of the church and are curved-top limestone markers.
- The remainder of the cemetery is predominantly 20th-century and arranged in east-facing rows.
- FHG identifies a wide range of marker types in its general classification: box/chest tombs, cross slabs, free-standing crosses, flat grave slabs, headstones, mausolea, mural tablets, reused architectural fragments, sarcophagi, table tombs, undecorated markers, vaults, iron markers and railings, and wooden crosses.
- FHG's site-specific description focuses on the old curved-top limestone headstones and newer east-facing rows; do not imply that every generic marker category is confirmed at this site.
- The oldest stone is described as an early-18th-century headstone dedicated to Robert Lacy. Obtain a full transcription and exact death date from the stone and/or Egan's 1991 transcription before publishing a biographical claim.
- Several older stones have broken sections and worn inscriptions. Photograph obliquely under varied natural light; do not use chalk, wetting, rubbing, scraping or chemical treatment.
- FHG's 2008 condition assessment says the graveyard was well maintained and the modern section remained in use.
- The 2012 FMA assessment reported that the graveyard walls had deteriorated and showed signs of collapse, and recommended urgent stabilisation and ongoing monitoring. This is a significant historical condition alert, not a verified statement of present-day wall condition.
- The 2012 FMA report says most headstones were extremely modern (1980s onward) and notes only three older stones south of the church. This reinforces the need to prioritise the small historic group rather than treating all rows as medieval.

## 8. Access, protection and conservation
- The FMA report describes access by gate and stile.
- Fingal County Council owns the site. The 2012 management report states that Ministerial Consent is required before works on Council-owned archaeological monuments, with a detailed works plan.
- Do not undertake vegetation clearance, wall repairs, stone straightening, digging, well access works or other intervention as part of CICAN fieldwork.
- Fingal County Council warns that historic graveyards are protected archaeological monuments and that even pulling ivy from a wall can cause damage; maintenance and clearance should be professionally planned.
- Photography should be non-contact and from safe public/authorised access. Obtain permission where necessary.
- The 2012 report's wall-collapse recommendation should be checked against current council maintenance records and an on-site visual inspection from safe access.

## 9. Critical reconciliation and open questions
1. **Historic FHG 52 vs modern Ward Cemetery database:** use the Fingal Buried in Fingal historic site locator; do not transfer the Interment.net “opened c.1930” statement to the medieval churchyard.
2. **Robert Lacy vs 1720:** FHG calls Robert Lacy's stone the oldest; Buried in Fingal says Egan's oldest stone is 1720. Match the original transcription to the stone before joining these statements.
3. **Reused window jamb:** FHG 2008 records it; FMA 2012 reports no sign. Treat as unresolved lost/moved/obscured object.
4. **Church fabric:** FHG calls it a low mound with retaining wall; FMA describes a significantly improved rectilinear structure and cannot determine how much is original. Separate medieval remains from later interventions.
5. **Holy well:** FHG places it southwest of church; RMP DU011-038 is a separate possible well. Do not assign the RMP ID without spatial proof.
6. **Grotto vs Sacred Heart niche:** two separate devotional installations, east end of mound vs eastern boundary wall.
7. **Boundary enclosure:** FHG notes the oval/curving form may denote an early ecclesiastical enclosure. The full line is not confirmed; do not map it as a proven early-medieval boundary.

## 10. Field photography and research priorities

2. Church mound from all sides, including the later retaining wall, grass ramp, eastern alcove and any construction joints.
3. Grotto and Blessed Virgin statue, with close-ups of masonry and inscriptions.
4. Sacred Heart niche in the eastern boundary wall.
5. Search for the reused limestone window jamb east of the church using FHG 2008 plate references; compare with FMA 2012 “no sign” observation.
6. Robert Lacy headstone and every older curved-top stone south of the church; transcribe each line.
7. Entrance gate, stile, wall coping, repair joints, cracks and any displaced masonry.
8. Potential holy well only after RMP DU011-038 and FHG location are reconciled.
9. Preserve original photographs, filenames, timestamps, GPS accuracy and photographer notes; distinguish field-verified observations from source-derived records.

## 11. Duplicate prevention and archive decision
- FHG 52 is the historic Ward Lower churchyard, not the modern cemetery described as opened around 1930 by a third-party burial transcription site.
- Church DU011-03901 and graveyard DU011-03902 are separate records linked to one site.
- Possible holy well DU011-038 is not yet linked to this graveyard.
- The reused limestone jamb is a single unresolved object candidate; do not create separate records for the FHG and FMA references until its identity/location is settled.
- Grotto and Sacred Heart niche are distinct physical objects.
- No permanent CICAN IDs, production catalogue records or map pins created.
