# CICAN Dublin Archive — Tobertown, Balscadden & Holmpatrick, Skerries (Batch 174)

**Status:** Research report committed and fetched back for verification.  
**Scope:** Fingal Historic Graveyards Project FHG 50 Tobertown, Balscadden and FHG 51 Holmpatrick, Skerries.  
**Evidence rule:** The principal survey is Fingal County Council's 2008 field survey. Its condition statements are historical baselines, not current assessments. No permanent CICAN IDs, production catalogue records or map pins created.

## Numbering correction
The official Fingal Historic Graveyards Project Volume 2 lists FHG 50 as Tobertown, Balscadden and FHG 51 as Holmpatrick. St Patrick's Donabate is FHG 17, not FHG 51, and has already been researched in CICAN Batch 157. This batch follows the official sequence and avoids renumbering.

## 1. Tobertown, Balscadden — FHG 50

### Site identity
- Survey reference FHG 50; field survey 24 July 2008.
- Address: Tobertown, Balscadden, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG coordinate pair X 316737, Y 264461. The survey does not specify a coordinate reference system; preserve as raw source coordinates and do not convert or publish as GPS.
- Townland Tobertown; parish Balscadden.
- Ownership: Fingal County Council; denomination unknown.
- RMP DU001-00302 (graveyard); related components DU001-00301 (church), DU001-00303 (holy well), DU001-00304 (grave-slab fragment), DU001-00305 (possible ecclesiastical enclosure).
- RPS 7; NIAH 11304010 (graveyard); archaeological excavation reference 1996:145.
- The graveyard lies in fields west of Balscadden village, on the north side of a local road.

### Medieval church and enclosure
- The church of Baliscadan is documented in 1275 as a possession of the Prior of Holy Trinity.
- In 1630 the church and chancel were described as in good repair; they apparently fell into ruin afterwards.
- Surviving church remains are the northwest and northeast ends. The eastern end is reported at approximately 0.6m high, built of rubble limestone, with an opening in its east wall. The northeast corner survives to about 1.5m and appears rebuilt.
- Grassed-over wall footings connect the surviving sections. Treat the visible wall ends and buried/grass-covered footings as separate architectural components only after field mapping.
- A curving earthen bank/boundary to the east and south may represent an early ecclesiastical enclosure. This is a hypothesis, not a confirmed full enclosure line.
- The raised ground, surviving church masonry and curved bank are separate evidence features; do not combine them into one assumed structure.

### St Mary's Well
- FHG records St Mary's Well within the graveyard and describes historic pattern-day customs: a whitethorn bush, cloth offerings, graves dressed on 15 August (Lady Day in the source's wording), and a traditional belief that the well cured eye complaints.
- There is a location discrepancy within the 2008 entry: the historical narrative calls the well central to the graveyard, while the architectural-features section places it southwest of the church. Preserve both descriptions until the exact well aperture is field-located.
- A modern stone surround is mentioned in the survey, but the line is truncated in the digital text extraction; do not invent its shape or dimensions.
- Healing is folklore, not a medical claim.
- Potentially distinct well features should not be merged until the actual aperture, source map and field position are checked.

### Memorials and unusual grave markers
1. **William and Elizabeth Hamilton cast-iron cross** — Plate 50.2 depicts a black cast-iron cross with circular ring and white lettering. The inscription is reported as “In Loving memory of William & Elizabeth Hamilton & their deceased children RIP.” Photograph the front and reverse, fixing details of the cross, ring, shaft, base, paint/coating and any maker's mark.
2. **Rev. Patrick Kirwin headstone** — 18th-century curved-top marker with IHS motif. The survey transcribes: “Here lies interred the body of the Revd. Partrick Kirwin of ye Chapel of Adam & Eve in the city of Dublin who departed this life ye 23rd of April 1794 Aged 54 years. Pray for him”. Preserve the source spelling “Partrick” as a transcription, but field-check the original spelling and all line breaks.
3. **Richard Landy and Bridget Sweet headstone** — early-18th-century marker with IHS and cross. FHG transcribes Richard Landy of the Ring, died 22 January 1732, and his wife Bridget Sweet. Verify whether the date belongs to Richard alone and record exact inscription.
4. **Other cast-iron crosses** — The survey notes an unusual range of cast-iron crosses. Each is a distinct object; capture form, condition, inscription, paint, corrosion and plot context.
5. **Undercoated stone markers** — FHG notes several markers that may be earlier than the main 18th–20th-century group. Dating remains uncertain; do not assign medieval dates without specialist assessment.
6. **Mounds** — Slight mounds may represent grassed-over grave markers or other archaeological features. They are not confirmed graves or tumuli; map as unverified surface anomalies only after a field survey and with appropriate permissions.
7. **Other memorial forms** — FHG lists headstones, free-standing crosses, table tombs, obelisks, flat slabs, chest tombs, tablets, vaults, railings and reused architectural fragments. Inventory individually from the full survey plates and field inspection; do not create placeholder objects without physical identification.

### Condition and conservation
- FHG reported the cemetery as in use and well-kept in 2008, with grass regularly mown.
- The same survey reported modern headstones inserted into church remains, leaning headstones and cast-iron crosses, and mounds of uncertain significance.
- Recommendations included assessing stability, avoiding further burials within the church body, carefully clearing boundary vegetation and undertaking aerial/geophysical analysis of the surrounding area for possible additional enclosures.
- These are 2008 observations/recommendations. Current implementation and condition are unknown.
- No stone, cross, wall or vegetation should be moved, cleaned or altered as part of CICAN documentation.

## 2. Holmpatrick, Skerries — FHG 51

### Site identity
- Survey reference FHG 51; field survey 25 July 2008.
- Address: Miller's Lane, Skerries, County Dublin, Ireland.
- FHG coordinate pair X 325585, Y 259834; CRS not stated in survey. Preserve as source coordinates, not GPS.
- Townland Townparks; parish Holmpatrick.
- Ownership Fingal County Council; denomination unknown.
- RMP DU005-03105 (graveyard), related church site DU005-03101; RPS 225; NIAH 11311041 (graveyard).
- Located at the southeast end of Skerries village, behind the Protestant church. The cemetery is walled and rises over three levels.

### Priory landscape and tower
- Holmpatrick is on the site of a medieval priory. Tradition and the survey's historical account place an earlier foundation on Inis Patric in the 6th century, moved to the mainland around 1220. This is a historical account, not proof that visible medieval priory buildings survive.
- Medieval priory buildings are no longer extant. Seven fragmentary medieval line-impressed floor tiles were found in a grave and were considered likely associated with the priory.
- The upper cemetery level is thought to occupy the former priory site and contains the oldest burials.
- The surviving square tower was erected in 1819 as an addition to a church building now gone. It is two-stage and crenellated, roughcast-rendered, with a scar of the former nave on its east wall and Tudor-arched openings with dressed-stone surrounds on the first floor's east and west walls.
- Do not describe the tower as a surviving medieval priory tower; the FHG dates the extant tower to 1819.
- The current church to the north was rebuilt in 1868 according to the survey's historical account.

### Peter Manne memorial group
1. **Peter Manne memorial slabs** — FHG records two 16th-century slabs dedicated to Peter Manne, last prior of the Augustinian Priory, who died in 1520. One is near the south face of the tower. The Latin inscription is translated as “Here lies Peter Manne formerly Prior of the House on who soul God have mercy. He died in the year of Christ 1520.” Record the slabs separately if they are physically distinct, and photograph inscription, carving, edge profile, material, reuse and exact location.
2. **Richard de la Hoyde / Delahoyde memorial** — Commemorates Richard de la Hoyde of Loughshinny, died 1587 / late 16th century. FHG locates it at the base of the tower beside the Manne stone. A hole in the stone is associated in local tradition with wart cures; document the hole but describe the healing association as folklore.
3. **Elizabeth Finglas memorial slab** — Commemorates Elizabeth Finglas, wife of Thomas Hussey of Holmpatrick, died 1577. The slab is incorporated into a table tomb north of the tower and has a crest and Latin inscription. Record the slab and table-tomb structure as linked but distinct components.
4. **Richard Farrel headstone** — Late 17th-century stone inscribed with IHS, cross, name and 1691. FHG suggests it may be a reused architectural fragment; retain this as a possibility, not a confirmed original function.

### Maritime and military memorials
1. **Thomas McClerey shipwreck memorial** — The stone commemorates Thomas McClerey, aged 16, son of John McClerey of Portaferry and one of the crew of the *Savage* wrecked at Skerries on 12 February 1756. FHG describes a curved top, winged cherub head and floriated roundels. This is a high-priority visual object: capture the full inscription and sculptural details.
2. **Skerries Coastguard / Sarah wreck victims** — FHG records that members of the Skerries Coastguard crew died during a rescue attempt after the wreck of the *Sarah* and are buried here. Individual grave identities and exact memorials require inscription-level verification; do not invent names or allocate a generic marker.
3. **Unnamed Royal Navy seaman of the Great War** — Standard military-style headstone inscribed “A Seaman of the Great War, Royal Navy 18th March 1918”. Photograph all text and service details; do not infer identity.
4. **Easter Rising / 1916–1922 memorial** — FHG notes a significant memorial dedicated to people who died in the Easter Rising between 1916 and 1922. This wording is broad; verify the actual inscription and whether it commemorates the Rising specifically or a wider independence period.
5. **Shipwreck and coastguard burials** — Treat each individual grave marker as a separate object, while linking it to the relevant maritime event only where supported by its inscription or archival record.

### Cemetery structure and later burials
- The cemetery is sub-rectangular, bounded by a rubble limestone wall, with squared rubble limestone entrance pillars and cast-iron vehicular and pedestrian gates.
- It has three levels. In 1837 the cemetery reportedly consisted only of the walled area around the tower, called the Old Graveyard. The New Graveyard was built around it during the 19th century and extended in the 20th century.
- The majority of markers are 18th–20th century. Modern sections include low limestone or marble headstones and individual timber crosses.
- FHG records a mixture of table tombs, chest tombs, slabs, free-standing crosses, railings, vaults and reused fragments.
- Buried in Fingal's current Holmpatrick page gives a locator of 53.57358631, -6.10495137 and describes the cemetery as the burial ground surrounding the ruins of the old church. Use this modern locator as a cross-check, not as a substitute for the FHG source coordinate pair.
- A modern council summary describes Holmpatrick as containing 15 historic tombs in the Fingal Historic Stone Tombs survey. This is a useful prioritisation lead, not a count of all significant memorials or a statement that all 15 require repair.

### Conservation and condition
- FHG 2008 described the graveyard as well kept and no longer in use.
- It recorded fallen and broken headstones, with some broken pieces reset into the ground.
- Recommendations: continue maintenance; assess leaning stones; re-erect toppled headstones; accurately record the positions of loose fragments before any relocation; formulate a conservation plan for the Portaferry Stone, Peter Manne and Delahoyde slabs.
- Current condition and completion of conservation works are not verified.
- The 2023 Fingal Historic Stone Tombs Project reported 99 tombs across 19 council-managed historic graveyards, with 15 tombs at Holmpatrick. The council report states that 20 tombs across 12 graveyards required urgent repair, but the excerpt does not identify Holmpatrick as one of those 12. Do not claim the Holmpatrick tombs were among the urgent cases without the individual schedule.
- Prioritise non-contact photography of the Manne, Delahoyde and Elizabeth Finglas stones, table tomb, Portaferry Stone, shipwreck memorials, military headstone, tower, gate and wall details.

## 5. Data integrity and publication controls
- FHG 50 Tobertown and FHG 51 Holmpatrick are distinct sites, not extensions of one another.
- FHG coordinates have no stated CRS; do not use them as GPS or convert without establishing the coordinate system.
- Tobertown's possible enclosure and mounds remain hypotheses.
- St Mary's Well has conflicting positional descriptions in the same FHG entry; field reconciliation required.
- Holmpatrick's medieval priory buildings are no longer extant; the extant tower is dated 1819 by FHG.
- Peter Manne, Delahoyde and Elizabeth Finglas objects were researched in Batch 150; preserve existing identities and add evidence rather than duplicate.
- No permanent CICAN IDs, production catalogue objects or map pins created.
