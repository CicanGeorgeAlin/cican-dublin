# CICAN Dublin Archive — Reverend Peter James Tyrrell Memorial Obelisk, Lusk Research Batch 193

**Date:** 17 November 2026  
**Scope:** Identify and document the pyramid/obelisk memorial erected over the grave of Rev. Peter James Tyrrell in Lusk; preserve its inscription, sponsoring body, historical context, date evidence and distinction from the lost Lusk “Idol”.  
**Status:** Research-only. This is a separate memorial object from the lost carved “Idol”. No permanent CICAN child ID, source-registry entry, visual record or production map pin created while the complete canonical catalogue remains unavailable for validation.

## 1. Object identity

**Working name:** Reverend Peter James Tyrrell Memorial Obelisk  
**Alternative source description:** Pyramid monument over Tyrrell's grave  
**Object type:** Funerary / commemorative monument  
**Dedicated person:** Rev. Peter James Tyrrell (Parish Priest of Lusk, 1841–1843)  
**Commissioning body:** Loyal National Repeal Association of Ireland, according to the transcribed inscription.  
**Historical setting:** Cemetery surrounding the church at Lusk; described as erected over Tyrrell's grave.  
**Present status:** A memorial is still identified locally as the Father Tyrrell Memorial / Lusk Obelisk, but current fabric, exact position, material, dimensions, condition and inscription legibility have not been independently field-verified by CICAN.

The historical parish account calls the structure a monument “in the shape of a pyramid”. Local heritage material refers to it as the Obelisk. Both names are retained; do not assume a precise architectural typology without field measurement and inspection.

## 2. Historical inscription — transcribed text

Donnelly's parish history records the following inscription on the monument:

> “This monument was erected by the Loyal National Repeal Association of Ireland to the Reverend Peter James Tyrrell, Parish Priest of Lusk, a Martyr for Ireland. Born 24th day of July, 1792, arrested for seeking a Repeal of the Union on the 14th day of October, 1843. Died from illness produced by his prosecution and exertions for liberty on the 4th day of December, 1843. May his soul rest in peace.”

This is a published transcription, not a fresh epigraphic reading. Preserve its punctuation and wording as the historical source gives it, but verify the surviving inscription photographically before treating every letter as legible today.

The inscription attributes the erection to the Loyal National Repeal Association of Ireland. This is direct evidence of the memorial's stated sponsor, subject to verification against the stone itself.

Source: Donnelly, *Short Histories of Dublin Parishes*, Part XVI, Tyrrell section (online transcription), lines 297–300:
https://www.chaptersofdublin.ie/shortpar/shortpar4.htm

## 3. Date and biography

The inscription gives Tyrrell's birth as 24 July 1792, arrest as 14 October 1843 and death as 4 December 1843. The same parish history identifies him as Parish Priest of Lusk from 1841 to 1843 and says he was appointed on 2 January 1841.

The National Library of Ireland catalogue for John O'Connell's 1843 commemorative broadside gives the subject authority form “Tyrrell, Peter James, 1793–1843”. This conflicts with the memorial transcription's 1792 birth date. The NLI record describes the broadside as containing extracts from the Freeman's Journal, a response by John O'Connell on behalf of the Loyal National Repeal Association, and a detailed description of Tyrrell's funeral. It also notes that the broadside included a portrait described as a correct likeness taken from an original.

**Archive decision:** retain both birth-year records with source attribution. Do not silently harmonise 1792 and 1793. The monument transcription states 1792; the NLI catalogue authority metadata states 1793.

Sources:

## 4. Funeral and burial context

The NLI catalogue describes the 1843 broadside as including a detailed account of Tyrrell's funeral, a portrait and an illustration of his hearse. A contemporary newspaper report reproduced in *The Newfoundland Indicator* (3 February 1844) describes the Repeal Association's funeral procession from Dublin, the chapel at Lusk being selected as the place of rest, and Tyrrell's body being deposited in the part of the church used by the poorer congregation. It reports the coffin plate as reading: “The Rev. Peter James Tyrrell, died 4th Dec., 1843, aged 51 years.”

Donnelly's later parish history says the monument was erected over Tyrrell's grave in the cemetery surrounding the church. This provides a documentary basis for associating the memorial with his burial, although the exact grave position and the current relationship between grave and monument have not been independently surveyed by CICAN.

The 1844 newspaper's reported age of 51 is consistent with a 1792 birth year and the memorial transcription, but it does not resolve the NLI catalogue's 1793 authority metadata. Keep the discrepancy visible.

## 5. Political context and historical interpretation

Tyrrell was Parish Priest of Lusk from 1841. Donnelly's account says he took part in Daniel O'Connell's Repeal movement and was prosecuted following the proclamation banning the planned Clontarf meeting in October 1843. He died before the trial could take place. The memorial's inscription describes his arrest and death as resulting from his advocacy for repeal.

Local heritage accounts describe him as one of the Repeal Martyrs and say his funeral drew nearly 1,000 people. The contemporary 1844 report is the stronger source for the funeral procession and burial arrangements; exact crowd numbers should remain attributed to later local accounts unless verified in the original newspaper report.

The inscription's phrase “a Martyr for Ireland” is the memorial's commemorative language. It should be quoted as such, not presented as a formal religious canonisation.

## 6. Relationship to the lost Lusk “Idol” — separate events and objects

Batch 192 documented the lost Lusk “Idol”, a carved figure described by Austin Cooper in 1783 and reportedly buried by Rev. Mr Tyrrell around 1843–1844. This memorial commemorates Tyrrell himself.

These are two different objects:
- **The Lusk “Idol”:** an unlocated carved figure, external RMP reference DU008-010008-, reportedly buried by Tyrrell; no confirmed burial location.
- **Tyrrell Memorial Obelisk:** a commemorative monument erected by the Loyal National Repeal Association over Tyrrell's grave, with a published inscription.

The shared association with Tyrrell does not establish that the “Idol” was buried beneath the memorial, beside Tyrrell's grave, or anywhere in the same cemetery area. No source consulted identifies the obelisk as a marker for the “Idol” burial.

**Critical archive rule:** never use the obelisk's location as the lost “Idol” location. Never merge the memorial and the lost carving.

## 7. Form, material, dimensions and condition — unknown pending field survey

The parish history describes the memorial as pyramid-shaped; local heritage pages call it an obelisk. No reliable published material identification, dimensions, construction date, architect, sculptor, foundation inscription, conservation history or current condition report was identified in this research pass.

Do not invent:
- Granite, limestone, marble or other material.
- Height, width, base dimensions or number of courses.
- Exact erection date.
- Architect, mason or sculptor.
- A claim that the present structure is wholly original or has never been moved/restored.

A photographic and measured field survey should establish whether the existing memorial matches Donnelly's pyramid description and the local “obelisk” name, and whether the full inscription remains legible.

## 8. Current location and access

Love Lusk identifies the memorial at St Macullin's Church and says it is visible from surrounding public space at all times. The historic parish account places it in the cemetery surrounding the church. These are useful location descriptions but not a measured survey.

For archive mapping, use the churchyard/memorial site as a place locator only after the exact monument position is verified. Do not use the church's building centroid as if it were the obelisk's exact coordinate. Respect churchyard access and any parish/site guidance. Do not step on graves, move objects or use chalk/rubbing to read the inscription.

## 12. Conclusion and next steps

A memorial to Rev. Peter James Tyrrell was erected by the Loyal National Repeal Association of Ireland over his grave in Lusk's churchyard. A parish history describes it as a pyramid and preserves a full inscription that identifies Tyrrell, gives his birth, arrest and death dates, and calls him “a Martyr for Ireland”. Local heritage sources call it the Lusk Obelisk / Father Tyrrell Memorial.

The object is distinct from the lost Lusk “Idol” that Tyrrell was reportedly said to have buried. No source places that lost carving beneath or beside this memorial. The obelisk's exact construction date, material, dimensions, current condition and precise coordinates remain unverified. The memorial inscription's 1792 birth year conflicts with the NLI catalogue's 1793 authority form; retain both with attribution.

**Next research target:** the historic 1753 stone commemorated by Lusk's modern “Christianity Stone”. Establish what the 1753 stone actually was, where it survives, what its inscription records and how the modern marker relates to it. Keep the historic stone and the modern commemorative marker as separate objects unless evidence proves they are one physical item.
