## Research findings

### 1. Beech Lodge, 118 Kincora Grove — DUB-BLD-0080

The official NIAH entry (Reg. 50030335) confirms this is a former Clontarf Castle gate lodge, built c.1820, with a detailed description of its gabled breakfront, canted bays, dormers, chimneystacks and decorative fabric. NIAH provides a valuable identification record, but the search results inspected in this cycle did not establish a separately downloadable photograph with a reuse licence. A recent Daft sold-property page is not an archive source and explicitly provides no property photographs.

Decision: no image added. Continue looking for historic photographs, planning-file photographs with permission, or an openly licensed modern exterior photograph.

### 2. Warrenpoint, 84 Clontarf Road — DUB-BLD-0049

NIAH Reg. 50030185 confirms the c.1840 gate lodge and describes the entrance loggia, roof, windows, gate piers and masonry sphinxes. The official NIAH Stage 4 Ministerial Recommendations PDF repeats the architectural description. An An Bord Pleanála report (case 245926) contains planning context and refers to photographs in the case file, but the inspected text does not provide a rights-cleared image asset.

Decision: no image added. Planning case photographs are a lead only; their image source and reuse rights need to be established.

### 3. Clontarf Castle gate lodge — already-covered record DUB-BLD-0047

Two individual Wikimedia Commons file pages were inspected:

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dublin_-_Clontarf_Castle_gate_lodge_-_20220911164331.jpg
- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dublin_-_Clontarf_Castle_gate_lodge_-_20220911164353.jpg

Both pages identify the subject as Clontarf Castle gate lodge, credit Tdr112, give the date 11 September 2022, and state CC BY-SA 4.0. The files are 1,824 × 3,648 px and 3,648 × 1,824 px respectively. The first file page includes camera coordinates 53.363434, -6.205410 and a direct depiction statement. These are valid reusable visual resources for the gate lodge itself, but DUB-BLD-0047 is already among the covered catalogue locations in the live inventory. They do not prove that separately catalogued details such as the sculpted shield (DUB-STR-0042) have been photographed clearly enough.

Decision: do not count these as new covered locations or duplicate the same image unnecessarily. Preserve them as additional-image candidates for the already-covered gate lodge, subject to checking whether the existing visual record already contains these file URLs.

### 4. Clontarf photographic collection strategy

The National Library of Ireland states that its photographic holdings contain over five million photographs, with digitised material available through its online catalogue. Dublin City Libraries' digital collections include photographs, postcards and other archival material in the Digital Repository of Ireland, and the council maintains curated Flickr galleries. The Irish Architectural Archive maintains a photographic record of thousands of buildings and streets.

These institutional catalogues are major research routes, but their existence does not itself confer permission to republish individual images.

## Rights and counting decision

- Do not count search-result thumbnails.
- Do not assume NIAH, NLI, planning-file or property-listing images are free to reuse.
- Record Commons creator, date, exact file page, direct file URL and licence before adding.
- A photograph of a gate lodge is not automatically a photograph of its separate carved shield, date stone or sphinxes.
- Additional photographs for an already-covered location increase image references, not location coverage.
