## 3. 23 Marlborough Street — NIAH record verified; no accepted photograph

NIAH records a terraced two-bay, four-storey late-eighteenth-century house, built c.1780, with a later shopfront. The description notes Flemish-bond red brickwork, gauged brick window openings, granite sills and surviving sash-window features. NIAH's appraisal identifies it as one of the relatively few eighteenth-century buildings surviving on the street.

The NIAH record provides strong architectural identification and a source-derived coordinate. The public search pass did not establish an independently inspectable, rights-cleared photograph of this exact façade. Search results for the street and neighbouring buildings are not sufficient proof that an image depicts No. 23.

**Decision:** no image added to DUB-BLD-0006. Keep the founder photography brief focused on the full façade, roofline, brick bond, exposed sash boxes, granite sills and shopfront/upper-façade relationship.

## 4. 77 Marlborough Street — specific press photograph lead, rights unresolved

NIAH 50010235 identifies the target as an end-of-terrace two-bay, three-storey house dating to c.1750. It describes the distinctive half-octagonal chimney-like feature on a limestone pedestal and console, red-brick Flemish bond, gauged brick voussoirs, granite sills and round-headed entrance.

A 10 March 2025 *Irish Times* article includes a photograph captioned as showing Gina's 168 Investments Ltd's mortgages in respect of **77 Marlborough Street**, with the photograph credited to **Chris Maddaloni**:
https://www.irishtimes.com/ireland/2025/03/10/chinese-investors-sink-large-sums-into-north-dublin-property/

A separate *Irish Times* article dated 11 February 2025 explicitly discusses No. 77 and identifies it as a derelict former clerical outfitters premises:
https://www.irishtimes.com/ireland/dublin/2025/02/11/the-constant-curse-of-dublin-marlborough-street-is-prime-example-of-urban-neglect-in-dublin/

The article page and caption provide a strong address-specific lead and photographer credit. However, the photograph's pixels were not independently inspected in this pass, the exact NIAH architectural details cannot be confirmed from the caption, and the publisher/photographer's reuse permission was not established.

**Decision:** do not download, reproduce or register the press photograph as an archive asset. Retain it as a permission/contact lead only. Seek written permission from the relevant rights holder and confirmation that the image shows the exact façade before any future inclusion.

## 5. 82 Marlborough Street — NIAH record verified; no accepted photograph

NIAH 50010230 records an end-of-terrace two-bay, three-storey Georgian house over a raised basement, built c.1810. Its identifying features include Flemish-bond red brickwork, granite ashlar basement, graduated window openings, a round-headed doorway with engaged pilasters and fanlight, a granite entrance platform, and wrought-iron railings on a granite plinth wall.

The public search pass did not yield an exact-address photograph whose pixels could be inspected and whose reuse rights were documented. A photograph of the nearby National Model Schools complex or another Marlborough Street building would not qualify.

**Decision:** no image added to DUB-BLD-0008. The founder brief remains a full façade and raised-basement view, plus doorcase, fanlight, railings, granite plinth and basement grille details.

## 6. National Model Schools — official historic photograph lead, direct inspection unavailable

NIAH 50010222 describes the main former college building as a detached seven-bay, three-storey structure over an exposed basement, built c.1838. It identifies an ashlar granite front elevation, entrance porch, dentillated granite cornice, moulded sill courses and a central Wyatt window.

The official NIAH publication *Dublin North City* includes a visual element captioned “Department of Education, Marlborough Street (1835)” and another captioned “Clocktower Building ... (1838)”. The accompanying text explains Jacob Owen's institutional complex and its architectural relationship to Tyrone House. The PDF is here:
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/app/uploads/2019/10/Dublin-North-City.pdf

The search result exposed the image descriptions and captions, but opening the PDF returned HTTP 403. Therefore the actual image pixels, photographic author, exact view direction and precise match to catalogue record DUB-BLD-0010 / NIAH 50010222 could not be independently inspected in this pass. The publication's image reuse terms were also not cleared. The complex contains multiple separately registered buildings, so a generic “National Model Schools” caption cannot by itself prove which structure is pictured.

**Decision:** retain the illustrated NIAH publication as a promising archival lead, not an accepted visual asset. Seek a downloadable copy and confirm the precise building before considering attribution or permission. Do not substitute photographs of other structures within the campus.

## 7. Lower Abbey Street post box — exact official record; no image cleared

NIAH 50010291 identifies a freestanding cast-iron pillar post box installed c.1905, still recorded as in use. Its description specifies a round plan, shallow domed cap, moulded corona, cylindrical shaft and plinth, raised POST OFFICE lettering, ER VII royal insignia and a Handyside maker's mark. Its stated location is the north side of Abbey Street Lower, on the northern footpath of the eastern side.

The NIAH record provides a detailed field-identification checklist and coordinates. Searches did not establish an independently inspectable image that proves the exact box at the exact recorded location and has a documented reuse licence. Generic Dublin post-box images and photographs of other Edward VII boxes are not substitutes.

**Decision:** no image added to DUB-STR-0007. Founder field photography should capture the entire box in situ, ER VII insignia, letter aperture, POST OFFICE lettering, Handyside mark, plinth and current condition.
