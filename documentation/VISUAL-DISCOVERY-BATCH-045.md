## 2. Official inventory evidence

The National Inventory of Architectural Heritage (NIAH) records the following identifying information:

### DUB-STR-0008 — Jervis Street (50010458)
NIAH describes a freestanding cast-iron twin post box installed c.1930, with an oval plan, shallow domed cap, two letter slots, enamelled plaques, raised “Post Office” lettering and Gaelic “P & T” lettering on the central panel. The official location is the east side of Jervis Street near Mary Street. The inventory describes it as a well-preserved and still active example of early Free State street infrastructure.
Source: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010458/jervis-street-dublin-1-co-dublin

### DUB-STR-0010 — O’Connell Street Upper (50010555)
NIAH describes a freestanding cast-iron twin post box installed c.1930, with two slots, raised “Post Box” lettering, framed notices and a central “P & T” Gaelic panel. Its recorded position is at the north end of O’Connell Street Upper at Cathal Brugha Street, in direct view of the GPO.
Source: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010555/oconnell-street-upper-dublin-dublin

### DUB-STR-0011 — North Circular Road (50060629)
NIAH identifies a c.1905 Edward VII cast-iron pillar box outside No. 530 on the southern side of the road. Distinguishing details include the E VII R intertwined royal cipher and crown, fluted frieze, “Post Office” lettering and Handyside Derby & London maker’s mark on the base.
Source: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50060629/north-circular-road-dublin-1-co-dublin

### DUB-STR-0013 — South Great George’s Street (50910076)
NIAH describes a c.1930 freestanding oval twin post box at the junction with Fade Street, outside the South City Markets. It has paired letter slots, raised “Post Office” lettering, a central P&T monogram, curved doors and a moulded plinth.
Source: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50910076/south-great-georges-street-dublin-2-co-dublin

### DUB-STR-0014 — Burgh Quay (50020294)
NIAH describes a c.1905 Handyside cast-iron pillar box at the Burgh Quay/Hawkins Street junction. Its identifying features are a shallow domed cap, fluted rim, “POST OFFICE” frieze, ER VII royal cipher and crown, and “Handyside, Derby & London” maker’s mark on the plinth.
Source: https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50020294/dublin-city

**Decision:** reject for DUB-STR-0008. The image is a potentially useful general Jervis Street context photograph, but it does not depict the target post box and cannot be registered as an object photograph.

## 4. Official transport heritage report — valuable leads, pixels not independently inspectable

An official planning/EIAR appendix hosted by An Bord Pleanála was found:
https://www.pleanala.ie/publicaccess/EIAR-NIS/316272/Environmental%20Impact%20Assessment%20Report%20%28EIAR%29/Volume%204/Part%204%20of%204/Chapter%2016%20-%20Architectural%20Heritage/Appendix-A16.1-16.4.pdf

The indexed report extract includes visual descriptions of post-box photographs and tabulated heritage records. It explicitly identifies NIAH 50910076 as the South Great George’s Street/Fade Street twin box and NIAH 50010555 as the O’Connell Street Upper pillar letter box outside the Gresham Hotel. It also includes a separate record for a Tonge & Taggart post box at **58 Richmond Street South**, NIAH 50110429.

Important limitation: opening the full PDF failed because the document exceeded the retrieval size limit. The search extract exposes captions/descriptions but does not permit a direct pixel-by-pixel inspection of the photographs, nor does it establish image author, reproduction permission or a suitable source licence. The 58 Richmond Street South box is a different object from the archive’s South Richmond Street/Lennox Street record and must not be substituted.

**Decision:** retain the EIAR appendix as a strong archival research lead for DUB-STR-0010 and DUB-STR-0013, not as an accepted image source. Obtain a manageable/downloadable copy, inspect the relevant pages and photographs, identify the exact photographed object and image credit, and clear rights before any reuse.
