## 2. Important new lead — National Library of Ireland Wiltshire Photographic Collection

A targeted search of the National Library of Ireland (NLI) catalogue surfaced multiple 1969 photographs of iron boot scrapers on Harcourt Street:

- “Detail of iron boot scraper with ‘dolphin’ motif, Harcourt Street, Dublin” (1969). Its catalogue note cross-references WIL60[12] and WIL61[1].
- “Detail of iron boot scraper with ‘hawk’ motif, Harcourt Street, Dublin” (1969), appearing in more than one catalogue entry.
- The same Wiltshire collection includes 1969 views of Harcourt Street and a “Row of houses, Harcourt Street, Dublin”.

This is a significant lead for the catalogue's **DUB-BLD-0012 — Harrington Hall, 69 Harcourt Street**, because the official NIAH record confirms cast-iron boot scrapers on the entrance platform at No. 69. However, the NLI catalogue descriptions currently identify only Harcourt Street, not a house number or NIAH registration. The image pixels were not accessible for independent inspection in this pass, and reproduction rights are controlled by the National Library.

**Decision:** do not attach these photographs to No. 69. Treat the NLI items as a high-priority archive enquiry. Ask NLI to identify the exact house number from the original negative/contact sheet and confirm the reproduction rights and fees for the website and any printed publication. If the item proves to show No. 69, it may become a valuable historic exact-object photograph, subject to written permission.

## 3. Contemporary boot-scraper project — Ste Murray

The Journal's June 2019 feature “Remove the street from your feet” identifies architectural and performance photographer **Ste Murray** as the creator of the “Bootscrapers of Dublin” photographic project, begun in 2018. It links to his portfolio and Instagram account. The feature says the project was focused mainly on Dublin 7 and 8 at that time, while the photographer hoped to document more of the city.

Ste Murray's own portfolio page contains a substantial gallery for “Bootscrapers of Dublin” and describes it as an ongoing documentation project. However, the public page does not assign street addresses or house numbers to each displayed photograph. The images are copyrighted and the page does not grant a Creative Commons licence.

**Decision:** do not add any image to DUB-BLD-0012 based on the series alone. A direct enquiry to the photographer could ask whether any images show 69 Harcourt Street or its specific entrance boot scrapers, and whether he would license one for the CICAN archive and a possible printed book. The exact object must be confirmed before attribution to No. 69.

## 4. Exchange Court — stronger source chain, but no rights clearance

**Target:** DUB-BLD-0004, Old Detective Office, 1 Exchange Court, NIAH 50910280.

The Royal Irish Constabulary Forum page is now fully readable. It identifies the Exchange Court detective office and contains a contemporary photograph labelled “Now2 mine.jpg” alongside maps and historical/current views. The page is locked against new replies. The first displayed contributor is “Peter Mc”; however, the image post's separate rightsholder identity and legal ownership have not been independently established. The word “mine” in the image label is a useful authorship clue, not a sufficient licence grant.

The NIAH record independently confirms the c.1830 building, its location at the south end of the cul-de-sac, and the rectangular commemorative granite plaque above the entrance. Dublin Castle's own history page confirms that G Division was located at Exchange Court beside City Hall. These records corroborate the target identity and historical context, but do not clear the forum image for reuse.

**Decision:** retain the image as a strong visual and permission lead only. Do not add until the photographer/rightsholder is positively identified and written permission covers online display and any commercial print use.

## 5. Additional identity checks and rejected assumptions

### 23 Hatch Street Lower — DUB-BLD-0011

NIAH 50110521 explicitly records the cast-iron coal-hole cover in granite paving and identifies the South City Foundry. No newly surfaced result established a close-up of that exact cover. The property-sale photograph remains a general building image, not proof of the pavement detail.

### Harrington Hall — No. 69 is not No. 70

The official NIAH record for No. 69 (50920250) lists cast-iron boot scrapers, a pavement coal-hole cover and lamp standards on the entrance steps. The hotel's own public address is 70 Harcourt Street, and the separate NIAH record 50920249 is for No. 70. Photos from the hotel's website or booking listings therefore cannot be attributed to No. 69 without independent evidence.

### NLI historical boot-scraper photographs

The Harcourt Street NLI boot-scraper images are not yet mapped to No. 69. They must not be treated as No. 69 merely because the street matches. The exact house number remains essential.
