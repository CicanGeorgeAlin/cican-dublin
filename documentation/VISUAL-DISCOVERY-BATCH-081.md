## DUB-BLD-0010 — National Model Schools main college building

Official NIAH record:
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010222/department-of-education-marlborough-street-dublin-1-dublin

The NIAH entry identifies a detached seven-bay, three-storey college over exposed basement, built circa 1838 and designed by Jacob Owen. The description records an ashlar-granite front, elaborate granite cornices and window surrounds, central entrance portico, granite flagged platform, wrought-iron railings and a cantilevered granite stair with cast-iron balusters. The appraisal says the building was designed to replicate Tyrone House and that it was restored after a fire.

Five individual gallery photographs were opened and visually inspected:
- `50010222_2.jpg`: oblique view of the side and rear elevations.
- `50010222_1.jpg`: clear oblique street-facing view of the ashlar-granite front and entrance.
- `50010222_9.jpg`: upper façade window and cornice detail.
- `50010222_11.jpg`: central Wyatt window detail.
- `50010222_18.jpg`: entrance portico, panelled door and granite columns.

These are strong exact-building visual identification leads. They are not imported because the NIAH terms state that website content is copyrighted by the Department of Housing, Local Government and Heritage; educational/private use is permitted with credit, while commercial use requires prior written agreement. No such permission is registered.

## DUB-BLD-0008 — 82 Marlborough Street

Official NIAH record:
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010230/82-marlborough-street-dublin-1-dublin

The NIAH record describes an end-of-terrace Georgian house, circa 1810, now in use as a gate lodge. The description records a Flemish-bond red-brick façade, granite ashlar basement and plinth, round-headed entrance with a painted stone doorcase, granite platform and step, and wrought-iron railings with cast-iron finials.

## Wider historical context — National Model Schools campus

The Department's publication *An Introduction to the Architectural Heritage of Dublin North City* contains a visual and written account of the Marlborough Street educational complex. It identifies the former Tyrone House, the Clocktower Building and the wider institutional composition. This is useful historical context, but the illustrations are not assumed to be open-licensed or interchangeable with photographs of the individual current archive objects.

PDF:
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/app/uploads/2019/10/Dublin-North-City.pdf

A 1878 illustrated article about the Marlborough Street Schools was also located as a historical-image lead:
https://victorianweb.org/periodicals/graphic/32.html
It describes the educational complex and its constituent buildings. The illustration is treated as historical/contextual material only, not as a verified current photograph of the individual DUB-BLD-0010 building.
