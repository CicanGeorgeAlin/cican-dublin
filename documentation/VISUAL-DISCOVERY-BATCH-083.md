## 17 Earlsfort Terrace — DUB-BLD-0087 / DUB-STR-0074–0076

Official NIAH record 50110448:
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50110448/17-earlsfort-terrace-dublin-2-dublin

Three distinct gallery image files were opened and visually inspected:
- `50110448_8.jpg`: wider streetscape showing the building within the Earlsfort Terrace row.
- `50110448_2.jpg`: frontal view of the paired red-brick Victorian houses and their projecting oriels.
- `50110448_7.jpg`: oblique streetscape view showing the target building and adjoining terrace.

The NIAH description identifies the c.1880 house and records the cast-iron boot-scrape, balustrade, matching gate, basement railings and coal-hole cover. The gallery photographs are useful exact-building/streetscape identification leads, but the inspected views do not provide a sufficiently close, independently verifiable view of the separately catalogued bootscraper or coal-hole cover.

A National Library of Ireland catalogue record by Michael S. Walker covers Nos. 14–18 Earlsfort Terrace, explicitly including “Stella Maris” at No. 17. It is a circa 1970–1980 colour slide (NPA WALK134). The catalogue states that reproduction rights are owned by the National Library of Ireland:
https://catalogue.nli.ie/Record/vtls000269590/HoldingsILS

The slide itself was not visually inspected in this batch. Treat it as a historical access/permission lead, not as a verified image asset.

## 18 Earlsfort Terrace — DUB-BLD-0090 / DUB-STR-0082–0084

Official NIAH record 50110449:
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50110449/18-earlsfort-terrace-dublin-2-dublin

Five individual NIAH gallery images were opened and visually inspected:
- `50110449_8.jpg`: broad street view of the building and neighbouring terrace.
- `50110449_2.jpg`: frontal view of the paired red-brick Victorian houses and oriels.
- `50110449_1.jpg`: frontal view of the target house and its façade.
- `50110449_5.jpg`: close view of the entrance door, brick surround, granite steps and threshold.
- `50110449_7.jpg`: oblique streetscape view showing the target building and adjoining terrace.

These are strong official visual leads for the exact host building and its entrance composition. The close entrance photograph helps identify the platform and threshold, but does not resolve every individual feature at the level required for a separate object photograph of the bootscrape, coal-hole cover or matching gate.

The NIAH written description independently confirms the c.1880 house, carved brick and terracotta façade, stained-glass fanlight, granite steps, cast-iron boot-scrape, balustrade, matching gate, basement panel railings and pavement coal-hole cover.

## 47 Hardwicke Street — DUB-BLD-0088 / DUB-STR-0077

Official NIAH record 50010889:
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50010889/47-hardwicke-street-dublin-1-dublin

The official NIAH record was found through search, but direct page access returned HTTP 403 during this batch. Its indexed record describes the c.1810 Georgian townhouse and its encaustic-tiled entrance platform, wrought-iron bootscraper and granite steps.

A public planning EIAR appendix was opened to printed Appendix A16 page 31 (PDF page index 32). The actual page image was visually inspected. It contains a small street-facing photograph for the protected-structure group “47 to 48 Hardwicke Street” and identifies NIAH 50010888–50010889. The low-resolution group photograph is relevant historical/architectural context but does not isolate No. 47 or verify its individual bootscraper:
https://www.pleanala.ie/publicaccess/EIAR-NIS/317121/1.%20S51%20Application/EIAR%20%28incl%20NTS%20in%20Irish%29/Volume%204%20-%20Appendices/Vol%204%20Part%203/Ch.%2016%20Architectural%20Heritage/A16.1-A16.3.pdf

A historic Wikimedia Commons image, “Hardwicke Street with St. George's Church at end, Dublin.jpg”, was opened at original resolution and visually inspected:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hardwicke_Street_with_St._George%27s_Church_at_end,_Dublin.jpg

The image is dated circa 1912 and attributed on Commons to the National Library of Ireland on The Commons. Its description places the camera around the North Frederick Street / Hardwicke Street corner and looks along the street towards St George's Church. Commons records “No known copyright restrictions”. It is a broad historic street-context photograph; it does not positively identify No. 47 or its entrance fittings. Do not attach it as an exact-object image.

A separate 2023 Commons photograph titled “Hardwicke Street, Dublin.jpg” by MOs810 was also inspected at original resolution. Its description identifies a wayside shrine, not No. 47. It is rejected as a target match, notwithstanding its CC BY 4.0 licence.

## Rights and publication decisions

NIAH terms:
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/terms-and-conditions/

The Department states that NIAH website content is its copyright. Educational or private use is permitted with NIAH credit; commercial use requires prior written agreement. No written permission for CICAN DUBLIN's intended publication was established for any NIAH gallery photograph.

The NLI slide's reproduction rights are owned by the National Library of Ireland. No open commercial licence or permission was established.

The EIAR photograph's image-specific photographer and reuse permission were not established. Public availability of the report does not itself establish an open image licence.

The circa-1912 Commons file carries a “No known copyright restrictions” designation, but it is not an exact match to No. 47 and is not being registered as an asset in this batch. The unrelated 2023 Commons wayside-shrine image is rejected as a mismatch.
