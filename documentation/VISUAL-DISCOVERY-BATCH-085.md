## Official NIAH evidence

Official record:
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030357/89-clontarf-road-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin

The live NIAH page was opened. Its gallery exposes two individual image files, both opened and visually inspected:

- `50030357_2.jpg`: oblique/wide street-facing view of the terrace. The frame includes a sequence of two-storey brick houses, entrances, upper windows, parapet roofline, boundary railings and parked vehicles. It provides terrace context but does not isolate every element of No. 89.
- `50030357_1.jpg`: closer view of the brick terrace and several entrance bays. The photograph shows upper-floor sash windows, door surrounds and part of the front boundary/vegetation. The framing is cropped and partly obstructed, so it is not a complete standalone façade record.

Direct image URLs:
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/building-images/niah/images/survey_specific/800/50030357_2.jpg
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/building-images/niah/images/survey_specific/800/50030357_1.jpg

The NIAH description identifies the target as a terraced two-bay, two-storey house over raised basement, built in 1847. It records the brown Flemish-bond brick façade, granite stringcourse and cornice, M-profile natural slate roof, petal-motif fanlight, corniced and friezed door surround on console brackets, granite entrance steps/platform, cast-iron balustrade, cast-iron railings on a granite plinth wall and pedestrian gate. The appraisal attributes the terrace's construction in 1847 to Isaac Warren, steward of the Vernon estate, on land obtained from that estate.

The record says the house is set back from Clontarf Road facing a shared enclosed green. This matters when interpreting the photographs: the building frontage and its shared green are part of the setting, and a street-level photo from the road may not show the principal entrance composition clearly.

## Additional exact-address photograph lead — Daft sold-property archive

A historic Daft sold-property page for the exact address was opened:
https://www.daft.ie/sold/89-clontarf-road-clontarf/1B0EB1B5BF10424880257A7D0056E47A

The page identifies the property as 89 Clontarf Road, Clontarf, Dublin 3, and records a sale on 3 June 2011. Its exterior lead photograph was opened and visually inspected. It shows a long red-brick terrace frontage with multiple arched entrance surrounds, a raised shared granite entrance stair and boundary railings. The photo is associated with the exact-address listing, but the displayed crop contains several adjoining entrance bays and does not independently isolate which doorway belongs to No. 89. The page also contains interior listing photographs; these are excluded from the archive's public exterior visual discovery.

The Daft listing photograph is a useful exact-address historical-condition lead, but its photographer and image reuse licence were not established. It is not imported or registered.

## Rights and publication decision

NIAH terms:

The Department states that NIAH website content is its copyright. Educational/private use is permitted with NIAH credit; commercial use requires prior written agreement. No written permission for the intended CICAN DUBLIN publication was established for either NIAH gallery image.

The Daft page identifies the listing and agent but does not provide a clear image-specific reuse licence or photographer attribution. Public accessibility of a property listing is not permission to republish its photographs.

No image in this batch meets both the exactness and rights requirements for archive registration. No source or image records were added.

## Founder fieldwork priorities

1. Photograph the complete No. 89 frontage from a lawful public viewpoint, with enough context to distinguish it from adjoining houses in the terrace.
2. Capture the petal-motif fanlight square-on, the door surround's cornice/frieze and console brackets, and the timber panelled door if visible from public space.
3. Capture the granite entrance steps and platform, cast-iron balustrade, railings, granite plinth wall and pedestrian gate without entering the shared private green or entrance platform.
4. Include a contextual view showing the relationship between the house, shared green and Clontarf Road.
5. Do not photograph interiors or residents. Do not enter the private green or rear outbuildings.
6. Record founder GPS only for the host building from an appropriate safe position. The published NIAH coordinate remains source-derived and is not a precise survey of individual architectural features.
