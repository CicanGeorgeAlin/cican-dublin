# CICAN DUBLIN — Visual Discovery Batch 087

**Date:** 29 October 2026  
**Scope:** DUB-STR-0042 — carved shield above the former carriage arch at Clontarf Castle gate lodge.  
**Decision:** No image imported. Three openly licensed photographs of the host gate lodge were opened and visually inspected; none provides a sufficiently clear, independently verifiable depiction of the specific carved shield.

## Catalogue target

- **Archive ID:** DUB-STR-0042
- **Name:** Clontarf Castle gate lodge — carved shield above the former carriage arch
- **Parent:** DUB-BLD-0047 — Clontarf Castle gate lodge
- **Location:** Former carriage-arch opening, Clontarf Castle gate lodge, Castle Avenue, Clontarf, Dublin 3.
- **NIAH registration:** 50030163 (associated host building)
- **Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030163/clontarf-castle-castle-avenue-clontarf-dublin-3-dublin

The official NIAH record describes a detached Gothic Revival gate lodge built in 1836–37. Its description explicitly records a carved limestone shield over the former carriage arch, alongside carved stringcourses, a corbel table with sculpted animal heads, and other limestone detailing. The catalogue correctly treats the shield as a separate micro-detail from the already catalogued gate lodge and animal-head corbels.

Their existing association with the parent gate lodge is not changed by this report.

## Other research leads

- The National Library of Ireland catalogue contains a 23 September 1842 drawing by Colonel William Denny titled “Gate at Clontarf Castle”. Its catalogue record states that reproduction rights are owned by the National Library of Ireland: https://catalogue.nli.ie/Record/vtls000553571
- A second NLI catalogue entry describes Edward McFarland’s 1853 watercolour, “The entrance gate to Clontarf Castle”. Its reproduction rights are also owned by the National Library of Ireland: https://catalogue.nli.ie/Record/vtls000552898
- These are historic depictions of the castle entrance, not verified close-up photographs of the present gate lodge shield. They are research leads only; no reproduction permission was established.

## Findings and decision

The NIAH description is strong evidence that the shield is a real, distinct architectural feature. The Commons photographs are exact views of the host gate lodge and are openly licensed, but their framing does not establish a clear view of the specific shield. The NLI historic drawings have separate reproduction-rights restrictions and cannot be treated as freely reusable.

**No image accepted for DUB-STR-0042.** A future founder photograph should capture the shield itself and its relationship to the former carriage arch from a safe, lawful public viewpoint. The existing navigation coordinate is inherited from the parent building and must not be represented as a field-verified precise shield coordinate.
