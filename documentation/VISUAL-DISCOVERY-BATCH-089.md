## Target

### DUB-BLD-0075 — Verville, Vernon Avenue, Clontarf, Dublin 3

Official NIAH registration 50030334:  
https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030334/verville-vernon-avenue-clontarf-sibylhill-dublin-3-dublin

The official register describes Verville as a detached house over basement, with an early pedimented five-bay house dating to circa 1730, later wings and additions, a bowed bay with cast-iron balcony, a pedimented timber doorcase, granite entrance steps and wrought-iron railings. It records the property as one of the oldest houses in Dublin's northern suburbs and notes its later use as a private asylum and nursing home.

## Planning and condition context

An Bord Pleanála Inspector's Report ABP-305842-19 identifies the site as being within the curtilage of the protected structure and states that the inspector visited on 5 February 2020. The report describes Verville's principal façade as facing the courtyard and records that the permitted development was under construction, while only limited works had then been undertaken at Verville itself. The report's text says the inspector's site photographs are attached, but the 18-page report file reviewed here does not contain an accessible photographic appendix. No missing photographs have been reconstructed or inferred.

Report: https://www.pleanala.ie/anbordpleanala/media/abp/cases/reports/305/r305842.pdf

A Dublin City Council committee report from January 2019 describes the proposal for 1–68 Verville and explicitly calls Verville a historic house of note dating to the mid-eighteenth century. It is planning-history context, not evidence of the current completed condition.

## Founder fieldwork checklist

Verville is within a managed residential development and its grounds are not public access by default. Photograph only from a lawful public viewpoint or with explicit permission from the relevant owner/management. Do not enter courtyards, grounds or buildings without permission.

If lawful access is arranged, prioritise:
1. The full early five-bay pedimented block and its relationship to later wings.
2. The pediment, central lunette and original façade brickwork.
3. The carved timber entrance doorcase, granite steps and railings.
4. The bowed bay and cast-iron balcony.
5. Any surviving historic sash windows and visible later additions.

Current condition, restoration status and access arrangements require fresh field verification. Do not infer them from the 2017 aerial or the 2020 planning report.
