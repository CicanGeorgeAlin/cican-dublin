# Visual Discovery Batch 096 — Clontarf Garda Station Handball Alley

**Project:** CICAN DUBLIN  
**Founder:** CICAN GEORGE ALIN

**Scope:** Exact-object visual research for one uncovered catalogue record.  
**Outcome:** Exact-object photographs visually inspected in official and historical sources; no image imported because reuse rights were not established.

## Catalogue target

- **Record:** `DUB-BLD-0052`
- **Object:** Clontarf handball alley — former Garda station recreation structure
- **Location:** Rear grounds of Clontarf Garda Síochána Station, Strandville Avenue East / Clontarf Road, Clontarf, Dublin 3.
- **NIAH registration:** 50030337.
- **Official record:** https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/buildings-search/building/50030337/strandville-road-clontarf-dublin-3-co-dublin
- **NIAH Irish Grid:** 318530, 236292.

The NIAH identifies a freestanding three-sided handball alley built circa 1935, with sloped side walls. It describes yellow Flemish-bond brick to the outer walls, yellow-brick pilasters on the south elevation, smooth rendered internal walls and remains of steel railings on top of the side walls. The record says it is currently disused and forms part of the adjacent Garda station, probably as an amenity for officers.

The catalogue keeps this alley distinct from the adjacent Garda station building (NIAH 50030274) and from the wider site. A photograph of the station alone is not an exact-object photograph of the alley.

## Official NIAH photograph opened and visually inspected

The NIAH record exposes one gallery image:

- https://www.buildingsofireland.ie/building-images/niah/images/survey_specific/800/50030337_1.jpg

The image was opened directly. It shows the three-sided alley from outside, including the high sloped side walls, yellow-brick outer face, surviving railings and rear-site setting. The visible form is consistent with the official record description and identifies the exact catalogue object.

This is an exact-object visual reference. It is not a founder-captured photograph.

## Historical photo lead — Royal Irish Constabulary Forum

A 2014 discussion thread, “C Division (Rotunda)”, includes a Clontarf Police Station section. The author states that the station continued in use as a Garda station and captions a separate image as showing that the handball alley remains in the rear grounds.

- Thread: https://irishconstabulary.com/c-division-rotunda-t1397.html
- Direct handball-alley image: https://attachment.tapatalk-cdn.com/16889/202008/3579887_360642d474a10618871728ad5aec5376_t.jpeg
- The thread's separate station-streetscape image is not the target: https://attachment.tapatalk-cdn.com/16889/202008/3579887_43c5674de585e0e6552ee7ccf204ccf7_t.jpeg

The handball image was opened and visually inspected. It shows the same distinctive sloped three-sided alley and its relationship to the rear of the Garda station. It is a strong exact-object historical/current-condition comparison lead. However, the forum page does not establish a reuse licence or sufficient image authorship/permission details for commercial republication. It must not be copied into the archive without permission from the rights holder.

The separate station image is context only and must not be registered as an alley photograph.

## CoisCéim production record — location corroboration, not a cleared image

CoisCéim Dance Theatre's page for *In the Magic Hour* identifies a performance at “Clontarf Garda Station Handball Alley”, Clontarf, Dublin, on 20–21 June 2021. It credits Ste Murray as cinematographer for the handball-alley footage and describes the production's use of the alley.

- Production page: https://www.coisceim.com/productions/in-the-magic-hour/
- Programme PDF: https://www.coisceim.com/assets/files/pdf/programme_in_the_magic_hour.pdf

This independently corroborates the site's use and exact venue identity. The page gallery includes material from multiple handball-alley venues; the displayed generic gallery item is not proven to depict Clontarf. No individual still was accepted as a Clontarf photograph, and no republication permission was established. Any still from the production would require direct subject verification and written rights clearance from the relevant photographer/rights holder.

## Fieldwork checklist

If the site is accessible from a lawful public viewpoint and photography is permitted:
1. Capture the full three-sided alley and its relationship to the former Garda station.
2. Photograph both sloped side walls and the playing wall.
3. Record the yellow Flemish-bond brickwork and south-elevation pilasters.
4. Capture the smooth rendered internal walls and surviving railings from a lawful vantage point.
5. Record current condition and any later alterations separately from the NIAH's 2014 survey description.

Do not enter Garda/private grounds or photograph through restricted areas. Respect security and access controls. Do not obstruct station operations.
