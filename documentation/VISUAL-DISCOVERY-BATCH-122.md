# Visual Discovery Batch 122 — Tropical Fruit Warehouse and Carlisle Bridge Riverine Keystones

**Project:** CICAN DUBLIN  
**Founder:** CICAN GEORGE ALIN  
**Date:** 7 November 2026  
**Scope:** Evidence-first catalogue expansion on Sir John Rogerson's Quay.  
**Outcome:** One protected warehouse and two individually significant façade details registered as three records. No third-party image imported because reuse permissions were not established.

## New catalogue records

### 1. `DUB-BLD-0125` — Dublin Tropical Fruit Company warehouse

- **Current identity:** Tropical Fruit Warehouse
- **Address:** 30–32 Sir John Rogerson's Quay, Dublin 2
- **NIAH:** 50020478
- **Dublin City Council RPS:** 7721 (2026 Record of Protected Structures)
- **NIAH Irish Grid:** 317154, 234277
- **Transformed WGS84:** 53.345982508377226, -6.241633033540952
- **Coordinate status:** Source-derived; not founder GPS; positional accuracy unspecified.
- **Period:** c.1890; IPUT records Tropical Fruit Company use from 1892.

NIAH describes an attached double-gable-fronted fourteen-bay two-storey warehouse, with an M-profile pitched slate roof and clerestory, Dutch gables, terracotta ridge tiles, red-brick Flemish bond, limestone cornice and string courses, brick pilasters, coping, orb finials and carved keystones. The original warehouse fabric was later restored and integrated with a substantial modern office redevelopment. The archive description distinguishes the historic warehouse from the elevated glazed extension and rear office building.

IPUT records the Tropical Fruit Company warehouse history and its redevelopment. Henry J Lyons, the project architect, describes the retention/restoration of the historic brick façades, slate roof and 30 Scots Pine timber trusses, as well as the later elevated extension. Dublin City Council's 2026 RPS lists the Tropical Fruit Co. at 30–32 Sir John Rogerson's Quay as RPS 7721.

### 2. `DUB-DET-0003` — Anna Liffey riverine keystone

The west-side riverine keystone on the warehouse façade. NIAH identifies it as Anna Liffey and attributes the pair to Edward Smyth, sculpted in 1794 for Carlisle Bridge. Its precise relocation date to the warehouse is not established. It is registered as a child micro-detail of `DUB-BLD-0125`, without its own coordinates or map pin.

### 3. `DUB-DET-0004` — Atlantic riverine keystone

The east-side riverine keystone on the warehouse façade. NIAH identifies it as representing the Atlantic Ocean and attributes the pair to Edward Smyth, sculpted in 1794 for Carlisle Bridge. Its precise relocation date to the warehouse is not established. It is registered as a child micro-detail of `DUB-BLD-0125`, without its own coordinates or map pin.

The two riverine keystones are distinguished from the warehouse's other carved limestone keystones. Those are separate architectural details and are not misidentified as the Carlisle Bridge originals.

## Image investigation and rights decision

The NIAH gallery was visually inspected. It includes photographs of the exact warehouse, including its quay-side frontage and relationship to the pedestrian bridge. Additional exact-object imagery was located through Henry J Lyons and other property/architecture sources.

No image has been embedded in the archive during this batch. NIAH terms allow educational/private use with credit but require prior written agreement for commercial use; other located images do not carry a verified reuse licence in the material inspected. Do not hotlink or republish these photographs without confirming the applicable rights. The visual records therefore remain awaiting founder photographs or a properly cleared reference image.

## Field photography plan

1. Photograph the complete historic double-gabled warehouse frontage from the public quay.
2. Capture the west and east riverine keystones individually and in their façade context.
3. Record the six other limestone keystones separately if identifiable; do not confuse them with the Carlisle Bridge pair.
4. Capture brickwork, Dutch gables, terracotta apex details, limestone dressings and surviving historic openings.
5. Show the junction between the original warehouse and the modern elevated glazed extension.
6. Do not enter private areas, touch the carvings or obstruct the public footpath.

## Evidence and uncertainty controls

- NIAH is the primary source for the historic building fabric and the two riverine keystones.
- RPS 7721 establishes protected-structure registration; it does not replace the NIAH architectural survey.
- The 1892 Tropical Fruit Company date is attributed to IPUT's property history.
- The precise date and mechanism by which the original Carlisle Bridge keystones were relocated to the warehouse façade are not established.
- The source-derived coordinate locates the warehouse site only. It is not founder GPS or a field-verified building entrance point.
- The modern glazed extension and rear office building are later interventions, not original nineteenth-century fabric.
- No founder photograph, current condition assessment or physical field visit is claimed.
