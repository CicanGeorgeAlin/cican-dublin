## New catalogue records

### 1. `DUB-BLD-0126` — British & Irish Steam Packet Company offices

- **Address:** 20–24 Sir John Rogerson's Quay, at Lime Street, Dublin 2
- **Current development:** The Shipping Office
- **NIAH:** 50020477
- **Dublin City Council RPS:** 7547
- **NIAH Irish Grid:** 317069, 234299
- **Transformed WGS84:** 53.346198882800905, -6.242900549627485
- **Coordinate status:** Source-derived; not founder GPS; positional accuracy unspecified.
- **Date:** Built 1909; formally opened 1910.

NIAH describes a freestanding six-bay, two-storey former office building with attic storey, integral carriage arches, a west wing wall and a triple-pile goods shed to the rear. Its recorded details include pitched slate roofs, terracotta ridge cresting, brick chimneystacks, Flemish-bond brickwork, carved masonry capping, limestone details, round-headed ground-floor windows, oculi, elliptical carriage arches with terracotta keystones, cast-iron wheel stops, a loading platform and cobbled yard.

NIAH says the B&I Line moved its offices to the site in 1909 and that the façade was designed by W.H. Byrne & Sons and formally opened in 1910. Dublin City Council's RPS lists 20–24 Sir John Rogerson's Quay as RPS 7547. The historic structure has been integrated into The Shipping Office, a later mixed-use redevelopment. The modern atrium and new office building are not original 1909 fabric.

### 2. `DUB-INS-0009` — Company-name lettering and 1909 date panel

A child inscription record for the raised stone lettering naming the British & Irish Steam Packet Company and the date panel above the entrance. NIAH confirms the carved fascia lettering and date panel; Afloat's 2017 report identifies the date as 1909. Full wording and punctuation remain for exact transcription from a clear field photograph.

### 3. `DUB-DET-0005` — Elliptical carriage arches and terracotta keystones

A child micro-detail record for the carriage arches in the historic end bays and west wing wall. NIAH records terracotta keystones, timber-battened doors, a steel grille, cast-iron wheel stops and render impost details. The present survival and condition of each fitting require field photography.

An exact-object street photograph was located through Ship Spotter Steve, and architectural/project imagery is available from the redevelopment team. No image was embedded: the located sources did not provide a verified reuse licence in the material inspected. The visual records remain awaiting founder photographs or a cleared reference image.

## Field photography plan

1. Photograph the historic façade within the modern Shipping Office development from a public viewpoint.
2. Capture the raised company-name lettering and 1909 date panel as separate close-ups.
3. Photograph each visible carriage arch, terracotta keystone and wheel stop.
4. Capture the original brick and limestone fabric, round-headed windows and other surviving details.
5. Distinguish the preserved 1909 structure from the modern glazed atrium and office extension.
6. Do not enter private areas or touch the protected fabric.

## Evidence and uncertainty controls

- NIAH is the primary source for architectural description and the 1909 / 1910 chronology.

- The date-panel text is not fully transcribed; no wording beyond the source-supported company name and date has been invented.
- The source-derived coordinate is a site locator, not founder GPS or a field-verified entrance point.
- The historic office, rear goods shed and modern Shipping Office development are not treated as one undifferentiated historic structure.
