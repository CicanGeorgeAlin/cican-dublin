## New catalogue object

### DUB-BLD-0127 — The Ferryman, 35–36 Sir John Rogerson's Quay

- **NIAH:** 50020479
- **Dublin City Council RPS:** 7549 and 7550
- **Date:** c.1820
- **Original use:** Pair of houses
- **Current use recorded by NIAH:** Public house and guest house
- **NIAH Irish Grid:** 317215, 234283
- **Transformed WGS84:** 53.34602289438518, -6.240715353336317
- **Coordinate status:** Source-derived locator; not founder GPS and not field-verified.

The NIAH describes a corner-sited attached pair of three-bay, four-storey houses with an M-profile hipped slate roof, red-brick parapet and chimneystacks, granite coping, Flemish-bond brickwork, granite window sills, timber sliding-sash windows and carved Portland-stone door details. NIAH records marigold carvings to the lintel and a mix of square- and round-headed openings.

The NIAH appraisal records the two buildings as originally separate and notes nineteenth-century commercial activity: Michael Scanlon and James Lynch occupied the properties in the 1850s; trade directories identify a grocer/shipbuilder at No. 35 and a ship smith at No. 36.

The Ringsend to City Centre Core Bus Corridor Scheme EIAR, Volume 4, Appendix A16.3, page 30 independently identifies the Ferryman pair as c.1820 Georgian-style commercial/residential premises and lists RPS 7549–7550.

## Founder photography plan

1. Capture the full corner composition from lawful public viewpoints.
2. Photograph the slate roof, parapet, chimneystacks and granite coping.
3. Record brick bonds and the diminishing window proportions.
4. Capture surviving timber sash windows and granite sills.
5. Photograph the carved Portland-stone doorcase, marigold lintel carving and fanlight.
6. Distinguish original fabric from replacement or blocked openings without assuming their dates.

Do not enter private premises or photograph through private interiors. Current condition and field access remain to be confirmed.
