# Visual Discovery Batch 128 — Great South Wall / Ballast Office Wall

**Project:** CICAN DUBLIN  
**Founder:** CICAN GEORGE ALIN  
**Date:** 8 November 2026  
**Scope:** Great South Wall linear monument and visible remains of the earlier Ballast Office Wall.  
**Outcome:** One linear parent site, two parent-linked physical-detail records, one official report source and one research note added.

## New catalogue records

### DUB-INF-0034 — Great South Wall

A linear parent record for the Great South Wall, with cited monument references RMP DU018-066 and DU019-029 and RPS 6928 as identified in the 3FM heritage report. It intentionally has no single point coordinate: a long sea wall cannot responsibly be represented by an invented centre pin.

### DUB-DET-0012 — Ballast Office Wall surviving low parapet sections

The 3FM report states that the Ballast Office Wall, completed in 1759, now lies beneath Pigeon House Road in this stretch, but that some sections of its boundary walls remain visible as low parapets. Individual remnants are not enumerated or georeferenced. The record is a field-photography target, not a claim that every described segment remains unchanged today.

### DUB-DET-0013 — Exposed full-height north-facing wall at the cooling-water outfall

The same report describes a portion of the north-facing Ballast Office Wall exposed to full height along the ESB Dublin Bay Power Plant cooling-water outfall. The Area 4 plan identifies the outfall and says a significant part of the northern parapet remained intact in the report's survey context. This is an industrial/utility-edge location; no public access is assumed and no attempt to enter is recommended.

## Important historical distinction

The report distinguishes:
- **Ballast Office Wall (1759):** two parallel rubble walls with a sand-filled space between; much of its alignment is now beneath Pigeon House Road.
- **Later Great South Wall extension towards Poolbeg:** a different construction, described as a dry rubble core with granite ashlar façades.

The archive preserves that distinction and does not transfer construction details from one wall phase to the other.

## Fieldwork and access

- No coordinates or independent map pins are assigned to the linear parent or its two details.
- The full-height wall beside the cooling-water outfall may be inaccessible; do not enter ESB or port land.
- Photograph only from lawful public viewpoints.
- Do not cross fences, enter industrial yards, approach outfalls, climb the wall or stand in carriageways.
- If a remnant cannot be viewed safely, record it as inaccessible rather than attempting entry.
- Current condition must be checked; project-era plans are not a guarantee of present survival.

## Exclusions and unresolved leads

The project report's plan labels several steps and parapet works, but it does not establish an independently identifiable historic quay ladder in the accessible public realm. No ladder record is created in this batch. The next research step is to inspect the conservation/restoration specifications and engineering drawings for individually described coping stones, steps, iron fittings and documented repairs, while avoiding proposed elements mistaken for surviving historic fabric.

## Visual workflow and quality control

Three matching visual records created; all await original founder photography. No third-party images reused and no line art fabricated. IDs are unique, source links resolve, child-parent relationships are consistent, and no feature coordinates were invented.
