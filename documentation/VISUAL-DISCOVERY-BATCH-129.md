# Visual Discovery Batch 129 — Great South Wall parapet conservation details

**Project:** CICAN DUBLIN  
**Founder:** CICAN GEORGE ALIN  
**Date:** 10 November 2026  
**Scope:** Great South Wall parapet capping materials, historical timber coping, later repair and proposed conservation/reconstruction.  
**Outcome:** One official conservation-specification source registered; one research note added; one investigation task added. **No new physical catalogue records** were created because the documents do not identify individually locatable surviving examples.

## Findings

### 1. Original parapet capping included basalt

Dublin Port 3FM EIAR Appendix 16-6 (Southgate Associates, October 2023) states that the original capping material contains a mix of basalt stone. For rebuilding, the specification proposes omitting basalt so that new capping has a subtly different stone texture and remains distinguishable from original fabric. It also prefers salvaged material from areas carefully dismantled for the project, with similar stone used if salvaged material is exhausted.

This is a valuable material-identification clue for future photography and conservation interpretation. It is not a claim that a particular basalt stone remains in situ today.

### 2. Timber coping and cement replacement

The Great South Wall Overview, historical account p.5, says timber coping was used in some places, but most had rotted and been replaced with cement. The report does not give an exact inventory of surviving timber coping or locate any remaining examples. No timber-coping object record or pin was created.

### 3. Later square coping is not original

The same overview's mitigation summary (pp.18–19) explicitly distinguishes square stone coping on some rebuilt wall sections from the original profile. It says this later form is not to be followed for reconstruction or partial rebuilding; the typical reconstruction is shown with a domed capping profile based on adjacent original examples.

This distinction is essential: a visually historic-looking square coping should not automatically be labelled original.

### 4. Conservation method is not a condition survey

Appendix 16-6 includes careful pre-work recording, coded masonry units cross-referenced to drawings and photographs, lime mortar specifications, salvaged stone reuse, crack tying and grouting methods. These are conservation procedures and proposed methods, not proof that a specific intervention was completed at a particular point. The appendix's available extracted pages do not provide a chainage-indexed inventory of individual capping stones.
