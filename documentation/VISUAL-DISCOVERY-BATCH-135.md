# Visual Discovery Batch 135 — St Anne’s Well, pond temple and Naniken bridges

**Date:** 11 November 2026  
**Project:** CICAN DUBLIN  
**Founder:** CICAN GEORGE ALIN

## New catalogue records

1. **DUB-BLD-0138 — Temple folly overlooking the artificial pond** (NIAH 50030032), c.1860. A separate temple folly with carved granite frontage, broken pediment, south portico and pond-facing composition. It is distinct from the already catalogued Herculanean Temple (NIAH 50030024).
2. **DUB-INF-0137 — Naniken River footbridge with carved stone bracket** (NIAH 50030025), c.1860. Segmental arch, rubble-stone voussoirs, west-spandrel carved bracket and brick soffit.
3. **DUB-INF-0138 — Naniken River segmental-arch footbridge** (NIAH 50030027), c.1860. Squared-stone voussoirs and concrete path surface; part of the historic designed river walk.
4. **DUB-INF-0139 — Former kitchen-garden path bridge with groin-vaulted passage** (NIAH 50030036), c.1860. Round-headed arch, calp-limestone voussoirs and a groin-vaulted passage.
5. **DUB-MON-0013 — St Anne’s Well** (RMP DU019-012). Dublin City Council identifies the well as medieval and a protected recorded monument. Archaeological monitoring in 2016 documented a circular opening, a nineteenth-century metal grille and a granite-paved section extending 2.45m from the well.

## Coordinate and verification policy

The four NIAH-built features retain their published Irish Grid references only. No WGS84 coordinate or founder GPS has been assigned to them.

For St Anne’s Well, the published ITM coordinate and latitude/longitude from Excavations.ie report 2017:653 are stored as **source-derived**, not field-verified. The report's observation that the well was dry refers to the 2016 monitoring period; present water conditions are unknown.

No current physical condition is asserted without field inspection. Photograph only from authorised public paths. Do not enter, touch, clean or disturb the protected well or any fragile structure.

No online photograph was added without confirming a suitable reuse licence. All five entries have founder photography briefs and await original photographs before line-art production.
