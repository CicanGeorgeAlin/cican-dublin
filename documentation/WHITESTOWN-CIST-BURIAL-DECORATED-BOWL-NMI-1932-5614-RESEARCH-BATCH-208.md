# Whitestown Cist Burial and Decorated Bowl — NMI 1932:5614
## Research Batch 208

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Published excavation report reviewed; no independent field inspection  
**Place:** Whitestown, north-east County Dublin, in the parish of Lusk  
**NMI registration:** 1932:5614 (sub-items 1932:5614.1–.3)  
**Subject:** Early Bronze Age burial context and associated necked bowl

## 1. Discovery and excavation

The Excavations.ie record 1932:003 reproduces the NMI burial excavation report by Adolf Mahr and Liam Price. It states that in early February 1932, during ploughing in a field near Rush, an inhumation burial accompanied by a bowl was discovered beneath a large stone capstone. The capstone lay 0.31m below ground level.

The landowner, Lawrence McGuinness, removed the capstone and attempted to lift the vessel, breaking it. The skull was also removed and damaged before being replaced. The find was reported to the Gardaí, who informed the National Museum of Ireland. Adolf Mahr and Liam Price excavated the site on 6 February 1932, five days after discovery.

The report says its account is based on Mahr and Price's notes and sketches; the human remains were analysed by Laureen Buckley.

Source: Excavations.ie, 1932:003, “Whitestown, Co. Dublin”.  
https://excavations.ie/report/1932/Dublin/0028430/

## 2. Location: Whitestown, not an exact recoverable point

The report places the burial in Whitestown, north-east County Dublin, in the parish of Lusk. It describes the field as locally known as “William’s field”, south of the main Rush–Lusk road, west of St Maur’s Church and east of St Maur’s Well.

Crucially, footnote 96 states that the site was unfortunately not marked on the map, although the general area was known. It gives an approximate Irish Grid Reference only. The webpage metadata also displays ITM coordinates, but because the report explicitly warns that the site was not mapped and gives only an approximate location, those coordinates should not be promoted as a verified grave location.

**Archive location rule:** retain Whitestown as the find locality and the field description as historical location evidence. Do not place a precise map pin for the cist.

## 3. The burial structure and human remains

The cist consisted of a semicircular arrangement of three tall and four smaller irregular stones protecting the skull and vessel. It appears to have been aligned north–south. The tallest of the stones was 0.38m high.

Recorded measurements:
- Burial length: 0.71m
- Burial width: 0.38m
- Capstone: 1.19m × 0.76m × 0.13m
- The grave cut was not clearly identified.
- The grave floor was not paved.

The burial contained the unburnt remains of an adult male, alongside a small amount of cremated bone also interpreted as an adult male. The inhumed individual was assessed as a late middle adult, estimated age 35–44 years and stature approximately 174cm. The report explicitly states that the human remains from this site had not been dated.

The cremated bone comprised 57 fragments weighing 51g. The report's analysis says the remains appear to represent one adult male.

## 4. The decorated necked bowl — 1932:5614.1

The vessel is identified as a simple necked bowl, classified by Ó Ríordáin and Waddell. It was found standing upright immediately south of the skull.

The published description records:
- incised lines and slightly crescentic impressions;
- a flat rim with encircling incised decoration;
- cross-hatched strokes below the rim, bounded by wavy rows of nail-like impressions;
- diagonal incisions on the shoulder;
- another decorated zone of vertical incisions;
- a plain base.

**Dimensions:** height 12cm; external rim diameter 16.3cm; base diameter 7cm.

The report includes Fig. 3.68, captioned “Ceramic vessel, Whitestown, Co. Dublin”. The vessel was broken during discovery and subsequently repaired in part. The footnote says sherds were distributed among neighbours; Dr Mahr recovered many, while some did not reach the NMI until as late as 1974. The report does not state the present location or complete condition of every sherd.

## 5. Dating: distinguish the vessel from the human remains

The excavators' report says the human remains were not dated. The bowl's decorative scheme was compared with a necked bipartite bowl from Kiltalown, County Dublin. By analogy, the Whitestown bowl was assigned to stage 2 of the bowl tradition, which Brindley (2007) dates to **2080–1980 BC**.

This is a typological comparison for the vessel, not a direct radiocarbon date for the burial or the individual. The archive should describe the burial as an Early Bronze Age cist burial in the published excavation record, while retaining the explicit caveat that the human remains themselves were not dated.

## 6. Separate later find in the same townland

The 1988 *Urban Archaeology Survey: County Dublin* also lists a flat bronze axehead found seven feet below the surface while digging a ditch at Whitestown, NMI R.1695. This is a separate stray find, not part of the 1932 cist burial or its grave goods.

The same 1988 list separately identifies the 1932 Whitestown Food Vessel and skeleton as NMI 1932:5614. Keeping the axehead and burial as separate archive subjects avoids an accidental association based only on the shared townland.

Source: Bradley & King, *Urban Archaeology Survey: County Dublin* (1988), Lusk, list of stray finds, p.268.  
https://www.archaeology.ie/app/uploads/2025/05/County_Dublin_Urban_Archaeology_Survey_1988-1.pdf

## 7. Archive decision

**Research outcome:** A substantial published excavation account survives for a cist burial discovered in Whitestown in 1932. It documents the discovery, excavation date, cist construction, human remains, decorated necked bowl, measurements and the limitations of the findspot record.

**Production decision:** This is a strong candidate for a documented archaeological find record, but do not create a precise map pin. Before production promotion, check the canonical catalogue and duplicate status, preserve the composite NMI reference and its sub-items, and determine image reuse rights for Fig. 3.68. Do not imply the human remains have a direct scientific date or that every vessel sherd is currently held by the NMI.

**Next research target:** Investigate the separate Whitestown flat bronze axehead, NMI R.1695, including the cited Harbison 1969 entry, without conflating it with the cist burial.
