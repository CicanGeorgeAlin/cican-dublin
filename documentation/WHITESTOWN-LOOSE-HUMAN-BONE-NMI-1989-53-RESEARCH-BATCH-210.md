# Whitestown Loose Human Bone — NMI 1989:53
## Research Batch 210

**Project:** CICAN Dublin Archive  
**Research status:** Documentary research from a reproduced NMI Topographical Files entry; no human remains examined  
**Townland:** Whitestown, County Dublin  
**NMI registration:** 1989:53  
**Recorded subject:** Human skeletal remains  
**Key caution:** The NMI-file summary explicitly says loose bone was found in a sand pit and there was no evidence for a grave.

## 2. Interpretation: do not turn loose bone into a burial

The source's wording is unusually important. It records human bone but explicitly states **“no evidence for grave.”** The archive must not classify this as a cist, grave, cemetery, burial, prehistoric interment or human sacrifice.

The find may represent redeposited or disturbed human remains, but the accessible record does not identify how the bones came to be in the sand pit. No date, age, sex, number of individuals, skeletal elements, anatomical completeness, pathology, trauma, or taphonomic observations are supplied.

The safest object title is therefore **“Whitestown loose human bone (NMI 1989:53)”**, not “Whitestown burial”.

## 3. Location and spatial confidence

The recorded find place is descriptive: a sand pit across the road from the entrance to Rogerstown Hall. This is more informative than a townland-only reference, but it is not an exact archaeological coordinate.

The entry does not identify:
- the name or former boundary of the sand pit;
- which side of the road or the dimensions of the pit;
- the exact entrance or road alignment at the time of discovery;
- the date of discovery or the person who found the bone;
- whether the pit remains visible or has been infilled/redeveloped.

Do not geocode the phrase to a pin at Rogerstown Hall or its entrance. The hall is a locator in the written description, not the findspot itself. Any future field survey should first reconstruct the historical road, entrance and sand-pit footprint from maps and land records, then assess whether the location can be narrowed safely.

## 4. Relationship to the 1932 Whitestown cist burial

This record is separate from the 1932 Whitestown cist burial (NMI 1932:5614 / later summary 1932:5614A).

The 1932 excavation report documents a defined cist burial with an inhumation, cremated bone and a decorated necked bowl. In contrast, the NMI 1989:53 summary records loose bone in a sand pit and explicitly says there was no evidence for a grave.

The shared townland does not establish a relationship, shared date, common cemetery or continuity of use. Keep the two records separate.

## 5. Other Whitestown finds are also separate

The flat bronze axehead NMI R.1695 is another separate Whitestown find. No source connects it with the sand-pit bone.

The 1989:53 entry should not be merged with the human remains from the 1932 cist or the 1932 food-vessel record simply because they share the townland name.

## 6. What remains unknown

The accessible published summary does not provide:
- date of discovery;
- finder or landowner;
- exact sand-pit location or coordinates;
- number of individuals;
- skeletal elements represented;
- age or sex estimation;
- direct dating or archaeological period;
- evidence of articulation or original burial position;
- reason for the bones' presence in the sand pit;
- photographs, drawings or osteological report;
- current custody or condition.

A direct request for the NMI Topographical File 1989:53 is required to determine whether the original file contains any additional notes, correspondence, illustrations or later corrections.

## 7. Archive decision

**Research outcome:** NMI 1989:53 is a recorded Whitestown find of human skeletal remains from a sand pit across the road from the entrance to Rogerstown Hall. The reproduced NMI description says the bone was loose and there was no evidence for a grave.

**Production decision:** Documentation only pending duplicate check and primary-file inspection. Do not create a burial or cemetery object, assign a prehistoric or other date, infer an individual count, or add an exact map pin. If a catalogue record is later created, its object class must preserve the distinction “loose human bone / context unknown; no grave evidence recorded”.

**Next research target:** Investigate NMI 1946:438–443, listed in the same NMI Topographical Files appendix under Rush, to determine what objects or finds the grouped registrations represent. Keep this separate from Whitestown 1989:53.
