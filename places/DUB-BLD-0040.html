Vernon Lodge — 1885 carved limestone pedestrian entrance

41A Castle Avenue, at the junction with Kincora Avenue, Clontarf, Dublin 3

Freestanding carved ashlar limestone pedestrian entrance to Clontarf Castle, dated 1885 and now disused. NIAH records a Tudor-arched opening with a carved limestone surround and roll mouldings, a gable with crenellations, and a carved limestone date stone, family motto, coat of arms and crest. Octagonal piers with monolithic shafts and richly carved capitals flank the entrance; the structure is set into a snecked limestone boundary wall.

A highly crafted Gothic Revival entrance and a visible surviving element of the former Clontarf Castle demesne. NIAH identifies the coat of arms, crest and motto 'Vernon Semper Viret' as belonging to the Vernon family, which owned the castle for around 300 years.
