Dublin Tropical Fruit Company warehouse — Tropical Fruit Warehouse

30–32 Sir John Rogerson's Quay, Dublin 2

Attached double-gable-fronted fourteen-bay two-storey former warehouse built c.1890 for the Dublin Tropical Fruit Company, with its use for tropical-fruit imports recorded from 1892. The NIAH describes an M-profile pitched slate roof set perpendicular to the street with clerestory, terracotta ridge tiles, red-brick Dutch gables with decorative terracotta apex tiles, carved limestone cornice, brick pilasters, limestone string courses, coping and orb finials, Flemish-bond brickwork, polychrome and cogged brick string courses, segmental-headed openings and carved limestone keystones. The historic warehouse was later restored and integrated with a substantial modern office redevelopment, including elevated glazed office floors and a new building to the rear. The historic warehouse fabric must be distinguished from these later additions.

A prominent surviving late-Victorian docklands warehouse and a rare physical record of Dublin's tropical-fruit import trade. Its façade also preserves two earlier riverine keystones associated with Carlisle Bridge and sculptor Edward Smyth. The building's layered history includes the Hibernian Marine School site, the Tropical Fruit Company, later U2 management offices and contemporary adaptive reuse; these phases are documented by different sources and should not be conflated.
