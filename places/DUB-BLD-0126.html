British & Irish Steam Packet Company offices — The Shipping Office protected structure

20–24 Sir John Rogerson's Quay, at the junction with Lime Street, Dublin 2

Freestanding six-bay, two-storey former shipping-company offices with attic storey, built in 1909, with integral carriage arches to the end bays, shallow projections to the north frontage, a west wing wall and a triple-pile goods shed to the rear. NIAH records pitched slate roofs with terracotta ridge cresting, red-brick chimneystacks, a red-brick parapet with carved masonry capping, clerestories to the rear store roofs, Flemish-bond brickwork, carved limestone details, round-headed ground-floor windows, oculi, elliptical-headed carriage arches with terracotta keystones, cast-iron wheel stops, a loading platform and cobbled yard. The company moved its offices here in 1909 and the building was formally opened in 1910. The historic structure has since been integrated into the modern mixed-use development known as The Shipping Office; the modern glazed atrium and new office development are later additions and must not be mistaken for the 1909 building.

A surviving protected shipping-office building associated with the British & Irish Steam Packet Company, a major Irish Sea shipping operator established in Dublin in 1836. The building records the commercial and operational history of the port through its offices, carriage arches, rear goods sheds and loading infrastructure. Its classically influenced brick-and-stone façade was designed by W.H. Byrne & Sons. The historic structure now forms part of a contemporary redevelopment, illustrating adaptive reuse of a docklands industrial-era building.
