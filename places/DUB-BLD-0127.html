The Ferryman — former pair of Georgian houses, 35–36 Sir John Rogerson's Quay

35–36 Sir John Rogerson's Quay, at Cardiff Lane, Dublin 2

Corner-sited attached former pair of three-bay, four-storey houses, built c.1820 and now used as a public house and guest house. The National Inventory of Architectural Heritage records an M-profile hipped slate roof, red-brick chimneystacks, red-brick parapet with granite coping, Flemish-bond brick walls, square- and round-headed window openings, granite sills, surviving timber sliding-sash windows, and round-headed door openings with carved Portland-stone architrave and marigold lintel carvings. The two houses were originally separate. NIAH records Michael Scanlon and James Lynch as occupants in the 1850s; contemporary trade directories listed a grocer and shipbuilder at No. 35 and a ship smith at No. 36.

A rare surviving early nineteenth-century quay-side pair that preserves the scale, materials and commercial character of the historic Sir John Rogerson's Quay streetscape. Its carved stone doorcase, timber sash windows and diminishing fenestration are notable architectural details. The site also records the former mix of domestic, retail and maritime trades along the quay.
