Catholic Seamen's Institute — former Georgian house at 4 Sir John Rogerson's Quay

4 Sir John Rogerson's Quay, Dublin 2

Terraced two-bay, four-storey former house built c.1825. The National Inventory of Architectural Heritage records its later use as a charitable hostel and as the Catholic Seamen's Institute, founded by the Society of Saint Vincent de Paul. The recorded fabric includes a hipped corrugated-iron roof behind a brick parapet with granite coping, rendered chimneystacks, brown Flemish-bond brick walls, rendered quoins and platbands, and square-headed window openings with raised rendered reveals and masonry sills. NIAH records visible lettering on the first- and second-floor platbands and vestiges of the institute's sign on the fascia.

The building preserves evidence of the late-Georgian merchant-house group on Sir John Rogerson's Quay and its later adaptation for charitable support of seafarers. The documented sign remnants are a particularly valuable target for recording the building's social history.
