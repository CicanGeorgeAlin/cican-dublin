St Anne’s Park — double-arch bridge incorporating a hermitage

Naniken River and adjoining footpath, St Anne’s Park, Raheny, Dublin 5

A double-arch bridge carrying a road over the Naniken River and a pedestrian path, incorporating a small hermitage. The NIAH describes rubble-limestone spandrels and parapets with cobble coping, roughly squared limestone stringcourses, pointed arches over the river and southern footpath, and pointed-arch openings to the hermitage. A northern opening on the west elevation is recorded as blocked; the hermitage has a pointed-arch doorway to the pedestrian path.

A rare combined landscape structure serving both as a practical crossing and as a garden folly. The NIAH notes that it replaced an earlier timber bridge shown on the first-edition Ordnance Survey map, and that the hermitage reflects the nineteenth-century fashion for simulated rustic retreats.
