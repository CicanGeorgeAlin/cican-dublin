National College of Art and Design — former Thomas Street Fire Station

104–108 Thomas Street, Dublin 8, County Dublin, Ireland

Attached five-bay three-storey former fire station, built 1911, now in use as an art college. A glazed extension was added to the rear north elevation c.2009. The building has red-brick chimney stacks behind red-brick parapets with carved sandstone capping and a central panelled sandstone block flanked by decorative scrolls. Red brick and sandstone pilasters support a carved sandstone entablature; carved sandstone platband and cornice frame the first and ground floors. The façade includes timber sash windows, an arcaded ground floor with elliptical-headed arches, carved architrave surrounds, scrolled keystones, half-glazed timber panelled doors and spoked fanlights.

The NIAH appraisal identifies this as one of four early twentieth-century city fire stations designed by city architect Charles J. McCarthy and as Dublin’s first motorised fire station. The station was planned from 1898 by Chief Fire Officer Captain Purcell. The NIAH account records responses by its crew to fires during the 1913 Lock Out, at Magazine Fort during the 1916 Rising, at the Custom House in 1921 and at the Four Courts during the Civil War. The building now forms part of the National College of Art and Design campus and has been renamed after illustrator and stained-glass artist Harry Clarke.
