Saint James's Hospital — former workhouse linen factory

Saint James's Hospital campus, James's Street, Dublin 8, County Dublin, Ireland

Attached sixteen-bay two-storey former workhouse linen factory, built c.1730, with a recent full-width single-storey extension to the rear (west). Attached at the north end to the former workhouse master's house. Now in use as a third-level education department. Pitched slate roof, brown-brick chimneystacks and dressed calp-limestone walls. Square-headed windows have brown-brick surrounds and granite sills; small-pane timber casement and pivoting windows survive, with some eight-over-eight timber sash windows to ground floor. The main entrance has a recent glass door, steps and ramp; other openings include timber battened doors and a round-headed opening with fanlight.

Construction on the Saint James's site began in 1703 as the city workhouse. By 1730 it was used primarily as a foundling hospital and home for abandoned children. The linen factory was probably used to manufacture clothes and bedding for the workhouse. Along with the adjoining former master's house, it is one of two surviving eighteenth-century buildings identified on the campus by NIAH. Early fabric remains in the stonemasonry and timber windows. Extended and remodelled in 1999 by Moloney O'Beirne and Partners.
