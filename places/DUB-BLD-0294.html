Saint James's Hospital — former workhouse master's house

Saint James's Hospital campus, James's Street / South Circular Road, Dublin 8, County Dublin, Ireland

Attached five-bay three-storey former workhouse master's house, built c.1740, with a later three-storey flat-roofed rear return, attached at the south to the former linen factory. Now in third-level educational use. Hipped slate roof with gablets to north and south, lower hipped rear roofs and granite eaves course. Rubble calp-limestone walls, brown-brick chimneybreasts, rear arcade, square-headed windows with granite sills and surviving two-over-two timber sash windows. The north elevation has a round-arched second-floor window. The round-arched entrance has a carved granite Gibbsian surround and lintel, segmental-headed sidelights, timber-panelled door and plain fanlight.

The NIAH appraisal records that the site began as the city workhouse in 1703 and was used primarily as a foundling hospital by 1730. This former master's house and the adjoining linen factory are identified as the only surviving eighteenth-century buildings on the campus. Notable early features include its roof structure and carved granite door surround. Its survival is notable because most Irish workhouses post-date the Poor Law Unions established in 1838.
