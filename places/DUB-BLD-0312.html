National Library of Ireland, Kildare Street, Dublin 2, DUBLIN

National Library of Ireland, Kildare Street, Dublin 2, Ireland

Attached symmetrical E-plan library over a raised basement, built 1885–1890, facing south. The composition centres on a three-stage rotunda with a top storey set back, linked to two-storey blocks and flanking pavilions, with a semicircular Doric colonnade to the front. The exterior combines ashlar granite, channel-rusticated lower pavilion walls and yellow sandstone dressings. The rotunda and pavilions have elaborate classical detailing, including Corinthian columns, pediments, carved stonework, Venetian windows and balustrades. The NIAH record also documents richly decorated interiors, including stained glass, mosaic flooring, a bifurcated stone staircase and the domed main reading room.

The NIAH appraisal places the library within the major state and cultural complex on Kildare Street, opposite the National Museum and beside Leinster House. It links the building to the Dublin Science and Art Museum Act of 1877 and the nineteenth-century movement toward public benefit. Designed by Thomas Newenham Deane and his son Thomas Manly Deane after two competitions, it is assessed as an impressive composition whose granite, yellow sandstone, marble and composite-stone finishes enrich the classical ensemble. Its interior is noted for exceptionally well-preserved late-Victorian craftsmanship and its association with the 'Scylla and Charybdis' episode of Ulysses.
