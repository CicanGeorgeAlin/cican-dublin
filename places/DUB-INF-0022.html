George's Dock Lock — limestone lock to the River Liffey

South side of George's Dock, between the dock basin and River Liffey, Dublin 1

Limestone lock built around 1820 connecting George's Dock to the River Liffey. The NIAH records paired squared-coursed limestone ashlar lock walls, slightly battered and capped with granite at ground level, enclosing the lock to east and west. Timber and steel lock gates survive at the north end below a later steel-and-timber humpback footbridge. Granite steps lie beside the gates, with a pair of granite bollards at either end. Curved walls open northwards into George's Dock; the southern end is obscured by an enclosure below the Scherzer Bridges.

A distinct dock-engineering feature that should not be merged with George's Dock or the Scherzer bridges. The lock preserves the physical connection between the Georgian wet dock and the Liffey, including ashlar walls, gates, steps and mooring fittings.
