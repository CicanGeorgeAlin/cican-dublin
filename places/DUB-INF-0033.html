Dublin Port Diving Bell — Stoney's compressed-air quay-construction caisson

Sir John Rogerson's Quay, Dublin 2

Cast-iron and riveted plate-iron diving bell designed by Dublin Port engineer Bindon Blood Stoney and built by Grendon and Company of Drogheda. The NIAH describes a chamber approximately 23 feet square by 6.5 feet high, accessed by a vertical shaft with iron rungs and incorporating an air lock. The bell was lowered from a floating platform so workers could excavate and level the riverbed under compressed air before large precast concrete quay-wall blocks were placed. It was moved to Sir John Rogerson's Quay in 1989 and is mounted on a modern display structure.

An exceptionally important surviving artefact of nineteenth-century harbour engineering and compressed-air construction. Dublin Port's archive records its role in building the North Wall Extension and Alexandra Basin; the bell is now part of Dublin Port's Distributed Museum and a publicly accessible interpretive installation.
