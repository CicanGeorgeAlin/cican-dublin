St Anne’s Park — relocated nineteenth-century iron gate screen and granite piers

Entrance to a later garden within St Anne’s Park, Raheny, Dublin 5

A wrought- and cast-iron gate screen originally erected c.1850 and relocated c.1980 to form the entrance to a later garden. The NIAH describes a single-leaf cast-iron gate flanked by square-profile openwork piers topped with cast-iron urns, wrought-iron detailing on carved granite plinths, and matching railings with decorative scrolled brackets on a carved granite plinth wall terminating in rusticated granite piers with carved cornices.

A finely crafted surviving example of nineteenth-century estate ironwork, with an important relocation history. The ensemble combines cast and wrought iron, ornamental urn finials and carved granite, making it valuable for studying the decorative metalwork and boundary design of the estate.
