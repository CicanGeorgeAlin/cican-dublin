# Research batch 03 — Dublin 1 and Dublin 2 cast-iron street furniture

**Research date:** 24 October 2026  
**Scope:** NIAH-recorded post boxes and a historic municipal gas-lamp standard in Dublin 1 and Dublin 2.  
**Outcome:** Fourteen new archive records and fourteen official NIAH source entries added.

## New records

### DUB-STR-0013 — Early Free State twin post box, South Great George's Street
- NIAH 50910076; Circa 1930.
- Freestanding oval-plan cast-iron twin post box with moulded plinth, shallow domed cap, fluted cornice and paired letter slots. Raised POST OFFICE lettering sits between neck mouldings, with a P&T monogram on the central panel and curved doors to either side.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 315642, 233822.

### DUB-STR-0014 — Edward VII cast-iron pillar post box, Burgh Quay
- NIAH 50020294; Circa 1905.
- Freestanding cast-iron pillar post box with shallow domed cap, fluted rim, moulded neck and plinth. The front has a letter slot in a roll-moulded frieze, POST OFFICE lettering, an ER VII royal cipher and crown, and a Handyside, Derby & London maker's mark.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 316120, 234394.

### DUB-STR-0015 — Early Free State double post box, Dame Street
- NIAH 50020195; Circa 1930.
- Freestanding cast-iron double post box with oval plan, shallow domed cap, fluted cornice, moulded neck and plinth. Two letter slots are framed by raised POST OFFICE lettering; a P&T insignia occupies the central front panel, with curved doors on the side elevations.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 315762, 234060.

### DUB-STR-0016 — P&T double-opening post box with Áth Cliath lettering, Nassau Street
- NIAH 50100001; Circa 1930.
- Freestanding oval-plan cast-iron pillar box with double letter opening, shallow domed cap and moulded corona. It carries raised Post Office and Next Collection lettering, a P&T motif and enamel Áth Cliath (Dublin) lettering above the aperture. No maker's mark was visible in the NIAH record.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 315998, 233930.

### DUB-STR-0017 — Handyside cast-iron pillar post box, Townsend Street
- NIAH 50020308; Circa 1930.
- Freestanding cast-iron pillar post box with shallow domed cap, fluted rim, moulded neck, plinth and letter slot. Raised POST OFFICE lettering flanks the slot; the plinth bears A. Handyside, Derby & London maker's insignia.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 316154, 234259.

### DUB-STR-0018 — Victorian Handyside pillar post box, Mount Street Crescent
- NIAH 50100593; Circa 1900.
- Freestanding cylindrical cast-iron pillar post box with shallow domed cap, moulded corona and letter aperture flanked by raised POST OFFICE lettering. Its curved door carries a VR royal cipher and keyhole plate; the plinth bears the Andrew Handyside Derby & London maker's mark.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 317117, 233218.

### DUB-STR-0019 — Tonge & Taggart cast-iron pillar post box, South Richmond Street
- NIAH 50110429; Circa 1985.
- Freestanding cast-iron pillar post box with shallow domed cap, moulded neck and cornice. The south-facing door carries a Post logo and collection-notice panel; the rear north-facing panel bears the Tonge & Taggart Ltd Dublin foundry mark.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 315645, 232706.

### DUB-STR-0020 — Early Free State double post box, St Andrew Street
- NIAH 50910194; Circa 1940.
- Freestanding oval-plan cast-iron double post box on a plinth base. Curved door leaves carry raised P&T insignia; neck mouldings frame two letter slots beneath a dentillated frieze and shallow domed cap.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 315853, 233996.

### DUB-STR-0021 — George V cast-iron pillar post box, Westmoreland Street
- NIAH 50020222; Circa 1915.
- Freestanding cast-iron pillar post box with shallow domed cap, fluted cornice, moulded neck and plinth. The west-facing curved door bears a raised GR royal cipher and POST OFFICE lettering; the base carries the McDowall Steven & Co Ltd London & Glasgow maker's mark.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 315978, 234247.

### DUB-STR-0022 — Edward VII cast-iron pillar post box, Merrion Square
- NIAH 50100400; Circa 1905.
- Freestanding cylindrical cast-iron pillar post box with shallow domed cap, fluted cornice, moulded neck and plinth. The letter aperture is set in a raised frame above a curved door bearing Edward VII's royal cipher and crown; the plinth carries the Handyside, Derby & London maker's mark.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 316489, 233551.

### DUB-STR-0023 — Edward VII pillar post box, Fitzwilliam Place
- NIAH 50930153; Circa 1910.
- Freestanding cast-iron pillar post box with circular plan, moulded plinth, cornice and dentillated shallow domed cap. The curved door incorporates the letter slot, handle and keyhole plate, with ER VII / Post Office lettering and crown motif; the plinth bears a McDowall Steven & Co Ltd London and Glasgow maker's mark.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 316436, 232786.

### DUB-STR-0024 — Victorian anonymous cast-iron pillar post box, Ely Place
- NIAH 50930014; Circa 1880.
- Freestanding circular-plan cast-iron pillar post box with moulded plinth and cornice, shallow domed cap with toothed perimeter markings, and a curved door containing the letter slot, handle and raised Next Collection lettering. The plinth bears the McDowell Steven & Co Ltd London & Glasgow maker's mark. The NIAH record notes the unusual absence of a crown motif or royal cipher.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 316374, 233375.

### DUB-STR-0025 — Victorian Handyside pillar post box, Fitzwilliam Square South
- NIAH 50930229; Circa 1890.
- Freestanding circular-plan cast-iron pillar post box with moulded plinth and cornice, shallow domed cap with toothed perimeter markings, and a curved door bearing a raised VR insignia and keyhole plate. The plinth carries an A. Handyside & Co maker's mark.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 316402, 233065.

### DUB-STR-0026 — Victorian cast-iron triple gas-lamp standard, Amiens Street
- NIAH 50010116; Circa 1870; converted to electricity circa 1900.
- Freestanding cast-iron gas-lamp standard with an acanthus-detailed shaft on a round-plan pedestal. Decorative ironwork carries Dublin Corporation's motto and crest; three lamp brackets have hexagonal lanterns with moulded caps and crown motifs. NIAH records that it was converted to electricity circa 1900 and that the pedestal motto reads Obedientia Civium Urbis Felicitas.
- Published Irish Grid reference: 316652, 235032.


## Research observations

The post-box records preserve a useful sequence of Irish public postal design: Victorian VR examples, Edward VII and George V royal ciphers, early Free State P&T boxes with Gaelic lettering, bilingual Áth Cliath signage, and a late twentieth-century Tonge & Taggart Dublin foundry example. The Townsend Street box is explicitly noted by NIAH as having been relocated from its original site. The Ely Place box is notable for its lack of royal cipher.

The Amiens Street gas-lamp standard is a separate street-furniture discovery: NIAH dates it to c.1870, records three lanterns, Dublin Corporation crest and motto, and notes conversion from gas to electricity around 1900.
